Grilled corn is one of the easiest things to cook outdoors. It is also one of the easiest to get wrong.

Ask three cooks how to do it and you'll hear three answers. Husk on. Foil. Right on the grates.

All three work. This guide explains what each one does to the corn, how long each takes, and how to fix the usual problems.

## The Short Answer

Preheat the grill to medium or medium-high. Put the corn on, close the lid, and turn it every few minutes.

Corn in the husk takes about 15 to 22 minutes. Corn in foil takes about 13 to 20. Shucked corn on the grates takes about 8 to 15.

The corn is done when the kernels are bright yellow, plump, and tender. Butter it, salt it, and eat it hot.

## The Three Methods at a Glance

| Method | Prep | Time | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In the husk | Peel back, remove silk, re-cover, optional soak | 15 to 22 minutes | Juicy, lightly smoky, easy |
| In foil | Shuck, butter, wrap tightly | 13 to 20 minutes | Juiciest, buttery, little char |
| Shucked on the grates | Shuck, oil or butter | 8 to 15 minutes | Charred, smoky, faster |

## Start With Good Corn

Grilling cannot fix bad corn. Start with the freshest ears you can find.

Fresh corn is sweetest right after picking. Left at room temperature, its sugars slowly turn to starch, so keep it cold until you cook it.

Look at the husk first. It should be green, snug, and slightly damp. Small brown holes can mean bugs, and pink or red spots can mean mold.

Check the silk at the top. It should be soft and light-colored, not dry and black.

Plan on one or two ears per person. If you cook for one, our guide to [stocking a small kitchen for solo cooking](/blog/how-to-stock-a-small-kitchen-for-solo-cooking/) covers how to shop and cook in single portions without waste.

## Set Up the Grill

Method matters, but heat matters just as much. Corn wants medium to medium-high heat.

Too low and the kernels steam without browning. Too high and the outside chars before the middle warms through.

On a gas grill, preheat with the lid closed for about ten minutes. Turn the burners to medium or medium-high, roughly 400 to 425 degrees.

On a charcoal grill, build a two-zone fire. Pile coals on one side and leave the other side cooler. Test kitchens that cook corn in the husk suggest placing the stem ends toward the cooler side.

Clean the grates before you start. Old residue sticks to corn and adds off flavors.

## Method One: Grill Corn in the Husk

This is the easiest way, and it works on both gas and charcoal. The husk acts as a natural steamer.

1. Peel the husks back without pulling them off. Keep them attached at the base.
2. Pull off the silk and discard it.
3. Fold the husks back over the corn. Tie them with a strip of husk if you like.
4. Soak the ears in cold water for 10 to 15 minutes. This keeps the husks from burning.
5. Shake off extra water and place the corn on the grates.
6. Grill over medium to medium-high heat, covered, for about 15 to 22 minutes.
7. Turn every 5 minutes so the husks blacken evenly.
8. Peel back a small section to check. The kernels should look bright yellow.

Let the corn sit a minute, then peel the husks back and use them as a handle. Brush on butter while it's hot.

The husk method gives juicy corn with a hint of smoky flavor. It is milder than shucked corn and richer than foil.

## Method Two: Grill Corn in Foil

Foil is the most forgiving method. It is also the best for butter and seasoning.

1. Shuck the corn and remove the silk.
2. Rub each ear with softened butter, salt, and any seasoning.
3. Set each ear on a square of heavy-duty foil.
4. Roll the foil around the corn and twist the ends closed.
5. Grill over medium heat for about 13 to 20 minutes.
6. Turn the packets every few minutes.

The corn cooks in its own steam and the melted butter. Nothing dries out.

The tradeoff is flavor. Foil blocks the smoke, so you lose the grilled taste. If you want char, unwrap the ears for the last minute or two and set them right on the grates.

Use heavy-duty foil so it doesn't tear. [Reynolds Wrap Heavy Duty Aluminum Foil](https://www.amazon.com/Reynolds-Wrap-Heavy-Aluminum-Square/dp/B00QIT8T6E/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is a common pick and handles the heat well.

## Method Three: Grill Shucked Corn on the Grates

This method gives the most char and the most grilled flavor. It is also the fastest.

1. Shuck the corn and remove the silk.
2. Rub the ears with oil or softened butter.
3. Place them directly on a clean, hot grate.
4. Grill over medium-high heat for about 8 to 15 minutes.
5. Turn every 2 to 3 minutes so the kernels char evenly.
6. Pull the corn when it has spots of gold and brown.

Watch it closely. Shucked corn goes from perfect to dry in a few minutes.

Long-handled tongs make turning easy and keep your hands away from the heat. [OXO Good Grips 16-Inch Locking Tongs](https://www.amazon.com/OXO-Grips-16-Inch-Locking-Tongs/dp/B00004OCM3/?tag=kitchenpot-20) are a popular choice.

America's Test Kitchen found that grilling shucked corn can leave the kernels dry or rubbery. Their fix is a short soak in salted water before grilling. Read their full [grilled corn testing notes](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/6505-the-best-way-to-grill-corn) for the details.

## The Soaking Debate

Nobody agrees on soaking, and each side has a reason.

Food Network's how-to says to soak corn with the husks on for 10 to 15 minutes, so the husks don't ignite. It says shucked corn needs no soak. See their [guide to grilling corn](https://www.foodnetwork.com/grilling/grilling-central-how-tos/how-to-grill-corn) for their timings.

Some cooks skip soaking entirely. They say the husks steam the corn just fine on their own.

Others soak longer, from 30 minutes to a couple of hours, for extra moisture.

America's Test Kitchen goes the other way. They shuck the corn first, then brine it in salted water for about 30 minutes.

A simple rule works for most cooks. Soak for 10 to 15 minutes if you grill in the husk. Skip the soak for foil and for shucked corn.

## How Long to Grill Corn on the Cob

Time depends on the method and the heat. Use these ranges as a starting point.

* **In the husk:** about 15 to 22 minutes over medium to medium-high heat.
* **In foil:** about 13 to 20 minutes over medium heat.
* **Shucked on the grates:** about 8 to 15 minutes over medium to medium-high heat.
* **Frozen, pre-cooked corn in buttered foil:** about 8 to 10 minutes, just to heat through and pick up color.

Big ears take longer than small ones. Hotter grills take less time.

## How to Tell When It's Done

Timers are guides. Your eyes and a knife are better.

Look for kernels that are bright yellow and plump. They should look glossy, not dull or shriveled.

Pierce a kernel with a paring knife. It should feel tender, with no resistance.

If you cook in the husk, peel back a small section and check the color down to the base of the cob.

A few dark or charred kernels are normal. A whole ear of black kernels means it went too long.

## How to Grill Corn Without a Grill

No grill, no problem. Two indoor methods come close.

On the stove, use a grill pan or a cast iron skillet. Shuck the corn, brush it with oil, and cook it over medium to medium-high heat for about 8 to 10 minutes. Turn often, until it's lightly charred in spots.

## Grill Corn on a Griddle

A flat griddle works too. It gives even browning instead of grill marks.

Shuck the corn, oil it, and turn it every couple of minutes. It cooks quickly, since the whole surface touches the metal.

## Camping and Campfire Corn

Corn is a campfire classic. Foil packets and husk-on ears both work over coals.

Keep the ears away from direct flame. Set them on a grate or beside the coals so the outside doesn't burn before the inside cooks.

## Flavor: Butters, Spices, and Toppings

Plain butter and salt are great. A few simple upgrades make corn special.

Flavored butter is the easiest. Mix softened butter with garlic, herbs, chili flakes, or lime zest, and brush it on right after grilling.

For a street-corn style, top hot corn with a creamy sauce, chili powder, lime, and crumbled cheese.

Parmesan, garlic, and parsley make an Italian version.

Add seasoning after the corn comes off the grill, unless you're using foil. Foil lets you butter and season before cooking.

## Why Your Grilled Corn Turned Out Wrong

Most problems have simple causes.

**Tough or chewy corn.** The corn may be old, or the grill may be too cool. Fresh corn cooked at medium-high heat stays tender.

**Dry corn.** It probably cooked too long, or it was shucked and left over direct heat. Use foil, cook it in the husk, or brine it briefly.

**Burnt outside, raw inside.** The heat was too high. Turn it down to medium or move the corn to the cooler side.

**Husks caught fire.** They weren't soaked. Soak for 10 to 15 minutes and keep the ears away from open flame.

**No grilled flavor.** Foil or the husk blocked the smoke. Finish the ears on the grates for a minute.

**Bland corn.** Salt it well, and don't skip the butter or a splash of lime.

## Make Ahead, Store, and Reheat

Grilled corn keeps well. That makes it handy for cookouts.

Let the ears cool. Wrap each one in foil and refrigerate.

To reheat, place the foil-wrapped ears on a 350 degree grill for about 5 to 6 minutes. An oven at 350 degrees takes about 8 minutes.

Grilled corn stores in the fridge for several days. Cut kernels keep in an airtight container.

## Serve It Off the Cob

Grilled kernels work in salads, salsas, and sides.

Let the ear cool a bit. Stand it on its end in a shallow bowl, and slice down the cob with a sharp knife, cutting away from your body.

Toss the kernels with tomatoes, herbs, and lime for a quick salad.

## Tools Worth Having

You don't need much. A few basics make grilling corn easier.

Long tongs turn the ears safely. Heavy-duty foil makes packets that don't tear. A basting brush spreads butter evenly.

A grill pan covers rainy days. And a good cast iron pan doubles for dozens of other meals.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

* **Grilling on low heat.** Corn steams without browning.
* **Skipping the clean grate.** Old residue sticks to the corn.
* **Soaking shucked corn.** It isn't needed and can make it soggy.
* **Walking away from shucked corn.** It dries out in minutes.
* **Salting foil packets with too much butter and not sealing them.** Leaks cause flare-ups.

## **The Bottom Line**

Corn is easy on the grill. Pick a method. Husk gives juicy. Foil gives buttery. Shucked gives char. Use medium heat and turn often. Soak only if the husk stays on. Pull it when the kernels turn bright yellow and tender.
