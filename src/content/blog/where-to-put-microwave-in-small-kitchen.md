Not the fridge top. That is where most people put the microwave in a small kitchen, and it is the worst spot: too high, poorly vented and wobbly.

A microwave is bulky, it throws heat and it wants prime counter space. You have more choices than you think: a cart, a shelf, a wall bracket, over the stove or a drawer. Below we rank all seven and show how to set each one up safely.

**What to do first:** Look for a counter corner you never prep on. That dead space is the best home for most microwaves, with a sturdy rolling cart or open shelf close behind. Over-the-range and built-in models save the most room but need an installer and their own circuit. Wherever it lands, give it the clearance in the manual, keep it out of closed cabinets and keep its bottom at or below shoulder height.

## Where Should a Microwave Go in a Small Kitchen?

A microwave should go where it gets open air, sits at or below shoulder height, plugs straight into a wall outlet and has a landing spot beside or below it. Those four rules matter more than the exact location.

- **Air:** The microwave pushes out heat and steam through vents. Those vents need open space, usually a few inches. Your manual lists the exact numbers for your model.
- **Height:** You will lift bowls of boiling soup and steaming water out of it. That should happen at chest level or below, never over your face.
- **Power:** A microwave pulls a lot of current. It needs a grounded wall outlet, not a power strip or extension cord.
- **Landing spot:** You need somewhere to set a hot dish down right away. Our rule is a clear 15-inch spot beside or below the microwave.

Any place that meets all four is a good place. If you are still planning the whole room, start with our [small kitchen layout ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-layout-ideas/) and come back here.

## The 7 Microwave Spots Ranked

Ranked on safety, space saved, cost and renter-friendliness:

| Rank | Spot | Saves counter? | Renter-friendly? | Main watch-out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Counter corner | No, but uses dead space | Yes | Needs side and back air |
| 2 | Rolling cart | Yes | Yes | Weight limit and locked wheels |
| 3 | Open shelf | Yes | Yes, as a freestanding unit | Shelf strength and depth |
| 4 | Over the range | Yes | Owners only | Weak venting and high reach |
| 5 | Built-in or drawer | Yes | Owners only | Cost and electrical work |
| 6 | Wall-mount brackets | Yes | Sometimes | Must hit studs, door swing |
| 7 | On top of the fridge | Yes | Yes | Too high, blocked vents, shaking |

## Spot 1: Counter Corner, the Easiest Good Spot

A counter corner is the best starting point for most people. The back of a corner is too deep to prep in comfortably, so it is often dead space anyway.

At 36 inches high, the counter puts the microwave at a perfect height. You lift food straight out at chest level.

### How to Set Up a Counter Corner Microwave

1. Measure the microwave, then add the clearance from the manual on the sides, back and top.
2. Check the depth. A standard 24 to 25-inch counter fits most compact and mid-size models with room behind.
3. Check the top. Upper cabinets sit about 18 inches above the counter, which leaves air above most models.
4. Plug it into a wall outlet right there, with no cord crossing the sink or stove.
5. Keep the counter in front of it clear as your landing spot.

## Spot 2: Rolling Cart, Best When Counter Space Is Gone

When you have no counter to spare, go with a sturdy cart. It holds the microwave at about counter height and adds storage underneath.

Pick a cart with these features:

- **A top shelf that holds the weight.** Most countertop microwaves weigh about 25 to 45 pounds. Buy a cart rated well above that.
- **A top deep enough for the whole microwave.** Feet must sit fully on the surface, with no overhang.
- **Locking wheels.** Lock at least two wheels before you open the door. A cart that rolls as you pull the door is how bowls get dropped.
- **An open frame.** Open sides and back keep air moving. Skip carts with closed boxes around the top shelf.

Park the cart beside an outlet and leave it there. Use the shelves below for plates, mugs and dry snacks.

## Spot 3: Open Shelf (Can You Put a Microwave on a Shelf?)

Yes, you can put a microwave on a shelf, as long as the shelf is strong, deep and open. It frees the counter without lifting the microwave too high.

The easiest version for renters is a freestanding shelving unit with one shelf at about counter height. Owners can add a wall shelf just above the counter, or use an open cubby at the end of an island.

### What Makes a Shelf Safe for a Microwave

Depth is the first problem. Most wall shelves are 12 inches deep, like upper cabinets, and most microwaves are deeper. Choose a shelf deeper than the microwave, with extra room for the back vents.

Strength is the second. Fasten a wall shelf into studs, not drywall anchors. Studs are usually 16 inches apart, center to center, so a 24-inch shelf can catch two.

Air is the third. Keep the sides, top and back open to the space the manual lists.

## Spot 4: Over the Range, a Big Space Saver With a Weak Vent

An over-the-range microwave, or OTR, hangs above the stove in place of a range hood. Most are 30 inches wide. It frees the counter and doubles as a stove light and fan.

Only a microwave built for over-the-range use can go there. A regular countertop model on a shelf above the stove is a fire risk.

### The Trade-Offs of an Over-the-Range Microwave

The fan in an OTR is usually weaker than a real range hood, and its shape catches less smoke. If you sear, stir-fry or cook with a lot of oil, a hood does a better job. ## Spot 5: Built-In or Microwave Drawer, Best for a Remodel

A built-in microwave sits inside a cabinet opening with a trim kit. A microwave drawer slides out from under the counter like a regular drawer. Both free the counter and look clean.

The microwave drawer is the safest height of all. You lift food straight up and out, never over your head. Kids can reach it too, so turn on the child lock if you have small children.

### Built-In Is Not the Same as In a Cabinet

A true built-in uses a trim kit made for that exact model. The kit sends air out through the front, so the microwave can breathe inside the cabinet walls. Without that kit, it will overheat.

Both options are for owners and often need a dedicated circuit. Our [small kitchen remodel ideas on a budget](/blog/small-kitchen-remodel-ideas-on-a-budget/) show how to fit them in without blowing the budget.

## Spot 6: Wall-Mount Brackets, for Walls With Nothing Else

Microwave wall brackets hold the unit on two arms. They suit a tiny kitchen whose only free space is a bare wall. They rank low for good reasons:

- **They must go into studs.** A loaded microwave pulls hard on the bracket every time you open the door. Drywall alone will not hold it.
- **The door swing needs room.** Check that the door opens fully without hitting a cabinet, window frame or light.
- **Renters often cannot drill.** Large screws into studs leave holes your lease may not allow.

Mount over counter, never over the sink or stove, so the counter becomes your landing spot.

## Spot 7: On Top of the Fridge, and Why It Is Usually a Bad Idea

You can, but we strongly advise against it. It is the most common microwave mistake in tiny kitchens:

- **It is too high.** Most full-size fridges stand well above shoulder height, often 5 to 6 feet tall. You end up lifting boiling water or soup above your face.
- **It blocks heat on both sides.** Many fridges release heat at the top and back. A microwave sitting there traps that heat, and the fridge works harder. The microwave's own vents get blocked by the cabinet above or the wall behind.
- **It shakes.** Every door slam nudges the microwave toward the edge.

A short under-counter mini fridge is a different case, since its top sits near counter height. Check its manual for a weight limit and keep the vents clear. Our guide to [organizing a small fridge](/blog/organize-a-small-fridge/) covers what that top surface can safely hold.

## Can a Microwave Go in a Cabinet?

A microwave should never run inside a closed cabinet. A closed box traps its heat and steam. The microwave overheats and wears out early, and moisture swells the wood.

The only exceptions are a true built-in with its matching trim kit, or an open cubby that meets every clearance in the manual. A cabinet with the door removed can work if the opening is large enough to give that clearance.

### What About an Appliance Garage?

An appliance garage is a small counter cabinet with a roll-up door. It is fine for storing a microwave, but not for running one with the door down. Our rule: the door goes fully up before you press start.

The same goes for pantry cabinets with doors that slide back. For other ideas on making cabinets work harder, see our [small kitchen cabinet organization ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-cabinet-organization-ideas/).

## Safety Rules for Any Microwave Spot

These rules apply no matter where yours ends up.

| Rule | What to do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Ventilation clearance | Follow the manual for top, sides and back | Trapped heat damages the microwave and nearby cabinets |
| Never in a closed cabinet | Run it only with open air around it | Heat and steam build up with nowhere to go |
| Height | Bottom no higher than about shoulder height | You never lift hot liquids over your face |
| Landing spot | A clear 15-inch spot beside or below it | Hot dishes go down right away |
| Outlet | Grounded wall outlet, no power strips or extension cords | Microwaves draw a lot of current |
| Away from water | Not directly beside the sink spray zone | Water and electricity do not mix |
| Away from the stove | Only an OTR model goes above a range | Burner heat and grease damage countertop models |

### How High Should a Microwave Be?

The bottom of the microwave should sit no higher than about your shoulder height. Measure your own shoulder rather than guessing. If more than one person cooks, set it for the shortest one.

Counter height, at 36 inches, is ideal for most people. A drawer under the counter is fine too, but skip floor-level shelves that make you bend with hot food.

## Dedicated Outlet and Circuit Basics

A countertop microwave can pull most of a standard kitchen circuit's capacity. Add a kettle or air fryer on the same circuit, and the breaker trips.

- **Outlet vs circuit.** An outlet is the plug in the wall. A circuit is the wire and breaker that feed one or more outlets. Two outlets side by side often share one circuit.
- **Plug straight into the wall.** No extension cords and no power strips. They are not made for that much steady current and can overheat.
- **Use a grounded, three-prong outlet.** Never cut off or bend the third prong.
- **Watch for trips.** If the breaker trips when the microwave and kettle run together, they share a circuit. Run them one at a time, or move one to an outlet on a different circuit.
- **Dedicated circuits for installed models.** Installed microwaves usually need their own circuit, which is a job for a licensed electrician.

To find which outlets share a circuit, flip one breaker off and see which outlets go dead. Our guide on [how to reduce kitchen energy use in a small apartment](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/) explains more about what your appliances draw.

## Where to Put the Microwave in a Small Galley Kitchen

In a galley, keep the microwave out of the narrow aisle. Our picks, in order:

1. **The end of one run,** farthest from the entry, where it does not crowd the main prep stretch.
2. **A wall shelf at the closed end,** if the galley ends in a wall instead of a window.
3. **Over the range,** if you own the place and the stove is on the run with fewer cabinets.

Avoid placing it right across from the stove. Put it on the same side as the fridge if you can, so cold food, prep and reheating stay together. Our [galley kitchen ideas](/blog/galley-kitchen-ideas/) cover the rest of the layout.

## Where to Put the Microwave in a Tiny Kitchen or Studio

In a studio with a few feet of counter, here is how we would decide:

- **Counter under 4 feet long:** Keep the counter for prep. Put the microwave on a cart or a freestanding shelving unit beside it.
- **No floor space for a cart:** Use a strong wall shelf above the counter end, below shoulder height.
- **No pantry:** A shelving unit can hold the microwave on one shelf and food on the others. ## How Do You Hide a Microwave in a Small Kitchen?

You can hide a microwave without breaking the no-closed-cabinet rule. Here are the methods we like:

- **Go low.** A lower open shelf or the bottom shelf of a cart sits below eye level, so you barely notice it.
- **Use a curtain.** A short fabric curtain on a tension rod hides an open shelf. Pull it fully aside before you cook and leave it open until the microwave cools.
- **Match the finish.** A white microwave in a white kitchen, or a black one against dark cabinets, fades into the background. Our [small kitchen colors](/blog/small-kitchen-colors/) guide helps you pick a scheme that hides appliances.
- **Choose a drawer model in a remodel.** It disappears completely below the counter.

## Where to Put the Microwave and Air Fryer in a Small Kitchen

Both get hot, so each needs open air, a safe height and its own wall outlet.

- **Never stack them.** Do not put an air fryer on top of the microwave, or the other way around. Microwaves often vent through the top, and air fryers blow hot air out the back.
- **Keep one out, store one.** Leave the one you use most out. Store the other in a low cabinet once it cools.
- **Separate their outlets.** If they share a circuit, run them one at a time.

## Alternatives: Do You Need a Separate Microwave?

The best way to free space is to own one fewer appliance. Ask whether another machine can do the microwave's job.

| Option | Replaces | Good at | Weak at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Microwave with air fry mode | Microwave and air fryer | Reheating plus crisping in one box | Smaller air fry capacity, cleanup inside |
| Microwave with convection | Microwave and small oven | Reheating, small bakes, roasting | Pricier, needs more care to clean |
| Toaster oven | Microwave for most reheating, toaster | Crisp leftovers, toast, small bakes | Slower for soup and drinks |
| Stovetop only | Microwave | Soups, rice, steaming veg | Takes longer, more dishes |

## Placement Mistakes That Waste the Most Space

Each of these costs you counter, airflow or a safe reach:

- **Microwave on top of the fridge.** Too high, poorly vented and shaky. Move it.
- **Pushing it flat against the wall.** The back vents need air. Pull it forward to the clearance in the manual.
- **A shelf that is too shallow.** A 12-inch shelf leaves a deep microwave hanging off the front.
- **Blocking the walkway.** A cart in the middle of a narrow aisle is a trip hazard while you carry hot food.

## How to Pick Your Spot in 10 Minutes

1. **Measure the microwave** and add the clearance from the manual.
2. **Measure your shoulder height.** The microwave bottom should sit no higher.
3. **Find your outlets,** and note which share a circuit with the kettle.
4. **List the spots that fit** the total size, sit below your shoulder and reach an outlet without a cord.
5. **Pick the highest-ranked spot** that also leaves a 15-inch landing space.
6. **Check the walkway.** Your main aisle should stay at 42 inches for one cook if possible, and never under 36 inches.
7. **Live with it for a week.** If you keep crossing the room with hot bowls, move it closer to the prep area.

Once it has a home, clean the space around it on a schedule.
