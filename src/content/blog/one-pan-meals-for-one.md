Plenty of solo cooks think a proper dinner needs three pots going at once. It doesn't. One pan meals for one give you protein, vegetables and a starch from a single skillet, sheet pan or pot.

What changes is timing, not equipment. Dense food goes in first, quick food goes in last, and the pan never gets crowded. Below are ten recipes with amounts, temperatures and times, plus the pans that make them easy.

**Short answer:** Reach for a 10-inch skillet, a 9-by-13-inch quarter sheet pan or a 3-quart pot, because nearly every dinner for one fits in one of those. Leave space between pieces so they brown. Then check with a thermometer: chicken at 165°F, ground meat at 160°F, fish at 145°F. One pan in, one pan to wash.

## What a Single Pan Saves You in a Small Kitchen

For a solo cook in a tight space, cooking in a single vessel adds up fast:

- **One burner, not three.** A two-burner stove or a single induction plate is enough. If you only have two burners, our guide on [cooking a full meal with only two burners](/blog/how-to-cook-a-full-meal-with-only-two-burners/) shows how to juggle more.
- **Less counter space.** You prep into the pan, not into five bowls.
- **One pan to wash.** In a tiny sink, that matters every single night.
- **Right-sized portions.** A small pan holds about one or two servings, so you waste less. Pair this with our tips to [cook for one without wasting food](/blog/cook-for-one-without-wasting-food/).

The trick is timing. Everything goes into the same pan, but not at the same moment.

## What Is the Best Pan for One-Pan Meals?

You need three pans at most, and many solo cooks only need two. Here is how they compare.

| Pan | Best size for one | Best for | Oven-safe? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skillet (cast iron or carbon steel) | 10 inches | Searing meat, crispy potatoes, eggs, oven finishes | Yes |
| Nonstick skillet | 8 to 10 inches | Eggs, fish, delicate food | Usually, at moderate heat |
| Saute pan with lid | 3 quarts | One-pot pasta, braises, saucy dinners | Most stainless ones |
| Small Dutch oven | 3 quarts | Rice dishes, chili, soup, stews | Yes |
| Quarter sheet pan | About 9 x 13 inches | Roasting a full dinner in the oven or toaster oven | Yes |

A skillet has sloped sides. A saute pan has straight, taller sides and a lid, so it holds liquid. If you are unsure which you own, our explainer on the [difference between a skillet and a frying pan](/blog/difference-between-skillet-and-frying-pan/) clears it up.

## The One-Pan Formula: Build Any Dinner for One

Every recipe below follows this pattern. Learn it and you can cook from whatever is in the fridge.

1. **Heat the pan first.** Give it 2 to 3 minutes over medium heat. Food that hits a cold pan sticks and steams.
2. **Start the slowest food.** Dense items like potatoes, carrots and raw rice go first.
3. **Add the protein.** Sear it where it touches the hot pan, or nestle it in once the slow food is part-cooked.
4. **Add quick vegetables last.** Spinach, peas, zucchini and asparagus need only a few minutes.
5. **Finish with flavor.** A squeeze of lemon, a spoon of butter, fresh herbs or a splash of soy sauce.

Use this timing table to plan your own combinations. Times are for a stove at medium to medium-high heat, or a 425°F oven.

| Food (one portion) | Cut size | Stove time | 425°F oven time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potatoes | 3/4-inch cubes | 15 to 18 min | 25 to 30 min |
| Sweet potato | 3/4-inch cubes | 12 to 15 min | 20 to 25 min |
| Carrots | 1/2-inch coins | 10 to 12 min | 20 to 25 min |
| Broccoli | Bite-size florets | 5 to 7 min | 12 to 15 min |
| Bell pepper and onion | 1/2-inch strips | 8 to 10 min | 15 to 20 min |
| Green beans, asparagus | Whole, trimmed | 4 to 6 min | 10 to 12 min |
| Spinach, kale | Torn | 1 to 3 min | Not recommended |
| Boneless chicken thighs | Whole | 10 to 12 min | 18 to 22 min |
| Salmon fillet, 6 oz | 1 inch thick | 8 to 10 min | 10 to 12 min |
| Shrimp | Large, peeled | 3 to 4 min | 6 to 8 min |

## One Skillet Meals for One

These four recipes use a 10-inch skillet. Each makes one generous serving.

### 1. Lemon-Garlic Chicken Thighs with Potatoes and Green Beans

**You need:** 2 boneless, skinless chicken thighs (about 10 ounces total), 1 medium Yukon Gold potato, 1 handful green beans, 2 cloves garlic, 1/2 lemon, 1 tablespoon oil, salt and pepper.

1. Cut the potato into 3/4-inch cubes. Heat the oil over medium heat. Add the potato, salt it and cover. Cook 8 minutes, stirring once.
2. Pat the chicken dry and season it. Push the potatoes to the edge. Lay the thighs in the center.
3. Cook the chicken 5 to 6 minutes without moving it. Flip and cook 4 to 5 minutes more.
4. Add the green beans and sliced garlic around the chicken. Cook 4 minutes, stirring the vegetables.
5. Check the thickest thigh. It must read at least 165°F. Squeeze the lemon over everything and serve from the pan.

### 2. Sausage, Peppers and Onions

**You need:** 2 raw Italian sausages, 1 bell pepper, 1/2 onion, 1 tablespoon oil, a splash of water, optional hoagie roll.

1. Brown the sausages in the oil over medium heat, about 5 minutes, turning them.
2. Add the sliced pepper and onion around them. Add 2 tablespoons of water and cover.
3. Cook 10 minutes, turning once. Uncover for the last 3 minutes so the vegetables brown.
4. Pork sausage is done at 160°F. Chicken or turkey sausage needs 165°F.

For the oven version, see our guide on [how to cook sausages in the oven](/blog/how-to-cook-sausages-in-the-oven/).

### 3. Shakshuka for One

**You need:** 1 cup canned crushed tomatoes, 1/4 onion, 1 clove garlic, 1/2 teaspoon cumin, 1/2 teaspoon paprika, 2 eggs, a little feta, bread for dipping.

Use the 8-inch skillet here. A small pan keeps the sauce deep enough to hold the eggs.

1. Soften the chopped onion in 2 teaspoons of oil, 4 minutes. Add garlic and spices for 30 seconds.
2. Add the tomatoes. Simmer 5 minutes until slightly thick.
3. Make two hollows and crack in the eggs. Cover and cook on low for 5 to 7 minutes.
4. Cook until the whites are fully set. Firm yolks are the safest choice for kids, older adults and anyone pregnant. Top with feta.

### 4. Shrimp and Broccoli Fried Rice

**You need:** 1 cup cold cooked rice, 4 ounces peeled shrimp, 1 cup small broccoli florets, 1 egg, 1 green onion, 1 tablespoon soy sauce, 1 tablespoon oil.

1. Heat the oil over medium-high heat. Stir-fry the broccoli 3 minutes with a splash of water.
2. Add the shrimp. Cook 2 minutes per side, until pink and opaque all the way through.
3. Push everything aside. Scramble the egg in the empty space.
4. Add the rice and soy sauce. Press it flat and leave it 2 minutes to crisp, then stir.

Cold rice from the fridge fries better than fresh rice. If you cook rice on the weekend, our [easy meal prep ideas for one person](/blog/easy-meal-prep-ideas-for-one-person-in-a-small-kitchen/) show how to plan around it. Cooked rice keeps 3 to 4 days in the fridge.

A thin, flexible turner makes skillet cooking easier. The [Misen Fish Silicone Spatula](https://www.amazon.com/Misen-Fish-Spatula-Silicone-Spatulas/dp/B09WGL9TXK/?tag=kitchenpot-20) slides under eggs, fish and crispy potatoes without scratching nonstick pans.

## Can You Make a Sheet-Pan Dinner for One?

Yes, but not on a full-size baking sheet. One portion on a big pan spreads too thin, and the juices burn on the empty metal.

Use a quarter sheet pan, about 9 by 13 inches. The [Nordic Ware Natural Aluminum Quarter Sheet](https://www.amazon.com/Nordic-Ware-Natural-Aluminum-Quarter-Sheet/dp/B000RYPHRK/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is the one we recommend. ### 5. Salmon with Asparagus and Baby Potatoes

**You need:** 1 salmon fillet (about 6 ounces), 6 baby potatoes, 6 to 8 asparagus spears, 1 tablespoon olive oil, 1/2 lemon, salt, pepper and dill.

1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Halve the potatoes, toss them with half the oil and salt, and roast 15 minutes.
2. Push the potatoes to one end. Add the salmon, skin side down, and the asparagus.
3. Brush both with the rest of the oil. Add lemon slices on the fish.
4. Roast 10 to 12 minutes, until the salmon flakes and reads 145°F in the center.

### 6. Chicken Thighs with Chickpeas and Sweet Potato

**You need:** 2 boneless chicken thighs, 1 small sweet potato, 1/2 can chickpeas (drained and patted dry), 1 tablespoon oil, 1 teaspoon smoked paprika, 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder.

1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Cube the sweet potato and toss it, the chickpeas and the thighs with the oil and spices.
2. Spread everything in one layer with space between pieces.
3. Roast 20 to 25 minutes, turning the vegetables once.
4. The chicken is done at 165°F. Serve with a spoon of plain yogurt.

The other half-can of chickpeas keeps 3 to 4 days in a sealed container.

### 7. Gnocchi with Cherry Tomatoes and Italian Sausage

**You need:** 1/2 package shelf-stable gnocchi (about 8 ounces), 1 cup cherry tomatoes, 1 raw Italian sausage (cut into 1-inch pieces), 1/2 red onion, 1 tablespoon oil, basil and parmesan.

1. Heat the oven to 425°F. Toss the uncooked gnocchi, tomatoes, onion wedges and sausage pieces with the oil.
2. Roast 20 to 25 minutes, stirring halfway, until the gnocchi are golden and the tomatoes burst.
3. Check a sausage piece. It must reach 160°F, or 165°F for poultry sausage.
4. Top with basil and grated parmesan.

Most store-bought recipes serve four. Our guide to [scaling down recipes for one](/blog/scale-down-recipes-for-one/) shows how to cut them without guessing.

## One Pot Meals for One

These three recipes use a 3-quart saute pan or Dutch oven. The tall sides hold liquid, and the lid traps steam to cook rice and pasta.

### 8. One-Pot Tomato Garlic Pasta

**You need:** 4 ounces short pasta (penne or rotini), 1 cup cherry tomatoes, 2 cloves garlic and 1/4 onion. Add 2 cups water, 1 tablespoon olive oil, 1/2 teaspoon salt, parmesan and basil.

1. Put everything except the cheese and basil in the cold saute pan.
2. Bring to a boil over high heat. Lower to a strong simmer.
3. Cook 9 to 11 minutes, stirring often, until the pasta is tender. Add a splash of water if it gets dry.
4. The starch thickens the water into a sauce. Stir in the parmesan and basil off the heat.

Add spinach in the last minute, or drained tuna or white beans for protein.

### 9. Chicken and Rice

**You need:** 1 boneless chicken thigh, 1/2 cup long-grain white rice, 1 cup chicken broth, 1/4 onion, 1/2 cup frozen peas, 1 teaspoon oil, salt, pepper and paprika.

1. Season the chicken. Brown it in the oil in the Dutch oven over medium-high heat, 3 minutes per side. Lift it out.
2. Soften the chopped onion in the same pot, 2 minutes. Stir in the rice for 1 minute.
3. Add the broth and bring it to a boil. Set the chicken on top and cover tightly.
4. Cook on low for 18 minutes. Scatter the peas over, cover again and rest 5 minutes off the heat.
5. Check the chicken. It must read 165°F.

If you would rather hand rice to an appliance, see [how to cook rice in a rice cooker](/blog/how-to-cook-rice-in-a-rice-cooker/).

### 10. Three-Bean Beef Chili (Makes 3 Portions)

**You need:** 1/2 pound ground beef, 1/2 onion, 2 cloves garlic, 1 tablespoon chili powder and 1 teaspoon cumin. Add one 15-ounce can each of kidney beans, black beans and diced tomatoes.

1. Brown the beef in the Dutch oven over medium-high heat, breaking it up, 6 to 8 minutes. Ground beef must reach 160°F.
2. Add onion and garlic. Cook 3 minutes. Stir in the spices for 30 seconds.
3. Add the drained beans and the tomatoes with their juice. Simmer, partly covered, for 20 minutes.
4. Eat one portion tonight. Cool and pack the other two (see the freezer section below).

A small pot of chili is one of our favorite [cheap meals for one person](/blog/cheap-meals-for-one-person/).

For a smaller, lighter pot, the [Tramontina Signature Tri-Ply Clad 2-Quart Covered Saucepan](https://www.amazon.com/Tramontina-Signature-Tri-Ply-Clad-2-Quart-Sauce-Pan/dp/B00JAQ4DNU/?tag=kitchenpot-20) handles half-batches of chili, soup or rice for one.

## Safe Doneness Temperatures for One-Pan Dinners

Color and cooking time do not tell you if meat is safe. A thermometer does, and it stops thin single portions from drying out.

| Food | Safe internal temperature |
|---|---|
| Chicken and turkey, whole pieces or ground | 165°F |
| Ground beef, pork and lamb | 160°F |
| Pork and chicken sausage | 160°F pork, 165°F poultry |
| Beef, pork and lamb steaks, chops and roasts | 145°F, then rest 3 minutes |
| Fish and shellfish | 145°F, or until opaque and flaky |
| Egg dishes | 160°F, or until whites and yolks are firm |
| Leftovers, reheated | 165°F |

Push the probe into the thickest part, away from bone and away from the hot pan. The [ThermoPro TP19H Digital Meat Thermometer](https://www.amazon.com/ThermoPro-TP19H/dp/B07XXSYLL8/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is a budget instant-read with a folding probe and a backlit display. If you want a faster reading and a magnet to stick it on the fridge, spend more on the [Lavatools Javelin PRO Duo](https://www.amazon.com/Lavatools-Javelin-PRO-Duo/dp/B07D3MBSZV/?tag=kitchenpot-20).

Raw chicken keeps only 1 to 2 days in the fridge. Our guide on [how long chicken lasts in the fridge](/blog/how-long-does-chicken-last-in-the-fridge/) covers cooked chicken too. Store raw meat on the bottom shelf in a tray or sealed container.

## How Do You Keep One-Pan Meals From Getting Soggy?

Soggy food is steamed food. Steam builds up when water cannot escape.

- **Dry everything.** Pat chicken, fish and rinsed beans with a paper towel before they hit the pan.
- **Leave space.** Pieces should not touch. In a 10-inch skillet, that means two thighs and one cup of vegetables, not a heaping pile.
- **Preheat.** Put oil in a hot pan, not a cold one. It should shimmer before food goes in.
- **Do not move it too soon.** Let meat sit 4 to 5 minutes before flipping. It releases when it has browned.
- **Lid on, then lid off.** A lid speeds up potatoes, but take it off for the last few minutes so the steam leaves and the edges crisp.
- **Watery vegetables go in last.** Zucchini, mushrooms and spinach dump water. Add them at the end, or brown them first and set them aside.

On a sheet pan, spread food in one layer and roast on the upper-middle rack. If juices pool, tip the pan and spoon them off halfway through.

## What One-Pot Meals Freeze Well?

Not every one-pan dinner freezes well. Crispy skillet and sheet-pan meals go soft when thawed. Saucy, soupy one-pot meals freeze best.

| Meal | Freezes well? | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Chili | Yes | Freeze in 1.5-cup portions |
| Lentil or bean soup | Yes | Add fresh greens when reheating |
| Curry with chicken or chickpeas | Yes | Freeze without rice if possible |
| Chicken and rice | Yes | Add a splash of broth when reheating |
| Tomato pasta | Okay | Undercook the pasta by 2 minutes |
| Roasted salmon and vegetables | No | Eat within 3 to 4 days instead |
| Fried rice | Okay | Reheat in a hot skillet, not the microwave |
| Crispy potatoes | No | Make them fresh |

To freeze safely:

1. Cool the food quickly. Spread it in a shallow container so it gets into the fridge within 2 hours of cooking.
2. Pack single portions in airtight containers. Leave 1/2 inch at the top for expansion.
3. Label each with the name and date. Freeze at 0°F.
4. Use within 2 to 3 months for the best taste. Thaw in the fridge overnight and reheat to 165°F.

For more, read [how to freeze single portions](/blog/how-to-freeze-single-portions/) and our guide to [the best airtight food storage containers](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/).

Unfrozen leftovers last 3 to 4 days in a fridge set at 40°F or below. If your fridge is tiny, [organizing a small fridge](/blog/organize-a-small-fridge/) helps the containers fit.

## Seven Things We Would Never Do With One Pan

Each of these turns a quick solo dinner into a smoky, soggy or ruined pan.

- **Using a pan that is too big.** Oil burns in the empty spots and smoke fills the room. Match the pan to the portion.
- **Using a pan that is too small.** Crowded food steams. If pieces touch, size up or cook in two rounds.
- **Adding everything at once.** Potatoes and spinach do not cook at the same speed. Stagger them.
- **Skipping the thermometer.** Thin cuts overcook fast and thick ones stay raw inside. Check every time.
- **Cooking a full family recipe.** You end up with four portions of something that does not freeze. Halve it or pick a freezer-friendly dish.
- **Leaving the pan to soak overnight.** Cast iron and carbon steel rust. Wash them while warm, dry them on the burner and wipe on a thin film of oil.
- **Heating nonstick on high with nothing in it.** It damages the coating. Use medium heat for nonstick and save high heat for cast iron or steel. Our guide on [what nonstick cookware is](/blog/what-is-nonstick-cookware/) explains why.
