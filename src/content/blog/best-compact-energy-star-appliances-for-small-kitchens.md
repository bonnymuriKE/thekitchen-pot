## What Energy Star Covers in the Kitchen

ENERGY STAR is run by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency. A certified product meets EPA efficiency criteria for its category and size.

In the kitchen, the program covers:

- **Refrigerators**, including compact fridges
- **Freezers**, including compact uprights and chests
- **Dishwashers**, including 18-inch and countertop models

It does not cover air fryers, toaster ovens, kettles, coffee makers or microwaves. For those, see our guide to the [most energy-efficient small kitchen appliances](/blog/most-energy-efficient-small-kitchen-appliances/).

Always check the exact model on the [ENERGY STAR product finder](https://www.energystar.gov/productfinder/). Some retailer listings say Energy Star when only a similar model is certified.

## How to Read the Yellow EnergyGuide Label

Every new fridge, freezer and dishwasher sold in the U.S. has a yellow EnergyGuide label. It shows the estimated yearly energy use in kWh and an estimated yearly cost.

The kWh figure is the useful number. The cost estimate uses a national average electricity price, which may be higher or lower than yours.

To estimate your own cost, multiply the kWh figure by your electricity rate. A fridge using 228 kWh a year at 17 cents per kWh costs about $39 a year to run.

The ENERGY STAR logo on the label means the model meets the EPA criteria. Compare kWh figures between models of the same size and type.

## Compact Does Not Always Mean Efficient Per Cubic Foot

Small fridges use less energy in total, but not always less per cubic foot. A 4.4 cubic foot fridge at 228 kWh uses about 52 kWh per cubic foot each year. A 12 cubic foot fridge at 312 kWh uses about 26 kWh per cubic foot.

That does not mean you should buy a bigger fridge. An empty fridge still uses energy. Choose the size you will actually fill.

If you live alone and cook often, a 10 to 12 cubic foot apartment fridge may serve you better than two mini fridges.

## Placement Matters as Much as the Model

Even a certified appliance wastes energy if it is placed badly.

- **Leave space around fridges and freezers.** Coils need air to release heat. Check the manual for clearances.
- **Keep fridges away from heat.** Do not put them next to an oven, radiator or sunny window.
- **Level the fridge.** A level door seals properly.
- **Check the door seal.** Close a sheet of paper in the door. If it slides out easily, the seal needs cleaning or replacing.
- **Run full dishwasher loads.** A half-empty dishwasher uses nearly the same energy and water.

## Keeping a Compact Dishwasher Efficient

A dishwasher only saves water and energy if you use it well.

- **Scrape, do not rinse.** Modern dishwashers are designed for unrinsed dishes. Pre-rinsing wastes water.
- **Run full loads.** A 6-place countertop model fills up in a day or two for one person.
- **Use the eco cycle.** It runs longer but uses less energy.
- **Clean the filter monthly.** A clogged filter leaves food on dishes and makes you re-wash. See [how to clean a dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/).
- **Air dry.** Open the door at the end of the cycle instead of using heated dry.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Trusting a retailer's Energy Star claim.** Check the exact model number on the ENERGY STAR product finder.
- **Buying too big.** An oversized fridge wastes energy keeping empty space cold.
- **Buying too small.** Two mini fridges usually use more energy than one apartment fridge.
- **Ignoring defrosting.** Thick ice on a manual-defrost fridge makes it work harder.
- **Blocking the coils.** Pushing a fridge flush against the wall traps heat.
