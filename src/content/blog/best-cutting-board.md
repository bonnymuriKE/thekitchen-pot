### What About Microplastics?

Plastic boards shed tiny bits of plastic as knives cut into them. How much that matters for health is not yet settled. If it bothers you, wood, rubber or a plant-based board like the Material MK Free Board avoids the question.

## End Grain vs Edge Grain: What Is the Difference?

Both are made from strips of wood glued together. The difference is which part of the wood faces up.

**Edge grain** boards use long strips laid on their sides. You cut across the length of the wood fibers, a bit like cutting across a bundle of uncooked spaghetti. The board is tough, flat and lower cost. The John Boos R03 and the Virginia Boys walnut board are edge grain.

**End grain** boards use short blocks standing on their ends. The top looks like a checkerboard. Here the knife slides between the fibers, like poking into the end of a brush. The fibers part and then close back up. That makes end grain the gentlest wood surface and the best at hiding knife marks.

End grain has costs, though. It is thicker, heavier and more expensive. It also drinks up more oil and needs oiling more often. For most people, a good edge-grain board does the job. End grain is worth it if you cut a lot and care about your knives.

## Why Not a Glass or Bamboo Cutting Board?

### Glass Dulls Knives Fast

A knife edge is a very thin strip of steel. Glass is harder than that steel. When the blade hits glass, the edge has nowhere to sink in. So it bends over to one side or chips. A few weeks of chopping on glass can undo a fresh sharpening.

Glass is also slippery. Food skids, and so does the knife. That makes it less safe, not more. The same problem applies to marble, slate, granite and stone boards. Keep them for serving cheese, not for cutting it.

### Bamboo Is Not the Best Pick

Bamboo looks like wood, but it is a grass. Bamboo boards are made from thin strips pressed together with a lot of glue. The result is harder than maple, so it wears down edges faster. Bamboo boards can also split along the glue lines over time. They are fine as a cheap serving board. For daily chopping, maple, walnut, teak or rubber will treat your knives better.

## Which Cutting Board Is Kindest to Knives?

Rubber is the kindest, then end-grain wood, then edge-grain walnut, maple and teak. Plastic comes next, then paper composite and bamboo. Glass, marble, slate and stone sit at the bottom.

If you have spent good money on knives, like the ones in our [Wusthof knives review](/blog/wusthof-knives-review/), match them with a board from the top three. A gentle board plus proper storage keeps an edge far longer. A [magnetic knife strip](/blog/best-magnetic-knife-strip/) is one easy way to stop blades knocking together in a drawer.

## Cutting Board Food Safety: What You Actually Need to Know

### Use a Separate Board for Raw Meat

Keep one board only for raw meat, poultry and seafood. Use another for bread, fruit, vegetables and anything you eat without cooking. This one habit does more for safety than any material choice. Color-coded boards make it easy to keep them straight.

Raw chicken is the main worry. Its juices can carry bacteria onto a salad or a sandwich. After prep, store leftovers quickly and follow our guide on [how long chicken lasts in the fridge](/blog/how-long-does-chicken-last-in-the-fridge/).

### Dishwasher Rules

| Board type | Dishwasher? | How to clean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Plastic | Yes | Dishwasher or hot soapy water |
| Paper composite | Yes | Dishwasher or hot soapy water |
| Plant-based bioplastic | Yes | Dishwasher or hot soapy water |
| Wood | No | Hot soapy water, rinse, dry on edge |
| Rubber | Usually no | Mild soap, damp sponge, dry fully |

Let boards dry completely before you stack them. Trapped moisture lets bacteria grow. A good [dish drying rack for a small kitchen](/blog/dish-drying-rack-ideas-for-small-kitchens/) with a slot for boards helps. A clean machine matters too, so keep up with [cleaning your dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/) as well.

To sanitize any board, mix one tablespoon of unscented liquid chlorine bleach into one gallon of water. Cover the surface, let it sit a few minutes, then rinse and air dry.

### When to Replace a Plastic Board

Replace a plastic board when any of these happen:

- Your fingernail catches in the knife marks.
- The surface looks fuzzy or rough.
- Stains or smells stay after a good scrub.
- The board has warped and rocks on the counter.

Deep grooves are hard to clean and can hold bacteria. For most home cooks, that means a new everyday plastic board every year or two.

## How to Oil and Care for a Wooden Cutting Board

Oil keeps wood from drying out and cracking. It also slows down how much water and juice soak in.

1. **Wash and dry the board fully.** Oil traps whatever is underneath it.
2. **Pour on food-grade mineral oil.** Spread it across every face, including the edges.
3. **Let it soak in for a few hours or overnight.** Stand the board on its edge.
4. **Wipe off the extra.** The board should feel smooth, not greasy.
5. **Finish with board cream if you like.** A beeswax cream adds a light seal.

Oil a new board several times in its first week. After that, once a month is about right for edge grain. End-grain teak, like the Teakhaus, drinks more and does best with oil two or three times a month. A pale, dry or patchy look is the sign it needs more.

Skip olive and vegetable oil, which can turn sticky and smell stale. Deep knife marks can be sanded out with fine sandpaper before you re-oil.
