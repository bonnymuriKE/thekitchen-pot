Most small kitchens have one awkward gap, beside the fridge, at the end of a counter or next to the wall. A slim trash can is made for that gap.

A regular round can sits in the floor space you stand in to cook. A slim kitchen trash can turns that around: it is narrow at the front and goes deep instead, so it tucks into a strip of floor you were not using anyway.

"Slim" still means different things. Some cans are slim but tall, some are slim but low, and some hide inside a cabinet. The seven picks below cover each kind.

**The short version:** For most small kitchens, buy the [simplehuman 45L Slim Step Can](https://www.amazon.com/simplehuman-Slim-Step-Trash-Can-Brushed-Stainless-Steel/dp/B071186RHH/?tag=kitchenpot-20). It is about 10.25 inches wide, holds 12 gallons, closes quietly and is built to last. Before you buy any slim can, measure the height above the spot as well as the width, because the lid needs room to open.

## How to Choose a Slim Kitchen Trash Can

A slim can solves a space problem, so the right one depends on your space first and your habits second. Start with a tape measure.

### How to Measure the Gap

Measure three things: width, depth and height.

1. **Width.** Measure the gap at the floor and again at counter height. Walls and cabinet sides are not always straight. Leave about half an inch on each side so the can slides in and out easily.
2. **Depth.** Measure from the wall to the front edge of the counter. A slim can that sticks out past the counter becomes a hip-height bump in a narrow aisle.
3. **Height above.** Measure from the floor to whatever sits above the spot. That might be a counter overhang, a shelf or an upper cabinet. Then compare it with the can's "lid open" height, not its closed height.

The third measurement catches most people out. A can that fits closed may hit the counter every time it opens.

Also check that the can will not block a drawer, the dishwasher door or the oven door when they open. Our [small kitchen layout ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-layout-ideas/) cover how to keep these paths clear, and our tips for a [small L-shaped kitchen](/blog/small-l-shaped-kitchen-ideas/) show where a slim can fits at the corner.

### How Wide Is a Slim Trash Can?

Most slim kitchen trash cans are 10 to 12 inches wide. They make up for it with depth.

| Can type | Typical width | Typical depth | Good spot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slim step can | 10 to 11 in | 19 to 22 in | Beside the fridge or at the end of a counter |
| Short slim sensor can | 10 to 11 in | 12 to 13 in | A short, narrow gap |
| Low-clearance can | About 10 in | About 16 in | Under a counter, shelf or table |
| Stacked recycling unit | About 12 in | About 15 in | Where you need two bins in one spot |
| Cabinet pull-out | Fits an 11 in or wider opening | About 22 in | Inside a base or sink cabinet |

A regular round 13-gallon can is much wider than any of these. So even a deep slim can usually frees up walking space.

### Slim Trash Can 13 Gallon: Capacity and Bag Size

Cans are often sold in liters, while bags are sold in gallons. One gallon is about 3.8 liters, so here is the quick conversion:

- **40 liters** is about 10.6 gallons.
- **45 liters** is about 12 gallons.
- **50 liters** is about 13.2 gallons.

A 50-liter can is a true 13-gallon can, and most of them take regular tall kitchen bags. A 40 or 45-liter slim can is a little smaller. A regular 13-gallon bag will fit, but it hangs loose and bunches at the rim.

That is why some brands sell fitted liners. They cost more per bag but sit snugly. The inside of a cabinet door is a good home for the spare roll, one of many [small kitchen cabinet organization ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-cabinet-organization-ideas/) that free up shelf space.

### Step vs. Sensor vs. Open Top

Each way of opening has a real trade-off.

| | Step pedal | Motion sensor | Open top |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| How it opens | Your foot presses a pedal that lifts the lid | A sensor sees your hand and a motor lifts the lid | No lid at all |
| Power | None | Batteries or a plug | None |
| Holds in smells | Yes | Yes | No |
| Quiet | Yes, with a soft-close lid | Small motor hum | Silent |
| Things that can go wrong | Pedal or hinge wear | Sensor, motor, batteries | Very little |
| Best for | Most kitchens | Messy prep, meat-heavy cooking | Inside a cabinet or pantry |

A step can is the safe default. It has no electronics, and a good pedal lasts for years.

A sensor can suits cooks with messy hands. It may open when you walk past, so place it slightly out of the main path.

An open-top can only makes sense behind a door. In the open, it lets out smells and invites fruit flies.

### Slim Kitchen Trash Can With Lid: Does It Help With Smell?

Yes. A closed lid is the single biggest help against smell, because it traps the air inside the can. A carbon filter in the lid helps a bit more, since carbon soaks up odor as air passes through it.

Habits matter as much as the can. Take out wet scraps often, rinse sticky containers, and sprinkle a little baking soda under the bag.

If you cook a lot of fish or strong spices, smell in a small room often comes from the stove as well. Our guide to [ventilation and cooking smells in a small kitchen](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) covers that side.

### Do You Need a Separate Recycling Bin?

In a small kitchen, you have three ways to sort recycling.

- **A stacked unit** like the Joseph Joseph Totem Compact puts both bins in one slim footprint.
- **A pull-out with two bins** hides both in a base cabinet, if you have one wide enough.
- **A second slim can** works if you have two gaps, such as one beside the fridge and one at the end of the counter.

Food scraps for compost are a different job. A sealed countertop bin or an electric unit handles those better. Our review of the [best electric kitchen composters](/blog/best-electric-kitchen-composter/) covers that, and our guide to [eco-friendly kitchen swaps](/blog/eco-friendly-alternatives-to-common-kitchen-appliances/) has simpler options.

### Should the Trash Go Under the Sink?

It can, but pipes and a disposal often leave too little depth for a pull-out. A nearby base cabinet may work better, and our guide on [how to declutter a small kitchen](/blog/declutter-a-small-kitchen/) can help you empty one. ## How to Keep a Slim Trash Can Clean

Slim cans are deep and narrow, so a long-handled brush helps reach the bottom.

1. **Wash the inside** with hot, soapy water, in the tub or outside.
2. **Wipe the lid and rim,** where sticky drips gather first.
3. **Dry it fully** before the new bag goes in. A damp bottom smells within days.
4. **Clean steel along the grain** with a soft cloth, never a scouring pad.
5. **Change the carbon filter** every few months if the can has one.

A weekly wipe and a monthly wash fit easily into a [small kitchen hacks](/blog/small-kitchen-hacks/) routine.

## Where to Put a Slim Trash Can in a Small Kitchen

Keep the can close to where you prep, so scraps go straight from the board to the bin. Good spots include:

- **The gap beside the fridge.** It is often the best use of that strip of floor.
- **The end of a counter run,** the natural spot in a [one-wall kitchen](/blog/one-wall-kitchen-ideas/).
- **Inside a base cabinet,** on a pull-out.
- **Under an overhang,** with a low-clearance can only.

Avoid the walkway and the oven door's path. Clear floor makes a room feel larger, as our guide to [making a small kitchen look bigger](/blog/make-a-small-kitchen-look-bigger/) explains.
