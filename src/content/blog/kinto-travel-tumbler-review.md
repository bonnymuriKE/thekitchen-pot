The thing that sets the Travel Tumbler apart is the cap. It unscrews completely, and what is left is a thin, smooth steel rim with no threads touching your lips. You drink from it like a glass, from any side. Most insulated mugs make you sip through a slot in a plastic lid.

Under the cap there is a small inner structure that slows the flow. It catches ice cubes and stops hot coffee from rushing out when you tip it.

Insulation comes from two steel walls with a vacuum between them. Heat needs something to travel through, and a vacuum gives it almost nothing. That is why the outside stays cool while your coffee stays hot. Preheat it with hot water from your [electric kettle](/blog/best-electric-kettle/) for a minute or two, and it holds heat even longer.

The inside is electro-polished, which means the steel is smoothed until it is close to mirror-like. A smoother surface holds on to fewer coffee oils, so it is less likely to keep yesterday's smell. The colored versions have a powder-coated outside that resists scratches.

How leak proof is it? With the lid and cap screwed down, it holds well upright in a cup holder or a tote. It is not designed to roll around sideways in a laptop bag, and It is made to ride upright. Do not fill it to the brim either, since it can spill as you close it. ## Kinto Travel Tumbler 350ml vs 500ml: Which Size?

This is the most common question about the Travel Tumbler, so here is a plain rule. Buy the size that matches the drink you actually make, not the biggest one.

| | 350ml (12 oz) | 500ml (17 oz) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Holds | One regular coffee | A large coffee or iced drink with ice |
| Height with lid | About 6.8 in | About 7.9 in |
| Width | About 2.8 in | About 3.0 in |
| Weight | About 245 g | About 310 g |
| Best for | Small bags, single cups | Long mornings, tea refills, iced coffee |

A tumbler stays hottest when it is full. If you brew 12 ounces, the 350ml is the better tumbler for you. If you drink iced coffee, go 500ml, because ice takes up a third of the space.

## How Does the Kinto Tumbler Compare to Yeti, Stanley and Fellow?

Kinto tumblers are lighter and slimmer than most big-name tumblers. The Travel Tumbler 500ml weighs about 310 g, a little under 11 ounces. Many 20 to 40-ounce tumblers with handles weigh a lot more, which you notice in a bag.

The lids differ more than the steel does. Most Yeti and Stanley tumblers use a slide lid or a straw lid that is not sealed. They work on a desk, but they spill if they tip over. The Kinto Travel Tumbler screws shut, so it handles a tote bag much better.

Fellow's Carter mugs are the closest rival. They also aim for a wide, cup-like sip. Fellow coats the inside with ceramic, which some people prefer for taste. Kinto uses plain polished steel, which is lighter and very durable.

The one place Kinto falls behind is washing. Many Yeti and Stanley tumblers can go in the dishwasher. Of the Kinto tumblers here, only the To Go models can.

## Is the Kinto Tumbler Dishwasher Safe?

Only the To Go Tumbler is. The Travel, Day Off, Trail and Active tumblers are hand wash only.

The reason is the vacuum seal and the finish. High heat and strong detergent can weaken the seal over time and dull the powder coating.

Hand washing takes about a minute:

1. Rinse it soon after you finish, before coffee oils dry.
2. Wash with warm water, mild soap and a soft sponge or a long [OXO bottle brush](https://www.amazon.com/OXO-Dual-Bristle-Cleaning-Glassware-Dishwasher/dp/B00004OCLJ/?tag=kitchenpot-20).
3. Pull out the silicone rings now and then and wash them separately.
4. Dry it right away and leave it open to air out.

Avoid steel wool, abrasive cleansers and chlorine bleach, and do not leave it soaking. If small rust-colored spots ever appear inside, use a 10 to 1 mix of warm water and citric acid, left for two to three hours, then rinsed. This is the same mild acid trick used for [descaling an electric kettle](/blog/how-to-clean-an-electric-kettle/).
