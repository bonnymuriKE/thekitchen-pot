Sticky glazed wings and a salmon fillet that falls apart when you lift it are the moments when a sheet of foil in the air fryer feels like the obvious fix.

So, can you put aluminum foil in an air fryer? Yes. In a basket-style air fryer, foil is safe when it sits in the basket under food, stays away from the heating element and leaves room for air to move. It goes wrong when it is loose, empty, in the wrong part of the machine or under the wrong food.

**Quick answer:** Foil is fine in an air fryer basket if food sits on top of it, it does not cover every hole, and it never touches the heating element. Never put foil in the drawer under the basket, never preheat with an empty sheet inside, and skip it for tomato, citrus or vinegar dishes. For everyday cooking, perforated parchment or a silicone liner usually gives crisper results. Oven-style and microwave combo air fryers have their own rules, so check the manual.

## Why Foil Is Safe in an Air Fryer Basket

An air fryer is a small, strong convection oven. A heating coil sits at the top, and a fan right above it pushes hot air down around the food. The air hits the bottom of the drawer, turns and comes back up through the holes in the basket. That loop is what makes food crisp.

Aluminum foil does not burn or melt at air fryer temperatures. Most basket air fryers top out at 400°F to 450°F, and aluminum melts at about 1,220°F. So the foil itself is not the danger. The two real problems are:

- **Air flow.** Foil is solid. If it covers the whole basket floor, the air cannot get under the food. You get a pale, steamed bottom and longer cooking times.
- **Loose foil.** That fan is strong. A light sheet with nothing on it can lift into the coil. Foil touching the element can scorch, spark or damage the coil.

Every rule below comes from those two points. For cake pans and cookies, see our guide to [baking in an air fryer](/blog/can-you-bake-in-an-air-fryer/).

## How to Use Aluminum Foil in an Air Fryer Basket

Here is the method we recommend for any basket model, from a small 2-quart unit to a large dual-basket one.

1. **Use heavy-duty foil.** Thin foil tears on the basket edges and flaps in the fan. A roll of [Reynolds Wrap Heavy Duty Aluminum Foil](https://www.amazon.com/Reynolds-Wrap-Heavy-Aluminum-Square/dp/B00M8ZEAW4/?tag=kitchenpot-20) holds its shape when you fold it into a tray and stays flat under food.
2. **Cut it smaller than the basket floor.** Leave about 1/2 inch of open holes all around the edge. Air can then still rise around the food.
3. **Fold the edges up about 1 inch** if the food is saucy. This makes a shallow tray that catches drips without blocking the sides.
4. **Preheat with the basket empty, no foil.** Then add the foil and food together.
5. **Put the food on the foil right away.** Food is the weight that keeps the foil down. Never leave a bare sheet in the basket, even for a minute.
6. **Check the gap to the coil.** Food and foil should stay at least 1 inch below the heating element. Tall foil packets or tented sheets can reach it.
7. **Add a few minutes.** Food on foil browns more slowly underneath. Plan on 2 to 5 extra minutes, and flip halfway if the recipe allows.

To lift a fillet or a small dish out, make a sling. Fold a strip of foil about 3 inches wide and long enough to run under the food and up both sides. Pull the two ends up together at the end. Use [Tastee silicone kitchen tongs](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=125334&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=can-you-put-aluminum-foil-in-an-air-fryer&ued=https%3A%2F%2Ftastee.net%2Fproducts%2Fsilicone-kitchen-tongs) to grip the sling. Their 9-inch length fits inside the basket, and the silicone tips will not scratch a coated floor.

### Should the Shiny or Dull Side Face Up?

It does not matter. The two sides look different only because of how foil is rolled at the factory. Both sides heat and cook the same. The exception is nonstick foil, which has a coated dull side that should face the food.

### Can You Wrap Food in Foil in an Air Fryer?

Yes. A closed packet of fish and vegetables cooks well, as long as the packet stays well below the coil. Just know that a sealed packet steams the food. It will be tender and juicy, not crisp. Open the top for the last 3 to 5 minutes if you want some color.

## Where Foil Should Never Go in an Air Fryer

Most foil problems come from putting it in the wrong place, not from using it at all.

| Spot in the air fryer | Foil OK? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basket floor, under food, edges open | Yes | Food holds it down and air still flows around it |
| Shaped tray or sling under food | Yes | Easy lifting and drip control |
| Loosely over food to stop it browning | Yes, if tucked in | Tuck the edges under the food or dish so it cannot lift |
| Drawer under the basket | No | Blocks the air path and traps hot grease |
| Basket with no food (preheating) | No | The fan can lift it into the heating coil |
| Touching or near the heating element | No | Can scorch, spark and damage the coil |
| Covering every hole in the basket | No | Steams the food and strains the unit |
| Microwave mode on a combo unit | No | Metal sparks in a microwave |

### Can You Put Foil in the Bottom of an Air Fryer?

No. The drawer under the basket looks like the perfect place to catch grease. It is also where the air turns around to flow back up. Foil there blocks that path and traps a pool of hot fat right under the food. That fat can smoke, and the blocked air can make the unit run hot.

If grease in the drawer is what bothers you, empty it after each cook and wash it while it is warm. Our guide to [cleaning an air fryer basket](/blog/how-to-clean-an-air-fryer-basket/) covers the drawer and the sticky film in detail.

### Can You Put Foil in an Air Fryer Without Food?

No, not even for a quick preheat. A bare sheet is how foil ends up stuck to the coil.

## When Not to Use Foil in an Air Fryer

### Acidic and Very Salty Foods

Tomato sauce, lemon juice, citrus marinades, vinegar and very salty brines react with aluminum. You will see small grey spots or tiny holes in the foil. The food can pick up a metallic taste, and a little extra aluminum moves into the food. For most people that amount is small, but there is no reason to add more. For these foods, use parchment, a silicone liner or a small oven-safe dish instead.

### Air Fryer Ovens and Toaster Oven Air Fryers

Oven-style air fryers with racks work differently. The heating elements often sit at both the top and bottom, and there is usually a crumb tray under the elements.

- Foil on a rack, under food, is usually fine. Leave space around the edges of the rack.
- Never lay foil on the oven floor or wrap the crumb tray in it. It reflects heat back onto the lower element and can damage it or the finish inside.
- Many models also say not to cover the whole rack or the air fry mesh tray.

Manuals for these ovens differ more than basket models do, so read yours once and follow it. If you are still choosing between an oven style and a basket, our [NuWave oven vs air fryers](/blog/nuwave-oven-vs-air-fryers/) comparison explains how each one moves heat. If you own the dome-style cooker, the rules in [how to use a NuWave oven](/blog/how-to-use-a-nuwave-oven/) apply too. For the bigger countertop ovens, see [baking in a toaster oven](/blog/can-you-bake-in-a-toaster-oven/).

### Can You Put Aluminum Foil in an Air Fryer Microwave Combo?

Only in air fry, convection or grill mode, and only if the manual says so. Never in microwave mode, and never in a combination mode that runs the microwave and the fan together. Metal in a microwave can spark and damage the inside of the oven. When in doubt with a combo unit, use a glass or ceramic dish instead. If you are short on space and shopping for a combo, our list of the [best small microwaves](/blog/best-small-microwave/) has compact choices.

### When Your Manual Says No

A small number of makers say not to use foil at all, usually in smaller units where any sheet sits close to the coil. Their manual beats any general advice, including ours.

## Foil vs Parchment vs Perforated Parchment vs Silicone Liners

People search "is it better to use foil or parchment paper in an air fryer" because both work and both have limits. Here is how the four common liners compare.

| | Aluminum foil | Plain parchment | Perforated parchment | Silicone liner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Air flow | Poor unless cut small | Poor unless cut small | Good, holes let air through | Fair, raised ridges help |
| Crispness on the bottom | Low | Low | Good | Fair |
| Acidic foods | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Sticky glazes and cheese | Very good | Very good | Good, some drips through | Very good |
| Reusable | No | No | No | Yes, hundreds of uses |
| Heat limit | Far above air fryer heat | Usually about 420°F to 450°F | Usually about 420°F to 450°F | Usually 450°F or more |
| Shapes into a tray | Yes | No | No | Comes pre-shaped |
| Main risk | Blocking air, touching the coil | Flying up if loose | Flying up if loose | Hard to clean in grooves |

### When Foil Wins

Foil is the best choice when you need shape. It folds into a boat for a wet marinade, wraps a packet, or makes a sling under a fragile fillet. It also handles any heat your air fryer can reach.

### When Parchment Wins

Plain parchment is the better liner for acidic or very sticky food. Cut it a little smaller than the basket floor, the same way you would cut foil. An unbleached roll such as [If You Care Parchment Baking Paper](https://www.amazon.com/If-You-Care-Unbleached-Greaseproof/dp/B09MSMYXYD/?tag=kitchenpot-20) works well, and you can poke a few holes in it with a skewer. Check the heat limit on the box. If your recipe calls for 450°F, plain parchment can brown and get brittle at the edges.

### When Perforated Parchment Wins

For most daily cooking, pre-cut perforated rounds give the best balance. The holes let air reach the food from below, so wings and nuggets still crisp. The [Katbite Air Fryer Parchment Paper Liners](https://www.amazon.com/Katbite-120Pcs-Parchment-Perforated-Steamer/dp/B08YN42TGG/?tag=kitchenpot-20) are 7.5-inch rounds that suit 3.5 to 3.7-quart baskets. If you cook in a Tastee, the [Tastee Air Fryer Paper Liners](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=125334&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=can-you-put-aluminum-foil-in-an-air-fryer&ued=https%3A%2F%2Ftastee.net%2Fproducts%2Ftastee-air-fryer-paper-liners-1) are made for its basket, so you are not trimming circles to fit.

### When Silicone Wins

A reusable silicone pot sits in the basket like a bowl. It never flies up, it handles tomato and lemon, and it cuts down on paper waste. The [Air Fryer Silicone Liners Pot 2-Pack](https://www.amazon.com/Air-Fryer-Silicone-Liners-Pot-2-Pack/dp/B0BGL1VMW8/?tag=kitchenpot-20) fits 3 to 5-quart baskets, with an 8-inch top and a 6.75-inch base. The trade-off is crispness. The solid sides block some air, so it suits saucy food, reheating and meatballs more than fries.

## What Else Is Safe to Put in an Air Fryer?

The same two rules decide everything here: nothing loose and light, and nothing that cannot take the heat.

| Item | Safe? | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oven-safe glass (like Pyrex) | Yes | Avoid sudden temperature changes; keep 1 inch below the coil |
| Ceramic ramekins and stoneware | Yes | Must be oven-safe; no cracks |
| Metal cake and pie pans | Yes | Small pans that leave a gap around the sides |
| Disposable foil pans | Yes | Weigh them down with food; trim tall sides |
| Silicone liners, cups and molds | Yes | Check the heat rating |
| Paper towels | No | They fly into the coil and burn |
| Wax paper | No | The wax melts and smokes |
| Plastic containers or wrap | No | They melt |
| Paper plates and paper cups | No | They scorch and can catch fire |
| Cold glass straight from the fridge | No | Sudden heat can crack it |

### Glass and Ceramic Dishes

Oven-safe glass is fine in an air fryer. [Pyrex 6-Ounce Custard Cups](https://www.amazon.com/Pyrex-6-Ounce-Custard-Cups-Set/dp/B0000CF3UK/?tag=kitchenpot-20) fit most baskets and work for single egg bakes, small crisps and reheating a scoop of leftovers. Let a chilled dish sit on the counter for 15 to 20 minutes first. Glass cracks when it goes from cold to very hot quickly. Also keep glass away from the coil, since the top of the dish gets hotter than it would in a regular oven.

### Metal Pans

A small metal pan is often better than foil because it holds its shape and gives the food an even base. A [Fat Daddio's 6 x 2-Inch Round Cake Pan](https://www.amazon.com/Fat-Daddios-Anodized-Aluminum-Round-Cake-Pans/dp/B01HDKCFAC/?tag=kitchenpot-20) fits most 4-quart and larger baskets with room around the sides. Use it for saucy chicken, a small frittata or a reheated casserole portion. The anodized surface does not react with tomato the way bare foil does. A small pan also suits half-size recipes; our guide to [small-batch baking](/blog/small-batch-baking-how-to-halve-recipes/) shows how to scale them down.

### Paper Towels: No

Never put a paper towel in an air fryer, even under food. It is light, soaks up grease and burns fast. Blot food on a plate after cooking instead.

## What Can You Not Put in an Air Fryer?

Beyond the liners above, a few foods cause most of the trouble.

- **Wet batters.** A beer batter drips through the basket before it sets. Use a dry breading instead.
- **Loose, light foods without weight.** Single spinach leaves or kale for chips can lift into the coil. Keep them in a basket with a rack on top, or skip them.
- **Large amounts of fatty food on foil.** Bacon or sausage fat pooling in a foil tray can smoke heavily. Cook bacon straight on the basket so fat drips away.
- **Too much food at once.** Piling food blocks air just like a full sheet of foil does. One layer with gaps is the goal.

Heavy smoke is a sign of too much fat, too much heat or the wrong oil. Our guide to the [best oil for an air fryer](/blog/best-oil-for-air-fryer/) explains smoke points, and a good [range hood setup for a small kitchen](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) clears what smoke does escape.

## Foods Where Foil Helps, and Foods It Ruins

Foil is a tool for some foods and a mistake for others. This is the quick guide we wish came in every air fryer box.

| Food | Foil? | Better approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glazed or sticky wings | Yes, small tray | Add glaze in the last 3 to 4 minutes on foil |
| Salmon and white fish fillets | Yes, sling or boat | 400°F for 8 to 12 minutes; fish to 145°F |
| Cheesy bakes and melts | Yes, tray | Foil catches cheese before it burns on the basket |
| Marinated chicken (no citrus or vinegar) | Yes, edges open | Chicken to 165°F |
| Baked potatoes | No, for crisp skin | 400°F for 35 to 45 minutes, unwrapped, oiled and salted |
| Frozen fries and nuggets | No | Straight on the basket in one layer |
| Breaded cutlets | No | Perforated parchment if they stick |
| Bacon | No | Straight on the basket so fat drains |
| Lemon chicken or tomato dishes | No | Silicone liner or a small metal or glass dish |
| Roasted vegetables | Usually no | Basket or perforated parchment for browning |

### Baked Potatoes in Foil in an Air Fryer

Wrapping potatoes in foil is one of the top searches for this topic. You can do it, and it is safe. But a foil-wrapped potato steams, so the skin turns soft. For crisp skin, rub a medium russet with oil and salt and cook it bare at 400°F for 35 to 45 minutes. It is done when a knife slides in with no push. Wrap it in foil only after cooking to keep it warm. Raw potatoes keep longer in a cool, dark cupboard than in the fridge, as our guide to [how long potatoes last](/blog/how-long-do-potatoes-last/) explains.

### Salmon on Foil in an Air Fryer

Salmon is where foil earns its place. Skin and soft flesh stick to a bare basket, and a fillet breaks when you lift it. Lay the fillet on a foil sling with the edges folded up about 1 inch. Cook at 400°F for 8 to 12 minutes, depending on thickness, until the center reaches 145°F. An instant-read thermometer such as the [ThermoPro TP03B](https://www.amazon.com/ThermoPro-Digital-Instant-Thermometer-Kitchen/dp/B01IHHLB3W/?tag=kitchenpot-20) takes the guessing out. Keep it clean between uses with our steps for [cleaning a meat thermometer](/blog/how-to-clean-a-meat-thermometer/).

### Fries, Wings and Anything Breaded

For crisp food, foil works against you. Frozen fries on foil come out soft on one side. Our guide to [cooking frozen French fries in an air fryer](/blog/how-to-cook-frozen-french-fries-in-an-air-fryer/) has the times by cut, all on a bare basket. Plain wings follow the same rule. Cook them bare until crisp, then move them onto a small foil tray only for the sauce. The same trick works when you [reheat chicken wings](/blog/how-to-reheat-chicken-wings/) that already have sauce on them.

### Reheating Leftovers

Foil helps with reheating saucy or cheesy leftovers. A loose foil cover keeps the top from drying out while the center warms. Remove it for the last 2 minutes. For pizza, skip foil and put the slice right on the basket so the crust crisps; our guide to [reheating pizza](/blog/how-to-reheat-pizza/) has the times. Always reheat leftovers to 165°F, and check our chart on [how long leftovers last](/blog/how-long-do-leftovers-last/) before you do.

## Foil Habits That Cause Smoke, Sparks or Soggy Food

These are the mistakes we see most often. Each one is easy to fix.

1. **Lining the drawer instead of the basket.** It blocks the air path and traps grease. Line the basket, or nothing.
2. **Preheating with foil inside.** The sheet lifts into the coil. Add foil with the food.
3. **Covering the whole basket floor.** Food steams and cooks unevenly. Leave 1/2 inch of open holes around the edge.
4. **Using thin foil in a strong fan.** It tears and flaps. Use heavy-duty foil, or fold regular foil double.
5. **Tenting food too high.** A tall dome of foil can touch the element. Keep at least 1 inch of clearance.
6. **Putting tomato or citrus on foil.** The foil pits and the food can taste metallic. Switch to parchment or silicone.
7. **Trusting the timer on foil.** Food on a liner takes longer underneath. Check chicken for 165°F and fish for 145°F.
8. **Reusing greasy foil.** Old fat on used foil smokes on the next cook. Throw it out each time.

## The Final Word on Foil in an Air Fryer

Foil belongs in the basket, under food, with the edges open and the coil well clear. It is the right tool for sticky glazes, fragile fish and cheesy bakes. It is the wrong tool for fries, breaded food, bacon and anything acidic. For daily use, perforated parchment or a silicone liner keeps food crisp and the basket clean, with less risk. When the manual for an oven-style or combo unit says something different, follow the manual.
