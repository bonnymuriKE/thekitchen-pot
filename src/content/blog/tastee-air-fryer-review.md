The Tastee Sensor Magic air fryer works differently. A probe goes into the food, reads the temperature inside it, and stops the cook when the center is right. It also has a ceramic basket, a window with a light, and a dehydrate mode.

## How Does the Tastee Air Fryer Probe Work?

Meat is done when its center reaches a set temperature. Time, color and juices are only rough hints. That is why a probe matters more in an air fryer than almost any other upgrade.

The Tastee's probe has two sensors. One sits near the tip and reads the center. The machine compares that reading with the target for the food and doneness you picked. As the center gets close, it changes the heat and fan to finish gently. At the target, it shuts off.

Here are the safe minimums to know. They come from the [USDA safe minimum internal temperature chart](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/safe-minimum-internal-temperatures).

| Food | Safe minimum inside temperature | Tip for the air fryer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chicken and turkey, any cut | 165°F | Thighs taste better taken a little higher |
| Ground beef, pork or lamb | 160°F | Probe the center of the thickest patty |
| Steak, chops and roasts | 145°F, then rest 3 minutes | Pick a lower doneness if you like it pink |
| Fish | 145°F | Probe sideways through the thick end of the fillet |
| Leftovers | 165°F | Reheat until hot in the middle |

Meat keeps cooking for a few minutes after it leaves the heat. This is called carryover. A steak can rise several degrees while it rests. The probe stops the machine on time, so you can rest the meat without it overshooting by much.

### Where to Put the Probe

Push it into the thickest part from the side, so the tip lands in the middle. Keep it off bone, which heats faster than meat and gives a false high reading. With a whole chicken breast, go in from the thick end. With thighs, pick the biggest one.

Owners often mention that the probe can slip out if the food shifts during a shake. So skip the shake reminder for a single steak, or settle the probe deeper before you start. For handling hot probes and baskets, a pair of [heat-resistant cooking gloves](/blog/10-best-heat-resistant-gloves-for-cooking/) is cheap insurance.

## Tastee vs a Regular Air Fryer

A regular air fryer is a small, strong convection oven. A heater sits above the food and a fan pushes hot air around it. That fast airflow dries the surface so it browns and crisps. Our [NuWave oven vs air fryer comparison](/blog/nuwave-oven-vs-air-fryers/) explains the same idea in more depth.

The Tastee uses that same method. What it adds is feedback. A regular model runs blind for the time you set. The Tastee knows when the inside of the food is ready.

| Question | Regular air fryer | Tastee Sensor Magic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How does it know food is done? | It does not; you set a time | Probe reads the center and stops |

| Can you watch food? | Usually only by opening the drawer | Window with a light |

## Is the Tastee Ceramic Basket Safe?

Yes. The basket coating is ceramic and made without PTFE, PFOA, PFOS and BPA. PTFE is the plastic nonstick layer often called Teflon. PFOA and PFOS are chemicals once used in making it. A ceramic coating is a thin, hard, glass-like layer instead.

We look at how these coatings compare in [what is nonstick cookware](/blog/what-is-nonstick-cookware/) and in [is ceramic titanium cookware safe](/blog/is-ceramic-titanium-cookware-safe/). The short of it: ceramic stays stable at high heat, but its slickness fades faster than PTFE if you treat it roughly.

To keep it slick:

- Use wooden or silicone tools, never metal tongs or forks
- Skip aerosol cooking sprays, which leave a sticky film
- Brush or toss food with a little oil instead; our guide to [the best oil for an air fryer](/blog/best-oil-for-air-fryer/) lists which ones handle 400°F and up
- Let the basket cool before washing, and use a soft sponge

## What Can You Cook in the Tastee Air Fryer?

Anything a normal air fryer cooks, plus a few things it handles better than most.

### Chicken Thighs and Breasts

This is where the probe earns its keep. Thighs are safe at 165°F but turn tender closer to 175°F to 185°F, because their extra connective tissue softens with heat. Breasts are the opposite. They dry out fast past 165°F, so stopping right on target matters. Our page on [how much a chicken breast weighs](/blog/how-much-does-a-chicken-breast-weigh/) helps if you are scaling a recipe.

Cooked chicken keeps three to four days in the fridge; see [how long chicken lasts in the fridge](/blog/how-long-does-chicken-last-in-the-fridge/). Reheat it to 165°F in the center. The same idea works for [reheating chicken wings](/blog/how-to-reheat-chicken-wings/) so the skin crisps again.

### Bacon

Bacon cooks flat and evenly in a basket, with no splatter on the stove. Put a cup of water or a slice of bread in the drawer below the basket if your bacon smokes. Pour the cooled fat into the [Tastee ceramic bacon grease container](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=125334&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=tastee-air-fryer-review&ued=https%3A%2F%2Ftastee.net%2Fproducts%2Ftastee-ceramic-bacon-grease-container) rather than down the sink, and use it for roast potatoes later.

### Salmon and Pork Chops

Fish dries out in a minute or two, so the auto fish program with the probe is a real help. Pork chops are done at 145°F with a short rest, which is lower than many people expect. That gap is why so many home pork chops come out tough.

### Baking and Dehydrating

Small cakes, muffins and cookies bake well in an air fryer with a few changes. Our guide on [whether you can bake in an air fryer](/blog/can-you-bake-in-an-air-fryer/) covers pan sizes and lower temperatures. The low 95°F setting also dries apple slices, herbs and jerky. If you dry food often in big batches, a stand-alone unit from our [best food dehydrator](/blog/best-food-dehydrator/) list holds far more trays.

## Honest Limits of the Tastee Air Fryer

No air fryer is perfect, and the Tastee has a few real limits.

**Size.** Five and a half quarts feeds two to four people. Food must sit in one layer to crisp, so a family of five cooks in rounds.

**Counter space.** At 13.6 pounds, it is not something you lift in and out of a cupboard every day. With a cord of only about 3.3 feet, it also needs a spot near an outlet. Our ideas for [making more counter space in a small kitchen](/blog/make-more-counter-space-in-a-small-kitchen/) and [storing small appliances](/blog/store-small-appliances-in-a-small-kitchen/) help it fit.

**The probe needs care.** It belongs in the thickest part, away from bone. Hand wash it, dry it and coil it loosely in its slot. Do not put it in the dishwasher.

**Wi-Fi quirks.** The app works only on 2.4 GHz networks. Many routers broadcast both bands under one name, which can confuse setup. The machine works fully without the app, so this is a nuisance, not a dealbreaker.

**Price.** It costs more than most basket air fryers of the same size. You are paying for the probe and the window. If you would not use them, that money is better spent elsewhere.

## Tips for Better Results in the Tastee

- Pat meat dry before cooking; a dry surface browns faster
- Leave space between pieces so the air can move
- Use a light coat of oil on breaded or starchy foods, not on fatty meats
- Pick your doneness on the high side for thighs and on target for breasts
- Rest steaks and chops for three to five minutes after the beep
- Wash the basket after each fatty cook so oil does not bake onto the coating
