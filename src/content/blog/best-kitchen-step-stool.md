## How Tall of a Step Stool Do You Need for Kitchen Cabinets?

Start with your cabinets, not the stool. Standard upper cabinets hang about 18 inches above a 36-inch counter. So the bottom of the cabinet sits about 54 inches off the floor. A standard 30-inch upper cabinet then tops out at about 84 inches.

Your reach decides the rest. A useful rule from ladder makers: a person can reach about 12 inches above their own height with their feet flat. A 5 foot 6 adult reaches about 78 inches without a stool.

That means the top shelf of a 30-inch cabinet is a short stretch away for many adults. The stool's job is to close that gap so you reach the shelf at chest or shoulder height, not with your arms fully raised.

| What you need to reach | Typical height off the floor | Stool that fits most adults |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First shelf of upper cabinets | About 54 to 66 in | None, or a one-step stool |
| Top shelf of 30-inch uppers | About 72 to 84 in | 2-step, 17 to 20 in top step |
| Top shelf of 36-inch uppers | About 84 to 90 in | 2-step for tall adults, 3-step for shorter |
| Cabinet above the fridge | About 72 to 96 in, set back 24 in | 3-step, about 28 to 30 in |
| Top of the cabinets, for dusting | About 84 to 96 in | 3-step |

The fridge cabinet is a special case. It sits deep, behind the fridge front, so you reach forward as well as up. Give yourself more height there than the numbers suggest. Our guide to [vertical storage ideas for small kitchens](/blog/vertical-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/) covers what earns a spot that high.

## 2-Step or 3-Step Stool: Which Is Better?

For most kitchens, a 2-step stool is better. It is lighter, folds smaller and puts most adults at the right height for standard cabinets. You will also use it more, because it is easy to grab.

A 3-step stool earns its place in three cases. You are shorter than about 5 foot 4. Your kitchen has tall uppers or a high cabinet over the fridge. Or you store things on top of the cabinets, like the baskets and platters in our [small pantry organization ideas](/blog/small-pantry-organization-ideas/).

A one-step stool is the third option. It adds about 9 to 10 inches and helps with the first shelf or the back of a deep counter. It will not get most people to the top shelf.

## Step Stool vs Step Ladder: What Is the Difference?

A step stool is short. It usually has one to three steps, and you can stand on the top step. It is built for quick reaches and opens in a second.

A step ladder is taller, with four or more steps. Its top cap and the step below it are not for standing. In a kitchen, a ladder is overkill for shelves and awkward to store. Keep one in the garage or a closet for ceilings and [light fixtures](/blog/small-kitchen-lighting-ideas/).

## Weight Ratings and ANSI Types, Explained Simply

In the US, step stools and ladders carry an ANSI duty rating. ANSI is the group that sets the safety standard, and the type tells you the maximum load. That load is you plus your clothes plus anything you carry.

| ANSI type | Duty | Maximum load |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type III | Light | 200 lb |
| Type II | Medium | 225 lb |
| Type I | Heavy | 250 lb |
| Type IA | Extra heavy | 300 lb |
| Type IAA | Special | 375 lb |

European stools such as Hailo follow their own standard, EN 14183. Hailo rates the stools here to 150 kg, about 330 pounds. Either way, buy a rating with room to spare. A 200-pound adult carrying a heavy [Dutch oven](/blog/best-dutch-oven/) down from a high shelf is already over a 200-pound rating.

## Non-Slip Feet, Treads and Kitchen Floors

Kitchen floors are often smooth and sometimes wet, so the feet and treads matter.

Good feet are soft plastic or rubber that grip without marking the floor. Check them every few months. A worn or missing foot lets a stool skid, and most makers sell replacements.

Treads should be ribbed or covered with a non-slip mat. Smooth painted steel gets slippery in socks. Before you climb, wipe up spills and make sure all four feet sit flat. A stool on the edge of a rug is not safe.

## How to Use a Kitchen Step Stool Safely

A few simple habits keep you safe on any stool.

1. **Open it fully and check the lock** before you step on it.
2. **Face the stool** as you climb and as you come down.
3. **Keep your belly button between the rails.** If you have to lean, climb down and move the stool.
4. **Hold on with one hand** whenever the stool has a rail or grip.
5. **Never stand on the top cap** of a ladder or on the seat of a chair-style stool.
6. **Carry light loads only.** Hand heavy pans or appliances to someone below, or move them to a lower shelf.
7. **Wear shoes with grip**, not loose slippers.

## Kids' Step Stools and Learning Towers Are a Different Job

A learning tower brings a child up to counter height, with rails on all sides. It is not built for an adult reaching a high shelf. Adult stools are not made for unsupervised kids either, so keep yours folded and stored when children are around.
