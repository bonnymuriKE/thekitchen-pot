## Why a Smooth, Thin Cast Iron Pan Cooks Differently

Most new cast iron is sand-cast and left as it comes from the mold. That leaves a surface like fine sandpaper. Older pans from the early 1900s were ground smooth after casting, and Lancaster follows that older method.

### Smooth Surface: Better Release Sooner

Seasoning is a thin layer of oil baked onto iron until it turns hard and slick. On a rough pan, that layer fills the low spots first. It takes many rounds of cooking before the high points are covered and food stops grabbing.

On a machined pan, there are no deep low spots to fill. The seasoning goes on as one even film from the start. So eggs, fish and potatoes release earlier in the pan's life. It still is not a [nonstick coating](/blog/what-is-nonstick-cookware/), but it gets close with a bit of butter or oil.

### Thinner Walls: Less Weight, Faster Heat

Thinner iron means less metal to lift and less metal to heat up. A Lancaster pan comes up to temperature faster than a thick one. That is why the company advises starting on low to medium-low.

The trade-off is heat storage. A heavy pan holds a big reserve of heat, so a cold steak barely dents it. A thinner pan drops a bit more when food goes in. The fix is simple: preheat for a few extra minutes and cook in batches instead of crowding.

## Who a Lighter Cast Iron Pan Changes Things For

Weight is the main reason people stop using cast iron. A Lancaster pan fixes that for several kinds of cooks.

### Cooks With Sore Wrists or Weak Grip

A full heavy skillet can top eight pounds. Tipping it to pour off fat puts real strain on the wrist. Dropping a pound or two from the pan itself makes one-handed moves safe again. The helper handle on every Lancaster skillet gives a second grip point.

### Glass and Ceramic Cooktops

Glass cooktops scratch when a pan is dragged or dropped. A lighter pan is easier to lift and set down gently. That is the best protection there is, as our guide on [how to protect a glass-top stove from cast iron](/blog/how-to-protect-glass-top-stove-from-cast-iron/) explains. Keep the base clean and lift instead of sliding.

### Induction Cooktops

All cast iron is magnetic, so every Lancaster pan works on induction. The flat base helps the pan sit evenly on the glass. ## Seasoning and Care for Lancaster Cast Iron

Lancaster pans come pre-seasoned, so you can cook right away. The first few meals should use some fat, like bacon, sausages or shallow-fried potatoes. That builds the seasoning faster.

Daily care is short:

1. Wipe or rinse the pan while it is still warm.
2. Use a little mild dish soap if needed. Modern dish soap does not strip baked-on seasoning.
3. Scrub stuck bits with a [chainmail scrubber](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=95395&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=lancaster-cast-iron-review&ued=https%3A%2F%2Flancastercastiron.com%2Fproducts%2Fchain-mail-scrubber). Lancaster's version is food-grade stainless steel and dishwasher safe.
4. Dry the pan fully, then warm it on the stove for a minute to drive off water.
5. Rub in a very thin layer of oil or a conditioner like [Crisbee Stik cast iron seasoning](https://www.amazon.com/Crisbee-Stik-Original-Seasoning-Conditioner/dp/B01E2SFV9A/?tag=kitchenpot-20), then wipe off the excess.

Thin is the key word in that last step. Too much oil turns sticky. If you ever see rust, scrub the spot with chainmail or coarse salt, rinse, dry, warm and re-oil. Surface rust is fixable.

| Problem | Likely Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food sticks | Pan not preheated, or too little fat | Preheat longer on medium-low, add oil |
| Sticky surface | Too much oil when seasoning | Bake upside down at high heat, wipe thinner next time |
| Rust spots | Pan left wet | Scrub, dry, warm, re-oil |
| Dull gray patches | Acidic food wore seasoning | Cook a few fatty meals, re-oil |

Never put a hot pan into cold water. A sudden temperature change can crack cast iron, and thermal shock is not covered by the warranty. If you suspect a pan has warped, our guide on [how to fix a warped pan](/blog/how-to-fix-a-warped-pan/) explains what can and cannot be saved.

### Handling Heat Safely

Cast iron handles get hot. Lancaster's [leather skillet handle cover](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=95395&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=lancaster-cast-iron-review&ued=https%3A%2F%2Flancastercastiron.com%2Fproducts%2Firon-skillet-handle-cover) slides over the handle for stovetop cooking. It is heat resistant to 450°F, but take it off before the pan goes into the oven. A thick dry towel or oven mitt works in the oven.

## How to Choose the Right Lancaster Size

Pick by how many people you cook for, then by your stove and storage.

| You Cook For | First Pan | Second Piece |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One person | No. 6 or No. 8 | No. 8 Griddle |
| Two to three | No. 8 | 5 Qt Dutch Oven |
| Four to five | No. 10 | No. 6 for eggs |
| Six or more | No. 12 | 5 Qt Dutch Oven |
| Bread bakers | No. 8 | 5 Qt Dutch Oven |

Measure your largest burner before ordering the No. 10 or No. 12. A pan much wider than the burner runs cooler at the edges. This matters most on [coil and small electric stoves](/blog/coil-stove-cookware-what-works-what-warps/).

### Is Lancaster Cast Iron Worth It?

That depends on what bothers you about cast iron now. If you already enjoy a heavy Lodge, a Lancaster will not cook better food. It will just feel better to use. If weight or sticking has kept your skillet in the cupboard, the change is large. Our piece on whether [expensive cookware is actually worth it](/blog/is-expensive-cookware-actually-worth-it/) covers the wider question.

A Lancaster pan is also a long-term buy. It has no coating to wear out, unlike most [nonstick pans](/blog/best-nonstick-pans-with-buying-guide/), and the lifetime warranty covers manufacturing defects. Spread over decades, the cost per year is small.

For an even fuller start, the [Everyday Cast Iron Essentials Bundle](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=95395&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=lancaster-cast-iron-review&ued=https%3A%2F%2Flancastercastiron.com%2Fproducts%2Feveryday-cast-iron-essentials-bundle) pairs the No. 8 skillet with a chainmail scrubber, seasoning and a wooden turner. ## Which Lancaster Piece Should You Buy?

Buy the No. 8 skillet first unless you cook for four or more, in which case start with the No. 10. Add the 5-quart Dutch oven next if you bake bread or braise, since it brings a lid for the No. 8 as well. Choose the No. 6 if you cook mostly for yourself in a small kitchen. Take the No. 12 only if you have a large burner and a big household.

If you want a cheaper way in, start with Lodge and see how often you reach for it. Many cooks who outgrow the weight end up on a Lancaster anyway.
