## How a Diatomaceous Earth Dish Drying Mat Works

Diatomaceous earth is a natural powder made from the fossil shells of tiny water plants called diatoms. Each shell is full of microscopic holes. Pressed into a board, the powder becomes a slab with millions of these tiny pores.

Those pores do the work. When water drips onto the stone, the pores pull it in, the same way a paper towel pulls up a spill. The water spreads out through the slab instead of sitting in a puddle on top.

Then it leaves. Because the water is spread so thin across so many pores, it evaporates fast. That is why the surface looks dry again within minutes, while a towel holding the same water can stay wet for hours. The material can hold up to 150% of its own weight in water.

Dorai's material is a blend of diatomaceous earth and paper fibers, which it calls Diomat.

### Why Air Matters as Much as the Stone

The stone can only dry as fast as the air around it allows. In a steamy kitchen with no window open, it slows down. This is why the silicone covers raise the dishes, and why Dorai sells the rack and caddies with raised bases.

You can help it along. Shake off big drips before you set dishes down. Keep the pad out of a closed corner. A [range hood fan](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) running after you cook also clears the steam faster.

## What Fits on a Stone Dish Pad, and What Does Not

The pads are made for everyday hand washing. Plates, bowls, glasses, mugs, bottles, cutlery, chopping boards and freshly rinsed fruit and vegetables all dry well on them.

The limits come down to two things: weight and water.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Plates, bowls, mugs, glasses | Yes | Light, and drips are small |
| Rinsed produce | Yes | The stone takes the water off the skins |
| Baby bottles and water bottles | Yes, upside down | Air reaches the inside through the silicone gaps |

### Heavy Pots

Diatomaceous earth is a hard, pressed stone. It handles normal pots and pans, but it does not like a sharp knock. Set a [cast iron skillet](/blog/lodge-cast-iron-skillet-review/) down gently, or dry it on the stovetop over low heat the way you would anyway. The Over the Sink Dish Pad is the better choice for heavy pans, because the rods carry the weight.

### Very Wet Loads

The stone takes in a lot of water, but not an endless amount. If you pile on a full sink of dripping dishes at once, the pores fill and water can pool at the edges. Shake each piece first, or wash in two rounds. That one habit makes the pad work much better.

## How to Clean a Dorai Dish Pad

The stone dries itself, but it still needs a monthly clean. Soap film and food bits slowly coat the surface, and that slows drying.

1. Take the stones out of the silicone cover.
2. Wash the silicone cover in the dishwasher or by hand with warm, soapy water.
3. Wet each stone and spray or rub on a little mild liquid soap.
4. Scrub in small circles with an abrasive sponge or a stiff brush.
5. Rinse with clean water. Repeat if it still looks dull.
6. Stand the stones on edge and let them dry completely before you put them back in the cover.

Drying fully before reassembly is the step people skip. If damp stones go back into the cover, the trapped moisture slows them down and can start to smell.

### How to Sand a Dorai Stone to Restore Absorbency

Over time, water starts to bead on the stone instead of soaking in. That means the surface pores are clogged with soap, minerals from hard water, or food residue. Sanding fixes it.

Dampen the stone, then sand it lightly with medium-grit sandpaper, about 150 grit. Use gentle, even strokes over the whole surface, not just one spot. Work by an open window or outside so you are not breathing in the fine dust. Rinse the stone, let it dry fully, and it will soak up water like new.

The same sanding removes stains. Coffee, tea, tomato sauce and red wine are the usual culprits. Wiping dark spills right away saves you most of the sanding.

### Never Use the Dishwasher, and Never Soak

The stones must never go in the dishwasher. Hot water, harsh detergent and the drying cycle clog the pores and can damage the board. Do not leave the stones soaking in a sink of water either, since that fills every pore and leaves them slow to dry. Skip bleach and strong cleaners as well. Only the silicone cover is dishwasher safe.

When a set of stones finally wears out, you do not need a new pad. ## Does a Dorai Dish Pad Get Moldy?

Not often, and that is most of its appeal. Mold needs a surface that stays wet. A stone that dries within minutes does not give it much of a chance.

Mold can still turn up in a few places. Water can creep under the pad and sit on the counter. The silicone cover can trap gunk in its corners. Stones that are soaked over and over with no time to dry can also start to darken.

Lift the pad once a week and wipe the counter underneath. Wash the cover monthly. Let the stones dry between big loads. A dry sink area also helps you [keep pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/), since standing water draws them in.

## Is a Diatomaceous Earth Dish Drying Mat Safe?

Yes. Dorai uses food-grade diatomaceous earth. The Diomat material is independently tested to FDA food-safe standards and passes Prop 65 testing, which covers asbestos and lead. You can set fruit and vegetables straight onto it.

The fine dust from sanding is the only thing to treat with care. Sand the stone damp, in a ventilated spot, and rinse it afterward. That keeps the dust out of the air.

## Small Kitchen Tips for Using a Stone Pad

A stone pad works best as part of a simple wash-up routine.

- Wash in the order things will be put away, so the pad empties as fast as it fills.
- Put dry items away before you start the next round. This is one of our favorite [small kitchen hacks](/blog/small-kitchen-hacks/), and it keeps the pad from becoming a second cupboard.
- Fold the pad into a drawer at night if counter space is tight. Our guide to [organizing kitchen drawers](/blog/how-to-organize-kitchen-drawers-in-a-small-kitchen/) helps you find the spot.
- Keep sandpaper and a spare sponge in the cabinet below the sink. Our [under-sink organizing guide](/blog/organize-under-the-kitchen-sink/) shows how to fit them in.
- Choose a color that matches your counter. A pale pad on a dark counter draws the eye, while a matching one blends in and helps [a small kitchen look bigger](/blog/make-a-small-kitchen-look-bigger/).
