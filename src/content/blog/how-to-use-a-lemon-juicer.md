You pick up a lemon squeezer, put the lemon in, squeeze, and juice shoots straight into your eye. Or you get three sad drops and a lemon that still looks full. Almost everyone has been there. The tool isn't the problem. It's usually the direction of the lemon.

This guide covers how to use a lemon juicer of every common type, from the yellow handheld squeezer in most kitchen drawers to wooden reamers, lever presses and electric citrus juicers. You'll learn which way the lemon goes, how to get noticeably more juice from each one, and how to pick the right juicer for how often you actually juice lemons.

> **Quick answer:** Cut the lemon in half across the middle. For a handheld squeezer, put the half in cut side down, facing the holes, and squeeze the handles together slowly and firmly. For a reamer, press the cut side onto the ridged cone and twist. For an electric juicer, press the cut half onto the spinning cone. A room-temperature or briefly microwaved lemon gives more juice.

## How to Prep Lemons for Juicing

A few seconds of prep makes a real difference, no matter which juicer you use.

1. **Bring the lemon to room temperature.** Cold lemons are firmer and give up less juice. If you store lemons in the fridge, take one out a while before you need it.
2. **Or microwave it briefly.** The Kitchn [compared cold lemons with microwaved ones](https://www.thekitchn.com/does-microwaving-a-lemon-really-give-you-more-juice-228766) and found that 20 seconds on high gave noticeably more juice. Their batch of unheated lemons gave just under 2/3 cup, while the batch microwaved for 20 seconds gave 3/4 cup. Ten seconds barely helped.
3. **Roll it on the counter.** Press down with your palm and roll the lemon back and forth a few times. This softens the fruit and makes it easier to squeeze.
4. **Zest first if you need zest.** Once a lemon is juiced, it's almost impossible to zest. Grate it while it's whole.
5. **Cut it across the middle.** Slice the lemon in half through its "equator," not end to end. This exposes all the juice segments to the juicer.

A sharp knife and a stable board help here. ## How to Use a Handheld Lemon Squeezer

This is the hinged, two-handled tool most people mean when they say "lemon juicer." It's also the one people most often use the wrong way.

### Which way does the lemon go?

**Cut side down, facing the holes.** The rounded peel side faces up, toward the top cup. When you squeeze, the top cup pushes the lemon half inside out, and juice flows down through the holes into your bowl or glass.

It feels backward because the bottom cup is shaped like a bowl, so it looks like the lemon should sit in it like an egg in a cup. But if you put the cut side up, the juice has nowhere to go but sideways and up. America's Test Kitchen's guide to [manual citrus juicers](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/1651-manual-citrus-juicers) confirms that halved citrus goes in cut side down in a press.

### Step by step

1. Cut the lemon in half across the middle.
2. Open the squeezer and place one half in the bottom cup, cut side down against the holes.
3. Hold it over a bowl, measuring cup or glass. Holding it over a small strainer catches any seeds that slip through.
4. Squeeze the handles together slowly and firmly. A slow squeeze sprays less than a quick snap.
5. Once the handles meet, open the squeezer, flip the spent half out, and repeat.

**Want to squeeze a little more?** After the first press, give the lemon a quarter turn and squeeze again. Some people also slice a small piece off the far end of the lemon half before squeezing, a trick Food Republic covered, which gives the juice another way out and can make squeezing easier on your hands.

### Why press-style squeezers work well

America's Test Kitchen found that press designs beat reamers on both speed and yield. Their top handheld pick, the Chef'n FreshForce, uses a gear mechanism for extra leverage. ATK also noticed a flavor difference: presses squeeze the rind too, releasing some of the peel's oils, which made the juice more aromatic than juice from reamers.

Consumer Reports also named the Chef'n FreshForce its Editor's Choice among citrus squeezers, noting its deep well cuts down on splashing, though it should be washed by hand.

## How to Use a Citrus Reamer

A reamer is a ridged cone. The handheld wooden kind looks a bit like a small club. Others sit on top of a bowl or cup with a strainer.

### Handheld wooden reamer

1. Hold a lemon half in one hand, cut side up, over a bowl or a small strainer.
2. Push the tip of the reamer into the center of the fruit.
3. Twist back and forth while pressing down, moving toward the edges as the middle empties.
4. Squeeze the peel with your hand at the end to get the last bit.

This is the smallest and cheapest option, and it lives happily in a utensil drawer. The downside is that seeds and pulp go right into your bowl, so juice over a strainer.

### Reamer with a cup or bowl

1. Set the juicer on the counter so it's stable.
2. Press a lemon half, cut side down, onto the cone.
3. Twist the fruit back and forth while pressing down. The strainer catches seeds and the cup below collects the juice.
4. Check the measurement marks on the cup, then pour.

Juicers like the [OXO Good Grips 2-in-1 Citrus Juicer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PY5HP46/?tag=kitchenpot-20) come with two cones, one for lemons and limes and one for oranges. Consumer Reports picked it as the most versatile squeezer and found it produced the most juice of the models it tested. The small OXO version has a 2.5-ounce cup that's handy for measuring juice straight into cocktails or dressings.

## How to Use a Lever Citrus Press

A lever press is a heavy countertop juicer with a long handle. It's the kind you'll see at juice bars and lemonade stands.

1. Set the press on a flat, stable counter. Place a glass or pitcher under the spout.
2. Raise the handle all the way up.
3. Place a lemon half in the bottom cone or cup, cut side down.
4. Pull the handle down slowly and firmly. The top cup presses the fruit against the bottom.
5. Lift the handle, remove the peel, and repeat.

These are fast. ATK found its top countertop press, from Zulay Kitchen, juiced 10 lemons in under 3 minutes, the quickest of all the juicers it compared. The catch is size and weight. A cast iron press takes up real counter or cupboard space, so it makes the most sense if you juice a lot of citrus often, and these [small kitchen cabinet organization ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-cabinet-organization-ideas/) help you find a shelf for it.

## How to Use an Electric Citrus Juicer

Electric citrus juicers do the twisting for you. Most start spinning when you press fruit down on the cone.

1. Assemble the juicer: base, juice container, strainer and cone. Choose the small cone for lemons and limes.
2. Plug it in.
3. Press a lemon half, cut side down, onto the cone. On pressure-activated models like the BLACK+DECKER CJ625, the motor starts when you push down.
4. Keep steady pressure. The CJ625's cone switches direction automatically to pull out more juice.
5. Lift the fruit to stop the motor, remove the peel, and repeat.
6. Pour the juice from the built-in spout.

The CJ625 comes with two cones (a smaller one for lemons, limes and oranges and a larger one for grapefruit), an adjustable pulp control, and dishwasher-safe removable parts. An electric juicer is a good choice if you make fresh orange juice regularly or have trouble squeezing with your hands, though it competes for outlet space with the other [small kitchen appliances for cooking for one](/blog/best-small-kitchen-appliances-for-cooking-for-one/).

## How to Get More Juice From Every Lemon

Here's everything that helps, in one place:

- **Warm it up.** Room temperature, or 20 seconds in the microwave for a cold lemon.
- **Roll it.** A firm roll on the counter softens the fruit.
- **Cut crosswise.** Across the middle, not end to end.
- **Use a press.** ATK found presses outperformed reamers.
- **Squeeze slowly.** A slow, firm squeeze gets more juice and makes less mess.
- **Turn and squeeze again.** A quarter turn and a second press gets the last bit.
- **Pick heavy lemons.** A lemon that feels heavy for its size usually has more juice.

## Common Lemon Juicing Mistakes

- **Putting the lemon in upside down.** In a handheld squeezer, cut side down is right. Cut side up sprays juice everywhere.
- **Juicing lemons straight from the fridge.** Cold fruit gives less juice.
- **Cutting lemons end to end.** You miss much of the juice this way.
- **Squeezing too fast.** A hard snap sprays pulp and juice around the sides.
- **Using the wrong size squeezer.** A lime squeezer can't close fully on a big lemon, and an orange squeezer is loose on a lime.
- **Letting pulp dry on the juicer.** Dried pulp is much harder to scrub off. Rinse right away.
- **Skipping the strainer.** Most handheld squeezers catch seeds well, but small seeds can slip through. A strainer costs nothing and saves you picking seeds out of a dressing.

## How to Clean and Care for a Lemon Juicer

Rinse your juicer right after you use it. Lemon pulp and juice dry on fast, and dried pulp sticks in the holes.

1. Rinse under warm water, flushing the holes or strainer from the back.
2. Wash with warm soapy water and a small brush for the holes.
3. Dry fully before putting it away.

**Dishwasher or hand wash?** Check the care instructions. Many plastic juicers and enameled or coated aluminum squeezers are dishwasher safe, while others are hand wash only. A Consumer Reports materials expert explained that citrus juice has little or no effect on stainless steel or coated aluminum, but bare aluminum is badly affected by dishwasher detergent. If your squeezer's coating is chipping or peeling, it's time to replace it.

Electric juicers usually have dishwasher-safe removable parts, but the motor base should only be wiped with a damp cloth. If your dishwasher itself is leaving spots and film on tools, it may need attention too. Here's [how to clean a dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/).

## How to Store Fresh Lemon Juice

Squeezed more than you need? It keeps.

- **Fridge:** About 2 to 3 days in a sealed glass or plastic container, according to StillTasty.
- **Freezer:** Pour juice into an ice cube tray or a small silicone mold, like [Souper Cubes 2 Tbsp trays](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C3GV3QFX/?tag=kitchenpot-20), freeze, then move the cubes to a freezer bag. It keeps its best quality for about 3 to 4 months. Leave a little headspace in any container, since liquid expands as it freezes.

Measured frozen portions make it easy to drop exactly what you need into a recipe. A set of [airtight food storage containers](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/) is handy for keeping the fridge juice fresh and the frozen cubes from picking up odors.

## The Bottom Line on Using a Lemon Juicer

Using a lemon juicer comes down to a few simple moves. Warm and roll the lemon, cut it across the middle, and in a handheld squeezer put it in cut side down, facing the holes. Squeeze slowly. For small jobs, a handheld squeezer or reamer is all you need. For big batches, a lever press or electric juicer saves time and hand strength. Rinse it right away, and you'll get the most juice from every lemon with none in your eye.
