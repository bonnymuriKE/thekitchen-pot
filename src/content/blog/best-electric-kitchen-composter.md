Most electric composters do not make compost. They dry and grind your scraps into a crumbly, coffee-ground-like material, and that is still useful.

A few machines do make something close to real compost, using live microbes instead of heat alone. Which kind suits you depends on where the output is going.

This is a review of the machines themselves. For simpler swaps like a plain bin, see our guide to [eco-friendly alternatives to common kitchen appliances](/blog/eco-friendly-alternatives-to-common-kitchen-appliances/).

**The short version:** The [Lomi 3](https://www.amazon.com/LOMI-Lomi-3/dp/B0HLNJ9D9S/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is the best electric kitchen composter for most homes. It is small, fast and takes most food, including meat. If you want moist, living compost for a garden, get the [Reencle Prime](https://www.amazon.com/Reencle-Electric-Composter-Whisper-Quiet-Decomposition/dp/B0CVNNKPRP/?tag=kitchenpot-20) instead. The one rule that matters most: buy a dryer if you want less trash, and a microbial machine if you want compost.

## Dryer or Microbial: Which Kind of Electric Composter Do You Need?

This is the single biggest choice, and it matters more than any brand name.

| | Dryer (Lomi, FoodCycler, Airthereal) | Microbial (Reencle, GEME) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it works | Heats, dries and grinds a batch | Live microbes eat scraps all day |
| What comes out | Dry, crumbly material | Moist, soil-like material |
| Is it compost? | No, it still needs to break down | Close to it |
| Time | A few hours per batch | Hours per addition, about a month to finish |
| Power | About 1 to 1.5 kWh per cycle | About 1 kWh per day |
| Size | Fits on most counters | Usually floor height |

A dryer is a trash shrinker. It turns a smelly bag of scraps into a small, dry, stable material that does not rot in the bin. That alone ends most fruit fly and odor trouble, which ties into [keeping pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/).

A microbial machine is a tiny compost pile. It is slower and larger, but the output is alive and ready for soil.

## What Does an Electric Composter Actually Make?

A dryer makes a dry, crumbly material that looks a little like coffee grounds. Most of the water is gone, so it no longer rots.

That material is not finished compost. Once it gets wet again in the soil, soil microbes start breaking it down. Mix small amounts into potting soil or garden beds. Too much at once, close to young roots, can cause mold or tie up nutrients while it breaks down.

You can also put it in a city green bin or an outdoor pile. There it finishes breaking down like any other scrap, just faster.

A microbial machine makes a moist, dark material that is already partly broken down. Mix it into soil at the ratio its maker gives, usually about one part to eight or ten parts soil.

## What Can You Put in an Electric Composter?

More than you can put in a backyard pile, but not everything.

| Usually fine | Small amounts only | Keep out |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fruit and vegetable peels | Bread, rice and pasta | Hard bones, such as beef bones |
| Coffee grounds and loose tea | Dairy and cheese | Large fruit pits (avocado, mango, peach) |
| Eggs and eggshells | Sauces and dressings | Hard nut shells and [coconut shells](/blog/how-to-crack-open-a-coconut/) |

Each maker's list differs a little. FoodCycler takes poultry and fish bones, while Lomi takes no paper towels at all. Tape your machine's list inside a cabinet door.

## How Much Electricity Does an Electric Composter Use?

Less than people fear, but not nothing.

A dryer uses about 1 to 1.5 kilowatt-hours per full cycle. One kilowatt-hour is about what a 1,000-watt microwave uses in an hour. For another everyday comparison, see [how many watts an Instant Pot uses](/blog/how-many-watts-does-an-instant-pot-use/).

Run a dryer every other day and you use roughly 15 to 25 kilowatt-hours a month. On a typical US power bill, that adds a few dollars a month.

Microbial machines sip power all day instead. The Reencle Prime averages about 52 watts, or about 1.25 kilowatt-hours a day. That works out to a little under 40 kilowatt-hours a month, similar to a dryer used daily.

To keep the cost down, run full loads and choose shorter modes when you only want the scraps gone. More ideas are in our guide to [cutting kitchen energy use in a small apartment](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/).

## Do Electric Composters Smell?

They should not, as long as the filter is fresh.

Dryers pull moist air out of the bucket and push it through activated carbon. Carbon is full of tiny pores that trap smell molecules. When those pores fill up, smell starts to escape, usually as a warm, cooked-food odor during a cycle.

That is your signal to change the filter or refill the pellets. Lomi filters last about 45 cycles. FoodCycler pellets last three to four months. Airthereal sells its filters in two-packs.

Microbial machines smell faintly earthy when you open the lid. A sour or rotten smell usually means too much wet food at once, or too much of one thing. A good [range hood and ventilation setup](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) also helps with any cooking smell in a small space.

## How Loud Are They?

All of these are quieter than a typical dishwasher.

The Reencle Prime is the quietest at about 28 decibels. The GEME runs at 35 to 40. The Lomi 3 stays under 45, which is about the level of a quiet office. Dryers are loudest in short bursts while the blades grind.

## Are Electric Composters Good for the Environment?

They help, with one condition. Food scraps in a landfill rot without air and release methane, a strong greenhouse gas. Drying or composting them at home keeps them out of that process.

The condition is that the output gets used. If dried scraps go into the trash, you have mainly made the trash lighter and less smelly. That still has value, but the real win comes from putting the output in soil, a green bin or a compost pile.

The electricity matters too, so run full loads and skip half-empty cycles. ## Tips for Living With a Countertop Food Recycler

- **Keep a small bowl by the cutting board.** Tip it into the machine once a day instead of opening the lid for every peel.
- **Freeze scraps if you skip days.** A bag in the freezer stops smells and fruit flies. Our guide to [organizing a small fridge](/blog/organize-a-small-fridge/) has room-saving ideas.
- **Drain wet food.** Soup, juice and sauce slow dryers down and can make microbial machines sour.
- **Chop big pieces.** Corn cobs, melon rinds and celery break down faster when cut small.
- **Run the cleaning cycle or wash the bucket weekly.** Dishwasher-safe buckets make this easy. Our [dishwasher cleaning guide](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/) keeps the dishwasher itself fresh too.
- **Feed it coffee grounds.** They suit every machine here, and a [coffee maker with a grinder](/blog/best-coffee-maker-with-a-grinder/) makes plenty.
