You make a big stack of pancakes on Sunday morning, everyone eats, and the electric griddle sits on the counter with a shiny layer of butter and a few burnt drips of batter. By the time you get to it, the grease has set. Next weekend there's a little more. A few months later the surface is dark, a bit sticky, and eggs don't slide the way they used to.

The good news is that most electric griddles are easy to clean if you catch the mess early and use the right tools. This guide covers how to clean an electric griddle after every use, how to deal with burnt grease and sticky film, and what to do with the drip tray and heat control. The steps come straight from manufacturer care guides from brands like Presto and Cuisinart, so you won't damage the nonstick coating along the way.

> **Quick answer:** Unplug the griddle, remove the heat control, and let it cool until just warm. Wipe out loose grease with paper towels, then wash the surface with warm soapy water and a non-scratch sponge. Empty and wash the drip tray, rinse, and dry everything completely before you reattach the control. Never use steel wool, abrasive cleaners, or put the heat control in water.

## What You Need to Clean an Electric Griddle

You don't need special griddle cleaner. Nearly every job is handled with a few things already in your kitchen.

| Item | What it's best for | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper towels | Soaking up loose grease while the griddle is warm | Use tongs or a spatula to hold them if the surface is still hot |
| Dish soap and warm water | Everyday washing | Any regular grease-cutting dish soap works |
| [Non-scratch scrub sponge](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=scotch-brite+non-scratch+scrub+sponge&tag=kitchenpot-20) | Stuck-on batter and food | Presto recommends a non-metal pad like Scotch-Brite |
| Wooden or silicone spatula | Scraping off loose bits without scratching | Keep metal utensils off nonstick |
| Baking soda | Stubborn burnt spots | Mixed into a paste with water |
| Powdered automatic dishwasher detergent | Dark, sticky grease buildup | Presto's own method for discoloration, used in a diluted solution |
| Rubber gloves | Protecting your hands during the deep clean | Needed for the detergent solution |
| Soft dish towel | Drying the surface and underside | Air drying can leave water spots |

If you cook on the griddle often, keep a pair of [heat resistant gloves](/blog/10-best-heat-resistant-gloves-for-cooking/) nearby too. They make it much safer to wipe grease while the surface is still warm, which is when it comes off easiest.

## First, Check What Kind of Electric Griddle You Have

Before you fill the sink, flip the griddle over and look at the label or the manual. How you clean it depends on two things.

**Does the heat control come off?** Many classic griddles, including Presto's, use a removable probe-style heat control that plugs into the side. Once that's out, the griddle body is often fully immersible. Presto's product page for its [22-inch griddle](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B005FYF3OY/?tag=kitchenpot-20) says it's fully immersible with the heat control detached. If the cord and controls are built into the base, the griddle can't go in water at all.

**Is the cooking plate removable?** Grill-and-griddle combos like the Cuisinart Griddler have plates that pop out. Cuisinart's manual says the plates, drip tray and scraping tool can be washed by hand or on the top rack of the dishwasher. America's Test Kitchen's top-rated griddle, the Ninja Sizzle Pro XL, also has a removable plate, though the base itself is not submersible.

| Griddle type | Examples | Can the cooking surface go in water? | Dishwasher? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Removable heat control, fixed plate | Presto 22-inch, Presto Cool-Touch | Yes, once the control is removed | Depends on the model, so check the manual |
| Removable cooking plates | Cuisinart Griddler, Ninja Sizzle | Plates yes, base no | Plates often yes (top rack) |
| Built-in controls and cord | Many budget and compact griddles | No, wipe only | No |

This matters more than any cleaning trick. Water getting into the heat control or electrical parts is the fastest way to ruin a griddle.

## How to Clean an Electric Griddle After Every Use

This quick routine takes about five minutes and prevents almost every problem further down this page.

1. **Wipe the grease while it's warm.** Turn off the griddle and, while it's still warm but not hot, fold a few paper towels and wipe the surface toward the grease channel. Warm fat is liquid and lifts right off. Cold fat turns into a waxy layer.
2. **Unplug and remove the heat control.** Pull the plug from the wall first, then remove the control from the griddle. Set the control somewhere dry, away from the sink.
3. **Let it cool until you can touch it comfortably.** Don't pour cold water on a hot griddle. Sudden temperature changes can warp the plate.
4. **Scrape off loose bits.** Use a wooden or silicone spatula to push off any burnt batter or crumbs.
5. **Wash with warm soapy water.** For an immersible griddle, wash it in the sink like a big pan. For a non-immersible one, wet a sponge with warm soapy water, wring it out well, and wipe the surface. Use the non-scratch side for stuck spots. Presto says firm pressure is fine with a non-metal pad.
6. **Rinse.** Rinse the immersible griddle under the tap, or wipe the non-immersible one with a clean damp cloth two or three times to remove soap.
7. **Empty and wash the drip tray.** Pour the grease into a jar or can, not down the drain, then wash the tray in hot soapy water.
8. **Dry everything completely.** Towel dry the surface, the underside and the edges. Let the heat control socket air out so it's fully dry before you plug the control back in.

That's it. If you do this after every cook, you'll rarely need anything stronger.

### Should you oil the griddle after cleaning?

Presto's instructions say to season the nonstick surface before first use by rubbing it lightly with cooking oil and wiping off the excess with a paper towel. After a deep clean with detergent, Presto also recommends wiping the surface lightly with oil before you cook on it again. For everyday washes it's optional. A thin layer is all you need; any more just gets sticky.

## How to Clean Burnt-On Food From an Electric Griddle

Burnt batter, scorched cheese from grilled sandwiches and caramelized sauce from teriyaki chicken can all bond to the surface. Here's how to get them off without scratching.

### Method 1: The warm soak (immersible griddles and removable plates)

1. Unplug the griddle, remove the heat control, and let it cool.
2. Fill the sink with hot water and a good squeeze of dish soap.
3. Submerge the griddle or plate and let it soak for 20 to 60 minutes.
4. Scrub with a non-scratch sponge. Most of the burnt layer should wipe away.
5. Rinse, dry, and oil lightly if you like.

Don't leave it in the sink overnight. Presto warns that soaking for more than two hours may damage the nonstick finish.

### Method 2: The soapy towel (griddles you can't submerge)

1. Unplug the griddle and let it cool.
2. Soak a dish towel in hot soapy water and wring it out so it's damp but not dripping.
3. Lay the towel flat over the burnt area and leave it for 15 to 20 minutes. The steam and moisture soften the residue.
4. Remove the towel and wipe the spot with a non-scratch sponge.
5. Wipe with a clean damp cloth to remove soap, then dry.

This keeps water off the electrical parts while still giving you the benefit of a soak.

### Method 3: Baking soda paste for stubborn spots

1. Mix about 3 parts baking soda with 1 part water into a thick paste.
2. Spread it over the burnt spots on a cool griddle.
3. Leave it for 15 minutes.
4. Rub gently in small circles with a damp non-scratch sponge.
5. Wipe off all the paste with a damp cloth, rinse, and dry.

Baking soda is only mildly abrasive, so it's a common choice for nonstick. Go gently anyway. If you find yourself pressing hard, give the paste more time instead.

## How to Remove Sticky Residue and Dark Buildup

This is the problem most people search for: a brown, tacky film that soap won't remove. It feels like old varnish and food starts sticking to it.

### What causes it

The main cause is cooking spray. America's Test Kitchen explains that most cooking sprays contain lecithin, an emulsifier that has low heat stability. Under heat it discolors and forms a dark, sticky residue that builds up over time. Presto's griddle manuals say directly to avoid nonstick cooking sprays because they may make the cooking surface tacky.

Oil heated again and again without a proper wash does the same thing more slowly. Each layer bakes onto the last.

### Presto's method for discoloration and grease buildup

Presto's manuals describe a specific fix for a darkened nonstick surface. Here's how it works:

1. Slide the drip tray into place and preheat the griddle to 225°F.
2. Mix 1 tablespoon of powdered automatic dishwasher detergent into 1 cup of hot water.
3. Put on rubber gloves. Dip a non-metal cleaning pad into the solution and rub it over the cooking surface.
4. Keep cleaning until the solution is used up.
5. Turn the griddle off, unplug it, and let it cool.
6. Remove the heat control, then wash the griddle in hot soapy water and dry it.
7. Wipe the cooking surface lightly with cooking oil before you use it again.

The low heat helps the detergent soften the polymerized oil, and the gloves protect your skin from both the heat and the detergent. If the buildup is thick, it can take a second round on another day.

### How to stop it coming back

- Cook with a little butter or oil instead of spray. If you like the even coat a spray gives, use a refillable oil mister, which ATK suggests as an alternative.
- Wash the griddle after every use, even if it "looks clean." A thin film of oil you can't see is what turns sticky next time.
- Keep the heat moderate. Presto's temperature guide for its griddles runs from about 275°F to 400°F depending on the food, and most breakfast foods cook well in the middle of that range. Cranking the griddle to its maximum just burns oil faster.

If you want a good oil for high-heat cooking, many of the same oils we cover for [air fryer cooking](/blog/best-oil-for-air-fryer/) work well on a griddle, since both need oils that stand up to heat.

## Match the Problem to the Fix

Use this table to figure out what you're looking at and the quickest way to deal with it.

| What you see | Likely cause | Best fix | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shiny layer of liquid grease | Normal cooking fat | Paper towel wipe while warm, then soapy wash | 5 min |
| Crusty black bits | Burnt batter, cheese or sugar | Warm soak or soapy towel, then non-scratch sponge | 20 to 60 min |
| Small stubborn burnt spots | Food baked on over several uses | Baking soda paste | 15 to 20 min |
| Dark, tacky film | Cooking spray or polymerized oil | Presto's warm detergent solution method | 15 min plus cooling |
| Water spots on the outside | Air drying or hard water | Wipe with diluted white vinegar, then dry | 2 min |
| Grease in the drip channel | Normal runoff | Wipe with paper towel, then soapy sponge | 2 min |
| Flaking or peeling coating | Worn or scratched nonstick | Can't be fixed; time to replace | n/a |

## How to Clean the Drip Tray and Heat Control

### The drip tray

The drip tray catches everything that runs off, so it gets greasy fast. After each use:

1. Let the tray cool, then slide it out carefully so the grease doesn't spill.
2. Pour the fat into an old jar or can and throw it in the trash once it sets. Grease poured down the sink can clog your pipes.
3. Wipe the tray with a paper towel, then wash it in hot soapy water.
4. If your manual says the tray is dishwasher safe, as Cuisinart's does for the Griddler, you can put it on the top rack.

### The heat control

The removable heat control is the most important part to protect, because it holds the thermostat and electrical parts. Every Presto manual we checked says the same thing: do not immerse the heat control in water or let it touch any liquid.

- Unplug it from the wall before removing it from the griddle.
- If it gets greasy, wipe it with a barely damp cloth and dry it immediately.
- Keep it away from the sink while you wash the griddle.
- Before plugging it back in, make sure the socket on the griddle is completely dry.

## How to Clean the Outside and Underside

The bottom and sides of a griddle collect splatter too, and it bakes on the same way it does on the bottom of a pan.

- For immersible griddles, wash the underside at the same time as the top.
- For non-immersible ones, wipe the base with a damp soapy cloth, then a clean damp cloth, then dry.
- For painted or plastic exteriors, skip scouring powders. They can scratch or dull the finish.
- For water spots, a cloth with a little diluted white vinegar works well. Dry right after.

Be careful with the dishwasher. Presto's 22-inch griddle manual says not to wash it in the dishwasher because the caustic detergent will likely discolor the bottom. Its Cool-Touch Griddle/Warmer Plus, on the other hand, is dishwasher safe once the control is removed, according to America's Test Kitchen. Same brand, different rules, which is why checking your own manual matters.

## Mistakes That Ruin an Electric Griddle

Most griddles don't wear out. They get damaged by cleaning shortcuts. Avoid these:

| Avoid this | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Steel wool or metal scouring pads | Scratches and strips the nonstick coating |
| Abrasive powders like Comet or Bar Keepers Friend | Too harsh for nonstick; ATK warns against them on coated surfaces |
| Metal spatulas and knives on the surface | Scratch the coating; Presto says to avoid metal utensils |
| Nonstick cooking spray | Leaves sticky, dark residue over time |
| Submerging the heat control or a non-immersible griddle | Can damage the electrical parts and create a shock risk |
| Soaking longer than two hours | Presto says it may damage the nonstick finish |
| Pouring cold water on a hot griddle | Sudden temperature change can warp the plate |
| Plugging in the control before the socket is dry | Moisture and electricity don't mix |

A [set of metal spatulas](/blog/best-metal-spatula-set/) is great for cast iron and stainless steel, but for a nonstick griddle, stick with silicone, nylon or wood.

## Why a Clean Griddle Cooks Better

A clean surface isn't just about looks. Grease buildup acts like a thin layer of insulation and glue at the same time. Food sticks, pancakes brown unevenly, and old oil starts to smoke and give food a stale taste.

Nonstick coatings also last longer when you care for them. ## Cleaning an Electric Griddle in a Small Kitchen

A 22-inch griddle is a big thing to wrangle in a small apartment kitchen, especially if your sink is narrow. A few tricks help:

- **Clean it where it sits.** The paper-towel wipe, the soapy towel soak and Presto's detergent method all happen right on the counter. You don't need to fit the griddle in the sink at all.
- **Remove the handles.** Some models, like Presto's 22-inch griddle, have handles that come off without tools. That makes the griddle easier to wash and slimmer to store.
- **Use a sheet pan as a tray.** Set the griddle's drip tray on a rimmed sheet pan while you carry it to the trash so grease doesn't drip across the floor.
- **Dry and store it standing up.** Once it's fully dry, slide the griddle upright into a cabinet or next to the fridge. These [vertical storage ideas for small kitchens](/blog/vertical-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/) show how to make room for flat, bulky appliances.
- **Don't leave it on the counter.** A griddle you only use on weekends takes up prime prep space. Our guide to [countertop organization in a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/) covers what's worth leaving out and what isn't.

If you cook mostly for yourself, a big griddle may not be the best fit at all. A compact grill or a good skillet can do the same job in less space. ## When to Replace Your Electric Griddle

Cleaning can fix grease, stains and stickiness. It can't fix damage. It's time for a new griddle if:

- The nonstick coating is flaking, peeling or deeply scratched.
- The plate is warped and oil pools on one side.
- The heat control no longer holds a steady temperature, or it sparks, smells burnt or has a damaged cord.
- The surface stays sticky even after the detergent method and food sticks badly every time.

If you're shopping for a replacement, look for a model with a removable heat control or removable plates. Either one makes cleaning far easier.
