These small kitchen storage ideas work zone by zone, so you can fix the spots you waste without adding a single cabinet.

Most small kitchens are not short on space. They are short on space in use. Below are more than 50 ideas, grouped by zone, with links to our deeper guides when you want the full method.

**The short version:** Declutter first, then work zone by zone. Add risers and bins inside cabinets, racks on the doors and one rail on the wall. Use the side of the fridge, the space under the sink and the gap beside the fridge for slim storage. Put rarely used items above the cabinets, and keep food in square containers in one spot.

## How Do I Get More Storage in a Small Kitchen?

You get more storage in a small kitchen by using height and hidden surfaces, not by buying more furniture. A standard upper cabinet is 12 inches deep and often 30 inches tall. Most people put two shelves of plates in it and waste the air above each stack.

Our rule of thumb has three steps:

1. **Cut first.** Donate duplicates, single-use gadgets and anything you have not touched in a year. Storage only helps once the extra stuff is gone.
2. **Store by frequency.** Daily items go between waist and shoulder height. Weekly items go low. Yearly items go up high or out of the kitchen.
3. **Fill one zone at a time.** Finish the cabinets before you buy a cart. Finish the doors before you drill the wall.

If you want the full planning method, our guide on [how to organize a small kitchen with no pantry](/blog/how-to-organize-a-small-kitchen-with-no-pantry/) walks through it step by step.

## Inside Cabinets: Use the Height You Already Have

The goal is to stop wasting the space above each stack and at the back of each shelf.

A wire riser turns one shelf into two. Put plates underneath and bowls or mugs on top. Lay baking sheets flat and you can only reach the top one. Stand them on edge with a simple divider and you pull out any one in a second. Group packets, spice refills and tea bags in a clear bin. Pull the whole bin out like a drawer, and label the front.

## Cabinet Doors: The Storage You Forget

The back of every cabinet door is free space. Before you add anything, check the clearance. Close the door slowly and measure how much room sits between the door and the shelf edge. Most cabinets have 1 to 2 inches.

### Idea 7: Hang a Rack for Lids or Cutting Boards

A slim door-mounted rack holds pot lids or thin cutting boards. Mount it low enough that it clears the shelves when the door closes.

### Idea 8: Use Adhesive Hooks for Measuring Cups and Spoons

Measuring cups hang flat on small hooks.

### Idea 9: Add a Pocket for Foil and Wrap

A shallow bin on the door under the sink or beside the stove holds foil, plastic wrap and parchment rolls. That frees a whole drawer. The [madesmart Over-Door Wrap Organizer](https://www.amazon.com/madesmart-Over-Door-Wrap-Organizer/dp/B0792FF476/?tag=kitchenpot-20) hooks over the top of the door with soft grips, so it needs no tools and leaves no marks. Check your door gap first, since the hooks need a little room at the top.

### Idea 10: Use the Pantry or Closet Door

If you have a full-height door nearby, an over-the-door rack with six or more baskets holds a small pantry's worth of cans and jars. It needs no tools at all.

## Walls: Get Daily Tools Off the Counter

The wall between your counter and the upper cabinets is about 18 inches tall. In most kitchens it holds nothing but a backsplash. That makes it the best real estate you have for daily tools.

### Idea 11: One Rail With S-Hooks

A single metal rail holds spatulas, ladles, a whisk and a mug or two. Mount it where you cook, not where it looks tidy. ### Idea 13: Pegboard for Renters and Tinkerers

## Under the Sink: Work Around the Pipes

Under the sink is awkward because of the drain pipe. That U-shaped bend in the pipe is called the P-trap. It cuts the space in half. The fix is to build storage around it, not to fight it.

### Idea 16: A Two-Tier Sliding Organizer

Pick one that fits on either side of the pipes. Sliding baskets bring sponges and soap to the front, so nothing gets lost at the back. On a budget, the [Simple Houseware Under Sink 2-Tier Expandable Shelf](https://www.amazon.com/Simple-Houseware-Under-Sink-Expandable-Shelf/dp/B01LWX3P9O/?tag=kitchenpot-20) stretches from 15.75 to 25 inches wide, and its panels lift out to clear the pipes. If you can spend more, the [REALINN L-Shaped 2-Tier Pull Out Under Sink Organizer](https://www.amazon.com/REALINN-L-Shaped-2-Tier-Under-Sink-Organizer/dp/B0C5DBMYZF/?tag=kitchenpot-20) wraps around the drain in an L, with a pull-out drawer on the bottom tier.

### Idea 17: A Tension Rod for Spray Bottles

Fit a tension rod across the top of the cabinet. Hang spray bottles by their triggers. That clears the whole floor of the cabinet for bins.

### Idea 18: A Tray on the Cabinet Floor

A plastic boot tray or drip tray catches leaks and makes cleanup easy.

### Idea 19: Door Bins for Sponges and Gloves

A small adhesive bin on the inside of the door holds gloves, scrub brushes and spare sponges.

Keep cleaning products here and food somewhere else. Never store food, pet food or paper plates under the sink. Leaks, chemicals and pests all start here. Our guide on [how to keep pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/) explains why this cabinet matters.

## Fridge Top and Sides: Free Surfaces in Plain Sight

The fridge is the biggest box in the kitchen, and its outside is often bare.

### Idea 20: Magnetic Shelves on the Side

A magnetic spice rack or small shelf on the side of the fridge holds spices, foil or paper towels. Check the side is steel with a fridge magnet first. Many stainless fronts are not magnetic, but most side panels are. The [Roysili Magnetic Spice Rack for Refrigerator, Set of 2](https://www.amazon.com/Roysili-Magnetic-Refrigerator-Organizer-Spice-Rack/dp/B07WZG66D1/?tag=kitchenpot-20) gives you two shelves that each hold up to 5 pounds, with no drilling.

### Idea 21: A Magnetic Towel Bar or Hooks

Hang a towel, an oven mitt or a small bag of reusable produce bags. Keep heavy items low on the panel so they do not slide.

### Idea 22: A Light Basket on Top

The top of the fridge suits light items you use rarely, such as party napkins or spare paper towels. Skip heavy appliances up there. Lifting them down over your head is risky, and many fridges vent heat near the top or back.

### Idea 23: A Slim Cart in the Gap

Most fridges sit in a slot with a few inches of gap on one side. A slim rolling cart, 5 to 7 inches wide, slides into that gap and holds cans, oils and spices. Measure the gap before you shop. The [3-Tier Slim Rolling Cart](https://www.amazon.com/3-Tier-Slim-Rolling-Cart/dp/B07QRH2PZS/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is built for exactly this kind of narrow slot beside a fridge or washer.

## Floor and Toe-Kick: The Lowest Storage in the Room

### What Is the Toe-Kick?

The toe-kick is the recessed strip at the bottom of your base cabinets. It lets you stand close to the counter. It is usually about 4 inches tall and runs the length of every base cabinet. In most kitchens it is empty.

### Idea 24: Toe-Kick Drawers

If you own your home, a carpenter can fit shallow drawers into the toe-kick. They hold baking sheets, placemats, trays and a step stool. This is an owner's project, not a renter's.

### Idea 25: A Flat Bin Behind the Toe-Kick Panel

Some toe-kick panels pop off with a gentle pull. Behind them is a hollow space where a flat bin can hold rarely used trays. Check for pipes and wires first.

### Idea 26: Small Kitchen Storage Cabinets on Wheels

A rolling cart or wheeled cabinet is the most flexible storage you can buy. It parks against a wall, rolls out for prep and rolls back when you finish. Lock the wheels when you use it as a work surface. For plain storage, the [Honey-Can-Do 3-Tier Metal Rolling Cart](https://www.amazon.com/Honey-Can-Do-3-Tier-Metal-Rolling-Cart/dp/B077STDKJM/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has three deep metal tiers for produce, cans and snacks. Keep toasters and air fryers off its lower tiers. A heat appliance boxed in under another shelf has nowhere to vent.

### Idea 27: A Freestanding Shelf or Bakers Rack

One open shelf unit at the end of a counter run can hold cookbooks, bins and a fruit basket. Pick one no deeper than 16 inches so it stays out of the walkway. If a microwave goes on it, use the top shelf with open air above, never a lower shelf under another one.

Floor storage only works if it leaves room to move. A clear walkway of 42 inches suits one cook. ## Above the Cabinets and the Ceiling: Storage for Rare Items

Most upper cabinets stop 12 to 18 inches below an 8-foot ceiling. That gap is perfect for things you use a few times a year.

### Idea 28: Matching Lidded Baskets

Lidded baskets keep dust and grease off and look calm from below. Store holiday platters, the roasting pan and spare paper goods up there. Label the side that faces you.

### Idea 29: Extend the Uppers With a Shelf

A plain shelf on top of the cabinets, set back from the edge, adds a second level for very light items.

### Idea 30: A Ceiling Pot Rack

A ceiling rack frees a whole cabinet of pans. It must screw into a ceiling joist, the wooden beam behind the drywall. Drywall alone will not hold the weight. Hang it over an island or a table, not over the walkway.

### Idea 31: Keep a Step Stool Nearby

High storage only works if you can reach it safely. A folding step stool stored beside the fridge makes the top shelf usable. The [COSCO 2-Step Big Step Folding Steel Step Stool](https://www.amazon.com/Cosco-2-Step-Big-Step-Folding-Stool/dp/B0032JRVPI/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has two wide steps and folds to about 2 inches thin, so it slips beside the fridge.

Keep the space above the cabinets sparse. Our guide to [make a small kitchen look bigger](/blog/make-a-small-kitchen-look-bigger/) explains why sparse looks larger.

## How Do I Store Food in a Small Kitchen?

You store food in a small kitchen by decanting it into square containers, keeping it in one zone and buying less at a time. Round boxes and half-empty bags waste more space than anything else in a cabinet.

### Idea 32: Square, Stackable Containers

Square containers fit together with no gaps, and clear ones show you what is low. The [Rubbermaid Brilliance 14-Piece Set](https://www.amazon.com/Rubbermaid-Brilliance-14-Piece-Set/dp/B01JCNETC0/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is our pick for a starter set: crystal-clear, airtight latching lids, and the containers nest when empty. Our guide to the [best airtight food storage containers](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/) covers sizes that stack well.

### Idea 33: One Food Zone

Keep all dry food in one cabinet or on one shelf near the prep area.

### Idea 34: First In, First Out

Put new cans and boxes behind the old ones. You use the older food first, and nothing expires at the back.

### Idea 35: Keep Produce Where It Lasts

Keep onions and potatoes apart in a cool, dark spot, since onions make potatoes sprout faster. A wire basket hanging under a shelf works well. Keep oil and spices away from the stove, where heat makes them go stale faster.

### Idea 36: Buy for This Week

Stock less and you need less storage. Our guide on [how to stock a small kitchen for solo cooking](/blog/how-to-stock-a-small-kitchen-for-solo-cooking/) helps you buy only what you will use. Batch cooking with our [easy meal prep ideas for one person](/blog/easy-meal-prep-ideas-for-one-person-in-a-small-kitchen/) cuts the number of half-used packets too.

Use the fridge well, too. Keep it at 40°F or below and eat leftovers within 3 to 4 days.

## Small Kitchen Storage Hacks Most People Miss

These quick hacks take minutes and cost little or nothing.

- **Store mugs on hooks under a shelf.** Screw-in cup hooks under the upper cabinet free a whole shelf.
- **Keep the bakeware stack inside the oven.** If you bake rarely, store one sheet pan and a cake pan in the oven. Take them out before preheating.
- **Use the microwave top.** A countertop microwave can hold a light basket of paper towels, if the vents stay clear.
- **Use a file box for pans.** A metal file sorter in a cabinet holds skillets on edge.
- **Choose cookware that nests.** A set of [stackable pots and pans](/blog/7-best-stackable-pots-and-pans/) fits in one cabinet instead of three.
- **Pick smaller pans.** Most solo cooks only need a few [cookware sizes](/blog/best-cookware-sizes-for-cooking-for-one/).
- **Fold bakeware down.** Collapsible bowls and measuring cups fill a third of the space. Our [space-saving baking tool essentials](/blog/space-saving-baking-tool-essentials/) list the best ones.

Bakers with tight cabinets should also read [how to bake in a small kitchen without extra counter space](/blog/how-to-bake-in-a-small-kitchen-without-extra-counter-space/).

## Kitchen Storage Ideas for Small Apartments and Renters

Renters cannot drill freely or change cabinets. These ideas leave no damage behind.

1. **Use tension rods** under the sink and between cabinet sides.
2. **Pick adhesive hooks rated for the weight** you will hang, and stick them to clean, dry, smooth surfaces only.
3. **Rely on freestanding pieces**, such as a rolling cart, a bakers rack or a slim shelf.
4. **Use over-door racks** on any full-height door.
5. **Keep every original part** you remove, such as cabinet doors or shelf pins, in a labeled bag.

In a studio, choose pieces that look like furniture.

## Common Small Kitchen Storage Mistakes

- **Buying organizers before decluttering.** Cut first, then buy what fills a real gap.
- **Storing by category instead of use.** Your favorite pan belongs by the stove, not with the other pans in the far corner.
- **Overloading adhesive hooks.** They fail without warning. Respect the weight on the package, and use screws for anything heavy.
- **Blocking the walkway.** A cart parked in a 36-inch aisle makes the kitchen worse, not better.
- **Filling the top of the fridge.** Heavy items up there are hard to lift down, and they can trap heat.
- **Storing food under the sink.** Leaks and chemicals do not belong near food.
- **Adding open storage near the stove.** Grease builds up on anything stored within a couple of feet of the burners. Our guide on [small kitchen ventilation](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) helps keep it down.
