Air fryers do not fry. They blow hot air fast, and oil is there to carry heat and flavor onto the surface of your food.

That changes which oil you want. The one number that matters is the smoke point, and most kitchen staples clear the bar with room to spare.

**The short version:** refined avocado oil handles any air fryer setting, refined peanut and sunflower oil come close, and extra virgin olive oil is fine below about 375°F. Skip aerosol spray entirely.

| Avocado, refined | About 520°F | Neutral, slightly buttery | Anything, at any setting |
| Safflower, refined | About 510°F | Neutral | High-heat roasting |
| Peanut, refined | About 450°F | Faintly nutty | Chicken, fries, wings |
| Sunflower, refined or high-oleic | About 450°F | Neutral | Vegetables, fish, general use |
| Soybean or generic vegetable oil | About 450°F | Neutral | Everyday cooking on a budget |

The olive oil ranges come from the North American Olive Oil Association, which lists [350°F to 410°F for extra virgin and 390°F to 470°F for refined](https://www.aboutoliveoil.org/olive-oil-smoke-point).

## Why Smoke Point Is the One Spec That Matters

Every oil has a temperature at which it starts breaking down and giving off visible smoke. Past that point the fat splits into compounds that taste bitter and smell sharp. The oil also stops protecting your food and starts flavoring it, badly.

In a normal pan you notice this straight away. In an air fryer you often do not, because the basket sits inside a closed drawer with a fan pushing air past it. The first sign is usually a smell, then a kitchen full of haze.

### Your Air Fryer Tops Out Lower Than You Think

Most air fryers max out at 400°F. Instant's Vortex manual, for example, lists 205°C as the highest setting across every cooking mode. A few models go higher, and the Ninja Air Fryer Max XL is rated to 450°F on its Max Crisp setting.

So the real bar is 400°F to 450°F. Refined avocado, peanut, sunflower and light olive oil all clear it. That is most of the decision made already, and it is why the [air fryers under $100](/blog/best-air-fryers-under-100/) you can buy today work fine with supermarket oils.

### What Happens Past the Smoke Point

Three things go wrong at once, and they compound.

1. **Taste.** Broken-down fat tastes acrid. It clings to everything in the basket, so one over-hot batch ruins the whole tray.
2. **Smoke.** Air fryers have small vents. Smoke has nowhere to go but your kitchen, which is bad news in an apartment with one window and no [range hood](/blog/6-best-ductless-range-hood/).
3. **Residue.** Burnt oil polymerizes into a hard brown varnish on the basket. It is the same sticky layer that builds on [sheet pans and stainless pans](/blog/how-to-clean-stainless-steel-pans/), and it is just as stubborn.

That third one costs you the most over time. Our guide to [cleaning an air fryer basket](/blog/how-to-clean-an-air-fryer-basket/) covers how to lift it once it is there.

## Why Extra Virgin Olive Oil Works Better Than Its Reputation

Extra virgin olive oil has a bad name in air fryer circles, and the reason is a misreading of the numbers.

Yes, its smoke point is lower, roughly 350°F to 410°F depending on the bottle. The catch is that **oven temperature is not food temperature**. While food still holds water, the water evaporates and holds the surface well below the set temperature. Food only shoots up in temperature once it is properly dry, which is also when it burns.

Smoke point is also a weak measure of how an oil actually behaves. The North American Olive Oil Association points out that stability matters more, and extra virgin olive oil carries polyphenols and antioxidants that slow breakdown. A refined oil with a higher number can oxidize faster in practice.

So here is the practical rule. Use extra virgin at 375°F or below for vegetables, chicken thighs, halloumi and potatoes. Switch to a refined oil at 400°F or above, or for long cooks. The flavor trade is real, and roasted vegetables taste better with the good stuff.

If you mostly cook one or two portions, the bottle size matters more than the grade. A small bottle used up in two months beats a large one that turns rancid, which is the same logic behind our [small kitchen appliance picks for solo cooks](/blog/best-small-kitchen-appliances-for-cooking-for-one/).

## How Little Oil an Air Fryer Really Needs

Far less than most recipes suggest. One to two teaspoons coats a full basket of vegetables or potatoes.

The method matters more than the amount. Put the food in a bowl, add the oil, and toss it with your hands until every piece has a faint sheen. Then season and load the basket. Oil poured on top of packed food runs straight to the bottom.

Never pour oil into the drawer itself. Instant's manual is blunt about this: excess cooking oil can cause a fire. The drawer under the basket is for dripping fat, not for a pool of oil.

For scale, one teaspoon of oil is about 40 calories and 4.5 grams of fat. A whole basket of chips seasoned this way uses roughly what you would put on a single salad. Compare that to deep frying, where the food sits submerged.

Some foods need no oil at all:

- Frozen chips, nuggets and other coated freezer food are already oiled. Adding more makes them greasy, as our guide to [frozen french fries in an air fryer](/blog/how-to-cook-frozen-french-fries-in-an-air-fryer/) explains.
- Bacon, sausages and chicken thighs with skin render their own fat.
- Leftovers you are reheating, whether that is [pizza](/blog/how-to-reheat-pizza/) or [chicken wings](/blog/how-to-reheat-chicken-wings/), already have fat in them.

Lean food is the opposite. Chicken breast, tofu, chickpeas and fresh-cut potatoes dry out and stick without a thin coat. A film of oil also glues seasoning in place, so your paprika ends up on the food rather than in the fan.

## Why Aerosol Cooking Spray Wrecks Air Fryer Baskets

This is the single most expensive mistake air fryer owners make, and the manufacturers say so directly.

Instant's air fryer [user manual](https://instantpot.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Vortex-Compact-5L-User-Manual-UK-29.05.25.pdf) states: "DO NOT use pressurised, aerosol oil sprays, since they contain chemicals that can damage the non-stick finish of the cooking tray." The same manual recommends a non-aerosol, pump-style sprayer instead.

Two ingredients cause the damage. The first is the **propellant**, a pressurized gas that carries the oil out of the can. The second is **lecithin**, an emulsifier that helps the oil spread as a thin film.

Lecithin is the real problem. America's Test Kitchen reported that lecithin has low thermal stability, so it discolors, degrades and forms a dark sticky residue at far lower temperatures than plain oil does. In an air fryer that residue bakes onto the mesh and the corners of the basket.

Then comes the part that finishes the coating off. The film will not wash off, so people attack it with a scouring pad, and the scrubbing takes the non-stick layer with it. The spray does not strip the coating on its own. It creates the mess that makes you strip it yourself.

**What to use instead:**

- A refillable pump mister filled with the oil of your choice.
- A silicone brush, which wastes less oil than a mister and is easier to wash.
- Your hands and a mixing bowl, which is still the most even method.

If your basket is already patchy, treat it gently from here. The advice in our guide to [nonstick cookware care](/blog/what-is-nonstick-cookware/) applies to air fryer baskets too, and the same goes for [ceramic-coated pans](/blog/how-to-clean-ceramic-pan/).

## How to Match the Oil to the Food

Temperature decides the oil, and the food decides the temperature. This table puts the two together.

| Food | Typical setting | Oil to use | How much |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fresh-cut chips | 380°F to 400°F | Avocado, peanut or canola | 2 tsp per basket |
| Frozen chips or nuggets | 400°F | None needed | None |
| Chicken wings | 400°F to 450°F | Peanut or avocado | 1 tsp, or none if skin-on |
| Chicken breast | 375°F | Extra virgin olive | 1 tsp |
| Roast vegetables | 375°F | Extra virgin olive or grapeseed | 2 tsp per basket |
| Fish fillets | 375°F to 390°F | Grapeseed or sunflower | 1 tsp |
| Tofu or chickpeas | 375°F to 400°F | Sunflower or avocado | 2 tsp, tossed with cornstarch |
| Reheating leftovers | 350°F | None needed | None |

A quick note on the last row. Reheating works better with no extra oil, whether you are warming [sausages](/blog/how-to-cook-sausages-in-the-oven/) or last night's takeout. ## How to Store Cooking Oil

Oil goes off, and rancid oil tastes of cardboard long before it smells wrong. Three things speed up the damage: heat, light and air.

- **Keep it away from the hob.** The cupboard above the cooker is the worst spot in the kitchen. Pick a cool, dark one instead.
- **Choose dark glass or steel where you can.** Clear plastic lets light through, which is why the good extra virgin oils come in tinted bottles.
- **Close it properly.** Every hour the cap is off is more oxidation. A mister with a sealed top helps here too.
- **Buy the size you will finish.** Most oils keep well for a few months once opened. A gallon jug is only a bargain if you get through it.
- **Smell before you pour.** Crayon, putty or old nuts means it has turned. Throw it out.

If counter and cupboard space is your constraint, decanting a large bottle into a mister and storing the rest elsewhere works well. ## Mistakes That Ruin Air Fryer Food

- **Spraying the empty basket.** Oil hits hot metal with nothing to absorb it and burns on. Oil the food, not the basket.
- **Using too much.** Excess oil drips through the mesh, pools in the drawer and smokes. Two teaspoons is plenty.
- **Overfilling the basket.** Air needs to move. A crowded basket steams food instead of crisping it, whatever oil you used.
- **Using the wrong oil grade.** Unrefined peanut and sunflower oils smoke far lower than their refined versions.
- **Scrubbing burnt oil with steel wool.** That is what actually removes a non-stick coating. Soak in warm soapy water instead, the same way you would treat [hard anodized cookware](/blog/how-to-clean-hard-anodized-cookware/).
- **Oiling food that is already oily.** Frozen coated food and fatty cuts need nothing at all.

Skip those six and your basket will outlast the machine. The rest is seasoning, and a decent [pepper mill](/blog/best-pepper-mill/) does more for air fryer chips than any oil.
