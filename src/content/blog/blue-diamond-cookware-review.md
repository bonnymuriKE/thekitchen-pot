Diamonds do not make a pan nonstick. They make it conduct heat and resist scratches, which is not the same thing at all.

That gap between what the marketing implies and what the coating does explains almost every disappointed Blue Diamond review you will read.

## How Diamond-Infused Ceramic Nonstick Is Made

There is no ceramic tile involved, and the pan is not made of ceramic. Under the colour, a Blue Diamond pan is forged aluminum with a thin coating sprayed and baked onto the cooking surface.

That coating is a sol-gel. A liquid solution of silicon-based compounds ("sol") is applied to the metal and then cured with heat until it cross-links into a hard, glassy solid ("gel"), forming a ceramic-like film only a few microns thick. Inside that film are additives with very low surface energy, which is the physics term for how strongly things want to stick to it. Low surface energy means sauce beads up instead of grabbing.

Now add the diamonds. Diamond dust is mixed into the coating before curing, and it does two measurable things:

- **It conducts heat.** Diamond is one of the most thermally conductive materials known, so the particles help pull heat through a coating layer that would otherwise insulate the food slightly from the aluminum below.
- **It adds hardness.** Diamond is the hardest natural material, so a coating loaded with it shrugs off scraping far better than a plain sol-gel. That is why Blue Diamond can call its pans metal utensil safe.

Both of those claims hold up. Independent reviewers who have used the pans with metal tools report the surface stays free of visible scratches longer than most coated pans do.

### So why does it stop releasing food?

Because scratches were never the main way a ceramic coating dies.

Traditional PTFE nonstick usually fails mechanically: something scrapes it off. Sol-gel ceramic fails chemically and thermally. Coating manufacturers are direct about this. PPG, one of the largest makers of nonstick coatings, states that for sol-gel formulations, [thermal stress and chemicals, not abrasions and scratches, are the biggest cause of failures](https://www.ppg.com/en-US/industrialcoatings/industrial-blog/testing-standard-for-sol-gel-non-stick).

In your kitchen that means three things are quietly ageing the coating every time you cook:

1. **Heat cycles.** Each heat-and-cool cycle stresses the bond between a rigid glassy film and an aluminum base that expands more than the film does. Microscopic cracks open up.
2. **Polymerised oil.** Thin films of burnt-on oil bake into an invisible varnish that raises the surface energy. The pan is not worn out; it is glazed. This is why food starts sticking in patches rather than all at once.
3. **Detergent and alkalinity.** Dishwasher detergent is aggressive, and repeated cycles degrade sol-gel films faster than hand washing.

None of that is unique to Blue Diamond. It is true of every ceramic-coated pan on the market, which is why our guide to [cleaning a ceramic pan](/blog/how-to-clean-ceramic-pan/) leans so hard on low heat and gentle washing, and why the [best ceramic cookware sets](/blog/best-ceramic-cookware-set/) are the ones that slow the decline rather than prevent it.

> **The realistic expectation:** superb release for the first year or so, decent release for a while after that, then a pan you need oil in. Buy accordingly, and price it as a consumable rather than an heirloom.

## Making a Ceramic Coating Last as Long as It Can

You cannot stop the ageing process, but you can slow it down a lot. These habits are the difference between two good years and six months.

- **Cook on medium or lower.** Ceramic coatings fail from thermal stress more than from scratches. High heat is the single biggest accelerant.
- **Never preheat an empty pan.** With nothing to absorb the energy, the coating takes all of it. Add oil or food first.
- **Skip aerosol cooking spray.** The propellants and emulsifiers polymerise into a sticky varnish that builds up invisibly and makes the pan seem worn out long before it is.
- **Hand wash in warm soapy water.** Dishwasher detergent is harsh on sol-gel films. Blue Diamond calls its cookware dishwasher safe and then recommends hand washing, which tells you what you need to know.
- **Let the pan cool before it meets water.** Thermal shock warps aluminum and cracks coatings. Warping is one of the recurring complaints in reviews of budget aluminum pans.
- **Stack with protection.** A cloth, paper towel or felt pan protector between nested pans. Our guide to [organizing pots and pans](/blog/how-to-organize-pots-and-pans/) covers rails and racks that avoid stacking altogether.
- **Deglaze instead of scrubbing.** Warm water in a still-warm pan lifts residue without abrasives. The same principle as [cleaning stainless steel pans](/blog/how-to-clean-stainless-steel-pans/), minus the scouring powder.
