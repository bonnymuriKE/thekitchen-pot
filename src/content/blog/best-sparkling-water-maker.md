The harder part is choosing one. Cylinder types, bottle rules and "carbonates anything" claims all vary from brand to brand.

**The short version:** Get the [SodaStream Terra](https://www.amazon.com/SodaStream-Terra-Sparkling-Water-Maker-Starter-Kit/dp/B0B2X132WK/?tag=kitchenpot-20) if you only want fizzy water. If you also want to fizz juice or wine, buy a machine made for it, like the SodaStream Mix or Drinkmate OmniFizz. The one rule that matters most: only carbonate in bottles made for your machine.

## How a Sparkling Water Maker Works

Every machine on this list does the same basic thing. A cylinder holds carbon dioxide under high pressure. When you press the button or pull the lever, a valve opens and gas rushes through a thin tube into the cold water.

Cold water holds gas much better than warm water. That is why every maker tells you to chill the water first. Warm water lets the gas escape, and you get weak bubbles and a lot of hissing.

Once the bottle is sealed, the gas stays dissolved under pressure. Open the cap and it starts to escape, slowly at first. A tight cap and a cold fridge keep the bubbles longest.

## Quick Connect vs Screw-In CO2 Cylinders

This is the first thing to check, because each machine takes only one type.

- **Quick Connect (pink top):** Used by newer SodaStream machines like the Terra, Art, E-Duo and Mix. It clicks in without twisting.
- **Screw-in (blue top on SodaStream):** Used by older SodaStreams and by most other brands, including Aarke and Drinkmate. You thread it in by hand.

The two do not swap. A pink cylinder will not fit a screw-in machine, and a blue one will not fit a Quick Connect machine.

### How Refills and Exchanges Work

You do not refill a cylinder yourself. You trade the empty one for a full one. Many supermarkets and hardware stores run an exchange counter, mostly for SodaStream cylinders. SodaStream, Drinkmate and Aarke also run mail exchanges, where you send empties back in a prepaid box.

Read the terms on mail bundles. Some charge a fee if you do not return your empties within a set time, often 90 days.

Never try to refill a cylinder at home, and never tamper with the valve on top. The valve has a safety feature that stops the cylinder from over-pressurizing.

## What Does Homemade Sparkling Water Cost per Liter?

A cylinder exchange is the main running cost. A 60-liter exchange usually costs somewhere around $15 to $25, depending on the brand and the store. That works out to well under 50 cents a liter if you get the full 60 liters.

## Bottles: Plastic, Glass and Dishwasher Rules

**Plastic carbonating bottles** are made from PET, a tough, light plastic. They are built to hold pressure, but they wear out. Every one has an expiry date printed on it. After that date, stop using it for carbonation. You can still use it to store still water.

**Glass carafes** only work in machines designed for glass, like the E-Duo. They have no expiry date. Use them for as long as they have no cracks or chips.

**Dishwasher rules** vary by brand:

| Bottle | Material | Dishwasher | Expires |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SodaStream (Terra, Art, Mix) | Plastic | Yes, lower rack | Yes, date on bottle |
| SodaStream E-Duo carafe | Glass | Yes | No, unless chipped |
| Aarke | Plastic | No, hand wash | Yes |
| Drinkmate | Plastic | No, hand wash | Yes |
| Breville InFizz | Plastic | No, hand wash | Yes |

Hand wash in cool or lukewarm water with a little mild dish soap.

Heat is the enemy of plastic bottles. Hot water and the drying cycle can warp them, and a warped bottle is a weak bottle. The exact temperature limits are on the [SodaStream bottle care page](https://support-us.sodastream.com/hc/en-us/articles/4420074556827-Bottles-compatibility-and-Cleaning-instructions).

If a bottle starts to smell, soak it overnight in warm water with a little baking soda or white vinegar, then rinse well. It is the same gentle approach as [cleaning an electric kettle](/blog/how-to-clean-an-electric-kettle/). A clean dishwasher matters too, since a dirty filter can leave a film on bottles. ## Can You Carbonate Juice, Wine or Other Drinks?

Only in a machine built for it.

Water-only machines, like the Terra, Art, E-Duo, Aarke and Breville, push gas straight into the bottle. Sweet or alcoholic drinks foam up fast and can push sticky liquid back into the machine. Fizzing anything but water in a water-only SodaStream voids the warranty.

Machines like the Mix and OmniFizz solve this with a special cap that lets the pressure out slowly. Even then, a few habits help:

1. Chill the drink as cold as you can without freezing it.
2. Fill the bottle only halfway with foamy drinks.
3. Add gas in short bursts, with a pause between each one.
4. Let it settle before you release the pressure slowly.
5. Strain out pulp first, since it can clog the cap.

On a water-only machine, flavor goes in after the fizz. Add a splash of juice, a squeeze of citrus, or a syrup to the carbonated water, then tip the bottle gently. A good [lemon juicer](/blog/how-to-use-a-lemon-juicer/) makes fresh citrus fizz easy. ## How Much Fizz Control Do You Need?

Fizz control comes in three styles.

- **Button presses (Terra, OmniFizz):** Simple and quick. More presses mean more fizz.
- **Lever (Art, Aarke, Breville):** You feel each burst, so it is easy to stop at the exact level you like.
- **Electric presets (E-Duo):** Pick a level and press once. Every bottle comes out the same.

If one person uses the machine, any style works. If a whole family shares it, electric presets avoid the "who made this so flat" argument.

## Safety Rules Worth Following

Most problems come from the wrong bottle or badly stored gas.

- **Only carbonate in bottles approved for your machine.** Never use a soda bottle, a water bottle or a glass bottle the machine was not made for.
- **Replace plastic bottles at their expiry date,** or sooner if they are scratched, bent or have been near heat.
- **Store cylinders away from heat.** Keep them below 120°F, away from stoves, ovens, radiators, dishwashers and hot cars.
- **Do not store spare cylinders under the sink next to cleaning chemicals.** A cool pantry or cupboard is better. Our guide to [organizing under the kitchen sink](/blog/organize-under-the-kitchen-sink/) shows what belongs there instead.
- **Let a cold cylinder warm up first.** If one has been in a cold car, leave it at room temperature before use.
- **Never put ice or milk in the carbonating bottle.**
