Rice, lentils, eggs, canned beans and a head of cabbage cover most cheap meals for one person. The catch is that the cheapest food is the food you actually finish.

A family pack of chicken looks like a bargain until half of it goes grey in the fridge. So the recipes below share ingredients on purpose. You get eight of them, a seven-day plan and one shopping list that feeds the whole week.

**What to do first:** Stock the staples (rice, oats, pasta, lentils, canned beans, canned tomatoes, eggs, and cabbage or frozen vegetables), then pick recipes that reuse them. Cook twice a week in big batches and split everything into single portions. A small rice cooker, an 8- or 10-inch skillet, a quarter sheet pan and decent containers handle almost all of it, and most meals come in at a few dollars a serving.

## Three Habits That Keep a Solo Kitchen Cheap

For solo cooks, spoiled leftovers cost more than any price difference at the store. These three habits close that gap:

- **Shared ingredients.** One head of cabbage goes into fried rice, noodles and bowls.
- **Batch cooking.** Four portions take about the same time and energy as one.
- **Cheap protein.** Eggs, beans, lentils and chicken thighs give the most protein for the least money.

If your kitchen is not set up for this yet, start with our guide on [how to stock a small kitchen for solo cooking](/blog/how-to-stock-a-small-kitchen-for-solo-cooking/). It covers the basics you need before any recipe.

Most budget meals come from the same short list. Keep these on hand and you can always make dinner without a trip to the store.

| Staple | Why it is cheap | Shelf life | What it turns into |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long-grain rice | Pennies per serving, keeps for months | 1 year or more, sealed | Bowls, fried rice, burritos |
| Rolled oats | Very low cost per bowl | 1 year, sealed | Breakfast, overnight oats |
| Dried lentils | Cheaper than meat, no soaking | 1 year, sealed | Soup, lentil rice |
| Canned beans | Ready protein, no cooking | 2 years or more | Bowls, pasta, tacos |
| Canned diced tomatoes | Sauce base for pennies | 1 to 2 years | Pasta, soup, baked eggs |
| Dry pasta | Filling and quick | 1 to 2 years | Pasta night, cold lunches |

| Cabbage | Lasts much longer than lettuce | 2 weeks or more, fridge | Stir-fry, slaw, soup |
| Onions and garlic | Flavor base for everything | Several weeks, cool and dark | Every savory recipe |
| Frozen mixed vegetables | No waste, already cut | Months at 0°F | Fried rice, soup, bowls |
| Peanut butter and soy sauce | Big flavor, small amounts | Months | Noodles, sauces |

## Eight Cheap Recipes for One Person

These are the eight recipes in our weekly plan. Each one uses ingredients from the shared shopping list below. Most take 30 minutes or less of active cooking.

### 1. Overnight Oats (Breakfast)

Makes 1 serving. Make three or four jars at once.

- 1/2 cup rolled oats
- 1/2 cup milk or water
- 1/4 cup plain yogurt (optional)
- 1 tablespoon peanut butter
- 1/2 banana, sliced
- Pinch of cinnamon

1. Stir the oats, milk, yogurt and cinnamon in a jar or 16-ounce deli container.
2. Top with the peanut butter and banana.
3. Cover and chill overnight. Eat cold, or warm it for 1 minute in the microwave.

The jars keep 4 days in the fridge. Add the banana the morning you eat it.

### 2. Rice, Black Bean and Egg Bowl

Makes 1 serving. This is our go-to cheap dinner and the base for many lunches.

- 1 cup cooked rice
- 1/2 can black beans, rinsed
- 1 egg
- 1 cup shredded cabbage
- 1 teaspoon oil
- Salt, cumin, hot sauce

1. Warm the beans in a small pan with a pinch of cumin and salt and a splash of water.
2. Push the beans aside and add the cabbage. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes until it softens.
3. Fry the egg in the oil in the same pan.
4. Pile the rice, beans and cabbage in a bowl and top with the egg and hot sauce.

Save the other half of the beans in a small container for burritos later in the week.

### 3. Egg Fried Rice

Makes 1 serving. Day-old rice works best because it is drier.

- 1 1/2 cups cold cooked rice
- 2 eggs
- 1 cup frozen mixed vegetables
- 1 green onion or 1/4 onion, chopped
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 tablespoon oil

1. Heat the oil in your skillet over medium-high heat.
2. Add the onion and frozen vegetables. Cook for 3 minutes.
3. Add the rice and break up the clumps. Cook for 3 to 4 minutes until hot.
4. Push the rice aside, crack in the eggs and scramble them. Stir everything together.
5. Add the soy sauce, toss and serve.

Cooked rice should go into the fridge within 2 hours, like any cooked food. Spread it in a shallow container so it cools fast.

### 4. Sheet Pan Chicken Thighs and Potatoes

Makes 2 servings. Eat one now and pack one for lunch.

- 2 bone-in chicken thighs
- 2 medium potatoes, cut into 1-inch chunks
- 2 carrots, cut into 1-inch pieces
- 1/2 onion, cut into wedges
- 1 1/2 tablespoons oil
- Salt, pepper, garlic powder, paprika

1. Heat the oven to 425°F.
2. Toss the potatoes, carrots and onion with 1 tablespoon oil and seasoning on a quarter sheet pan.
3. Rub the chicken with the rest of the oil and seasoning. Set it on top of the vegetables, skin up.
4. Roast for 35 to 45 minutes, until the chicken reaches 165°F inside and the potatoes are brown.

Thighs cost less than breasts and reheat well. Our guide to [how long chicken lasts in the fridge](/blog/how-long-does-chicken-last-in-the-fridge/) covers storage. Keep raw chicken on the bottom shelf in a sealed container so it cannot drip on anything.

### 5. Red Lentil Soup

Makes 4 servings. This is the freezer workhorse.

- 1 cup red lentils, rinsed
- 1 can diced tomatoes
- 1 onion, chopped
- 2 carrots, chopped
- 2 cloves garlic, chopped
- 4 cups water or broth
- 1 tablespoon oil
- 1 teaspoon cumin, salt to taste

1. Cook the onion and carrots in the oil in a saucepan for 5 minutes.
2. Add the garlic and cumin. Cook for 1 minute.
3. Add the lentils, tomatoes and water. Simmer for 20 to 25 minutes, until the lentils fall apart.
4. Salt to taste. Eat one bowl and portion the rest.

In an Instant Pot Mini, cook the onion on Saute, add everything else and pressure cook for 10 minutes. Let the pressure drop on its own. Freeze two portions in Souper Cubes or deli containers.

### 6. Pasta With Tomatoes and White Beans

Makes 2 servings.

- 4 ounces dry pasta
- 1 can diced tomatoes (or the other half of a can)
- 1/2 can white beans or chickpeas, rinsed
- 2 cloves garlic, sliced
- 1 tablespoon oil
- Red pepper flakes, salt, grated cheese

1. Boil the pasta in salted water in your saucepan.
2. While it cooks, warm the oil and garlic in your skillet for 1 minute.
3. Add the tomatoes and beans. Simmer for 8 minutes.
4. Drain the pasta, saving a splash of the water. Toss the pasta into the sauce with the water.
5. Top with cheese and pepper flakes.

### 7. Peanut Noodles With Cabbage

Makes 1 serving. A cheap takeout swap.

- 3 ounces dry spaghetti or ramen noodles (skip the flavor packet)
- 1 1/2 tablespoons peanut butter
- 1 tablespoon soy sauce
- 1 teaspoon vinegar and a pinch of sugar
- 1 cup shredded cabbage
- 1 egg (optional)

1. Cook the noodles. Add the cabbage to the pot for the last minute.
2. Stir the peanut butter, soy sauce, vinegar and sugar with 2 tablespoons hot noodle water.
3. Drain the noodles and cabbage. Toss with the sauce.
4. Top with a fried or boiled egg.

### 8. Bean and Cheese Burritos

Makes 4 burritos. Freeze two for busy nights.

- 4 flour tortillas
- 1/2 can black beans, mashed
- 1 cup cooked rice
- 1/2 cup shredded cheese
- Salt, cumin, hot sauce

1. Mash the beans with cumin and salt.
2. Spread beans on each tortilla. Add rice and cheese.
3. Roll tightly, folding in the ends.
4. Toast seam-side down in your skillet for 2 to 3 minutes per side.

To freeze, wrap each cooled burrito in foil and bag them. Reheat from frozen to 165°F. Our [easy meal prep ideas for one person](/blog/easy-meal-prep-ideas-for-one-person-in-a-small-kitchen/) has more make-ahead recipes like this.

## A Weekly Meal Plan for One Person

Here is how the recipes fit together across seven days. Sunday is the main cooking day. Wednesday is a short top-up session.

| Day | Breakfast | Lunch | Dinner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunday | Eggs and toast | Rice and black bean bowl | Sheet pan chicken and potatoes |
| Monday | Overnight oats | Leftover chicken and potatoes | Red lentil soup |
| Tuesday | Overnight oats | Lentil soup | Egg fried rice |
| Wednesday | Overnight oats | Rice and black bean bowl | Pasta with tomatoes and white beans |
| Thursday | Overnight oats | Leftover pasta | Peanut noodles with cabbage |
| Friday | Eggs and toast | Lentil soup (from the freezer) | Bean and cheese burritos |
| Saturday | Oats or eggs | Burrito (from the freezer or fridge) | Rice bowl with whatever is left |

### Sunday Prep (About 90 Minutes)

1. Start a pot of rice in the rice cooker. Cook 1 1/2 cups dry rice for the week.
2. Make the sheet pan chicken and potatoes.
3. While it roasts, make the lentil soup on the stove or in the Instant Pot Mini.
4. Mix three jars of overnight oats.
5. Cool everything, then portion it: two soups in the fridge, two in the freezer, one chicken lunch, rice in a shallow container.

### Wednesday Top-Up (About 30 Minutes)

Make the pasta and pack the second serving for Thursday lunch. Mix two more jars of oats, and plan Saturday around whatever needs using up.

Cooked leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in a fridge set to 40°F or below. Anything you will not eat by then goes in the freezer on day one or two, not day four. Our guide to [organizing a small fridge](/blog/organize-a-small-fridge/) helps you see every container so nothing gets lost in the back.

## Can You Eat for 50 Dollars a Week as a Single Person?

In many parts of the country, yes, but only if you cook almost every meal. The plan above is built for that kind of budget. Costs depend a lot on where you live and where you shop.

Here is what makes a tight weekly budget work:

- **Cook from staples, not kits.** Rice, oats, lentils and beans cost a fraction of frozen meals.
- **Make meat a side.** One pack of chicken thighs stretches across two or three meals.
- **Buy store brands** of beans, tomatoes, oats and pasta. It is usually the same food for less.
- **Skip drinks and snacks.** Soda, chips and coffee drinks eat a solo budget faster than dinner does.

If you live in a high-cost city, the same plan will cost more. It will still cost far less than eating out.

## What Is the Cheapest Healthy Meal to Cook for One?

Our answer is the rice, black bean and egg bowl. It gives you protein from the beans and egg, fiber from the beans and cabbage, and steady energy from the rice. It costs very little per bowl and takes about 20 minutes.

Red lentil soup is a close second. Lentils are high in protein and fiber, cook without soaking, and freeze well. A bowl of soup with a slice of toast is a full meal.

To make any cheap meal healthier, add a handful of frozen vegetables to everything, use brown rice some nights, and rinse canned beans to wash off some of the salt.

A boiled egg is also one of the cheapest healthy snacks. The [Dash Rapid Egg Cooker](https://www.amazon.com/Dash-Rapid-Egg-Cooker/dp/B00DDXWFY0/?tag=kitchenpot-20) cooks up to six eggs with a small splash of water and shuts off on its own. Make a batch on Sunday for snacks and toppings all week. We compare more models in our [best hard-boiled egg cooker](/blog/best-hard-boiled-egg-cooker/) guide.

## How to Meal Plan on a Budget for One

Meal planning for one comes down to overlap: every ingredient should show up in at least two meals. This is the method behind our plan.

1. **Start with what you have.** Check the fridge, freezer and shelves first. Plan meals that use those items before they spoil.
2. **Pick four or five recipes, not seven.** Repeating meals is what makes cooking for one cheap. You will eat each recipe two or three times.
3. **Make sure they share ingredients.** If one recipe uses half a can of beans, plan another that uses the other half.
4. **Write one list from the recipes.** Group it by store section so you shop fast and skip the aisles you do not need.
5. **Plan one big cook and one small cook.** A main session on your day off and a short top-up midweek keeps food fresh.
6. **Leave one "use it up" night.** Fried rice, a rice bowl or an omelet will take any leftover vegetable.

If your cooking space is tight, plan meals around the burners and pans you have. Our guide on [how to cook a full meal with only two burners](/blog/how-to-cook-a-full-meal-with-only-two-burners/) shows how to time it.

## Cheap Swaps That Save Money Every Week

Small swaps add up fast when you cook for one. These are the ones we would make first.

| Instead of | Buy this | Why it saves |
|---|---|---|
| Boneless chicken breasts | Bone-in chicken thighs | Lower price per pound, more flavor |
| Canned beans every week | Dried beans in a pressure cooker | Much cheaper per serving |
| Pre-cut vegetables | Whole vegetables and a sharp knife | You pay for the cutting |
| Bagged salad | Cabbage or carrots | Lasts weeks, not days |
| Pre-shredded cheese | A block of cheese | Cheaper and keeps longer |
| Instant oatmeal packets | A big tub of rolled oats | Same food, a fraction of the price |

Spices turn cheap staples into meals you want to eat. Cumin, paprika, garlic powder, chili flakes and cinnamon cover every recipe in this guide. See our [spice storage ideas for small kitchens](/blog/spice-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/) to keep them where you can reach them.

## Where Most People Go Wrong on a Solo Food Budget

These habits push a solo food bill up week after week.

- **Buying for a family.** The big pack looks cheaper per pound, but not if half of it spoils. Buy big only for things that keep, like rice, oats and frozen food.
- **Cooking a new recipe every night.** Seven different recipes means seven sets of ingredients. Repeat meals instead.
- **Ignoring the freezer.** Freeze portions on day one or two, while they are fresh. Food frozen on day four is already near its limit.
- **Letting leftovers hide.** If you cannot see it, you will not eat it. Use clear containers and keep them at eye level.
- **Leaving food out to cool for hours.** Get cooked food into the fridge within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F. Spread hot food in shallow containers so it chills fast.

Keep your freezer at 0°F and label every container with the date. A strip of masking tape and a marker is all you need.

## Budget Dinners for One When You Do Not Want to Cook

Plan for lazy nights, or you will order takeout.

- **Freezer burritos.** Reheat one from the freezer in a few minutes.
- **Freezer soup.** Pop a block of lentil soup into a pot or a microwave-safe bowl and heat it to 165°F.
- **Eggs on toast.** Two eggs and bread is a real meal in five minutes.
- **Rice bowl from leftovers.** Rice, any vegetable, an egg and soy sauce.

Keep two or three of these ready at all times. Our [small kitchen ideas on a budget](/blog/small-kitchen-ideas-on-a-budget/) cover more ways to save in a small space.
