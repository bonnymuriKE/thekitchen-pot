## Rolled Sheets or Extruded Shapes: Which Do You Want?

Every pasta maker does one of two things.

**Rollers flatten dough.** Soft egg dough passes between two smooth cylinders that squeeze it thinner. You end up with a long sheet.

**Extruders push dough through a shape.** A screw or plunger forces a stiff dough through a disc with holes in it. The hole decides the shape, just like a cookie press. This is the only way to make hollow tubes and twists at home.

| Fettuccine, tagliolini | Rolled and cut | Atlas+, KitchenAid roller, Philips Viva Compact (fettuccine) |

## Manual, Motor, Mixer Attachment or Automatic?

Next, decide who does the work.

### Manual Hand-Crank Machines

You turn a handle and feed the dough. This is the cheapest way to get very even sheets. The catch is that one hand cranks while the other guides the dough.

### Add-On Motors

Some manual rollers, including the Atlas+, take a clip-on motor. It turns the rollers for you, so you get the same machine with free hands.

### Stand Mixer Attachments

If you own a KitchenAid, its motor can drive a roller or an extruder. There is no clamp, and the attachments store in a drawer. The trade-off is cost, and you need the mixer. A [hand mixer](/blog/best-hand-mixers/) has no attachment hub, so this route only works with a stand mixer.

### Automatic Extruders

You still measure the flour and liquid, but the machine does everything else. It mixes, kneads and extrudes without help. They are the easiest choice for short shapes and the biggest to store.

## Pasta Dough Basics for Each Kind of Machine

Rolled and extruded doughs are different. Using the wrong one is the most common reason a new machine seems to fail.

### Dough for Roller Machines

A common starting point is about 100 grams of flour for each large egg. Fine "00" flour gives silky sheets, and all-purpose flour works too. Mix, knead until smooth, then wrap it and let it rest for about 30 minutes so it relaxes and rolls more easily.

Start at the widest setting. Fold the sheet in thirds and roll it again a few times. This smooths the dough and squares the edges. Then go thinner one notch at a time, with a light dusting of flour or semolina between passes.

Weighing flour is far more reliable than using cups, because a cup of flour can vary a lot. Our guide to [scaling recipes down for one](/blog/scale-down-recipes-for-one/) covers how to cut a one-pound batch in half without guessing.

### Dough for Extruders

Extruder dough is much drier. It should look like coarse, damp sand and only hold together when you squeeze it. If it forms a smooth ball, it is too wet and will gum up the disc. Semolina and water is a common mix for extruded shapes.

## How to Clean a Pasta Maker

Never wash a roller machine in water. Water gets inside, rusts the parts and washes away the oil that keeps the rollers turning.

The safe way to clean any roller, including the ravioli add-on:

1. Let leftover bits of dough dry for about an hour, so they turn hard and brittle.
2. Run the crank and brush the rollers and cutters with a dry pastry brush.
3. Use a wooden skewer for dough stuck between the cutter teeth. Never use a knife.
4. Wipe the outside with a dry or barely damp cloth.
5. Store it in its box or a bag so dust stays off the rollers.

Extruders are different. Most extruder parts can be washed, and the Philips 7000 parts are dishwasher-safe. The easy trick is to let dough in the disc dry first. Dried dough pokes or taps out in pieces, while wet dough smears into every hole.

Wooden tools like the chitarra need a gentle hand wash and a full air dry before you put them away. Treat it the way you would treat a good [wooden cutting board](/blog/best-cutting-board/).

## Clamping a Pasta Machine in a Small Kitchen

Manual roller machines clamp to the edge of a counter or table. That clamp is what stops the machine sliding when you crank. In a small kitchen, finding a good edge is the real puzzle.

The clamp needs a flat lip to grip. Thick stone counters, rounded edges and counters with a drawer right under the top can all cause trouble.

A few things that help:

- **Use the table.** A sturdy dining or [small kitchen table](/blog/small-kitchen-table-ideas/) often clamps better than the counter.
- **Protect the surface.** Fold a cloth between the clamp and the wood to avoid dents.
- **Clear a runway.** You need space in front of the machine for the sheet. ## Drying and Storing Fresh Pasta Safely

Fresh egg pasta is a raw egg food. So the safe rules for eggs apply to it too.

For dinner today, let long pasta hang on a rack until the surface feels dry to the touch. Short shapes can rest on a floured tray. Keep this short rest under two hours, the usual limit for perishable food at room temperature.

For tomorrow, dust the pasta with flour or semolina, coil it into loose nests and keep it covered in the fridge. Cook it within a day or two. For cooked pasta, see [how long leftovers last](/blog/how-long-do-leftovers-last/).

For later, freeze it. Spread nests or short shapes on a tray and freeze them until firm, then move them to a bag or an [airtight container](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/). Cook straight from frozen. Freezing in single servings works well, as we explain in [how to freeze single portions](/blog/how-to-freeze-single-portions/), and a [vacuum sealer](/blog/how-to-use-a-vacuum-sealer/) helps it keep longer.
| Storage | How to prepare it | How long |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Counter | Hang or spread on a floured tray | Under 2 hours |
| Fridge | Floured nests, covered | 1 to 2 days |
| Freezer | Freeze on a tray, then bag | Best within a few months |

When you cook it, use plenty of water. Fresh pasta cooks fast, often in two to four minutes, so test early. A tall pot such as one of our [best stockpots with a lid](/blog/best-stockpot-with-a-lid/) gives the noodles room to move.
