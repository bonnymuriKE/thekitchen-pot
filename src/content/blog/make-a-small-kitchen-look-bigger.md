A cramped kitchen costs more than looks. You cook less, order takeout more and leave groceries on the floor. Make a small kitchen look bigger and most of that goes away.

You cannot move the walls, but you can change what the eye sees. Dark corners, busy counters, heavy cabinets and five competing colors do most of the damage. Below are the 15 tricks we recommend, in the order we would tackle them.

**Short answer:** Clearing the counters changes the room more than any purchase. After that, settle on one light color family and put LED strips under the upper cabinets. Swap fussy hardware, curtains and rugs for slim, plain ones. Last, shrink oversized appliances and set up work zones so the room works as well as it looks.

## How to Make a Small Kitchen Look Bigger: The Three Rules

A room feels big or small based on three things: how much light reaches every surface, how many times your eye has to stop, and how far you can see. Square footage matters less than you think.

Every object on a counter and every color change is a stop for the eye. Every dark corner reads as a wall closing in.

So our rule of thumb is simple: **fewer stops, more light, longer views.** None of the tricks below need a contractor.

## Start With Clutter: The Free Trick That Does the Most

### Trick 1: Clear Everything Off the Counters

This is the biggest change you can make, and it costs nothing. Take every item off the counter. Put back only what you use every single day. For most people that is a coffee maker, a knife block or strip, and maybe a utensil crock. Park the coffee maker on a [Handy Caddy sliding appliance tray](https://www.amazon.com/Handy-Caddy-Sliding-Appliance-Tray/dp/B003LQZKIC/?tag=kitchenpot-20) so it sits tucked back against the wall and slides forward when you brew.

Everything else needs a home. The toaster you use twice a week can live in a lower cabinet. The stand mixer you use once a month belongs on a high shelf or in a closet. Our full method is in [countertop organization ideas for a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/).

Aim for at least one clear stretch of counter that is 24 inches deep and 36 inches wide. It gives you room to cook and shows a long, unbroken surface that makes the room look wider.

### Trick 2: Give Everything a Home Behind a Door

Clutter comes back when things have nowhere to go. Empty one cabinet at a time and sort by use. Daily items go at waist to shoulder height. Monthly items go up high or down low. A [YouCopia UpSpace shelf riser](https://www.amazon.com/YouCopia-UpSpace-Adjustable-Height-Shelf-Riser/dp/B07XVQWCS5/?tag=kitchenpot-20) adds a second level inside a cabinet, so plates and bowls stop spilling onto the counter.

No pantry? See [how to organize a small kitchen with no pantry](/blog/how-to-organize-a-small-kitchen-with-no-pantry/). For cabinets, start with these [small kitchen cabinet organization ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-cabinet-organization-ideas/), then tackle the junk drawer with [how to organize kitchen drawers in a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-organize-kitchen-drawers-in-a-small-kitchen/).

Pots and pans are the bulkiest problem. Nest them and file the lids upright, as shown in [how to store pots and pans in a small kitchen](/blog/store-pots-and-pans-in-a-small-kitchen/) and [how to organize pot lids](/blog/how-to-organize-pot-lids-in-a-small-kitchen/).

## What Color Makes a Small Kitchen Look Bigger?

Light colors make a small kitchen look bigger. They reflect more light, so the room looks brighter and the walls seem to sit farther back. Dark colors absorb light and pull walls closer.

### Trick 3: Choose a Light Color With a High LRV

Paint chips list a number called LRV, short for light reflectance value. It runs from 0 (pure black) to 100 (pure white) and shows how much light a color bounces back.

For a small kitchen, we recommend an LRV of 70 or higher on the walls. Warm whites, soft greige (a gray-beige mix), pale gray and very light sage all sit in that range.

A north-facing kitchen gets cool, bluish daylight, so pick a warm white there. A south-facing kitchen can take a cooler white.

| Color family | Effect in a small kitchen | Best with |
|---|---|---|
| Warm white | Brightest feel, soft and welcoming | North-facing rooms, wood counters |
| Pale gray or greige | Clean and calm, hides scuffs better than white | White or gray counters, stainless appliances |
| Very light sage or blue | Fresh, recedes visually, adds color without weight | White cabinets, brass or black hardware |
| Mid-tone colors (LRV 40 to 60) | Cozy but makes walls feel closer | Large windows only |
| Dark navy, green or charcoal | Dramatic, shrinks the room unless light is strong | Lower cabinets only, paired with light uppers |

### Trick 4: Use One Color From Counter to Ceiling

Color changes are stops for the eye. The fewer you have, the bigger the room feels. Paint the walls the same color as the cabinets, or a shade or two lighter. Paint the ceiling white or the wall color lightened by half.

If you can paint cabinets, this is the most dramatic change on the list. If you rent, match the wall paint to the existing cabinet color instead. A white kitchen with white walls reads as one big bright box.

Sheen means how shiny the paint is. Use satin or eggshell on walls, since it reflects light and wipes clean. Flat paint marks easily. Semi-gloss suits trim and cabinet doors.

## Do Dark Cabinets Make a Small Kitchen Look Smaller?

Dark cabinets on every wall usually do make a small kitchen look smaller, especially with one small window or none. That does not mean you have to skip dark colors.

### Trick 5: Go Two-Tone, Dark Below and Light Above

Put the dark color on the lower cabinets and keep the upper cabinets and walls light. Your eye reads the bottom of the room as solid and grounded, and the top as open and bright. The room feels taller.

Dark lowers also hide kicks, splashes and fingerprints better than white.

Skip dark uppers in a small kitchen. Upper cabinets sit at eye level, so dark ones feel like they lean over you. If you love a dark color and the kitchen has strong daylight, use it on an island or a single run of lower cabinets.

## Layer Your Lighting So No Corner Hides in Shadow

Lighting is the most underrated fix on this list. A single ceiling fixture lights the middle of the room and leaves the counters and corners in shadow.

### Trick 6: Add Under-Cabinet Lights

Under-cabinet lights wash the counter and backsplash with light. That makes the back wall look farther away and lights the place where you actually work. Plug-in or battery LED strips and puck lights install in under an hour, with no wiring and no landlord approval.

For most kitchens we recommend the [Enbrighten 22-inch plug-in linkable LED light](https://www.amazon.com/Enbrighten-22-Inch-Linkable-Plug-In-Under-Cabinet-Light/dp/B01MQXV843/?tag=kitchenpot-20). It is 3000K, and several bars link so one outlet runs the whole cabinet run. With no free outlet, the [MCGOR 10-inch rechargeable motion sensor lights](https://www.amazon.com/MCGOR-10-Inch-Rechargeable-Motion-Sensor-Under-Cabinet-Light/dp/B0BDF8CVBN/?tag=kitchenpot-20) stick on with a magnetic plate and turn on when you walk in. Pick the warm white option so they match your other bulbs. Owners who can hardwire should step up to the [WAC Lighting 30-inch 3-CCT light bar](https://www.amazon.com/WAC-Lighting-30-Inch-3-CCT-LED-Light-Bar/dp/B087CD4PXX/?tag=kitchenpot-20), which has a 90 CRI rating and lets you pick 2700K or 3000K to match your ceiling.

Mount strips toward the front edge of the cabinet bottom, not against the wall. Front mounting spreads light across the whole counter and keeps the glare off shiny backsplashes.

### Trick 7: Match Your Bulbs

Mixed bulb colors make a kitchen look patchy and smaller. Use the same color temperature everywhere. Color temperature is measured in kelvins (K). Lower numbers look warm and yellow. Higher numbers look cool and blue.

| Light layer | What it does | Our pick |
|---|---|---|
| Ceiling (ambient) | Fills the whole room | 2700K to 3000K, flush or semi-flush fixture |
| Under-cabinet (task) | Lights the counter, pushes the back wall away | 3000K LED strips, front-mounted |
| Above sink or window | Brightens the darkest wet zone | Small pendant or flush light, same K as ceiling |
| Inside open shelves or glass cabinets | Adds depth at eye level | Battery puck lights, same K |
| All bulbs | Show true food and paint colors | CRI 90 or higher on the box |

CRI stands for color rendering index. It shows how true colors look under the bulb. A CRI of 90 or higher keeps your paint from looking muddy and your food from looking gray. For ceiling fixtures and lamps, the [Philips Ultra Definition 2700K A19 bulbs](https://www.amazon.com/Philips-Ultra-Definition-LED-A19-Bulbs-2700K-8-Pack/dp/B0B927WMTY/?tag=kitchenpot-20) hit both targets: soft white, 90+ CRI, 800 lumens and dimmable.

Swap a heavy, low-hanging ceiling fixture for a flush one so the ceiling feels higher. LED bulbs also cut your bill, as we explain in [how to reduce kitchen energy use in a small apartment](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/).

## Use Reflective Surfaces to Bounce Light Around

### Trick 8: Pick a Backsplash With Some Shine

Glossy tile, glass tile and polished stone reflect light back into the room. If you can choose, go with a light glossy tile and a grout color close to the tile color. Contrasting grout creates a busy grid of lines.

If you rent, peel-and-stick tile can cover an ugly backsplash in an afternoon. We like [Smart Tiles soft white subway](https://www.amazon.com/Smart-Tiles-Peel-Stick-Backsplash-Subway/dp/B07XCHLLGP/?tag=kitchenpot-20) because the glossy, raised surface catches light like real glazed tile. Keep it out of the area directly behind the burners unless the package says it is rated for heat.

A mirror or back-painted glass panel can work at the end of a galley or on the wall facing a window. It doubles the light and the view. For a small patch, [Lovdeco peel-and-stick beveled mirror subway tiles](https://www.amazon.com/Lovdeco-Peel-Stick-Beveled-Mirror-Subway-Tile/dp/B09VP9NPVR/?tag=kitchenpot-20) are real mirror glass in 3 by 6-inch pieces. They are harder to remove than vinyl, so ask before using them in a rental. Keep mirrors away from the stove, where grease and steam will cloud them within days.

Glossy cabinet fronts, light counters and a bright sink add reflection too. If you are replacing a sink, see the [best white undermount kitchen sink](/blog/best-white-undermount-kitchen-sink/).

## Does Open Shelving Make a Kitchen Look Bigger?

Yes, open shelving makes a kitchen look bigger when you keep it sparse. Removing a bank of upper cabinets opens the wall and lets light reach the corners. But shelves crammed with mismatched items look like clutter, and clutter shrinks a room.

### Trick 9: Replace One Run of Uppers With Shelves, or Take the Doors Off

Replace one short run, usually the one in the corner or beside the window, with two floating shelves. Keep the rest closed for the messy stuff.

Renters can get the same effect by removing a pair of cabinet doors. Bag the hinges and screws, label them, and store the doors until you move out.

Follow these rules for open shelves:

1. Store only items you use every week, so dust never settles on them.
2. Stick to one or two colors, such as white plates and clear glasses.
3. Leave about a third of each shelf empty.
4. Keep shelves away from the stove, where grease builds up fast.
5. Put pantry food in matching clear jars or move it to a cabinet. Our [best airtight food storage containers](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/) guide helps here.

Glass-front cabinet doors are a middle ground. They show depth like open shelves but keep dust and grease off. Add a battery puck light inside and the cabinet glows at eye level. [LEASTYLE remote-control puck lights](https://www.amazon.com/LEASTYLE-Puck-Lights-with-Remote-Wireless/dp/B07K84PSPX/?tag=kitchenpot-20) stick on with adhesive, dim and run on a timer, so you never open the door to switch them on.

## Draw the Eye Up to Use Every Inch of Height

Small kitchens are short on floor space, not height.

### Trick 10: Take Storage Vertical

Using the wall frees the counter, and a clear counter makes the room look bigger. A [magnetic knife strip](/blog/best-magnetic-knife-strip/) replaces a bulky knife block. A rail with S-hooks holds spatulas and ladles. The [Fasthomegoods stainless wall rail with 10 S-hooks](https://www.amazon.com/Fasthomegoods-Stainless-Steel-Kitchen-Wall-Rail-S-Hooks/dp/B00X1N67QO/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is the one we would hang on the backsplash wall. It screws into the wall, so patch the holes when you move out. A slim shelf above the sink holds dish soap and a plant.

Use one material and finish for all of it, such as matte black or brushed steel. Our [vertical storage ideas for small kitchens](/blog/vertical-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/) walk through every option, from pegboards to over-door racks.

### Trick 11: Lead the Eye to the Ceiling

Hang window shades at the top of the window frame, or even a few inches above it. The window looks taller and so does the wall. Paint the ceiling white or a very light tint of the wall color, never a darker shade.

If your upper cabinets stop short of the ceiling, the gap is a choice. Either leave it empty and paint it the wall color so it disappears, or use matching baskets for rarely used items. Skip rows of decorative bottles and cookbooks.

## Pick Hardware and Fixtures That Disappear

### Trick 12: Slim, Consistent Hardware

Chunky knobs and mixed finishes add dozens of stops for the eye. Swap them for slim bar pulls in one finish. If your cabinets have the common 5-inch (128 mm) hole spacing, the [Ravinte matte black slim pulls](https://www.amazon.com/Ravinte-Matte-Black-Cabinet-Pulls-5-Inch/dp/B09D83G753/?tag=kitchenpot-20) come 25 to a pack, enough for most small kitchens. A good rule is to size pulls at about one third of the drawer width. On doors, a 4 to 6-inch pull works for most standard cabinets.

Match the hardware to the cabinet color for the calmest look. White pulls on white cabinets almost vanish. If you want contrast, pick one finish, such as matte black or brass, and use it on every pull, the faucet and the light fixture.

Swapping hardware is renter-friendly if you keep the originals. Measure the distance between the screw holes on your current pulls (the center-to-center measurement) and buy new ones with the same spacing, so you drill no new holes.

An [over-the-sink cutting board](/blog/best-over-the-sink-cutting-board/) adds prep space without adding a thing to the counter.

## Open Up the Window and the Floor

### Trick 13: Strip the Window Down

The window is your biggest light source, and heavy curtains block it. Take down valances, swags and thick curtains. Use a simple roller shade or a light linen café curtain (a short curtain that covers only the bottom half of the window) instead. Hang the café curtain on a [Kenney Twist & Fit tension rod](https://www.amazon.com/Kenney-Twist-Fit-Tension-Curtain-Rod-48-84/dp/B08CSLGHY1/?tag=kitchenpot-20), which fits inside the window frame from 48 to 84 inches with no drilling.

Clear the windowsill too. One small herb pot is plenty.

### Trick 14: Make the Floor Read as One Surface

Treat the floor as one calm, continuous surface.

- If you can choose flooring, run planks along the longest wall. It makes the room look longer.
- Match the kitchen floor to the next room where possible. One floor flowing through makes both rooms feel larger.
- Skip small, busy rugs. Use one long, low-pile runner in a light, plain color, or no rug at all. The [PURRUGS washable 2 by 6-foot runner](https://www.amazon.com/PURRUGS-Machine-Washable-Kitchen-Runner-Rug/dp/B0CJBKVW8D/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has a low pile that clears cabinet doors and goes in the washing machine.

Then raise things off the floor. Furniture on legs and cabinets with a visible toe kick (the recessed strip at the base of lower cabinets) show more floor. The more floor you see, the bigger the room looks.

## How Do You Make a Small Kitchen Feel More Open?

You make a small kitchen feel more open by protecting the sightlines. A sightline is the view your eye follows from the doorway. If it runs straight to a window or far wall without stopping, the room feels open.

### Trick 15: Clear the Sightline From the Doorway

Stand in the doorway and look. Whatever your eye hits first needs fixing first. Often it is a fridge covered in magnets, a tall trash can or an overflowing dish rack.

- **Clear the fridge door.** Keep two or three items on it, or none.
- **Hide the trash.** Put it in a lower cabinet, or use a slim can beside the fridge. The [simplehuman 40-liter slim step can](https://www.amazon.com/simplehuman-40L-Slim-Step-Trash-Can/dp/B004L9L42O/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is about 10 inches wide and has a quiet foot pedal.
- **Fold the dish rack away.** The [Surpahs roll-up dish drying rack](https://www.amazon.com/Surpahs-Over-Sink-Roll-Up-Dish-Drying-Rack/dp/B00P8KHRIU/?tag=kitchenpot-20) rolls up into a drawer when the dishes are dry.
- **Use see-through furniture.** Clear acrylic stools and a small glass or round table let light pass through. Backless stools work too: the [MAISON ARTS 24-inch backless counter stools](https://www.amazon.com/MAISON-ARTS-24-Inch-Backless-Counter-Stools/dp/B08DN8M5W4/?tag=kitchenpot-20) slide fully under a 36-inch counter overhang. A fold-down wall table disappears when you are done eating.
- **Put the tallest things at the edges.** A tall pantry cabinet or fridge belongs at the end of a run, not in the middle of the view.

Bare counters also leave bugs nothing to find. See [how to keep pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/).

## How Can I Make a Small Kitchen More Functional?

A kitchen that works smoothly also looks bigger, because nothing piles up. Set up zones so each task happens in one place, with its tools within arm's reach.

Here are the zones we set up in every small kitchen:

1. **Prep zone:** Your clear counter stretch, with knives, cutting boards and mixing bowls in the drawer or cabinet right below.
2. **Cooking zone:** Beside the stove, with oils, salt, spatulas and the pans you use most.
3. **Cleanup zone:** Around the sink, with soap, towels and the trash can.
4. **Serving zone:** Plates and glasses in the upper cabinet closest to the dishwasher or dish rack.

Then check the room against these measurements.

| Measurement | Standard or target | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Counter depth | 24 inches | Standard; a clear 24 x 36-inch area fits most prep |
| Upper cabinet depth | 12 inches | Holds dinner plates, keeps the counter usable |
| Counter to upper cabinets | About 18 inches | Room for small appliances and lighting |
| Walkway for one cook | 42 inches | Lets you open the oven and dishwasher and still pass |
| Walkway for two cooks | 48 inches | Room to pass behind each other |
| Landing space beside fridge | At least 15 inches | A spot to set things down when the door is open |

An aisle under 42 inches is common in older apartments. Keep it completely clear of carts and bins.

Cook in a way that suits the space. Plan [a full meal on only two burners](/blog/how-to-cook-a-full-meal-with-only-two-burners/), and batch cook with our [easy meal prep ideas for one person](/blog/easy-meal-prep-ideas-for-one-person-in-a-small-kitchen/).

## Very Small Kitchen Ideas for Studios and Galleys

**Studio kitchens** sit in full view of the living area, so make them blend in. Match the wall color to the rest of the room and keep the counter almost bare. A rolling cart can hold small appliances and roll into a closet. The [Honey-Can-Do 3-tier metal rolling cart](https://www.amazon.com/Honey-Can-Do-3-Tier-Metal-Rolling-Cart/dp/B077STDKJM/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has deep tiers that hold a mixer, a blender and bags of flour. Let appliances cool before you roll them away.

**Galley kitchens** are two parallel runs with a narrow aisle. Keep one side emptier than the other so the aisle does not feel like a tunnel. A light or mirror at the far end pulls the eye down the room.

**Kitchens with no window** need extra light. Layer under-cabinet strips, a bright ceiling fixture and light inside any glass cabinets. Skip dark colors entirely.

**Kitchens with a mini fridge** need the fridge kept tidy. Keep it frost-free with [how to defrost a mini fridge](/blog/how-to-defrost-a-mini-fridge/). ## A Weekend Before-and-After Plan

Most of these tricks fit in one weekend. Take a photo from the doorway first, then work in this order.

**Saturday morning: clear and sort**

1. Take everything off the counters, the fridge door and the windowsill.
2. Empty one cabinet at a time. Donate duplicates and anything you have not used in a year.
3. Return only daily-use items to the counter.

**Saturday afternoon: light**

4. Install under-cabinet LED strips, mounted toward the front edge.
5. Replace every bulb with 2700K to 3000K, CRI 90 or higher.
6. Take down heavy curtains and hang a simple shade at the top of the window frame.

**Sunday: surfaces and finishes**

7. Paint the walls a light color with an LRV of 70 or higher, in satin or eggshell.
8. Swap the cabinet hardware for slim pulls in one finish.
9. Mount a magnetic knife strip and a utensil rail.
10. Replace small rugs with one plain runner, or remove them.

Take a second photo from the same spot, then live with it for a week before buying anything else.

|---|---|---|---|
| Clear counters and sort cabinets | Yes | 2 to 4 hours | Free |
| Under-cabinet LED lights | Yes (plug-in or battery) | 1 hour | Low |
| Matching bulbs | Yes | 30 minutes | Low |
| Paint walls | Usually, with permission | 1 day | Low |
| New hardware | Yes, keep the originals | 1 to 2 hours | Low to medium |
| Remove cabinet doors | Yes, store and replace | 1 hour | Free |
| Peel-and-stick backsplash | Yes | Half a day | Medium |
| Paint cabinets | Owners only | 2 to 3 days | Medium |
| Counter-depth or 24-inch appliances | Owners only | Varies | High |

## Habits to Drop If You Want a Bigger-Looking Kitchen

Each of these quietly undoes the work above.

- **Buying organizers before decluttering.** More bins just hold more stuff. Sort first, then buy only what fills a real gap.
- **Filling every shelf.** Open shelves packed edge to edge look worse than closed cabinets. Leave space empty on purpose.
- **Too many colors and finishes.** Pick one wood, one metal and one main color.
- **Dark paint in a dark room.** A moody color can look great in a sunny kitchen. In a north-facing galley with one window, it shrinks the space.
- **Countertop appliance creep.** An air fryer, toaster, blender and coffee maker all on the counter eat half your prep space. Keep one or two out, and store the rest.
- **Ignoring the floor.** Bins, bags and a pet bowl in the walkway make the room feel half its size. Keep the floor clear from wall to wall.
