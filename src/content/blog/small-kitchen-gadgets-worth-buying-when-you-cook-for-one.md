Kitchen gadgets have a way of multiplying. You buy one to solve a specific problem, and before long your drawers are full of single-purpose tools you used once and never touched again. In a small kitchen, that clutter is a bigger problem than it sounds, since every gadget you keep is space you can't use for something else.

Cooking for one actually makes this easier to fix, not harder. You don't need tools built for prepping large batches or feeding a family, which rules out a lot of the bulkier gadgets on store shelves. What's left is a shorter, more useful list of tools that genuinely make solo cooking faster and easier.

Here's what's actually worth buying, what to skip, and how to keep your gadget drawer from taking over your small kitchen.

It's worth separating "gadget" from "essential tool" before diving in. A chef's knife, a cutting board, and a basic set of measuring cups aren't gadgets, they're foundational tools everyone needs regardless of household size. Gadgets are the extras layered on top, the tools that make a specific task faster or easier but that you could technically live without. The list below focuses on gadgets that earn their keep for someone cooking solo, not the baseline tools you'd need either way.

## The Test for Whether a Gadget Is Worth It

Before adding any gadget to your kitchen, ask two questions: does it do something a basic tool you already own can't do reasonably well, and will you use it regularly, not just for one recipe? If the answer to either is no, skip it. This simple filter eliminates most of the impulse-buy gadgets that end up forgotten in a drawer.

A third question worth adding, especially for a small kitchen: where exactly will this live? If you can't picture the specific drawer, hook, or shelf a gadget will occupy before you buy it, that's often a sign it's going to end up as clutter on the counter or crammed into an already-full drawer. Gadgets that pass all three questions, useful, regularly used, and with a clear home, are the ones actually worth the money.

## 1. Kitchen Shears

A good pair of kitchen shears is one of the most underrated tools in any kitchen, and especially useful for solo cooking. They cut through packaging, trim herbs directly into a pan, break down a small piece of chicken, and even cut pizza in a pinch. Because they store flat, they take up almost no space compared to a cutting board and knife for quick tasks.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a sturdy pair of kitchen shears with a comfortable grip, usually $10 to $20. Look for ones that come apart for easier cleaning.

## 2. Garlic Press

If garlic is a regular part of your cooking, a garlic press saves real time over mincing by hand, especially for the small quantities a single-serving recipe usually calls for. It's a genuinely single-purpose tool, but it's compact enough that the space trade-off is worth it if you use garlic often.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a stainless steel garlic press with a self-cleaning mechanism, typically $10 to $20.

## 3. Compact Digital Kitchen Scale

A small digital scale is especially useful for solo cooking because it lets you portion exactly what you need, whether that's a single serving of pasta, rice, or a piece of meat. This helps avoid the common problem of cooking too much or too little for one person, and it's genuinely more accurate than eyeballing portions or relying on measuring cups for things like flour or rice.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a slim digital scale that folds flat or stores upright, usually $15 to $25.

## 4. Folding or Collapsible Colander

A standard colander is bulky and awkward to store, especially in a small kitchen with limited cabinet space. A collapsible silicone version folds down to a fraction of its usable size, which matters more than it sounds like it should when every inch of storage counts.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a collapsible silicone colander, usually $10 to $20. Check that it has a stable base so it doesn't tip over mid-use.

## 5. Multi-Function Peeler

Rather than a drawer full of single-purpose peelers, a multi-function version that handles regular peeling, julienning, and sometimes a zesting edge covers several jobs in one compact tool. For someone cooking for one, this kind of consolidation matters more than owning the "best" dedicated version of each individual tool.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a multi-function vegetable peeler, typically $8 to $15.

## 6. Silicone Stretch Lids

Storing small amounts of leftovers is a constant task when you're cooking for one, and mismatched container lids are a common source of drawer clutter. Silicone stretch lids fit over bowls, cups, and cans of any shape, cutting down on the number of dedicated containers you need to keep on hand.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a set of silicone stretch lids in a few sizes, usually $12 to $20 for a set.

## 7. A Sharp Paring Knife

While not exactly a "gadget," a good paring knife deserves a mention because it handles the bulk of single-serving prep work, like trimming, peeling, and cutting small fruits and vegetables, more precisely than a large chef's knife. It's a small, low-storage-footprint tool that gets daily use.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a basic paring knife, usually $10 to $25. You don't need a full knife block for solo cooking, a chef's knife and a paring knife cover almost everything.

## 8. Immersion Blender

Already covered as a small appliance in its own right, the immersion blender also functions like a gadget in terms of storage: it stores flat in a drawer rather than taking up counter or shelf space like a pitcher blender. For solo cooking, it blends soups directly in the pot and handles small batches of sauces or dressings without extra dishes.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a basic corded or cordless immersion blender, usually $20 to $40.

## 9. Herb Scissors or a Small Mezzaluna

If you cook with fresh herbs regularly, a multi-blade herb scissor or a small mezzaluna (a curved rocking blade) mince herbs faster than a knife, without the cutting board getting covered in scattered bits. For someone cooking single portions, where a recipe might only call for a tablespoon of chopped herbs, this small time-saver adds up.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a multi-blade herb scissor with a cleaning comb, usually $10 to $15.

## 10. Magnetic Knife Strip

For a small kitchen, a magnetic strip mounted on the wall or inside a cabinet door frees up an entire drawer that would otherwise hold a knife block, while also keeping your most-used knife (or two) in easy reach instead of buried under other utensils. It's a small change, but in a kitchen where every drawer counts, reclaiming one is a meaningful win.

**Renter-friendly:** check with your landlord before drilling, since this one does typically require a couple of small screws. A removable adhesive-mount version is a good workaround if drilling isn't an option, though it generally holds less weight, so it's better suited to one lighter knife than a full set.

**What to get:** a basic magnetic knife strip, usually $10 to $20, plus the adhesive-mount version if you need to avoid drilling, typically $15 to $25.

## 11. Bench Scraper

A bench scraper looks like a simple flat piece of metal or plastic, but it earns its spot by doing several small jobs well: transferring chopped vegetables from a cutting board to a pan without losing half of them, scraping down a counter after kneading dough, and portioning things like dough or ground meat cleanly. For someone with limited counter space, it also works as an impromptu dustpan for crumbs and scraps, cutting down on a separate cleaning tool.

**Renter-friendly:** yes.

**What to get:** a basic stainless steel bench scraper, usually $8 to $15.

## Gadgets That Aren't Worth the Space

Not every popular gadget earns a spot in a small, solo kitchen. A few to think twice about:

* **Avocado slicers.** A knife and a spoon do the same job just as fast, with one less single-purpose tool to store.
* **Egg separators.** Cracking an egg between your fingers or using the shell itself works fine for the occasional recipe that needs it.
* **Banana slicers, strawberry hullers, and similarly narrow single-use tools.** These solve a problem a basic knife already solves, just slightly slower.
* **Large salad spinners.** A collapsible or compact version, or simply drying greens with a clean towel, works nearly as well without the bulky storage footprint.
* **Specialty appliances for rarely-made dishes**, like a dedicated waffle iron if you make waffles twice a year. Rent, borrow, or make do with a pan instead.
* **Electric can openers.** A manual can opener is smaller, cheaper, doesn't need counter space or a plug, and opens a can just as fast once you're used to it.
* **Onion goggles and other one-trick novelty items.** These solve a minor annoyance at the cost of a drawer slot that a more versatile tool could use instead.

## How to Store Your Gadgets Without Clutter

Even a well-curated gadget collection can create clutter if it's not organized. A few approaches that work well in a small kitchen:

* **Use one drawer organizer** for your most-used small tools, keeping everything visible at a glance instead of digging through a jumbled drawer.
* **Hang what you can.** A small hook rail near your prep area can hold shears, a peeler, or measuring spoons within easy reach without using drawer space at all.
* **Keep a "trial" spot.** Before committing a new gadget to permanent storage, use it for a few weeks from an easily accessible spot. If it's not getting regular use, it's a candidate to donate or return.
* **Do a seasonal check-in.** Every few months, take everything out of your gadget drawer and ask whether each item has actually been used recently. It's an easy way to catch clutter before it builds up unnoticed.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

* **Buying gadgets for a single recipe.** If you don't expect to make that dish again soon, borrow the tool or find a workaround instead of buying one.
* **Duplicating functions across multiple gadgets.** A multi-function peeler and a dedicated julienne peeler do the same job. Pick one.
* **Ignoring how a gadget stores.** A tool that only fits in one bulky, awkward shape is a worse fit for a small kitchen than a slightly less "ideal" version that folds flat or stacks easily.
* **Keeping gadgets "just in case."** If you haven't used it in the last few months, it's very unlikely you'll need it in the next few either.

## The Bottom Line

A handful of well-chosen gadgets can genuinely make cooking for one faster and easier, but most gadget-aisle purchases end up as clutter in a small kitchen. Stick to tools that solve a real, recurring problem, store compactly, and ideally cover more than one function. That short list will do more for your daily cooking than a drawer full of single-use tools ever will.
