Kitchen gadgets multiply. You buy one for a recipe, then another, and soon a whole drawer holds tools you used once.

In a small kitchen, every gadget takes space from something else. Here are the eight that earn their place when you cook for one, and the ones to skip.

Then add [Henckels take-apart shears](https://www.amazon.com/Henckels-Take-Apart-Kitchen-Shears/dp/B000VXG7LW/?tag=kitchenpot-20) and a [Microplane zester](https://www.amazon.com/Microplane-Premium-Classic-Zester-Grater-Black/dp/B00151WA06/?tag=kitchenpot-20). Buy the rest only if you will use them weekly.

## The Three-Question Test for Any Gadget

Before buying any gadget, ask:

1. **Does it do something my knife, pan or hands cannot do well?**
2. **Will I use it every week, not just for one recipe?**
3. **Do I know exactly which drawer, hook or shelf it will live on?**

If any answer is no, skip it. Every pick below passes all three for most solo cooks.

## Gadgets to Skip in a Small Kitchen

These gadgets fail the three-question test for most solo cooks:

- **Avocado slicers and tools.** A knife and a spoon work as well.
- **Strawberry hullers and banana slicers.** A paring knife does the job.
- **Egg separators.** Your hands or the shell work fine.
- **Garlic peelers.** Crush the clove with the flat of your knife.
- **Herb strippers.** Run your fingers down the stem.
- **Quesadilla and sandwich makers.** A skillet does both.
- **Full-size food processors.** Too big for single portions.
- **Bread makers.** Bulky, single-use and slow to pay off for one.
- **Spiralizers.** Fun at first, then rarely used.

If you already own some of these, try a one-month rule. Anything you have not used in a month can probably go.

## Multi-Use Tools Beat Single-Use Gadgets

The best gadgets replace several others:

- **Shears** replace a pizza cutter, herb scissors and packaging knife.
- **A rasp zester** replaces a garlic press, nutmeg grater and small cheese grater.
- **An immersion blender** replaces a blender, hand mixer and whisk for small jobs.
- **A scale** replaces most measuring cups for baking and portioning.

Before buying a new gadget, check whether something you own already does the job.

Gadgets are hand tools and small plug-in helpers. Bigger appliances, like air fryers, toaster ovens and rice cookers, are covered in our guide to the [best small kitchen appliances for cooking for one](/blog/best-small-kitchen-appliances-for-cooking-for-one/).

The hardest parts of cooking for one are portion sizes, leftovers and motivation. The right gadgets help with all three.

## Why Cheap Gadgets Often Cost More

It is tempting to buy the cheapest version of every gadget. For tools you use daily, that usually backfires.

Cheap thermometers read slowly or drift out of accuracy, which means overcooked meat. Thin, cheap shears loosen at the pivot and stop cutting cleanly. Budget scales often have displays hidden under the bowl.

The picks above are not the most expensive in their categories. They are the ones that hold up to daily use, based on the reviews cited. Spending a little more once beats replacing a tool every year.

That said, do not overspend on gadgets you will use only sometimes. A $30 mini chopper is plenty for weekly use.

## Caring for Your Gadgets

- **Dry the scale's platform** after washing, and keep liquids away from the base.
- **Replace the thermometer battery** when the display dims.
- **Separate the shear blades** to clean the pivot after raw meat.
- **Rinse the zester right away** before food dries in the teeth.
- **Wash the stick blender shaft immediately** in a cup of soapy water. Never submerge the motor.
- **Descale the egg cooker** now and then with a little vinegar and water.
- **Turn Stasher bags inside out** to dry fully before storing.

Good care keeps these tools working for years, so you buy fewer replacements.
