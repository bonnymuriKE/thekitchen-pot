Kitchen gadgets multiply. You buy one for a recipe, then another, and soon a whole drawer holds tools you used once.

In a small kitchen, every gadget takes space from something you actually need. These are the eight we think earn their place when you cook for one, and the ones we would leave on the shelf.

**The short version:** Start with a [kitchen scale](https://www.amazon.com/OXO-Good-Grips-Stainless-Steel-Food-Scale-Pull-Out-Display/dp/B079D9B82W/?tag=kitchenpot-20) and an [instant-read thermometer](https://www.amazon.com/ThermoWorks-ThermoPop-2-Instant-Read-Thermometer/dp/B0DC8FD41B/?tag=kitchenpot-20). Together they fix the two hardest parts of solo cooking: portion sizes and not overcooking your only chicken breast. Then add [take-apart kitchen shears](https://www.amazon.com/Henckels-Take-Apart-Kitchen-Shears/dp/B000VXG7LW/?tag=kitchenpot-20) and a [Microplane zester](https://www.amazon.com/Microplane-Premium-Classic-Zester-Grater-Black/dp/B00151WA06/?tag=kitchenpot-20). Buy the rest only if you will use them every week.

## The Three-Question Test for Any Gadget

Before buying any gadget, ask:

1. **Does it do something my knife, pan or hands cannot do well?**
2. **Will I use it every week, not just for one recipe?**
3. **Do I know exactly which drawer, hook or shelf it will live on?**

If any answer is no, skip it. Every pick below passes all three for most solo cooks.

If we could recommend only one gadget to a solo cook, it would be a scale. The hardest part of cooking for one is portions, and a scale takes the guessing out.

Two ounces of dry pasta is one serving. A third of a cup of rice is one serving. Eyeball those and you either go hungry or throw food away. Weigh them and you get it right every time, which also makes your groceries last as long as you planned.

A scale is also how you shrink recipes. Most recipes serve four. Halving "one cup of flour" in cups is messy; halving 120 grams is easy.

What makes the OXO stand out is the display. It slides out on a short arm, so a big mixing bowl or dinner plate never hides the numbers. The steel platform lifts off for washing, which matters when you weigh raw chicken. It is also America's Test Kitchen's top-rated kitchen scale, and the pull-out display is the reason.

If $65 feels like a lot, OXO makes a 5-pound version for less. For single portions, 5 pounds is plenty.

**What to know before you buy:**

**Who should buy it:** Every solo cook, and anyone who bakes.

## Which Gadgets Fit Your Cooking Style?

Not every solo cook needs all eight. Match the gadgets to how you actually cook.

**If you meal prep on weekends:** The scale, thermometer and Stasher bags matter most. Weigh portions, cook them to the right temperature and freeze them flat. Our guide on [how long chicken lasts in the fridge](/blog/how-long-does-chicken-last-in-the-fridge/) helps you decide what to freeze. For dairy, see [can you freeze cream cheese](/blog/can-you-freeze-cream-cheese/).

**If you make smoothies and soups:** The stick blender covers both.

**If you cook a lot of fresh produce:** The zester, mini chopper and shears save the most time. For citrus, see [how to use a lemon juicer](/blog/how-to-use-a-lemon-juicer/) and [how much juice is in one lemon](/blog/how-much-juice-is-in-one-lemon/).

**If you mostly cook meat and fish:** The thermometer comes first. For grilling or smoking, a leave-in probe is also handy; see the [best meat thermometer for smoking](/blog/best-meat-thermometer-for-smoking/).

**If you eat a lot of eggs:** The egg cooker earns its space. Otherwise, a small nonstick skillet does the job.

**If you bake:** Buy the scale first. Baking by weight is far more reliable than cups, especially when you halve a recipe. See [space-saving baking tool essentials](/blog/space-saving-baking-tool-essentials/) for more.

## Gadgets to Skip in a Small Kitchen

These fail the three-question test for most solo cooks:

- **Avocado slicers.** A knife and a spoon work as well.
- **Strawberry hullers and banana slicers.** A paring knife does the job.
- **Egg separators.** Your hands or the shell work fine.
- **Garlic peelers.** Crush the clove with the flat of your knife.
- **Herb strippers.** Run your fingers down the stem.
- **Quesadilla and sandwich makers.** A skillet does both.
- **Full-size food processors.** Too big for single portions.
- **Bread makers.** Bulky, single-use and slow to pay off for one.
- **Spiralizers.** Fun for a month, then forgotten.

If you already own some of these, try a one-month rule. Anything you have not touched in a month can probably go.

## Why Multi-Use Tools Win

The best gadgets replace several others:

- **Shears** replace a pizza cutter, herb scissors and a packaging knife.
- **A rasp zester** replaces a garlic press, nutmeg grater and small cheese grater.
- **A stick blender** replaces a blender, hand mixer and whisk for small jobs.
- **A scale** replaces most measuring cups.

Before buying something new, check whether a tool you already own does the job.

## Why Cheap Gadgets Often Cost More

It is tempting to buy the cheapest version of every gadget. For tools you use daily, that usually backfires.

Cheap thermometers read slowly or drift out of accuracy, which means overcooked meat. Thin shears loosen at the pivot and start to chew instead of cut. Budget scales often hide their display under the bowl.

None of the picks above are the most expensive in their category. They are the ones built to survive daily use. Spend a little more once on the tools you reach for every day, and save on the ones you use once a week.

## Caring for Your Gadgets

- **Dry the scale platform** after washing, and keep liquids away from the base.
- **Replace the thermometer battery** when the display dims.
- **Separate the shear blades** and wash the pivot after raw meat.
- **Rinse the zester right away**, before food dries in the teeth.
- **Wash the stick blender shaft immediately** by running it in a cup of soapy water. Never submerge the motor.
- **Descale the egg cooker** now and then with a splash of vinegar.
- **Dry Stasher bags inside out** before storing.
