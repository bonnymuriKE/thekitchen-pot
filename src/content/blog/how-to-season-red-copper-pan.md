Red Copper pans are ceramic nonstick pans with a copper-colored finish. Like most ceramic pans, they release food best when the surface is clean and lightly oiled.

Seasoning takes about 10 minutes. Here is how to do it on the stovetop or in the oven, and how to keep the coating slick.

**The short version:** Wash and dry the pan, and rub a teaspoon of high smoke point oil over the inside. Heat it on medium for 1 to 2 minutes or in a 300°F oven for 20 minutes. Let it cool and wipe off the excess. Re-season every few months or when food starts sticking. If you are shopping, the [Red Copper 10-inch fry pan](https://www.amazon.com/Red-Copper-BulbHead-Non-Stick-Resistant/dp/B01CRBQ1JI/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is the classic single pan, but check stock first.

## What Is a Red Copper Pan?

Red Copper is a cookware line sold by BulbHead. Despite the name, the pans are not made of solid copper.

They are aluminum pans with a ceramic nonstick coating. BulbHead describes the coating as copper-infused, which gives it the copper color. Aluminum heats quickly and evenly, and the ceramic layer helps food slide off.

This matters for seasoning. You are not building a thick polymer layer like on cast iron. You are filling tiny pores in the ceramic with a thin film of oil so food releases more easily.

## Why Season a Ceramic Pan?

Ceramic coatings are slick when new, but they slowly lose that slickness. Seasoning helps in a few ways:

- **Better food release.** A thin oil film fills microscopic gaps in the surface.
- **Less sticking with eggs and fish.** Delicate foods are the first to stick on a worn pan.
- **A longer useful life.** A well-kept surface needs less scrubbing, which means less wear.

Seasoning cannot fix a scratched or peeling coating. If the coating is flaking, it is time to replace the pan.

## What You Need

- Mild dish soap and warm water
- A soft sponge or cloth
- A neutral, high smoke point oil
- Paper towels or a lint-free cloth
- Oven mitts
- A stovetop or an oven

## Best Oils for Seasoning

| Oil | Smoke Point (Approx.) | Good for Seasoning? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Canola or vegetable oil | About 400°F | Yes |
| Peanut oil | About 450°F | Yes |
| Grapeseed oil | About 420°F | Yes |
| Avocado oil (refined) | About 500°F | Yes |
| Extra-virgin olive oil | About 350 to 410°F | Not ideal |
| Butter | About 300 to 350°F | No |
| Cooking spray | Varies | No |

Smoke points vary by brand and refining. Any neutral, refined oil from the list works well. Our guide to the [best oil for an air fryer](/blog/best-oil-for-air-fryer/) explains smoke points in more detail.

Skip cooking spray completely. The additives in many sprays leave a sticky film that builds up on ceramic and makes food stick.

## How to Season a Red Copper Pan on the Stovetop

The stovetop method is the quickest.

### 1. Wash the Pan

Wash the pan in warm, soapy water with a soft sponge. New pans can carry dust and factory residue, so do this before first use.

### 2. Dry It Completely

Dry the pan with a towel, then set it on a burner over low heat for a minute to drive off any last moisture. Oil and water do not mix, so a dry pan seasons better.

### 3. Add a Thin Layer of Oil

Take the pan off the heat. Pour in about a teaspoon of oil and spread it over the whole cooking surface and up the sides with a paper towel.

Use only a thin layer. Too much oil turns sticky.

### 4. Heat It Gently

Set the pan on medium heat for 1 to 2 minutes, until the oil looks shiny and thin. Turn the heat off before it smokes heavily.

High heat can damage ceramic coatings. Medium is enough.

### 5. Cool and Wipe

Let the pan cool to room temperature on the stove. Then wipe out any extra oil with a clean paper towel. The surface should look slightly glossy, not greasy.

## How to Season a Red Copper Pan in the Oven

The oven method heats the pan more evenly. It is a good choice if your burner has hot spots.

1. Preheat the oven to 300°F.
2. Wash and dry the pan fully.
3. Rub a thin layer of oil over the cooking surface.
4. Place the pan in the oven for about 20 minutes.
5. Turn off the oven and let the pan cool inside it.
6. Wipe off any extra oil.

Check your pan's oven limit first. BulbHead lists the Red Copper 10-piece set as oven safe to 500°F, so 300°F is well within range. If you have a different brand, check its oven rating.

## How Often to Re-Season

Re-season your pan:

- Before first use
- Every few months with regular cooking
- When food starts to stick
- After a deep clean with baking soda

You do not need to season after every use. A small drop of oil when you cook is enough for daily upkeep.

## Signs Your Pan Needs Seasoning

You do not need a schedule if you watch the pan. Re-season when you notice any of these:

- **Eggs stick.** A fried egg should slide when you tilt the pan. If it clings, the surface needs help.
- **Water no longer beads.** On a slick surface, drops of water roll around. On a worn one, they spread flat.
- **The surface looks dull.** A new coating has a light sheen. Dullness often means a film of old grease or wear.
- **Food browns unevenly.** Patches of sticking can cause uneven cooking.

If re-seasoning does not bring the slickness back, the coating may be worn rather than dirty.

## Common Seasoning Mistakes

These mistakes cause most seasoning problems:

- **Using too much oil.** Thick oil turns gummy and sticky. Wipe until the pan looks almost dry.
- **Heating until it smokes hard.** Heavy smoke means the oil is breaking down. Medium heat is enough.
- **Seasoning a wet pan.** Water stops oil bonding evenly to the surface.
- **Using olive oil or butter.** Both burn at lower temperatures and can leave a brown, sticky film.
- **Treating it like cast iron.** Ceramic does not need many coats or very high heat. One thin coat is enough.
- **Skipping the cool-down.** Wiping a hot pan can smear the oil unevenly. Let it cool first.

## Cooking Tips for Red Copper Pans

The way you cook matters as much as seasoning.

### Eggs

Warm the pan on low to medium heat, then add a small knob of butter or a few drops of oil. Add eggs once the fat shimmers. Low heat and patience give soft eggs that slide out cleanly.

### Pancakes

Medium-low heat works best. A light wipe of oil before each batch keeps them releasing well. For bigger batches, a [griddle pan for pancakes](/blog/best-griddle-pan-for-pancakes/) gives more room.

### Fish and Meat

Pat food dry before cooking, and do not move it until it releases on its own. Ceramic pans are not ideal for very hard searing. A stainless steel or cast iron pan is better for a deep crust.

### Acidic Foods

Tomato sauces and citrus are fine in a ceramic pan for short cooking. Do not store food in the pan afterwards, as long soaking can dull the surface.

## How Long Does a Red Copper Pan Last?

Ceramic nonstick coatings generally wear out faster than PTFE nonstick or bare stainless steel. With daily use, many ceramic pans start to lose their release within a year or two.

Good care stretches that time. Low heat, soft utensils, hand washing and regular seasoning all help.

When the coating starts to chip or flake, replace the pan rather than keep seasoning it. A worn pan will keep sticking no matter how often you oil it, and a damaged coating is not worth the risk of flakes in your food.

## Daily Care That Keeps the Coating Slick

Seasoning helps, but daily habits matter more. Ceramic coatings wear faster than many people expect, and most damage comes from heat and tools.

- **Cook on low to medium heat.** High heat breaks down ceramic coatings over time. Aluminum heats fast, so medium is plenty.
- **Use a little oil or butter when cooking.** Butter is fine for cooking at moderate heat, just not for seasoning.
- **Use wood, silicone or nylon utensils.** Metal can scratch the surface.
- **Let the pan cool before washing.** Plunging a hot pan into cold water can warp it. If yours has warped, here is [how to fix a warped pan](/blog/how-to-fix-a-warped-pan/).
- **Hand wash it.** Dishwasher detergent is harsh on nonstick coatings.
- **Do not stack pans directly.** Place a cloth or pan protector between them.

## How to Clean a Red Copper Pan

Most of the time, warm water, a drop of dish soap and a soft sponge are all you need.

For stuck-on food or brown film:

1. Fill the pan with warm water and a little dish soap. Let it soak for 15 to 30 minutes.
2. Make a paste of baking soda and water.
3. Rub the paste gently over the stain with a soft sponge.
4. Rinse, dry and re-season.

Never use steel wool, scouring powder or oven cleaner. The same care applies to other ceramic pans; see our guide on [how to clean a ceramic pan](/blog/how-to-clean-ceramic-pan/).

## Why Food Sticks Even After Seasoning

If food still sticks, check these common causes:

- **Heat too high.** Food scorches and bonds to the surface.
- **Cooking spray buildup.** A sticky film forms over time. Clean with baking soda paste and re-season.
- **Pan not preheated.** Add food once the pan and oil are warm.
- **Worn coating.** Ceramic coatings wear with use. If the surface is dull, scratched or flaking, replace the pan.

## Is a Red Copper Pan Safe?

Ceramic nonstick coatings are made without PTFE (Teflon) and PFOA. That is one reason people choose them.

As with any nonstick pan, stop using it if the coating is chipping or peeling. For more on coating safety, see [is ceramic titanium cookware safe](/blog/is-ceramic-titanium-cookware-safe/) and [what is nonstick cookware](/blog/what-is-nonstick-cookware/).

## Red Copper vs Real Copper Cookware

Real copper cookware is heavy, conducts heat superbly and is usually lined with stainless steel or tin. It costs far more and needs polishing.

Red Copper is light, cheap and ceramic-coated. It is a nonstick pan with a copper look, not a copper pan. If you want copper's heat response with an easier surface, see the [All-Clad Copper Core set](/blog/all-clad-copper-core-cookware-set/).
