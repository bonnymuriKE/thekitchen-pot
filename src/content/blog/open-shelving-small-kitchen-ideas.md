Open shelving in a small kitchen can make the room feel twice as wide. It can also turn into a dusty, greasy wall of clutter. The difference is where you hang it and what you put on it.

This guide gives you our plain rules: when open shelves beat cabinets, how deep and high to hang them, how much weight they can really hold, and how to keep them clean. We also name the shelves, brackets and containers we would buy for the job.

**The short version:** Use open shelves for things you use at least once a week, like plates, glasses, mugs and staples in matching jars. Hang them 8 to 12 inches deep, starting about 18 inches above the counter, and screw them into studs or proper hollow-wall anchors. Never hang a shelf directly above the stove. Keep a third of each shelf empty, and everything else goes behind a door.

## Is Open Shelving Good for a Small Kitchen?

For most small kitchens, yes, in the right spot. A row of upper cabinets is a heavy box that sits at eye level and eats light. Swap one section for two open shelves and the wall opens up. You also save the time spent opening doors, which matters when you cook every day.

But open shelves do not fully replace cabinets. Mess shows. Rarely used items collect dust, shelves near the stove collect grease, and they hold less per foot.

Here is how we weigh it.

| Open shelves | Upper cabinets |
|---|---|
| Lighter look; the room feels wider | Heavier look; the wall feels closer |
| Grab a plate with one hand, no door to open | Doors hide clutter and mismatched items |
| Shows dust on items you rarely use | Keeps dust and grease off what is inside |
| Cheap: a pair of shelves costs far less than a cabinet | Holds more per foot of wall |
| Easy to add or move later | Fixed in place; a big job to change |
| Needs tidy, matching items to look good | Anything can go inside |

Our verdict: mix them. Keep closed storage for bulky, ugly or rarely used things. Add open shelves where you reach many times a day. If you are still planning the whole room, our [small kitchen storage ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-storage-ideas/) show how open and closed storage fit together.

## Open Shelving vs Upper Cabinets in a Tiny Kitchen

In a studio nook or one-wall layout, you may have only 6 to 8 feet of wall. You cannot swap all your cabinets for shelves.

Our rule for an open shelving tiny kitchen setup is the "one section" rule. Keep most of your upper cabinets. Add open shelves in one spot only, usually the end of the run or a gap by a window. You get the open feel without losing much storage.

If you have no upper cabinets at all, open shelves become your main wall storage. Then plan them like cabinets: two or three shelves stacked, each with a job. Our guide to [organizing a small kitchen without cabinets](/blog/organize-a-small-kitchen-without-cabinets/) covers that setup in more detail. For layouts with one long wall, see our [one-wall kitchen ideas](/blog/one-wall-kitchen-ideas/).

## Where Open Shelves Work Best (and the Spot to Skip)

Place shelves where the things on them get used. Good spots:

- **Beside the sink.** Clean plates and glasses go from the dish rack straight up to the shelf.
- **At the end of a cabinet run.** A short stack of shelves finishes the run and softens the corner.
- **Around a window.** Shelves on either side, or one across the top, frame the window without blocking light.
- **Over a coffee or tea station.** Mugs, beans and tea live right where you make the drink.
- **On a short wall or the side of a fridge alcove.** Narrow spaces that cannot fit a cabinet often fit a 24-inch shelf.

### Never Hang a Shelf Directly Above the Stove

This is the one hard rule. Do not put an open shelf directly above the cooktop or range. Heat and steam rise straight up and bake whatever sits there. Oil and jars warm up, and plastic warps. Grease coats every item. To reach the shelf, you lean over hot burners, and anything that slips falls onto a burner or into a pan.

The wall above the stove belongs to a range hood. If you need one, start with our [guide to range hood ventilation and cooking smells](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/). A slim model from our [under-cabinet range hood picks](/blog/best-under-cabinet-range-hood/) fits most small kitchens.

Shelves beside the stove are fine if you keep them off to the side. Our rule is at least 12 inches from the edge of the cooktop, and keep towels, paper and plastic off them. Store oils there only if they get used up within a few weeks, since warmth makes oil go stale faster.

## How to Size and Space an Open Shelf in a Small Kitchen

Most shelf mistakes are size mistakes. Too shallow, and a plate will not fit. Too deep, and it sticks out into your head space. Here are our numbers.

| What goes on it | Shelf depth | Space above it |
|---|---|---|
| Spices, small jars | 4 to 6 inches | 8 to 10 inches |
| Mugs and glasses | 6 to 8 inches | 7 to 9 inches |
| Jars and canisters of staples | 6 to 9 inches | 10 to 14 inches |
| Bowls and side plates | 8 to 10 inches | 10 to 12 inches |
| Dinner plates (10 to 11 inches wide) | 10 to 12 inches | 12 to 14 inches |

**Height.** Hang the lowest shelf about 18 inches above the counter, the same gap as an upper cabinet. Any lower and it blocks your work area and the light under it. Space the shelves 12 to 15 inches apart. Keep the top shelf low enough to reach without a stool, around 6 feet from the floor for most adults.

**Depth.** Do not go deeper than 12 inches, which is the depth of a standard upper cabinet. Anything deeper sticks out and gets bumped. For a narrow galley, 8 to 10 inches is often the sweet spot. Our [galley kitchen ideas](/blog/galley-kitchen-ideas/) cover how to keep that walkway clear.

**Length.** Studs are usually 16 inches apart, center to center. A 24-inch shelf may hit only one stud. A 32- to 36-inch shelf will usually reach two. If you plan to load a shelf with dishes, pick a length that spans two studs.

For a shallow shelf that suits jars and mugs, the [Fun Memories Solid Pine Floating Shelves](https://www.amazon.com/Fun-Memories-Pine-Floating-Shelves-24-Inch/dp/B0C89RBGJ1/?tag=kitchenpot-20) are a good pick. Each board is 24 inches long and about 5.7 inches deep, made of solid pine rather than particle board. ## How to Hang Open Shelves Safely: Studs, Anchors and Weight

Dishes are heavy. A stoneware dinner plate weighs about 1 to 2 pounds. A stack of eight plates is 10 to 15 pounds. Add bowls and a few full glass jars, and one shelf can carry 25 to 40 pounds. That load has to go into something solid.

### The Rule: Studs or Proper Anchors

Every kitchen shelf must be screwed into wall studs or held with proper hollow-wall anchors. Never hang a dish shelf on screws alone in drywall. Never use the small plastic expansion anchors that come in many shelf boxes for anything heavier than a few spice jars. They pull out slowly, then fail all at once.

A stud is a vertical wooden or metal post behind the drywall. A screw into a wood stud holds far more than any anchor. When there is no stud where you need one, use a toggle bolt. That is a bolt with metal wings that open behind the drywall and clamp against it.

To find studs, we recommend the [Franklin Sensors ProSensor 710 Stud Finder](https://www.amazon.com/Franklin-Sensors-ProSensor-710-Stud-Finder/dp/B0064EICKG/?tag=kitchenpot-20). It uses 13 sensors to light up the full width of the stud, so you see both edges at once instead of guessing at the center.

### How Much Weight Can an Open Shelf Hold?

Here is our plain weight guide. These are the loads we plan for per shelf, not the biggest number on the box.

| How the shelf is mounted | Plan for up to | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| Adhesive strips or stick-on ledge | Under 5 lb | Spices, a small plant, a few light jars |
| Plastic expansion anchors in drywall | Under 10 lb | Decor and light items only, never dishes |
| Toggle bolts in drywall | About 20 to 25 lb | Mugs, glasses, jars, a short stack of bowls |
| Screws into at least two studs | 40 lb or more | Full stacks of plates and bowls |

One more rule matters more than the table: **the lowest rating wins.** A shelf is only as strong as its weakest part: the wall, the anchor, the screw, the bracket or the board. If the shelf box says 22 pounds per shelf, the limit is 22 pounds, even if it is screwed into two studs. The Sorbus shelves above list 22 pounds each, so use them for bowls, side plates and glasses, not your whole dinner set.

### Steps to Hang a Shelf That Stays Up

1. Mark the height with a pencil, 18 inches above the counter for the first shelf.
2. Find and mark the studs along that line.
3. Check for pipes and wires. Do not drill directly above or below an outlet or switch, or straight above the sink where a pipe may run.
4. Hold the bracket or rail on the line, check it with a level, and mark the holes.
5. Drive screws into every stud the bracket crosses. Use at least 2-1/2-inch screws for wood studs, unless the bracket maker says otherwise.
6. Use toggle bolts for any holes that miss a stud.
7. Load the shelf slowly, heaviest items first, close to the wall.

Plaster walls in older buildings crack easily, so drill slowly and aim for studs. Tile needs a tile bit. If you are unsure, hire a handyman.

## Open Shelving Small Kitchen Shelf Ideas: Pick the Style

Not every open shelf is a floating board. Here are the main types and where each fits.

### Floating Shelves

A floating shelf hides its bracket inside the board, so it looks like a clean ledge. This is the most popular look, and it suits small kitchens because nothing sticks out below. The catch is weight. The hidden rods carry the whole load, so floating shelves usually hold less than bracket shelves. Use them for glasses, mugs, jars and lighter bowls.

### Bracket Shelves

A board on L-shaped brackets is the strongest, cheapest open shelf. The bracket supports the board from below, so it carries more weight. Choose this style for a shelf that holds a full stack of dinner plates.

The [Starunder Heavy Duty Floating Shelf Brackets, 6 Inch](https://www.amazon.com/Starunder-Heavy-Duty-Floating-Shelf-Brackets/dp/B09DSZ3LFW/?tag=kitchenpot-20) come six to a pack with screws and anchors, so one pack builds three shelves. Pair them with any 1-inch-thick wood board cut to length at a hardware store. Put one bracket on every stud the board crosses, and do not let the board hang more than about 6 inches past the last bracket.

### Rail and Hook Shelves

A rail is a metal bar on the wall with hooks for mugs, utensils, towels and small pans. It is the shallowest open storage there is, so it fits even narrow walls. The [Fasthomegoods Stainless Steel Kitchen Wall Rail](https://www.amazon.com/Fasthomegoods-Stainless-Steel-Kitchen-Wall-Rail-S-Hooks/dp/B00X1N67QO/?tag=kitchenpot-20) comes with 10 S-hooks and screws to the wall. ### Corner Shelves

The corner where two walls meet is often dead space. A corner shelf on the counter puts it to work. ## What Should You Put on Open Kitchen Shelves?

This is the question that decides whether open shelving works for you. Our rule: **if you do not use it at least once a week, it does not go on an open shelf.** Things you use often get washed often, so they never gather dust.

| Put it on the shelf | Keep it behind a door |
|---|---|
| Everyday plates, bowls and glasses | Holiday dishes and serving platters |
| Mugs you use daily | Mismatched plastic containers |
| Staples in matching airtight jars | Open bags and boxes of food |
| Cookbooks you cook from | Cleaning sprays and chemicals |
| A few small pans you use daily | Gadgets you use once a month |
| One or two plants or a small piece of art | Anything greasy, sticky or ugly |

## How to Style Open Shelves So They Stay Usable

Styling open shelves is about making them easy to use and easy to keep tidy. Here is our system.

1. **Give each shelf one job.** Bottom shelf for daily dishes, middle for glasses and mugs, top for jars and a cookbook or two. When every shelf has a job, things go back in the same place.
2. **Heavy and daily items go low.** Plates belong on the lowest shelf, closest to the counter. Lifting a stack of plates above your shoulders is how they get dropped.
3. **Leave a third of each shelf empty.** Packed shelves look cluttered and make it hard to grab one thing. Empty space is what makes open shelving look calm.
4. **Stick to two or three colors.** White dishes, clear glass and one wood tone look calm. A rainbow of mugs looks busy.
5. **Group in odd numbers.** Three jars, five mugs, one stack of plates. It looks planned, not random.
6. **Use a riser for small things.** A step shelf lifts the back row so you can see it. The [Seville Classics Bamboo 3-Tier Expandable Step Shelf](https://www.amazon.com/Seville-Classics-Bamboo-3-Tier-Expandable-Spice-Rack/dp/B001E2864K/?tag=kitchenpot-20) expands from 8.5 to 15 inches wide to fit the shelf.

### Hang Mugs Under the Shelf

The space under the lowest shelf is free storage. ## Does Open Shelving Make a Kitchen Look Bigger?

Yes, when it is done with restraint. Your eye stops at a solid cabinet box. An open shelf lets it run to the wall behind, so the room reads wider, and light spreads further.

Three things make the effect stronger:

- **Match the shelf to the wall.** A white shelf on a white wall almost disappears. A dark shelf on a light wall stands out and draws a line.
- **Keep the stuff on it light in color.** White dishes and clear glass look airy. Dark or busy items make the wall feel closer.
- **Do not fill it.** A crowded shelf looks smaller than a cabinet door.

## How to Light Open Shelves

Open shelves throw a shadow on the counter below, just like cabinets do. Add a light under the lowest shelf so you can still see what you are chopping.

Use warm white light only, 2700K to 3000K, so the shelf matches the rest of the room. Cool white light makes wood and food look flat. Keep any light at least a few inches from the edge so the bulb is hidden from view. Our [small kitchen lighting ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-lighting-ideas/) explain brightness and placement in full.

## How to Keep Open Shelves From Getting Greasy or Dusty

### Grease

Cooking grease travels as a fine mist and settles into a sticky film that traps dust. To keep it off your shelves:

- Keep shelves away from the stove, and never directly above it.
- Run the range hood every time you cook, and for 10 minutes after. Clean the filter often, too. Our guide on [how often to clean a range hood filter](/blog/how-often-to-clean-a-range-hood-filter/) gives a simple schedule.
- Wipe the shelves and anything that lives on them once a month with warm soapy water.

When a film has already built up, use a degreaser. [Zep Heavy-Duty Citrus Degreaser](https://www.amazon.com/ZEP-Heavy-Duty-Citrus-Degreaser-ZUCIT32/dp/B001PNEVK6/?tag=kitchenpot-20) cuts sticky kitchen grease fast. Dilute it as the label says, spray it on a cloth rather than the shelf, and wipe with clean water after. Test it on a hidden spot of a wood shelf first.

### Dust

Dust settles on what sits still. Follow the once-a-week rule and most items get washed before dust builds up. Give the rest a dry wipe when you clean the counters.

Sealed shelves are easier to clean. If you buy raw wood boards, coat them with a food-safe finish or a clear water-based polyurethane. A sealed surface wipes clean. Raw wood soaks up grease and stains for good.

## Can Renters Add Open Shelving?

Yes, but most real shelves need screws, so ask first. Send your landlord a short message with the size and spot of the shelf, and get the answer in writing. Many landlords say yes, since screw holes patch easily. Toggle bolt holes are bigger, so expect to patch and touch up paint at move-out.

## More Small Kitchen Ideas for Shelves

Open shelving does not have to mean a board on a wall.

- **Beside the fridge.** The narrow strip of wall next to a fridge often fits a 24-inch shelf for snacks, lunch containers and water bottles. Keep it clear of the fridge door swing.
- **Inside a cabinet with the doors removed.** Taking the doors off one cabinet gives you instant open shelving with no drilling. Store the doors and hinges in a labeled bag. This works well for a renter and is easy to reverse.
- **On the end of the counter.** A short shelf or two on the end wall of a counter makes a great coffee or snack spot.
- **Above a window.** One long shelf over a window holds jars or cookbooks without blocking light. Keep it within easy reach, or use it for items you rarely need, stored in closed containers.

## Common Mistakes With Open Shelving in a Small Kitchen

1. **Hanging a shelf above the stove.** Heat, steam and grease ruin what sits there, and you have to reach over burners to get to it. Keep that wall for a range hood.
2. **Using the anchors in the box.** Many shelves ship with thin plastic anchors. They are fine for a picture, not for plates. Use studs or toggle bolts.
3. **Trusting the biggest number.** An anchor rated for 100 pounds does not make a 22-pound shelf hold 100 pounds. The weakest part sets the limit.
4. **Getting the size wrong.** Deeper than 12 inches catches your head. Lower than 18 inches above the counter blocks your work area.
5. **Storing things you rarely use.** A once-a-year platter on an open shelf is a dust collector. Put it in a closed cabinet.
6. **Filling every inch.** A full shelf looks cluttered. Leave about a third empty.
7. **Removing all the cabinets.** In a small kitchen, you still need closed storage for bulk food, cleaning supplies and ugly things. Swap one section, not all of it.
8. **Skipping a seal on raw wood.** Raw boards soak up grease and water. Seal them before you hang them.
