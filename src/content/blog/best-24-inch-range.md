## Does a Half Sheet Pan Fit in a 24-Inch Range?

It depends on the oven's inside width, not its cubic feet. A half sheet pan measures about 18 by 13 inches. You need at least 18.5 inches of width so the pan clears the walls and the door closes.

| Oven inside width | Pans that fit | Ranges here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 19 in | Half sheet, quarter sheet, 9x13 dish | Summit REX2421WRT, Summit TEM665BW |
| 18.9 in | Half sheet (snug), quarter sheet, 9x13 dish | ZLINE RA24 |
| 16 to 18 in | Quarter sheet (13 x 9 in), 9x13 dish, 12-cup muffin tin | Frigidaire FFEH2422US, Avanti GR2416CSS |

If your oven only takes quarter sheets, buy two and bake in batches. Thin pans can buckle in a small, hot oven, and [why baking sheets warp in the oven](/blog/why-baking-sheets-warp-in-the-oven/) explains how to stop it. For extra baking room, a [countertop oven for small kitchens](/blog/best-small-ovens-and-toaster-ovens-for-baking-in-small-kitchens/) can run a second tray at the same time.

## Coil, Smooth Top, Induction or Gas?

Each cooktop type heats in its own way. Here is what that means day to day.

| Cooktop | How it heats | Good at | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coil | Metal coil glows red, heats the pan by contact | Cheap, tough, any pan works | Drip bowls collect spills |
| Smooth glass (radiant) | Coil under glass heats the glass, then the pan | Easy to wipe, extra flat space | Scratches; slow to cool |
| Induction | Magnetic field heats the pan directly | Fastest, coolest, most efficient | Needs magnetic pans |
| Gas | Open flame heats the pan | Instant control, woks, charring | Needs a gas line and good ventilation |

Coil is the hardest to damage. Glass tops look tidier but need flat-bottomed pans. A round-bottom wok sits better on gas, though a [flat-bottom wok for electric stoves](/blog/best-wok-for-electric-stove/) works on coil and glass.

Induction is the most efficient of the four. If you are not ready for a whole new range, a plug-in [induction cooktop](/blog/best-induction-cooktop-for-the-money/) is a cheap way to try it first. Check your pans with a magnet, or start with [cookware that works on induction](/blog/best-cookware-for-induction-cooktop/).

## Electric Needs 240 Volts; Gas Needs a Line and a Plug

This is the question to answer before anything else. Look behind your current stove.

**Electric and induction ranges** need a 220 to 240-volt outlet, which is larger than a normal outlet with three or four slots. The Summit, Frigidaire and Avanti electric models here want a 40-amp breaker. Most of them ship without a cord, so buy a 3-prong or 4-prong cord that matches your outlet.

**Gas ranges** need a gas pipe with a shutoff valve behind the range. They also plug into a standard 120-volt outlet. That small plug runs the igniters, oven light, clock and any convection fan.

**Dual fuel ranges** need both: gas for the burners and 240 volts for the oven. The ZLINE RA24 runs on a 30-amp circuit.

Switching from electric to gas, or the other way, means new wiring or a new gas line. That is a job for a licensed pro, and in a rental it needs the landlord's written approval.

## Freestanding vs Slide-In at 24 Inches

A freestanding range has finished sides and can stand anywhere, even at the end of a counter run. Every pick here is freestanding.

A slide-in sits between cabinets with no back panel, for a built-in look. True slide-ins are rare at 24 inches. The Summit REX2421WRT gets close: remove its 2-inch backguard and it can sit flush between cabinets.

Most 24-inch ranges are about 24 inches deep, matching standard counters. The Cosmo stands at 25.5 inches and the ZLINE at 27.5 inches, so they jut out a little. In a narrow [galley kitchen](/blog/galley-kitchen-ideas/), measure the walkway with the oven door open too.

## Clearances and the Anti-Tip Bracket

Read the manual's clearance list before you order. Most ask for about 30 inches between the cooktop and a wooden cabinet above. That gap is also where a range hood goes.

A 24-inch range needs at least a 24-inch hood. An [under-cabinet range hood](/blog/best-under-cabinet-range-hood/) is the usual choice. If there is no duct to the outside, a [ductless range hood](/blog/6-best-ductless-range-hood/) filters grease and smells and blows the air back into the room. An over-the-range microwave can also vent, as covered in our [over-the-range microwave picks](/blog/7-best-over-the-range-microwaves/).

Then there is the anti-tip bracket. It is a small metal bracket screwed to the floor or wall that hooks the back leg of the range. A light, narrow range can tip forward if a child stands on the open oven door, and the bracket stops that. The Avanti and Summit electric models ship with one. Install it, even in a rental, and check it when the range is pulled out for cleaning.

## 24-Inch vs 30-Inch Range: What You Give Up

You keep four burners. You give up oven space.

Most 30-inch ranges have ovens of about 5 cubic feet or more. That is roughly double the best 24-inch ovens. You lose the room to bake two sheet pans side by side or roast a large turkey.

In return you gain six inches of counter, which often matters more. Our [small kitchen layout ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-layout-ideas/) show how a narrower range can open up a work zone. You also gain a smaller oven that heats less air, so it preheats faster and uses less energy.

## 20 vs 24 Inch Range

A 20-inch range is the smallest full range you can buy. Most have ovens around 2 cubic feet, and a half sheet pan almost never fits. Burners sit very close together, so two large pans rarely fit side by side.

Choose 20 inches only when the gap truly cannot take 24, such as a tiny [studio apartment kitchen](/blog/studio-apartment-kitchen-ideas/), a cabin or a basement unit. If you have the 24 inches, use them.

## Energy Use in a Small Apartment

A smaller oven heats less empty air, so it reaches temperature faster and cycles less. Induction cooking goes further by putting the heat into the pan instead of the room. Both help on the power bill.

Match the pan to the burner and use lids. Our guide on how to [reduce kitchen energy use in a small apartment](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/) covers more. Flat-bottomed pans help too, as explained in [how to choose energy-efficient cookware](/blog/how-to-choose-energy-efficient-cookware-for-a-small-kitchen/).
