A burner that burns yellow, flickers on one side or clicks without lighting is telling you something. Nine times out of ten, it is dirt sitting exactly where gas and air need to meet.

So cleaning gas stove burners and grates properly is about more than a nice-looking stove. A clean burner lights on the first click and gives a steady blue flame. It also cooks evenly and does not leave black soot on your pans. Below is the full method, part by part, with the safe way to handle stuck-on grease and the one tool you should never stick in a burner port.

**Quick answer:** Turn off the burners and let the stove cool. Lift off the grates, caps and burner heads, then soak them in hot water with dish soap for 20 to 30 minutes and scrub. Use baking soda paste or a sealed bag with household ammonia for heavy grease. Clear blocked ports with a straight pin, wipe the igniter dry, and let every part dry completely before you put it back.

## What Your Flame and Igniter Are Telling You

Before you start scrubbing, look at the flame. It tells you which part is dirty and how deep a clean you need.

| What you see or hear | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Steady blue flame with small inner cones | Burner is healthy | Weekly wipe only |
| Yellow or orange tips, soot on pans | Dirty ports, crooked cap or poor air mix | Full clean of caps, heads and ports |
| Gaps in the flame ring, one side low | Some ports blocked with food or grease | Clear ports with a pin |
| Flame lifts off the burner or roars | Cap missing or not seated | Reseat the cap flat |
| Constant clicking after the flame lights | Igniter or burner is wet, or cap is off-center | Dry the burner fully, reseat the cap |
| Clicking but no flame | Gas not reaching the spark, often a wet or blocked burner | Dry, clean ports, reseat the cap |
| Whistling or hissing burner | Debris in the burner head or a misaligned cap | Clean the head, check the fit |
| Smell of rotten eggs | Possible gas leak | Stop. See the safety section below |

A few orange flecks in a blue flame are normal. Dust in the air or a nearby humidifier causes them. A flame that is mostly yellow and lazy is not normal. It means the gas is not burning cleanly, and that is where soot and extra fumes come from.

If cooking smells and greasy haze hang around for hours, the burners may not be the only problem. Check your ventilation too, using our guide on [range hood ventilation and cooking smells in a small kitchen](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/).

## Know the Parts: Grates, Caps, Heads and the Igniter

Each piece is made of a different material, so each needs different care.

- **Grates** are the heavy racks your pans sit on. They are usually cast iron, either bare (matte black) or coated in enamel (glossy). They catch most of the splatter.
- **Burner caps** are the flat round discs on top of each burner. They spread the flame into a ring. Most are steel or cast iron with a black enamel coating.
- **Burner heads** (also called burner bases) sit under the caps. They have the row of small holes, called ports, where the gas comes out. Most are aluminum or brass.
- **The igniter** is the small white ceramic nub with a metal tip next to each burner. It makes the spark that lights the gas.
- **The cooktop surface** is the stainless, enamel or glass top around everything.

On most modern ranges, the cap and head lift straight off. On some, the head is screwed down and only the cap comes off. Never force a part that does not lift freely. Check your manual, which also lists any cleaners it says to avoid.

## Sealed vs Open Burners: Which One Do You Have?

This changes how you clean, so check before you start.

**Sealed burners** are on nearly every gas range sold today. The burner base is fixed to the cooktop with no gap around it. Spills stay on the surface instead of falling inside the stove. You lift off the cap and head and wipe the surface below.

**Open burners** are common on older ranges and some pro-style stoves. There is a gap around each burner, and a removable drip pan sits underneath. The burner may be a tube that lifts out after you remove a screw or slide it off a gas valve.

| | Sealed burners | Open burners |
|---|---|---|
| Where spills go | Stay on the cooktop | Fall into drip pans below |
| What lifts off | Cap and usually the head | Grate, drip pan, sometimes the whole burner tube |
| Extra cleaning job | Wipe the surface around the base | Wash drip pans, clean under the top |
| Risk to watch | Water running into the gas opening | Food falling into the burner tube |
| Typical on | Most ranges made in the last 25 years | Older and some pro-style ranges |

With open burners, never pour water into the burner opening. Vacuum out crumbs, and wash the drip pans like the grates.

## How to Clean Gas Stove Burners and Grates, Step by Step

Here is the full deep clean. Plan for about an hour of work, plus soaking and drying time. A small sink can hold only two grates at a time, so do them in batches or use a bathtub lined with an old towel.

### What You Need

- Hot water, a basin or the sink, and grease-cutting dish soap
- Baking soda
- A stiff nylon brush, such as the [OXO Good Grips Heavy Duty Scrub Brush](https://www.amazon.com/OXO-Grips-Heavy-Scrub-Brush/dp/B00004OCLS/?tag=kitchenpot-20). Its firm bristles reach the ridges on the grates, and the scraper edge on the head helps on crusted spots.
- A non-scratch scrub sponge for enamel, like the [Scotch-Brite Zero Scratch Scrub Sponge](https://www.amazon.com/Scotch-Brite-Non-Scratch-Scrub-Sponge-3-Sponges/dp/B000SSS6AU/?tag=kitchenpot-20)
- A straight pin or an unfolded paper clip
- Rubber gloves and a couple of dry cotton towels

Keep these near the stove, not buried at the back of a cupboard. Our guide to [organizing under the kitchen sink](/blog/organize-under-the-kitchen-sink/) has a spot for every one of them.

### Step 1: Turn Everything Off and Let It Cool

Make sure every knob points to off. Wait at least 30 minutes after cooking. Grates and caps hold heat long after the flame is out. If your range plugs into the wall and the plug is easy to reach, unplug it. That stops the igniters from sparking while your hands are near them.

### Step 2: Remove the Grates, Caps and Heads

Take a phone photo first. Lift off the grates, then lift each cap and head straight up. Lay them on a towel in the same layout as the stove, since each one fits only its own burner.

### Step 3: Shake Off Loose Crumbs

Tap each part over the trash and brush loose bits off the cooktop, so grit stays out of the soaking water.

### Step 4: Soak in Hot, Soapy Water

Fill the sink with the hottest tap water you can handle. Add a good squeeze of dish soap. Soak the grates and caps for 20 to 30 minutes. Burner heads can go in the same water for 10 to 15 minutes, but no longer.

Bare cast iron grates are the exception to long soaks. Keep them to 15 minutes or less, then wash and dry them right away. More on that below.

### Step 5: Scrub

Scrub the grates along every bar, especially the fingers where pans sit and the feet that touch the cooktop. Scrub the tops and edges of the caps. For burner heads, brush along the ports in the same direction as the holes, not across them. That pushes debris out instead of in.

For tight corners and the space around the ports, a small detail brush works better than a big one. The [OXO Good Grips Deep Clean Brush Set](https://www.amazon.com/OXO-Good-Grips-Deep-Clean-Brush-Set/dp/B003M8GMS6/?tag=kitchenpot-20) gives you a small brush for the ports and a thin one for the gaps where a grate bar meets its frame.

If grease is still stuck after soaking, move to one of the stronger methods in the next section. Skip steel wool on enamel parts. It scratches the coating, and scratches hold grease faster.

### Step 6: Clean the Cooktop Surface

With the parts out of the way, wipe the sticky ring of grease where the grates sat. A gas-range spray like [Weiman Gas Range Cleaner and Degreaser](https://www.amazon.com/Weiman-Range-Cleaner-Degreaser-Burned/dp/B003S7YWT2/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is made for this job. Spray, wait a minute, then wipe with a damp cloth. Keep the spray away from burner bases and igniters.

### Step 7: Rinse, Dry and Reassemble

Rinse off every trace of soap and cleaner. Then dry each part fully, refit it and test the flame, as explained in the drying and reassembly sections below.

## Soaking Methods for Stuck-On Grease

Dish soap handles weekly grime. Months of baked-on grease need more help. Here are three methods, from mild to strong.

### Dish Soap and Hot Water

This is the base method above. It works for anything cleaned in the last few weeks. Add a quarter cup of baking soda to the sink water for a little more cutting power.

### Baking Soda Paste (With or Without Vinegar)

Mix 3 parts baking soda with 1 part water into a thick paste. Spread it over the greasy spots on grates and caps. Leave it for 20 to 30 minutes, then scrub and rinse.

You can spray white vinegar over the paste. It fizzes, and the fizz helps lift grease off the surface. Vinegar alone also cuts a light greasy film. Keep vinegar off bare cast iron for any length of time, because acid speeds up rust.

### The Ammonia Bag Method (for Heavy Grease)

This is the method we would use on grates that have not been deep cleaned in a year. You do not soak the grates in ammonia. The fumes do the work.

1. Put one or two grates in a large, heavy zip-top bag or a sturdy trash bag.
2. Add about a quarter cup of plain household ammonia. It does not need to cover the grates.
3. Seal the bag tightly and lay it flat in a tub, a bin or outside on a balcony.
4. Leave it overnight, or 8 to 12 hours.
5. Wearing gloves, open the bag outdoors or by an open window, away from your face.
6. Rinse the grates, then scrub. Most of the grease wipes off with little effort.

Safety rules for ammonia:

- **Never mix ammonia with bleach** or any cleaner that contains bleach. The mix makes a toxic gas.
- Work outside or by an open window, wear gloves, and do not lean over the bag.
- Use it on grates and enamel caps only. It darkens and pits aluminum burner heads.
- Pour the used ammonia down the drain with plenty of running water, away from children and pets.

### Oven Cleaner and Degreasers

A heavy-duty oven cleaner will strip burnt grease from cast iron grates, but it is harsh. Use it outside, with gloves, and never on aluminum burner heads. Lye in oven cleaner eats aluminum.

A citrus degreaser is a gentler middle step. [Zep Heavy-Duty Citrus Degreaser](https://www.amazon.com/ZEP-Heavy-Duty-Citrus-Degreaser-ZUCIT32/dp/B001PNEVK6/?tag=kitchenpot-20) cuts thick grease on grates and the cooktop without the strong fumes of ammonia. It is the same spray we suggest in our guide on [how often to clean a range hood filter](/blog/how-often-to-clean-a-range-hood-filter/), so one bottle covers both jobs. Spray it, let it sit 5 minutes, scrub and rinse well.

| Method | Best for | Time | Safe on bare cast iron? | Safe on aluminum heads? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dish soap and hot water | Weekly and light grease | 20 to 30 min | Yes, short soak | Yes, 10 to 15 min |
| Baking soda paste | Medium buildup, spots | 20 to 30 min | Yes | Yes, gently |
| Baking soda and vinegar | Light film, odors | 20 min | Short contact only | Yes, rinse well |
| Citrus degreaser | Thick grease | 5 to 10 min | Yes, then dry and oil | Wipe only, rinse well |
| Ammonia bag | Heavy, year-old grease | 8 to 12 hours | Yes | No |
| Oven cleaner | Burnt-on carbon | Per the label | Yes, outdoors | No |

## How to Clean Burner Caps and Heads

Caps are the easy part. Wash them like the grates. For brown heat stains or burnt-on rings on enamel caps, a paste of [Bar Keepers Friend Superior Cookware Cleanser & Polish](https://www.amazon.com/Bar-Keepers-Friend-Cookware-Cleanser/dp/B000V6YLNA/?tag=kitchenpot-20) and a few drops of water lifts them with a soft sponge. Rinse it off fully. It also polishes a stainless cooktop, the same way it does in our guide on [how to clean stainless steel pans](/blog/how-to-clean-stainless-steel-pans/).

Burner heads need a lighter touch. Most are aluminum, which scratches and discolors easily. Wash them in soapy water, brush the ports, and rinse. Do not use oven cleaner, ammonia or steel wool on them. Some dark color on an aluminum head is normal after years of use. It does not affect the flame. Blocked ports do.
## How to Clean Clogged Burner Holes

Blocked ports are the most common reason for an uneven flame or a burner that will not light. Boil-overs from pasta water and rice are the usual cause, because the starch dries hard inside the holes.

1. Make sure the burner is off and cool.
2. Take off the cap. If the head lifts off, take it off too.
3. Hold a straight pin or the straight end of a paper clip. Push it gently into each port and twist it slightly.
4. Work around the whole ring, hole by hole.
5. Brush away the loosened bits, then shake the head upside down.
6. If the head was washed, dry it fully before you refit it.

**Never use a toothpick, a wooden skewer or a match.** Wood snaps off inside the port, and a broken tip is very hard to get out. It can also catch fire the next time you light the burner.

Do not widen the holes. Port size controls how much gas comes out, and a forced-open port gives a taller, uneven flame on that side.

If your big pots boil over often, the fix is partly in the pot. A taller pot like those in our [best stockpot with a lid](/blog/best-stockpot-with-a-lid/) guide gives starchy foam more room before it spills.

## How to Clean the Igniter

A clicking burner that will not stop, or one that clicks without lighting, often has a wet or dirty igniter.

1. Turn off the burners. Unplug the range if you can reach the plug.
2. Find the white ceramic nub next to the burner.
3. Brush it gently with a dry, clean toothbrush to remove crumbs and grease.
4. For a greasy film, dip a cotton swab in rubbing alcohol, squeeze out the excess, and wipe the tip. Alcohol dries fast and leaves no residue.
5. Let it dry for a few minutes before you test it.

Never spray cleaner on the igniter, scrub it hard or bend the metal tip. The ceramic cracks easily, and the gap between tip and burner is set for a good spark.

Still clicking nonstop once the burner is fully dry? The switch behind the knob may be damp. Leave the stove off for a day, and call a technician if that does not fix it.

## Bare Cast Iron vs Enamel Grates

Look at your grates. Glossy and smooth means enamel coating. Matte, slightly rough and dark gray means bare cast iron. Each needs its own care.

| | Enamel-coated grates | Bare cast iron grates |
|---|---|---|
| Looks like | Glossy black or gray | Matte, dark, slightly rough |
| Soaking | 20 to 30 minutes is fine | 15 minutes or less |
| Scrubbing | Nylon brush or non-scratch sponge | Nylon brush; pumice works on hard spots |
| Avoid | Steel wool, pumice, metal scrapers | Long soaks, vinegar left on, air drying |
| Dishwasher | Only if the manual says so | No, it will rust |
| After washing | Dry with a towel | Dry, warm on the stove 2 minutes, wipe thin oil |
| Common problem | Chips that expose iron underneath | Rust spots |

### Caring for Bare Cast Iron Grates

Treat them like a skillet. Wash, dry with a towel, then set them back on the stove and run each burner on low for 1 to 2 minutes to drive off the last moisture. Turn off the flame, let them cool slightly, and wear [heat-resistant gloves](/blog/10-best-heat-resistant-gloves-for-cooking/) to wipe on a very thin coat of cooking oil with a paper towel. The oil keeps rust away. Our [Lodge cast iron skillet review](/blog/lodge-cast-iron-skillet-review/) explains why a thin coat beats a thick one.

If a grate already has rust, scrub the spot with the nylon brush and a little baking soda. A pumice stone works on bare cast iron for stubborn spots. Then dry and oil it as above.

### Caring for Enamel Grates

Enamel is glass fused onto metal. It shrugs off grease but chips if you drop it or bang a heavy pot on it. Clean with soap, baking soda and a non-scratch sponge. Never use pumice, steel wool or metal scrapers.

If a chip shows bare iron, dry that spot well after every wash. Replace a grate with large chips, since rust spreads under the coating.

## Can You Put Gas Stove Grates in the Dishwasher?

Only if your manual says yes. Bare cast iron will rust. Enamel grates labeled dishwasher safe will survive, but strong detergent dulls them over time. We prefer the sink. If you do use the dishwasher, run the grates alone and towel dry them right after. Then read [how to clean a dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/), because the grease ends up in its filter.

## How to Clean Gas Stove Burners Without Removing Them

Some burner heads are screwed down, and some days you only have five minutes. With the burners off and cool, lift off the caps. Wipe the head and the area around it with a barely damp, soapy cloth. Brush along the ports, clear any blocked ones with a pin, and brush the igniter dry. Wait at least 15 minutes before lighting.

The key is to keep water out of the burner opening. A damp cloth is fine. Pouring or spraying is not.

## Drying Before Reassembly

Wet burners cause most of the problems people see right after cleaning: endless clicking, no light, and yellow, sputtering flames. Towel dry every part and shake water out of the ports. Then air dry burner heads upside down for at least an hour after a soak, a few hours if you can. Caps and enamel grates can go back once towel dry.

Short on counter space? Dry the parts on a sheet pan on top of the fridge. A thick towel from our [best kitchen towels](/blog/best-kitchen-towels/) guide pulls water from the ports faster than a thin one, and a [Dorai dish pad](/blog/dorai-dish-pad-review/) dries fast between uses.

## Putting It Back Together and Testing the Flame

1. Set each burner head on its base. Most have a notch or tab that lines up with the igniter. It should sit flat and not rock.
2. Place the cap on top. Center it so the edge sits evenly on the head all the way around.
3. Set the grates back in place. Many grates fit only one way, so match the feet to the marks or dips in the cooktop.
4. Light each burner on high, then turn it to low.

You want a full ring of blue flame with no gaps. Each small flame should have a short, sharp inner cone. On low, the flame should stay lit all the way around.

If one burner gives a big, uneven flame or roars, the cap is off-center. Turn it off, let it cool and reseat it. If one side is weak, a port is still blocked or wet.

## Why Your Burner Is Still Yellow, Black or Not Working

Cleaned everything and still have a problem? Check these.

**Still clicking but not lighting.** The burner is probably still wet. Wait a few more hours, then check the cap is centered.

**Only one burner not working.** The problem is local: a wet igniter, blocked ports or a crooked cap. If no burner lights and you smell no gas, the supply valve may be off or your gas service interrupted.

**Black soot or a steady orange flame.** Recheck the ports first. If the stove was recently moved, installed or converted to propane, the air mix may need adjusting by a technician.

**Burner smoking.** Leftover soap, cleaner or grease is burning off. Wipe and rinse again.

A gas grill with the same symptoms has different causes, such as a tripped regulator or a spider web in the tubes. Our guide on [why your gas grill has a weak flame](/blog/why-your-gas-grill-has-a-weak-flame/) covers those.

## A Weekly Routine That Keeps Deep Cleans Rare

Five minutes a week saves you an hour every few months.

| When | Job | Time |
|---|---|---|
| After any boil-over | Wipe the spill once the burner cools, check the ports | 2 minutes |
| Weekly | Wipe grates and caps with a soapy cloth, wipe the cooktop | 5 minutes |
| Every 2 to 4 weeks | Soak grates and caps in soapy water, brush the ports | 30 minutes |
| Every 3 to 6 months | Full deep clean, including igniters and heavy grease | 1 hour plus drying |
| Once a year | Ammonia bag or degreaser on old grease | Overnight |

If you fry or sear often, move everything up one step. If you mostly cook on two burners, as in our guide to [cooking a full meal with only two burners](/blog/how-to-cook-a-full-meal-with-only-two-burners/), those two need the weekly attention most. A small caddy holding the brush, sponge, pins and spray makes the weekly wipe happen; our [small kitchen hacks](/blog/small-kitchen-hacks/) have more ideas like it.

## Cleaning Mistakes That Damage Burners and Grates

- **Lighting a wet burner.** It clicks, sputters or burns yellow.
- **Mixing ammonia and bleach.** This creates a toxic gas. Use one cleaner at a time.
- **Harsh cleaners on aluminum heads.** Oven cleaner and ammonia darken and pit them.
- **Spraying cleaner straight at the burner or igniter.** Liquid runs into the gas opening and the spark switch. Spray the cloth instead.
- **Mixing up caps and heads.** A cap on the wrong burner gives an uneven flame. Photograph first.
- **Wood or anything thicker than a pin in the ports.** Wood snaps off, and wider holes give a lopsided flame.

## When to Call a Pro (and When to Leave Right Away)

**If you smell gas, stop cleaning.** Natural gas and propane have an added smell like rotten eggs. If it is strong or does not fade within a minute of turning the knobs off:

- Do not light anything, flip light switches, or unplug appliances. A spark can ignite the gas.
- Leave the home with everyone in it, and leave the door open behind you.
- Call your gas company or 911 from outside.

Call a gas technician or appliance repair service, not a general handyman, if:

- The flame stays yellow or sooty after a full clean
- A burner will not light even when it is dry, clean and seated
- An igniter clicks nonstop for days after everything is dry
- The burner flame is very low even on high
- The igniter is cracked
- You see a damaged gas line or a loose connection behind the stove

Renters should report these to the landlord. A gas range is the owner's appliance, and gas repairs should be done by a licensed pro. Our list of [renter-friendly kitchen upgrades](/blog/renter-friendly-kitchen-upgrades/) covers what you can change yourself and what you should not touch.
