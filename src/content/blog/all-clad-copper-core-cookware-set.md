## What Makes Copper Core Different From D3 and D5?

All-Clad makes three main fully clad stainless lines. They all look alike, and they all go up to 600°F in the oven. The difference is what sits inside the sandwich.

- **D3** is three layers: stainless, aluminum, stainless. Our [All-Clad D3 cookware set review](/blog/all-clad-d3-cookware-set-review/) covers it in detail.
- **D5** is five layers: stainless, aluminum, stainless, aluminum, stainless.
- **Copper Core** is five layers: stainless, aluminum, copper, aluminum, stainless.

So Copper Core is D5 with the middle steel swapped for copper. Copper moves heat faster than steel does. That is the only real change, and everything else in this review follows from it.

## How the 5-Ply Copper Core Build Works

Every Copper Core pan starts as one flat sheet of five metals. The layers are bonded under heat and pressure until they act like a single piece of metal. Only then is the sheet pressed into a pan shape.

Here is what each layer does, from the inside out:

1. **Stainless steel (cooking surface).** It does not react with tomatoes, wine or lemon. It also takes a hard sear. But steel moves heat slowly, so it cannot work alone.
2. **Aluminum.** Aluminum spreads heat sideways fast. It takes energy from the hot spot over the burner and moves it across the base.
3. **Copper (the core).** Copper moves heat even faster than aluminum. It evens out the heat further and helps the pan react quickly when you change the flame.
4. **Aluminum.** A second spreading layer, which also shields the copper.
5. **Stainless steel (outside).** This layer is magnetic, which is why the pans work on induction. It is also tough and easy to clean.

If clad metal is new to you, our explainer on [what stainless steel cookware is](/blog/what-is-stainless-steel-cookware/) covers the basics.

### Why the Copper Sits in the Middle

Copper is a great heat mover, but it is a poor cooking surface. It reacts with acidic food, and bare copper does not work on induction. Tucked in the middle, it does the job it is good at and nothing else.

The layers also run all the way up the walls, not just across the base. All-Clad calls this fully bonded. It means the sides of a saucepan heat too, so sauces do not scorch in a ring at the bottom edge.

### What the Copper Band on the Side Is

The copper stripe around the base is where the copper layer shows. All-Clad cuts a thin band through the outer steel near the bottom, which exposes the metal underneath.

It is mostly a signature. You can spot Copper Core across a store at a glance. It does not make the pan cook better.

Because it is bare copper, the band can darken or tarnish over time. That is normal copper behavior and does not affect cooking. A copper polish brings the shine back if it bothers you.

## Is Copper Core Good on Induction, Gas and Electric?

Yes, on all three. All-Clad lists Copper Core as compatible with every stovetop, induction included.

On induction, the magnetic outer steel is what the coil heats. The inner layers then spread that heat. Induction zones are often smaller than the pan, so good spreading helps. Our guide to [the best induction cooktops for the money](/blog/best-induction-cooktop-for-the-money/) covers burner sizes.

On gas, the flame hits the base and climbs the sides. Fully bonded walls handle that well. On a coil or smooth electric top, flat, heavy bases make good contact, which helps. Our guide to [coil stove cookware](/blog/coil-stove-cookware-what-works-what-warps/) explains why flatness matters.

Some buyers notice that heavy 5-ply pans take longer to come up to heat than thinner pans. That is the mass at work. Give them an extra minute of preheat on medium, and they reward you with steadier heat.

## Oven, Broiler and Heat Limits

All-Clad rates Copper Core oven and broiler safe to 600°F, or 315°C. The stainless lids share that rating.

That covers most real cooking. You can sear chops on the stove and finish them in a hot oven. You can brown a gratin under the broiler in the sauté pan.

The handles do get hot in the oven, and they stay hot for a while after. Keep a mitt nearby. Our roundup of [heat-resistant gloves for cooking](/blog/10-best-heat-resistant-gloves-for-cooking/) has options that grip well.

On the stovetop, you rarely need high heat with clad pans. Medium does most jobs because the layers spread the heat so well. Running an empty pan on high is the fastest way to cause the blue and gold marks explained in [why stainless steel turns rainbow](/blog/why-does-stainless-steel-turn-rainbow/).

## How to Care for Copper Core (Hand Wash Only)

All-Clad's current Copper Core pages list every piece as hand wash only. Some older Amazon titles still say "dishwasher safe." Go by the maker's page.

There are two good reasons for this. Harsh dishwasher detergent dulls the polished steel over time. It can also darken the exposed copper band.

Daily care is simple:

1. Let the pan cool before it touches water, to avoid thermal shock.
2. Wash with warm water, dish soap and a soft sponge.
3. For stuck food, simmer a little water in the pan and scrape with a wooden spoon.
4. For white spots or stains, use a paste of Bar Keepers Friend and water.
5. Dry right away to prevent water spots.

Our full guide on [how to clean stainless steel pans](/blog/how-to-clean-stainless-steel-pans/) covers burnt food, stains and discoloration step by step. Skip steel wool on the polished outside, since it leaves scratches.

## Which One Should You Buy?

Start with how you cook, not with the brand.

- **You cook sauces, fish and delicate pan work most nights, and budget is not tight:** get the Copper Core 10-Piece Set.
- **You live alone or as a couple and want the copper build:** get the Copper Core 5-Piece Set.
- **You want to try Copper Core before committing:** buy the 12-Inch Fry Pan or the 3-Quart Sauté Pan.
- **You cook for four or more and have room to store it:** get the Copper Core 15-Piece Set.
- **You want great clad steel and a sensible price:** get the D3 10-Piece Set.
- **You like slow, steady heat for braising and do not care about quick response:** look at D5 in our [D3 vs D5 guide](/blog/all-clad-d3-vs-d5/).

One simple rule helps. If you cannot name a dish that went wrong because your pan reacted too slowly, you probably do not need Copper Core. D3 will cook everything you already make.

If you do go for Copper Core, buy the pieces you will use every week. Skip the set sizes that pad the box. The copper is worth the most in a fry pan and a sauté pan, and the least in a stockpot.
