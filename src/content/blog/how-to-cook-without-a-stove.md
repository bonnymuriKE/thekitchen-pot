Never trust one appliance to do a stove's whole job. That is rule one when you cook without a stove, whether the range died or the dorm never had one.

A burner boils, simmers and fries. An oven roasts and bakes. No plug-in box does all five well, but two of them chosen with care will cover a normal week of meals. Below is which appliance cooks what, how to make pasta, rice and eggs with no burner, and how to run it all safely.

**The short version:** Pair a portable induction burner with a rice cooker, a small multi-cooker or an air fryer. Let a kettle and a microwave handle the quick jobs. Every heat appliance lives on open counter with air around it, one per circuit, never inside a cabinet. Read your lease or dorm rules before buying anything that looks like a hot plate.

## How Can I Cook Without a Stove?

Split the stove's jobs across small appliances. Give the boiling and searing to one, the slow, wet cooking to another, and the crisping to a third if you need it.

If your kitchen still has a stove but only two working burners, you may not need all this. Our guide on [how to cook a full meal with only two burners](/blog/how-to-cook-a-full-meal-with-only-two-burners/) covers that case. This guide is for when the stove is gone, broken or banned.

## What Appliances Replace a Stove?

This is the map we would hand a friend moving into a studio with no range. Each appliance has a main job and a few side jobs.

| Appliance | Replaces | Best at | Also handles | Weak spot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Portable induction burner | Stove burners | Boiling, searing, simmering | Stir-fry, eggs, pasta, sauces | Needs magnetic pans |
| Rice cooker | A pot on a low burner | Rice and grains | Oats, soup, steamed vegetables, one-pot meals | No browning |
| Multi-cooker (pressure cooker) | Stockpot and slow cooker | Beans, stews, tough meat, fast rice | Pasta, eggs, yogurt | Small batches only in a 3-quart model |
| Electric kettle | A pot of boiling water | Hot water in 3 to 4 minutes | Oats, couscous, instant noodles, blanching | Water only; never cook food in it |
| Microwave | Saucepan for small jobs | Reheating, potatoes, eggs | Steamed fish and vegetables, mug meals | Uneven heat, no browning |
| Air fryer | Oven for small portions | Crisp chicken, potatoes, frozen foods | Roasted vegetables, salmon, reheating | Small basket |
| Toaster oven | Full oven | Baking, roasting, broiling | Toast, sheet-pan meals for one | Slower than an air fryer |
| Electric skillet or griddle | Frying pan on a burner | Pancakes, eggs, burgers, stir-fry | Braising with the lid on | Takes counter space |

Most people do not need all eight. Start with the pair that matches how you cook, shown near the end.

## Portable Induction Burners: The Closest Thing to a Real Stove

### Check Your Pans With a Magnet

Induction only works with magnetic pans. Hold a fridge magnet to the bottom of each pan. If it sticks firmly, the pan works. Cast iron, enameled cast iron and most clad stainless steel pass. Plain aluminum, copper and glass do not.

If you need one good pan to start with, the [Misen 10-inch stainless steel pan](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=92257&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=how-to-cook-without-a-stove&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fmisen.com%2Fproducts%2Fstainless-steel-pan-10-inch) has a magnetic base and sears evenly on a single burner. For more options, see our [best cookware for an induction cooktop](/blog/best-cookware-for-induction-cooktop/) picks. If you already own pans that fail the magnet test, [how to use non-induction cookware on an induction cooktop](/blog/how-to-use-non-induction-cookware-on-induction-cooktop/) explains the workarounds.

### Induction vs Coil Hot Plates

Skip the cheap coil hot plate. The coil stays red hot after you turn it off, and it is the appliance most leases and dorms ban by name. Induction stops heating the moment you lift the pan.

A rice cooker is the most forgiving no-stove appliance. You add food and water, press one button, and it switches to keep-warm when the water is gone. Nothing burns while you are in the other room.

## Small Multi-Cookers: Stews, Beans and Fast Grains

A multi-cooker is an electric pressure cooker that also slow cooks, steams, sautés and keeps food warm. Pressure cooking raises the boiling point of water inside a sealed pot, so food cooks much faster. Dried beans that take hours in a pot are done in under an hour.

For a small kitchen, get the [Instant Pot Duo Mini 3-Quart](https://www.amazon.com/Instant-Pot-Duo-Mini-Programmable/dp/B06Y1YD5W7/?tag=kitchenpot-20). Three quarts makes about four servings of stew, enough for one person with leftovers, and it takes up far less room than the 6-quart size. It runs on about 700 watts, so it is easy on your circuit too. If the controls are new to you, start with [what is an Instant Pot](/blog/what-is-an-instant-pot/) and [how many watts an Instant Pot uses](/blog/how-many-watts-does-an-instant-pot-use/).

A multi-cooker shines for:

- Dried beans and lentils with no soaking.
- Stews, curries and pulled meat from cheap, tough cuts.
- Hard-boiled eggs that peel easily.
- Steamed vegetables in a basket above water. Our [best steamer basket for the Instant Pot](/blog/best-steamer-basket-for-instant-pot/) list has ones that fit.

The one thing it cannot do is crisp or brown well. Pair it with an air fryer or toaster oven for that.

## Electric Kettles: The Fastest Hot Water

An electric kettle boils water faster than almost any burner and shuts itself off. That makes it the starting point for a lot of no-stove meals.

We like the [BUYDEEM electric kettle with temperature control](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=101781&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=how-to-cook-without-a-stove&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fus.buydeem.com%2Fproducts%2Felectric-kettle-with-temperature-control). You can set it to a full boil for noodles or a lower heat for tea, and it holds that heat while you get the bowl ready. Read [how electric kettles work](/blog/how-do-electric-kettles-work/) to see why they beat a pot for speed.

What a kettle can cook, with no other heat:

- **Couscous:** pour 1 cup of boiling water over 1 cup of couscous, cover and wait 5 minutes.
- **Instant oats:** boiling water over oats, covered, for 2 to 3 minutes.
- **Instant noodles and rice noodles:** cover with boiling water and let them sit for the time on the package.
- **Blanched greens:** pour boiling water over spinach or peas in a colander.

Two hard rules. Only ever put water in the kettle, never food, eggs or noodles. Food burns onto the heating plate and can ruin the kettle. And descale it every month or two if you have hard water, as we explain in [how to clean an electric kettle](/blog/how-to-clean-an-electric-kettle/).

### Microwave Rules That Keep Food Safe

- **Cover the food** with a vented lid or a plate. Trapped steam cooks it more evenly.
- **Stir or turn it halfway through.** Microwaves leave cold spots.
- **Let it stand for a minute or two** after cooking. The heat keeps spreading.
- **Check meat and leftovers with a thermometer.** Reheat leftovers to 165°F all the way through.
- **Only use containers marked microwave-safe.** Never use metal or foil.

## Air Fryers and Toaster Ovens: Your Oven Replacement

### Air Fryers

An air fryer is a small countertop oven with a strong fan. The moving hot air crisps food fast, so it beats a microwave for anything you want brown and crunchy.

For one person, the [Cosori Lite 2.1-Quart Air Fryer](https://www.amazon.com/Cosori-Lite-Small-Air-Fryer-Gray/dp/B0BDFRZX3F/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is the right size. Air fryer jobs with no oven:

- Chicken thighs at 380°F for about 20 to 25 minutes, until they read 165°F inside.
- Salmon at 390°F for about 7 to 9 minutes.
- Potato wedges or [frozen french fries](/blog/how-to-cook-frozen-french-fries-in-an-air-fryer/) at 400°F, shaken halfway.

## How to Cook Pasta Without a Stove

Pasta is the question we hear most. There are three good ways, plus one quick trick.

### In the Microwave

1. Put 2 ounces of short pasta, about one serving, in a large microwave-safe bowl.
2. Cover it with water by 2 inches and add a pinch of salt.
3. Microwave on high for the package time plus 3 minutes, uncovered.
4. Stir every 3 to 4 minutes so it does not clump.
5. Taste a piece, then drain.

Use a bowl twice as big as you think. Pasta water foams and boils over in a small one. Set the bowl on a plate to catch spills.

### In a Rice Cooker

Add pasta, a jar of sauce and enough water to just cover everything. Run it on the white rice setting, stirring once halfway. When it switches to warm, the pasta is done and the sauce has thickened.

### In a Multi-Cooker

Add pasta and enough water or broth to just cover it. Pressure cook for half the package time, then release the pressure right away. Stir in sauce and cheese. One pot, no draining.

### The Kettle Shortcut

Thin rice noodles and instant noodles only need a soak in water from the kettle. Regular dried pasta does not cook through this way, so use one of the methods above for that.

## How to Cook Rice Without a Stove

A rice cooker is the easiest way, but you have options.

| Method | White rice ratio (rice to water) | Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rice cooker | Use the lines in the pot | 25 to 40 minutes | Hands-off; holds it warm |
| Multi-cooker | 1 to 1 | 4 minutes at pressure plus 10 minutes natural release | Fastest hands-off way |
| Microwave | 1 to 2 | 10 minutes on high, then 5 minutes at half power | Use a large covered bowl; let it rest 5 minutes |
| Induction burner | 1 to 1.5 | 15 to 18 minutes on low, covered | Same as a stove |

Rinse white rice first in a fine strainer until the water runs mostly clear. That washes off loose starch and stops gummy rice.

Microwave power varies a lot, so treat those times as a starting point. If the rice is still firm, add a splash of water, cover and give it 1 to 2 more minutes.

Cooked rice needs care, too. Cool leftovers fast and get them in the fridge within 2 hours, then eat them within 3 to 4 days. To keep single servings longer, follow our guide on [how to freeze single portions](/blog/how-to-freeze-single-portions/).

## How to Cook Eggs Without a Stove

Eggs are the easiest protein to cook with no burner. Every appliance in this guide can make them.

- **Scrambled in the microwave:** whisk 2 eggs with a splash of milk in a greased mug. Microwave for 30 seconds, stir, then cook in 20-second bursts until just set, about 60 to 90 seconds total.
- **Poached in the microwave:** crack an egg into the poacher or a mug with 1/2 cup of water. Cover and cook for about 1 minute.
- **Hard-boiled in a multi-cooker:** 1 cup of water, eggs on the rack, 5 minutes at pressure, 5 minutes natural release, 5 minutes in ice water.
- **Steamed in a rice cooker:** set eggs in the steam tray while the rice cooks.
- **Fried on induction or an electric skillet:** same as a stove.

Never microwave an egg in its shell. Steam builds up inside and it can burst, sometimes after you take it out.

If you eat eggs most days, the [Dash Rapid Egg Cooker](https://www.amazon.com/Dash-Rapid-Egg-Cooker/dp/B00DDXWFY0/?tag=kitchenpot-20) earns its spot. It hard-boils up to 6 eggs, poaches with its tray and shuts off with a buzzer when they are done. ## Can You Cook in a Dorm or Studio Without a Kitchen?

Yes, but the rules come first.

### Check Your Lease or Housing Rules

Most dorms ban hot plates, toaster ovens and anything with an open heating element. Many allow a microwave, a mini fridge and sometimes a small rice cooker or kettle. Read the list before you buy, because a banned appliance can be taken away or cost you a fine.

Leases vary. Some ban "hot plates" by name, and a landlord may count an induction burner as one. If your lease is unclear, ask your landlord in writing and keep the answer. If your stove is broken, report it in writing too. A working stove is often part of what you are paying for.

### Set Up a Cooking Corner

In a studio, pick one stretch of counter or a sturdy table near an outlet as your cooking zone. Keep it clear of paper, curtains and anything that can melt. Our [studio apartment kitchen ideas](/blog/studio-apartment-kitchen-ideas/) and [renter-friendly kitchen upgrades](/blog/renter-friendly-kitchen-upgrades/) show how to carve out space without changing the walls. If counter space is the problem, see [how to make more counter space in a small kitchen](/blog/make-more-counter-space-in-a-small-kitchen/).

Put away appliances you do not use daily only once they are fully cool. Our guide on [how to store small appliances in a small kitchen](/blog/store-small-appliances-in-a-small-kitchen/) shows where.

## Run Your Appliances Safely

Heat appliances in a small room need more care than a built-in stove. These are our rules.

### Keep Them on an Open Counter

Run every heat appliance on an open, flat, heat-safe surface with several inches of air on all sides. Follow the clearance in the manual. Never run one inside a cabinet, on a closed shelf, on a lower shelf of a rolling cart or directly under upper cabinets. Air fryers and toaster ovens vent hot air out of the back and top, and that heat builds up fast in a closed space.

### One Heat Appliance per Circuit

Most kitchen outlets sit on a 15-amp or 20-amp circuit. A 1,800-watt induction burner uses about 15 amps on its own, the full load of a 15-amp circuit. Add a kettle or toaster oven to the same circuit and the breaker trips, or worse, the wires overheat.

- Run only one high-wattage appliance per circuit at a time.
- Plug heat appliances straight into a wall outlet. Never use an extension cord or a power strip.
- If you do not know which outlets share a circuit, flip one breaker off and see which outlets go dead.

Lower-wattage appliances help. A 700-watt Instant Pot Mini or a 1,300-watt induction burner gives you more room on a shared circuit. Our [most energy-efficient small kitchen appliances](/blog/most-energy-efficient-small-kitchen-appliances/) guide lists the power draw of common picks.

### Handle Smoke and Steam

With no range hood, open a window and run a fan when you sear or air fry. Keep the smoke alarm working, and never pull its battery. Our [small kitchen ventilation guide](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) covers ways to clear cooking smells without a hood.

## Common Mistakes When Cooking Without a Stove

- **Buying a coil hot plate.** It is slow, stays hot and is often banned. Get induction instead.
- **Stacking appliances on one circuit.** Kettle plus burner plus toaster oven trips the breaker. Run one at a time.
- **Using an extension cord.** Plug heat appliances straight into the wall.
- **Running an air fryer under a cabinet.** The vented heat can damage the cabinet. Pull it out onto open counter.
- **Cooking food in the kettle.** Water only. Food burns onto the plate.
- **Using a tiny bowl for microwave pasta.** It boils over. Use a bowl twice the size you need.
- **Skipping the thermometer.** Small appliances heat unevenly. Check chicken and leftovers for 165°F.
- **Buying every gadget at once.** Start with two appliances, cook for a few weeks, then fill the real gaps.
