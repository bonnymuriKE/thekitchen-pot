## Propane vs Electric vs Offset Smokers

**Propane** smokers run anywhere and hold steady low heat. They are the best middle ground between ease and flavor.

**Electric** smokers are the easiest of all, but need an outlet and give milder smoke. Our guides on [how to use an electric smoker](/blog/how-to-use-an-electric-smoker/) and [how to use a Masterbuilt electric smoker](/blog/how-to-use-masterbuilt-electric-smoker/) cover them.

**Offset** smokers burn wood or charcoal and give the deepest flavor and bark. They also need the most attention. See the [best offset smoker for beginners](/blog/best-offset-smoker-for-beginners/) if you want to try one.

If you already own a gas grill, a [smoker box for a gas grill](/blog/best-smoker-box-for-gas-grills/) adds smoke flavor for very little money. Here is [how to use a smoker box on a gas grill](/blog/how-to-use-a-smoker-box-on-a-gas-grill/).

## What to Look for in a Propane Smoker

**Temperature control.** A thermostat like Masterbuilt's ThermoTemp saves the most effort. Manual valves work fine but need checking.

**Cooking area.** 600 square inches suits a small family. 900 or more suits big batches and parties.

**Doors.** Two doors let you add chips and water without losing heat from the meat. Most picks here have them.

**Burner.** Dual burners spread heat more evenly. Cast iron burners last longer than thin steel.

**Build.** Thicker steel holds heat better in wind and cold. Check the door seals; leaks waste gas and smoke.

**Size.** Measure your patio. Some smokers are over 31 inches deep.

## How to Use a Propane Smoker

1. **Season it first.** Run the empty smoker at high heat for an hour or two to burn off factory oils.
2. **Fill the water pan.** Water stabilizes temperature and adds moisture.
3. **Add wood.** Fill the chip tray with chips or chunks. Soaking is optional; dry wood smokes sooner.
4. **Preheat.** Bring the smoker to 225°F to 250°F before adding meat.
5. **Load the meat.** Put fatty cuts on lower racks so drips baste what is below.
6. **Monitor with a probe.** Door gauges can be off. A good probe from our [meat thermometer for smoking](/blog/best-meat-thermometer-for-smoking/) guide reads both the meat and the cabinet.
7. **Add chips as needed.** Most chip trays need a refill every 45 to 90 minutes.

## Propane Safety and Tank Tips

Always set up the smoker outdoors, on a flat surface, well away from walls. Never use it in a garage or enclosed porch.

Check the hose and regulator for leaks with soapy water before each season. Bubbles mean a leak.

## What to Smoke First

Start with forgiving cuts.

- **Pork shoulder:** Very hard to ruin. Smoke at 225°F until it pulls apart.
- **Chicken thighs:** Done in a few hours and stay juicy.
- **Sausages:** Ready in about two hours. For indoor days, see [how to cook sausages in the oven](/blog/how-to-cook-sausages-in-the-oven/).
- **Salmon:** Smoke low for a delicate flavor. A good [electric fillet knife](/blog/best-electric-fillet-knife/) makes trimming easier.
- **Corn and vegetables:** Smoke alongside the meat. See [how to grill corn on the cob](/blog/how-to-grill-corn-on-the-cob/) for a finish on the grill.

Brisket is the big prize, but it takes practice. Try it after a few easier cooks.

## Troubleshooting Common Propane Smoker Problems

**It runs too hot.** Even on the lowest setting, some smokers run hot on summer days. Open the top vent fully and keep the water pan full. Shade from the afternoon sun helps too.

**It will not get hot enough.** Wind is the usual cause. Turn the smoker so the wind hits its back, or set up a windbreak. A low tank or a blocked burner port can also starve the flame.

**The wood chips catch fire.** The chip tray is too close to the flame, or the chips are very dry. Use chunks instead, or wrap chips in a foil pouch with a few holes.

**There is not enough smoke flavor.** Gas smokers give a lighter smoke than wood fires. Add chips early, while the meat is cold, because cold meat takes on smoke best. Chunks give a steadier smoke than chips.

**The door gauge disagrees with your probe.** Trust the probe. Door gauges measure air near the door, which runs cooler or hotter than the grates.

**Grease flare-ups.** Empty the grease cup before every cook. Line the water pan with foil for easier cleanup.

## Cleaning and Storing Your Smoker

After each cook, let the smoker cool. Empty the water pan and grease cup, and scrape the racks.

Every few cooks, wash the racks with hot soapy water. Brush loose soot from the walls, but leave the seasoned coating in place.

Before winter, disconnect the tank and store it upright outdoors, never indoors. Cover the smoker to keep rain and pests out of the burner.
