A range hood that roars gets switched off early, and then it clears nothing. Here is how to make a range hood quieter, so it becomes a fan you actually run.

Most loud hoods are not broken. They are dirty, loose, starved of air or pushing through the wrong duct. The fixes below go from free and five minutes to a weekend job, so start at the top and stop when the noise does.

**Quick answer:** Turn the power off at the breaker, then clean or replace the grease filters and make sure they sit flat. Tighten every screw you can reach, and seal duct joints with foil tape. If it is still loud, check for flexible or undersized duct, too many bends and a sticky backdraft flap. Run the fan on a lower speed from the moment you start cooking. If none of that works, a hood with baffle filters, a lower sone rating or a remote blower is the real cure.

## Sones and Decibels: How Loud Is Your Range Hood?

Hood noise is usually given in sones. A sone measures how loud a sound feels to a person. The scale is simple: 2 sones sounds twice as loud as 1 sone, and 4 sones twice as loud as 2. Decibels (dB) measure sound pressure on a curved scale. Every extra 10 dB sounds about twice as loud.

The two do not convert perfectly, because sones also account for pitch. As a working guide, 1 sone is about 40 dB, and each doubling of sones adds about 10 dB.

| Sones | About dB | Sounds like | What it means at the stove |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 to 1 | 30 to 40 | A quiet refrigerator humming | You barely notice it is on |
| 1.5 | 45 | A quiet office | Easy to talk and listen to music |
| 2 | 50 | Light rain on a window | Quiet for a range hood |
| 3 | 55 | Normal face-to-face talking | Normal for a mid speed |
| 4 | 60 | A running dishwasher | You raise your voice a little |
| 6 | 65 | A busy restaurant | Tiring after 20 minutes |
| 8 | 70 | A vacuum cleaner across the room | Most people turn it off early |

A hood often lists only one sone number, usually the low speed, and a dB rating is not directly comparable to a sone rating.

### How to Measure Your Hood's Noise

You do not need lab gear. A free sound meter app on your phone gives a fair reading for comparing before and after. Stand where your head is when you cook, about 2 feet from the front edge of the hood. Write down the reading on each speed with the kitchen quiet.

Repeat after each fix. A drop of 10 dB sounds about half as loud.

## Why Is My Range Hood So Loud?

Every hood makes some sound. Trouble starts when something makes the fan work harder or lets parts shake. These are the common causes, roughly from most to least likely.

- **Greasy or clogged filters.** A filter thick with grease chokes the airflow. The motor strains, the pitch rises, and air squeals through the open spots that are left.
- **Loose filters, screws or panels.** Filters that do not latch fully buzz against the frame. A loose light cover, front panel or mounting screw rattles at certain speeds.
- **A dirty or loose fan wheel.** Grease builds up unevenly on the blades, which throws the wheel off balance. It wobbles, hums and shakes the hood.
- **Worn motor bearings.** Bearings let the motor shaft spin smoothly. As they wear, you hear a growl, a squeal or grinding that gets worse over months.
- **An undersized or crushed duct.** Too much air through too small a pipe moves fast and loud, like blowing through a straw.
- **Too many elbows.** Each tight bend slows the air and adds turbulence, which you hear as a roar.
- **Flexible duct.** The ridges inside foil flex duct stir the air up. It is louder and weaker than smooth metal pipe.
- **A stuck backdraft damper.** This is the flap that stops outside air coming back in. If grease glues it half shut, it flutters, rattles or whistles.
- **Running too high a speed.** Top speed on almost any hood is loud. Many people use it far more often than the cooking needs.
- **A recirculating hood with charcoal filters.** These hoods push air through a dense carbon filter and blow it back into the room, often right at your face. They sound louder at the same speed as a ducted hood.

If your hood vents straight out the back wall or recirculates, the duct items may not apply. Our [guide to ductless range hoods](/blog/6-best-ductless-range-hood/) explains how recirculating models move air.

## Safety First: Power Off at the Breaker

Before you open anything beyond the grease filters, switch the hood off at the breaker panel, not just at its own buttons. Many hoods are hardwired, and the light and fan circuits stay live while the switch is off. Test that both the fan and the light are dead before you touch screws or wiring.

Let the hood and the cooktop cool for at least 30 minutes. Grease on a warm hood is slippery and the edges inside are sharp sheet metal, so wear work gloves. Never spray cleaner into the motor housing or onto wiring.

### A Note for Renters

Cleaning filters, latching them properly and tightening screws you can reach are normal upkeep in a rental. Opening the wiring box, removing the motor or changing duct inside walls is the landlord's job. Send a short note describing the noise and when it happens, and ask for a repair. ## How to Make a Range Hood Quieter, Step by Step

Work through these in order. The early steps are cheap and fix most loud hoods.

### Step 1: Clean or Replace the Grease Filters

Pull the filters and hold them up to a light. If you cannot see through most of the mesh, they are too dirty. Soak them in very hot water with dish soap and a cup of baking soda for 15 minutes, then scrub with a soft brush. For thick, old grease, a citrus degreaser such as [Zep Heavy-Duty Citrus Degreaser](https://www.amazon.com/ZEP-Heavy-Duty-Citrus-Degreaser-ZUCIT32/dp/B001PNEVK6/?tag=kitchenpot-20) cuts through it faster than soap alone.

Filters that are bent, torn or still sticky after cleaning should be replaced. The [Broan-NuTone Open Mesh Aluminum Grease Filter](https://www.amazon.com/HPFA-Type-Aluminum-Replacement-Grease-Filter/dp/B07BT5H4XP/?tag=kitchenpot-20) fits many Broan under-cabinet hoods, so match the size in your manual first. How often to wash them depends on how you cook, and our [range hood filter cleaning schedule](/blog/how-often-to-clean-a-range-hood-filter/) lays that out.

### Step 2: Seat the Filters and Tighten Everything

Put the filters back so every latch clicks and the filter sits flat in its frame. A filter that is a quarter inch off will buzz against the metal. If the clips are bent, gently bend them back with your fingers.

Then, with the power off, go around the hood with a screwdriver:

1. Tighten the screws holding the hood to the cabinet or wall.
2. Tighten the light cover and any front or bottom panels.
3. Check the screws on the motor mount, if you can reach them through the filter opening.
4. Look for wires or a loose foil strip touching the fan housing.

A thin strip of foil tape or a small felt pad between two metal parts that touch can stop a stubborn buzz. Do not put padding near the motor or the fan wheel.

### Step 3: Clean the Fan Wheel

On most under-cabinet hoods, the fan wheel sits behind a cover just above the filters. With the power off, take the cover off and look at the blades. If they wear a coat of brown grease, take the wheel off, often with a single nut or a set screw, and soak it the same way as the filters.

Before you put it back, spin it by hand. It should turn freely and not rub the housing. Tighten the set screw firmly onto the flat side of the motor shaft. A loose wheel is one of the most common causes of a rhythmic knock.

### Step 4: Seal Duct Joints with Foil Tape

Air leaking from a duct joint whistles, and loose joints rattle against the framing. Where you can reach the duct, in a cabinet above the hood or in the attic, wrap each joint with real foil tape. [Nashua 322 Multi-Purpose Foil Tape](https://www.amazon.com/NASHUA-TAPE-617001B-Nashua-Multi-Purpose/dp/B00LS3H2PE/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is the kind made for metal duct. Press it down hard and smooth out every wrinkle with the back of a spoon.

Skip cloth duct tape. The glue dries out with heat and grease and the tape peels off within a year or two.

### Step 5: Replace Flex Duct with Rigid Duct of the Right Size

This is the fix that makes the biggest difference on most noisy ducted hoods. Foil flex duct is loud and sags into dips that collect grease. Most building codes require smooth metal duct for a range hood anyway.

Swap it for smooth galvanized pipe, such as [Imperial 6-Inch x 24-Inch Galvanized Steel Vent Pipe](https://www.amazon.com/Imperial-Manufacturing-Pipe-Galvanized-Steel/dp/B000BPGM3I/?tag=kitchenpot-20), and match the diameter to the hood outlet. Never shrink the duct below the size of the hood's outlet. Going one size up, such as a 6-inch outlet into 7-inch pipe with an adapter, lowers air speed and noise.

Keep the run short and straight. Use two 45-degree bends instead of one sharp 90 where you can. Each 90-degree elbow adds about as much resistance as 10 to 15 feet of straight pipe, so three elbows can make a short run behave like a long one. For a small kitchen, that ducting work is a sensible add-on to [a budget kitchen remodel](/blog/small-kitchen-remodel-ideas-on-a-budget/).

### Step 6: Fix the Backdraft Damper

The backdraft damper is a light metal flap, sometimes two, at the top of the hood or in the cap on the outside wall or roof. Air from the fan pushes it open, and gravity or a spring closes it when the fan stops.

With the power off, reach up through the hood outlet or look at the outside cap. Clean any grease from the hinge with degreaser and make sure the flap swings fully open and closes fully. If it is bent, warped or stuck, replace the cap. The [Broan-NuTone 641 Wall Cap for 6-Inch Round Duct](https://www.amazon.com/Broan-641-Round-Range-Ventilation/dp/B00QW2BDYW/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has its own damper and fits the most common duct size.

### Step 7: Run a Lower Speed, Earlier

Turn the hood on low a few minutes before you start cooking. That sets up a steady draft, so the hood catches smoke as it rises instead of chasing a cloud later on high. Leave it running for 10 to 15 minutes after you finish.

A few cooking habits let a low speed do the job:

- Cook on the back burners. They sit right under the hood, where capture is strongest. That matters even more if you [cook a full meal on only two burners](/blog/how-to-cook-a-full-meal-with-only-two-burners/).
- Put lids on pots. Less steam means less work for the fan, and a [stockpot with a tight lid](/blog/best-stockpot-with-a-lid/) keeps pasta water from fogging the room.
- Save the top speed for searing and stir-frying. A smoking-hot [wok on an electric stove](/blog/best-wok-for-electric-stove/) needs it. A simmering sauce does not.
- Move the greasiest frying off the stove when you can. One of our [budget air fryer picks](/blog/best-air-fryers-under-100/) makes far less airborne grease.

### Step 8: Fix Whistling with Makeup Air

If the hood whistles on high and goes quiet when you crack a window, the house is short of air. A strong hood pulls air out faster than a tight home lets it back in, so air screams through door gaps and the filters. Open a nearby window an inch or two while you cook. Many building codes require a makeup air system for hoods over 400 CFM, and an HVAC contractor can add one.

## Range Hood Noise by Type: A Troubleshooting Table

The kind of sound tells you where to look. Match what you hear to the row below.

| Noise | Most likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Rattle or buzz | Loose filter, loose screw or panel, duct joint shaking | Reseat filters, tighten screws, tape and strap the duct |
| Rattle with the fan off | Damper flapping in the wind | Clean the flap, or fit a better wall or roof cap |
| Whine or high whir | Clogged filter, duct too small, motor straining | Clean filters, check duct size, lower the speed |
| Whistle | Air squeezing through a gap, a half-shut damper, no makeup air | Free the damper, seal leaks, crack a window |
| Steady loud hum | Greasy, unbalanced fan wheel, or a motor near the end | Clean the wheel; if the hum stays, call for a motor check |
| Hum or buzz on low only | A failing speed control or capacitor | Have an electrician or appliance tech test it |
| Grinding or squeal | Worn motor bearings, wheel rubbing the housing | Stop using it and replace the motor or the hood |
| Rhythmic knock | Loose fan wheel or a bent blade | Tighten the set screw, replace a bent wheel |
| Whooshing roar | Flex duct, too many elbows, top speed | Rigid duct, fewer bends, use a lower speed |

### When It Is the Motor

Grinding, a smell of hot plastic, or a fan that slows down, speeds up or stops are signs of a motor problem. Most modern hood motors have sealed bearings, so there is nothing to oil. A replacement motor from the maker can work on a good hood. On an older, low-cost hood, a new motor often costs a large part of a new hood, and replacing the hood is the better move.

Treat a burning smell as urgent. Turn the hood off at the breaker and leave it off until it has been checked.

## Range Hood Rattling and Vibration

Rattling deserves its own look because it often comes from parts outside the hood. Feel the cabinet above the hood while the fan runs. If the cabinet shakes, the hood is not fixed firmly to it, or the duct is touching the cabinet walls.

Fixes that work:

- Add a screw or two through the hood's top flange into the cabinet bottom, using the existing holes.
- Pack a little mineral wool around the duct where it passes through a cabinet so metal does not touch wood.
- Strap long duct runs to the framing every few feet with metal hanger strap.
- Check that nothing in the cabinet above, such as cans or baking sheets, is sitting on the duct.

## Why Is My Range Hood Making Noise When It Is Off?

A tap, flutter or clank with the fan off is almost always the damper. On a windy day, gusts push the flap open and it slaps shut again. 
Clean and free the flap first. If it still flaps, fit a wall or roof cap with a better seal, or add an inline backdraft damper with a light spring in the duct. A whistling sound in wind usually means the cap has no flap at all or the flap is stuck open. That also lets in cold air and bugs, and our tips to [keep pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/) start with gaps like this one.

## Recirculating Hoods: How to Quiet a Ductless Hood

A ductless hood has no outside duct to fix, so the work is all in the hood. Clean the metal grease filter and replace the charcoal filter on schedule. A charcoal filter that is full of grease is as hard to breathe through as a wet towel, and the fan screams trying.

For many Broan models, the [Broan-NuTone 41F Replacement Charcoal Filter](https://www.amazon.com/Broan-NuTone-Ductless-Carbon-Charcoal-Kitchen/dp/B00DM8KL0G/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is the standard fit. Check the deflector at the front or top of the hood too. If it points the air straight at your face, the hood will feel much louder than it is.

If your hood is an over-the-range microwave vent, the same filter and speed advice applies; our [over-the-range microwave guide](/blog/7-best-over-the-range-microwaves/) explains those vents. For the wider fight against smoke and smells in a small home, see our [small-kitchen ventilation guide](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/).

## Upgrade: Inline or Remote Blower

When the hood itself is the noise source, moving the motor away from the kitchen is the most effective fix. An inline blower sits in the duct run, often in the attic or a soffit. A remote blower mounts on the outside wall or roof. The hood above the stove then holds only filters and lights.

You still hear the air moving, but the motor hum and whine mostly disappear from the kitchen. This works only with a hood or insert made to run with an external blower, so it is usually part of a replacement or remodel. It needs a dedicated electrical run and a firm mount, so hire a licensed electrician and an installer who works with ventilation.

## Choosing a Quieter Range Hood

If your hood is old, underpowered for your cooking or loud even when clean, a new hood makes more sense than chasing noise. Look at these features, in this order.

### Look at the Sone Rating at the Speed You Will Use

Ask what the hood does on the speed you will run most nights, not just the low or high number. A hood that lists 1.5 sones on its normal setting will feel calm. One that lists 7 sones on high will be loud when you need it.

The [Broan-NuTone GLA2303SS](https://www.amazon.com/Broan-NuTone-GLA2303SS/dp/B0DHF5CVQP/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is a good example: it runs at 1.5 sones on its normal speed, which is quiet enough to talk over. The [Hauslane Chef Series UC-PS18](https://www.amazon.com/Hauslane-Chef-Series-UC-PS18/dp/B06XWFPJLH/?tag=kitchenpot-20) takes a different approach with six speeds, so low-heat cooking stays near 1.5 sones and only hard searing pushes it louder.

### Choose Baffle Filters

Baffle filters are rows of angled stainless steel channels. Grease hits the channels and drains away while air flows around them. Because air moves around the baffles instead of through fine mesh, the sound is smoother. They also do not clog the way mesh does, so the fan stays at its normal pitch between cleanings. The [Cosmo COS-QS75](https://www.amazon.com/Cosmo-COS-QS75/dp/B013JVO1U6/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is an under-cabinet hood built around baffle filters.

### Go Bigger and Run It Slower

A hood that is wider than the cooktop catches more smoke on a lower speed, and a lower speed is a quieter speed. On a 30-inch cooktop, a 36-inch hood gives 3 inches of overhang on each side. The [Empava 36-Inch 500 CFM Ducted Under Cabinet Range Hood](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=114924&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=how-to-make-a-range-hood-quieter&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fempava.com%2Fproducts%2F36-inch-under-cabinet-range-hood-36rh14) is a solid choice for that setup.

The same idea applies to power. A hood with more capacity than you need, run on medium, is usually quieter than a smaller hood run flat out. That said, do not go far beyond what your duct can carry. CFM, the cubic feet of air a fan moves per minute, should match your cooktop, and our [under-cabinet hood sizing guide](/blog/best-under-cabinet-range-hood/) covers the numbers.

### Pick a Hood That Fits Your Duct

A new hood should match the duct you have, or you should plan to change the duct with it. A 6-inch round outlet is the most common size on 30-inch hoods. The [Empava 30-Inch 400 CFM Ducted Under Cabinet Range Hood](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=114924&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=how-to-make-a-range-hood-quieter&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fempava.com%2Fproducts%2F30-inch-ducted-range-hood-30rh07) uses a 6-inch duct, has three fan speeds and LED lights, and suits most apartment and condo cooktops. Its 400 CFM is enough for everyday cooking without needing the top speed every night.

If your stove sits against a bare wall with no cabinet above, a chimney hood is the swap. The [Empava 30-Inch 400 CFM Wall Mount Range Hood](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=114924&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=how-to-make-a-range-hood-quieter&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fempava.com%2Fproducts%2F30-inch-wall-mounted-range-hood-30rh05) has three speeds and runs under 65 dB, about the level of a lively conversation.

### Mount It at the Right Height

A hood mounted too high has to run faster to catch the same smoke. Most makers call for about 24 to 30 inches above the cooking surface, and your manual sets the exact range. Follow the manual's minimum for a gas cooktop; it exists for fire safety, not just noise. ## Mistakes That Keep a Range Hood Loud

- **Turning the fan up instead of on early.** High speed at the end of cooking is louder and catches less.
- **Using duct tape on the duct.** It peels off with heat and leaves leaks that whistle.
- **Shrinking the duct.** Squeezing a 7-inch outlet into 6-inch pipe adds noise and cuts suction.
- **Ignoring the fan wheel.** People wash filters for years and never see the greasy wheel behind them.
- **Leaving the cabinet above packed.** Stored items touching the duct or hood carry the vibration.
- **Buying by the low-speed sone number.** Check the number at the speed you will use.
- **Opening the hood with the power on.** Always cut power at the breaker first.

A hood running quietly also cuts the grease film on nearby surfaces. You will see it on [open kitchen shelves](/blog/open-shelving-small-kitchen-ideas/) and the [tops of the kitchen cabinets](/blog/what-to-put-on-top-of-kitchen-cabinets/), so a fan you actually run means less scrubbing. In a [studio apartment kitchen](/blog/studio-apartment-kitchen-ideas/), where the stove is a few steps from the couch, a quiet hood is the difference between using it and putting up with the smell.

## A Quieter Hood in a Small Kitchen

Small kitchens make noise feel louder, because hard surfaces bounce sound and you stand closer. In a [galley kitchen](/blog/galley-kitchen-ideas/) the hood is within arm's reach of anyone passing. With a [24-inch range](/blog/best-24-inch-range/), a 24-inch or 30-inch hood on medium will be quiet. A cooler cooking method also helps. An [induction cooktop](/blog/best-induction-cooktop-for-the-money/) puts less heat into the air than gas, so the hood can run slower. Running the fan at a lower speed also uses less power, one more small win from our list of ways to [reduce kitchen energy use in a small apartment](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/).

Some hood noise is not the hood at all. A pan that pops, ticks or hisses on a burner is telling you something else, and our guide on [what your cookware sounds are telling you](/blog/what-your-cookware-sounds-are-telling-you/) sorts those out. Grease that builds up around the burners also ends up in the filters, so keeping [gas stove burners and grates clean](/blog/how-to-clean-gas-stove-burners-and-grates/) helps the hood stay quiet longer.
