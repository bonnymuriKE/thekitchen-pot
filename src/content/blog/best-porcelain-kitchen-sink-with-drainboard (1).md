## Fireclay vs Porcelain Enamel vs Cast Iron

People use "porcelain sink" for three different things.

**Fireclay** is clay glazed and fired at very high heat for many hours. It is dense, heavy and resists chips and stains. Most built-in drainboard sinks today are fireclay.

**Porcelain enamel over cast iron** is a glass coating fused onto an iron sink. It is very heavy and very quiet. Kohler and American Standard are the best-known makers. Whatever the sink, a low-flow faucet aerator cuts hot water use, one easy way to [reduce kitchen energy use](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/).

**Porcelain enamel over steel**, like the Houzer, is a glass coating on a steel shell. It is lighter and cheaper, but the steel can flex and chip the enamel if hit hard.

All three clean the same way and look similar. Fireclay is the best choice for a drainboard, because the drainboard can be molded into the sink.

If you are weighing other materials, our guide to [what gauge stainless steel sink is best](/blog/what-gauge-stainless-steel-sink-is-best/) covers steel. The [best white undermount kitchen sinks](/blog/best-white-undermount-kitchen-sink/) covers more white sinks without drainboards.

## Built-In Drainboard vs Add-On Drainboard

A built-in drainboard is part of the sink. It slopes toward the bowl and never moves. It also takes up 10 to 12 inches of counter beside the bowl.

An add-on drainboard sits over the sink or on the counter. It costs much less and stores away. But it covers part of the bowl, or part of the counter, while you use it.

In a small kitchen, counter space matters most. A workstation sink like the Bocchi, or a roll-up rack over any sink, keeps the counter clear. Our ideas for [countertop organization in a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/) show how to free up more room.

If you have a long run of counter, a built-in drainboard looks better and works better. It also means one less thing to store.

## What to Consider Before You Buy

**Cabinet size.** A 36-inch drainboard sink needs a 36-inch or wider base. Apron-front models need a special farmhouse base cabinet.

**Bowl depth.** Nine inches is standard and hides dishes well. Deeper bowls are harder on your back.

**Drainboard side.** Reversible sinks let you put the drainboard on either side. Place it on the side away from the dishwasher, so you can load from the bowl.

**Weight.** Fireclay sinks weigh 60 to over 100 pounds. The cabinet needs extra support, and installation is a two-person job.

**Drain size.** Most use a 3.5-inch drain. Thick fireclay may need an extended flange for a garbage disposal.

**Accessories.** Grids protect the bowl floor from pots. Most makers sell them separately. Budget for a grid, strainer and drain.

## Installing a Fireclay Drainboard Sink

These sinks are heavy and must sit level, so most people hire a pro.

1. **Order the sink first.** Fireclay shrinks when fired, so each sink varies. Build or cut the cabinet to the sink you receive.
2. **Reinforce the cabinet.** Add a frame or cleats inside the base to carry the weight.
3. **Set it level.** Shim until the sink is level front to back. The drainboard needs its slope to drain toward the bowl.
4. **Seal it.** Run a bead of silicone where the sink meets the counter.
5. **Fit the drain.** Check whether you need an extended flange for the sink's thickness.

For a [drop-in or undermount porcelain sink](/blog/best-white-undermount-kitchen-sink/), the install is closer to a standard sink swap.

## Cleaning and Caring for a Porcelain Sink

Porcelain and fireclay glazes are tough but can dull with harsh cleaners.

- **Daily:** Rinse and wipe with a soft sponge and dish soap. Dry it to avoid water spots.
- **Weekly:** Scrub with a paste of baking soda and water. It lifts stains without scratching.
- **Metal marks:** Gray marks from pans rub off with a mild cream cleanser.
- **Avoid:** Steel wool, bleach left sitting, and abrasive powders.

A bottom grid stops heavy pans from marking or chipping the bowl. Be careful with cast iron. See our [Lodge cast iron skillet review](/blog/lodge-cast-iron-skillet-review/) and [Le Creuset cast iron skillet](/blog/le-creuset-cast-iron-skillet/) guide for how heavy those pans are.

## Making the Most of a Drainboard

A drainboard is not just for drying dishes.

- **Prep station:** Rinse vegetables on the drainboard so water runs into the sink. An [over-the-sink cutting board](/blog/best-over-the-sink-cutting-board/) adds more prep space.
- **Thawing:** Set frozen food on the drainboard so meltwater drains away.
- **Plant care:** Water houseplants on it and let them drip.
- **Knife care:** Dry knives here, then hang them on a [magnetic knife strip](/blog/best-magnetic-knife-strip/). Touch up the edge first with a good [knife sharpener](/blog/best-knife-sharpener/).
- **Meal prep:** Wash and drain a week of produce at once. Our [easy meal prep ideas for one person](/blog/easy-meal-prep-ideas-for-one-person-in-a-small-kitchen/) show how to batch it.

If you run a dishwasher too, keep it clean so it does its share. See [how to clean a dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/). A clean, dry sink area also helps [keep pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/).
