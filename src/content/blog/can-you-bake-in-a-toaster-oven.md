The timer still says eight minutes, but your first try to bake in a toaster oven already smells of scorched sugar. You open the door: cookies black underneath, raw in the middle.

That batch does not mean the little oven cannot bake. It can, and cookies, muffins, small cakes and even bread come out well. The heat just sits closer to the food, the temperature swings more and big pans do not fit. Below are the pan sizes, rack rules and temperature fixes that change the result.

**Quick answer:**

- Pick pans that leave about an inch of air on every side, and bake on the middle or lower rack, at least 2 inches below the top element.
- Trust an oven thermometer over the dial, drop 25°F for convection, and check for doneness at two-thirds of the recipe time.
- Run it only on open counter with nothing overhead.

## Can You Bake in a Toaster Oven?

You can. A toaster oven has heating elements at the top and bottom, a thermostat and a door. That is all baking needs. Most models have a "bake" setting that runs both elements at a steady, lower heat.

What changes is the scale. A typical 4-slice toaster oven holds one small pan on one rack, so you bake smaller batches with more care. For a solo cook or a couple, that is often a win. You heat a box the size of a microwave, not a cavity the size of a closet. ## How Toaster Oven Baking Differs From a Full Oven

### The Heat Is Close

In a wall oven, the food sits 6 inches or more from the elements. In a toaster oven, it may sit 2 or 3 inches away. That means tops and bottoms brown faster than the middle cooks. Cakes dome and crack. Cookie bottoms burn before the centers set.

The fix is distance and lower heat. Keep the food on the middle rack, use a light-colored metal pan, and drop the temperature 25°F for anything thick.

### The Temperature Swings

A small oven loses heat fast when you open the door, then the elements blast to catch up. Many toaster ovens also run hotter or cooler than the dial, sometimes by 25°F or more. The fix is an oven thermometer, covered below.

### Some Have a Fan

A convection toaster oven has a fan that moves hot air around the food. Moving air cooks faster and browns more evenly. Our rule: with the fan on, lower the recipe temperature by 25°F and check early. If your oven has no fan, keep the recipe temperature but still check early, because the elements are so close. Our guide on [how to bake cookies in a convection oven](/blog/how-to-bake-cookies-in-a-convection-oven/) goes deeper on fan baking.

## What Bakes Well in a Toaster Oven (and What Doesn't)

Most baking works if it fits. Here is our cheat sheet.

| Bakes well | Works with care | Skip it |
|---|---|---|
| Drop cookies, 4 to 8 at a time | 6-inch cakes (lower heat, foil tent) | Full 9-by-13 sheet cakes in a small oven |
| Muffins and cupcakes (6-cup pan) | Small sandwich loaves in mini pans | Tall angel food or bundt cakes |
| Biscuits and scones | Focaccia on a small sheet | Big boules in a Dutch oven (most will not fit) |
| Brownies in a small pan | Cheesecake in a 6-inch pan | Anything that touches the top element |
| Rolls, flatbread, pizza | Pie in a small dish (shield the crust) | Two pans at once on two racks |
| Granola, croutons, toasted nuts | Cream puffs and other puffed doughs | Glass dishes not rated for toaster ovens |

The pattern is simple. Short, flat, small items bake best. Tall items rise toward the top element and scorch.

## Do You Need Special Pans for a Toaster Oven?

No, you need pans that fit. Our rule: leave about 1 inch of air on every side of the pan. Hot air has to flow around it, or the edges burn and the middle stays raw. Then leave at least 2 inches above anything that rises.

Measure the rack side to side and front to back, plus the height up to the top element. Compare that with the pan's outside size, rim included.

| Pan | Outside size (about) | Fits in |
|---|---|---|
| Eighth sheet | 10 x 7 in | Nearly every 4-slice toaster oven |
| Quarter sheet | 13 x 9.5 in | Mid-size and large ovens (12-inch pizza and up) |
| 6-inch round cake pan | 6 to 7 in across, 2 in tall | Almost every toaster oven |
| Mini loaf pan | About 6 x 3.6 in | Almost every toaster oven, 2 side by side in most |
| Toaster oven 6-cup muffin pan | Sized for small ovens | Most 4-slice and larger ovens |
| 8-inch square pan | About 9 to 10 in with handles | Mid-size and large ovens only |
| 9-by-13 pan | About 10 x 15 in with handles | Large ovens sold as 9x13-ready |

## Rack Position Rules for Toaster Oven Baking

Rack position matters more in a toaster oven than in a full oven, because every slot changes the distance to an element by an inch or more. Most toaster ovens have two or three rack positions. Here is where we put each bake.

| What you're baking | Rack | Temperature change | Start checking |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cookies | Middle | Recipe temp (minus 25°F with fan) | 2 to 3 min early |
| Muffins and cupcakes | Middle or lower-middle | Minus 25°F | At two-thirds of the time |
| 6-inch cake | Lower-middle | Minus 25°F | At two-thirds of the time |
| Mini loaves and rolls | Lowest | Minus 25°F for enriched dough | At two-thirds of the time |
| Pizza and flatbread | Middle | Recipe temp | 2 to 3 min early |
| Granola and nuts | Middle | Minus 25°F | Stir and check every 5 min |

Two rules sit behind this table:

1. **Taller food goes lower.** Bread and cakes need room to rise, so they go on the bottom slot. That keeps the top at least 2 inches from the upper element.
2. **Flat food goes in the middle.** Cookies and pizza sit between both elements, so the top and bottom cook at the same speed.

Never bake on the very top slot. It is a broiling position. Toaster ovens put it there for melting cheese and browning toast, not for baking.

Turn the pan front to back halfway through every bake. Most toaster ovens run hotter at the back, where the door does not leak heat. One turn evens that out.

## Check the Real Temperature First

This is the step most people skip, and it explains most failed toaster oven bakes. If the dial says 350°F and the oven is really at 390°F, cookies burn on the bottom. If it is really at 320°F, cakes sink.

Buy a small oven thermometer and find out. The [KitchenAid 3-Inch Dial Oven Thermometer](https://www.amazon.com/KitchenAid-KQ903-Dial-Oven-Thermometer/dp/B07YP9G4Z7/?tag=kitchenpot-20) reads from 100°F to 600°F and can hang from the rack or stand on it. A 3-inch dial is easy to read through a small glass door.

Here is how to check your oven:

1. Put the thermometer in the center of the middle rack.
2. Set the oven to 350°F on bake, with the fan off if it has one.
3. Wait 15 minutes, then read the thermometer through the glass without opening the door.
4. Note the difference. If it reads 375°F, your oven runs 25°F hot.
5. Repeat at 425°F. Some ovens are accurate at one setting and off at another.

Write the result on tape inside a cabinet door, and set the dial higher or lower to match from then on.

Also, preheat every time. A toaster oven heats in about 5 to 10 minutes. Many models beep before the inside is truly hot, so give it a few more minutes after the signal for cakes and bread.

## Can You Bake Cookies in a Toaster Oven?

Yes, and cookies are the best place to start. Follow these steps:

1. Line an eighth or quarter sheet with parchment or a silicone mat.
2. Scoop 4 to 6 cookies onto an eighth sheet, or 6 to 8 onto a quarter sheet, about 2 inches apart.
3. Bake on the middle rack at the recipe temperature, or 25°F lower with the fan on.
4. Turn the pan halfway through.
5. Start checking 2 to 3 minutes before the recipe says. Pull them when the edges are set and the centers still look slightly soft.
6. Let them rest on the sheet for 2 minutes, then move them to a rack.

For the liner, a silicone mat cut to toaster oven size works best. The [WildCow Silicone Baking Mats Quarter Sheet, Set of 2](https://www.amazon.com/WildCow-Silicone-Quarter-Nonstick-Reusable/dp/B01I8POY28/?tag=kitchenpot-20) measure 11.75 by 8.25 inches, which drops straight into a quarter sheet. For an eighth sheet, trim one with kitchen scissors so it lies flat with no edge curling up.

Bake one sheet at a time. A second sheet on another rack blocks the heat. Instead, alternate two sheets, and never put raw dough on a hot one.

Freeze extra dough as scooped balls and bake a few at a time, adding 1 to 2 minutes. Our guide on [how to freeze single portions](/blog/how-to-freeze-single-portions/) covers the freezer side.

## Can You Bake a Cake in a Toaster Oven?

Yes, as long as you size it down. A full 9-inch layer is too wide for most toaster ovens and too close to the elements. A 6-inch cake fits almost any model and bakes evenly.

### Use 6-Inch Pans

The [Fat Daddio's Anodized Aluminum Round Cake Pans, 6 x 2 Inch](https://www.amazon.com/Fat-Daddios-Anodized-Aluminum-Round-Cake-Pans/dp/B01HDKCFAC/?tag=kitchenpot-20) come as a pair. Their light aluminum and straight sides bake evenly with no dark, crusty edges. A 6-inch round holds a little more than half the batter of an 8-inch round, so one standard 8-inch layer recipe fills one 6-inch pan with a little left for a cupcake.

Bake one pan at a time on the lower-middle rack. Fill it no more than two-thirds full. Then check the space above the pan: you want at least 2 inches between the rim and the top element.

### Keep the Top From Burning

Lower the heat by 25°F from the recipe. Then watch the top for the first half of the bake. If it browns before the center sets, lay a loose sheet of foil over the pan. Keep the foil at least an inch away from the elements and tuck nothing around the sides.

The cake is done when a toothpick in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs and the top springs back when you press it lightly. Let it cool in the pan for 10 minutes before you turn it out.

### Cupcakes and Muffins

For cupcakes and muffins, the [USA Pan Nonstick Toaster Oven 6-Cup Muffin Pan](https://www.amazon.com/USA-Pan-Toaster-Oven-Muffin-Pan/dp/B00TUHMT3K/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is built for small ovens. Six standard cups is half a normal batch, which is the right amount for most households of one or two. Fill each cup about three-quarters full and bake on the middle rack.

Cream-filled cakes and cheesecake go in the fridge once cool. Do not leave them out more than 2 hours. Keep the fridge at 40°F or below.

## Can You Bake Bread in a Toaster Oven?

Yes, if you bake small. A full 9-by-5 loaf usually rises too close to the top element. Split the dough into mini loaves, rolls or a flat bread instead.

### Mini Loaves

The [USA Pan Bakeware Nonstick Mini Loaf Pan, Set of 4](https://www.amazon.com/USA-Pan-Bakeware-Nonstick-Mini-Loaf-Pan/dp/B0047N13JI/?tag=kitchenpot-20) gives you four separate pans, each about 6 by 3.6 inches. Separate pans let you bake one or two at a time. Half a standard 9-by-5 recipe fills two of them, and they also suit banana bread and pound cake.

### Rolls, Flatbread and Focaccia

Rolls fit almost anywhere. Bake 6 dinner rolls in a 6-inch round, or 4 to 6 on an eighth sheet. Focaccia spreads into a quarter sheet and only rises an inch or so, which suits a toaster oven well. For pizza nights, our guide to the [best pizza stone](/blog/best-pizza-stone/) covers stones and steels, but measure first. Most full-size stones are too big for a toaster oven.

### Bake Bread Low and Check the Center

Bread goes on the lowest rack. That gives the dough the most room to rise and keeps the crust from scorching. Tent with foil if the top is dark brown before the time is up.

The surest test is the inside temperature. Soft sandwich bread and enriched dough are done at about 190°F. Crusty lean bread is done at about 200°F to 210°F. An instant-read thermometer like the [ThermoPro TP19H](https://www.amazon.com/ThermoPro-TP19H/dp/B09FLTW388/?tag=kitchenpot-20) answers the question in a few seconds, and it doubles for meat. ## Toaster Oven Baking Recipes That Work Well for One or Two

You do not need special recipes, just normal ones cut to size. Here is where we would start.

- **Chocolate chip cookies:** a half batch, baked 6 at a time on an eighth sheet, with the rest frozen as dough balls.
- **Blueberry muffins:** a half batch in the 6-cup toaster oven muffin pan.
- **Banana bread:** half a recipe split between two mini loaf pans.
- **Brownies:** a half batch in two mini loaf pans, or a quarter batch in one 6-inch round.
- **Vanilla cake:** one 8-inch layer recipe baked in a 6-inch round.
- **Biscuits or scones:** 4 to 6 on an eighth sheet, middle rack.
- **Focaccia:** a small batch of dough pressed into a quarter sheet.
- **Fruit crisp:** fruit and topping in a 6-inch round or two mini loaf pans.
- **Granola:** one thin layer on a quarter sheet, stirred every 5 minutes.

## Parchment, Foil and Silicone Mats: What Is Safe

**Parchment paper:** Safe for normal baking temperatures. Most parchment is rated to about 420°F to 450°F, so check the box. Trim it so nothing hangs past the pan. A loose corner that touches an element can scorch or catch fire. Never use parchment under the broiler setting.

**Foil:** Fine for tenting a cake or lining a pan. Never line the crumb tray or the oven floor with foil unless your manual says you can. It traps heat and blocks airflow. Keep foil at least an inch from every element.

**Silicone mats:** The best liner for cookies. They do not scorch at normal baking temperatures and they last for years. Cut them to fit so no edge curls up toward the top element.

**Wire racks:** A small rack does two jobs. It cools cookies and cakes, and it sits inside a sheet pan to roast food so the hot air reaches underneath. On a tight counter, set the cooling rack across a cold stovetop.

Protect your hands too. It is easy to brush the top element with a wrist. Silicone gloves like the [Ekogrips Max Heat Silicone Gloves](https://www.amazon.com/Ekogrips-Premium-Heat-Resistant-BBQ-Silicone-Gloves/dp/B00HLPXL80/?tag=kitchenpot-20) let you grip a pan deep in the back without a bulky mitt. ## Where to Put a Toaster Oven You Bake In

Baking runs a toaster oven hot for 20 to 60 minutes, and the top of the case gets hot. So placement is a safety question.

Follow these rules every time:

- **Never run it under upper cabinets.** Uppers usually hang about 18 inches above the counter. Heat and steam rise into the cabinet bottoms, which can scorch the wood, warp doors and damage what is inside. Pull it out to an open stretch of counter, or put it on a cart top with nothing above it.
- **Leave clearance on every side.** Leave several inches of air around the back, sides and top, and follow the clearance in your manual. Do not push it tight against the backsplash or a wall.
- **Never run it inside a cabinet or appliance garage.** Even with the door open, a cabinet traps heat around the case.
- **Use the top shelf of a cart, never a lower shelf.** A lower shelf has a shelf right above it, which is the same problem as a cabinet.
- **Keep the top clear.** Do not set bread bags, paper towels or spice jars on top of the oven while it runs.
- **Plug it straight into the wall.** Toaster ovens draw a lot of power. Skip extension cords and power strips.
- **Clean the crumb tray.** Crumbs and grease on the tray can smoke or ignite during a long bake.

Once cool, it can slide back under the cabinets for storage. Pull it out before you switch it on. ## What Usually Goes Wrong in Toaster Oven Baking

- **Using a pan that touches the walls.** No airflow means burnt edges and a raw middle. Leave about an inch on every side.
- **Using dark or glass pans.** Dark pans over-brown bottoms in a small oven. Most glass bakeware is not rated for toaster ovens, so check the label before you use it.
- **Skipping the preheat.** A cold start dries cookies and flattens cakes. Give it 5 to 10 minutes, plus a few after the beep.
- **Opening the door too often.** A small oven loses heat fast. Look through the glass and only open it to turn the pan or test.
- **Using full recipe times.** The heat is closer, so food finishes sooner. Start checking at about two-thirds of the time.
- **Running it under the cabinets.** Bakes are long and hot. Pull the oven to open counter with nothing overhead.
