How long do leftovers last? Most cooked leftovers last 3 to 4 days in the fridge and stay safe indefinitely in a 0°F freezer. The hard part is keeping track.

In a small household, you cook four portions, eat one, and the rest drift to the back of a tiny fridge. Below is our full chart by food, the rules for cooling, storing and reheating, and a simple rotation system that gets every portion eaten on time.

**The short version:** Get cooked food into the fridge within 2 hours, or 1 hour when it is above 90°F. Keep the fridge at 40°F or below and the freezer at 0°F. Eat cooked leftovers within 3 to 4 days, or freeze them by day 2 or 3. Reheat everything to 165°F. When you cannot remember how old something is, throw it out.

## How Long Do Leftovers Last in the Fridge?

Our rule for almost every cooked dish is **3 to 4 days**. That includes cooked meat, chicken, fish, soups, stews, casseroles, rice, pasta, pizza and takeout.

The clock starts when the food was cooked. Not when you put it in a container, and not when you first opened it. If you cooked chicken on Sunday night, it is done by Thursday night.

Why 4 days? Cold slows bacteria down, but it does not stop all of them. Some, like *Listeria*, keep growing slowly even at fridge temperature. After about 4 days, the risk climbs enough that we do not take the chance.

Two more facts matter:

- **Your fridge has to actually be at 40°F or below.** Every number in this guide assumes that. A fridge running at 45°F shortens all of these times.
- **You cannot smell or see the bacteria that make people sick.** Spoilage bacteria cause the slime, sour smell and mold. Food poisoning bacteria usually cause none of those. Food can look perfect on day 6 and still make you ill.

For the full detail on one of the most common leftovers, see our guide to [how long chicken lasts in the fridge](/blog/how-long-does-chicken-last-in-the-fridge/).

## Leftover Storage Chart by Food

Here is our chart for the foods small households cook most. The fridge column is for safety. The freezer column is for quality only, because food held at 0°F stays safe for as long as it stays frozen.

| Food | Fridge (40°F or below) | Freezer (0°F), best quality |
|---|---|---|
| Cooked chicken or turkey | 3 to 4 days | About 4 months |
| Cooked beef, pork or lamb | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months |
| Cooked fish | 3 to 4 days | 4 to 6 months |
| Soups and stews | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months |
| Casseroles and other cooked meat dishes | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months |
| Chicken nuggets or patties, cooked | 3 to 4 days | 1 to 3 months |
| Pizza | 3 to 4 days | 1 to 2 months |
| Cooked rice | 3 to 4 days | Up to 1 month |
| Cooked pasta | 3 to 4 days | 1 to 2 months |
| Cooked vegetables | 3 to 4 days | Freezes, but turns soft |
| Takeout and restaurant leftovers | 3 to 4 days | Same as the matching food above |
| Egg, chicken, tuna or pasta salad | 3 to 5 days | Do not freeze |
| Hard-boiled eggs, in the shell | 1 week | Do not freeze |
| Opened deli meat | 3 to 5 days | 1 to 2 months |
| Opened hot dogs | 1 week | 1 to 2 months |

A few notes on reading the chart:

- **When two dishes are mixed, use the shorter time.** Chicken over rice is a 3 to 4 day dish, full stop.
- **Freezing pauses the clock.** If you freeze soup on day 2, it comes out of the freezer with about 2 days left once it thaws.
- **Freezer times are about taste and texture.** A 6-month-old frozen stew is safe. It may just be dry or icy at the edges.

The official version of this chart is the [USDA FoodKeeper and the cold storage charts on FoodSafety.gov](https://www.foodsafety.gov/food-safety-charts/cold-food-storage-charts). Our numbers follow it, rounded to the safer side where it gives a range.

For foods that are not leftovers, we have separate guides. Raw potatoes keep for weeks, as we explain in [how long potatoes last](/blog/how-long-do-potatoes-last/). Dairy is its own case, like [freezing cream cheese](/blog/can-you-freeze-cream-cheese/).

## Can You Eat Leftovers After 7 Days?

No. For cooked leftovers, day 7 is three days past our limit. Throw them out.

The only items in the chart that reach a week are hard-boiled eggs in the shell and opened hot dogs. Those are not the same as a pot of chili or a box of fried rice.

This is the mistake most people make: they open the container, sniff it, see nothing wrong and eat it. The sniff test only tells you about spoilage. It tells you nothing about *Salmonella*, *Listeria* or the toxins some bacteria leave behind. Reheating does not fix it either, because some of those toxins survive heat.

Our rule is simple: **when in doubt, throw it out.** One lost portion costs far less than a day of food poisoning.

## How Long Do Leftovers Last in the Freezer?

At 0°F, bacteria cannot grow at all. That means frozen food stays safe for as long as it stays frozen, whether that is one month or one year.

What changes is quality. Over time, frozen food loses moisture and picks up freezer burn. Freezer burn shows up as dry, grayish or white patches where air touched the food. It is safe to eat. Cut those spots off, or use the food in a soup or sauce where texture matters less.

Our freezer rules for small households:

- **Freeze by day 2 or 3.** If you can tell you will not finish a batch in time, freeze it while it still has fridge days left.
- **Freeze in single portions.** One frozen block of chili for four is useless when you cook for one. See [how to freeze single portions](/blog/how-to-freeze-single-portions/).
- **Push out the air.** Air causes freezer burn. Squeeze bags flat, or use a [vacuum sealer](/blog/how-to-use-a-vacuum-sealer/) for anything you plan to keep more than a couple of months.
- **Aim to eat it within 2 to 3 months.** Not for safety, for flavor.

## Check Your Fridge and Freezer Temperatures

Every time in this guide depends on two numbers: **40°F or below** in the fridge and **0°F** in the freezer. Most people have never checked theirs. The dial inside the fridge marked 1 to 7 is not a thermometer. It is just a power setting.

Put a thermometer on the middle shelf, toward the front, and read it the next morning. Do the same in the freezer.

That high reading tells you whether food was safe after a power cut.

### What to Do After a Power Outage

Keep both doors shut. A closed fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours. A full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours, and a half-full one for about 24.

After 4 hours without power, throw out leftovers and other perishable food from the fridge. Frozen food that still has ice crystals, or reads 40°F or below, can be refrozen.

## Should Leftovers Cool Before Going in the Fridge?

No. This is an old habit that does more harm than good. Bacteria grow fastest between 40°F and 140°F. This range is called the danger zone. A pot of soup left on the stove to cool spends hours in it.

Here is the rule: **refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours of cooking.** If the room is hotter than 90°F, like a summer kitchen with no air conditioning, the limit is **1 hour**.

A modern fridge can handle warm food. The real problem is a big, deep mass of hot food. The center of a full stockpot can stay warm for many hours even inside the fridge. The fix is to make the food shallow, not to wait.

## How to Put Leftovers in the Fridge So They Cool Fast

Small pieces and shallow layers chill quickly. Follow these steps every time you cook more than one meal's worth.

1. **Serve your portion first.** Then pack the rest right away, before you sit down to eat.
2. **Divide big batches.** Split soup, chili or stew into containers no more than about 2 inches deep.
3. **Cut large pieces down.** Slice a roast or a whole chicken into smaller portions. Take meat off the bone.
4. **Leave the lids off or loose for a few minutes.** Once the food stops steaming, close the lids.
5. **Leave space around the containers.** Cold air has to move around them. Do not stack hot containers on top of each other.
6. **Put them on a middle or upper shelf, not in the door.** The door is the warmest part of any fridge.
7. **Keep raw meat on the bottom shelf, in a tray or sealed container,** below everything that is ready to eat.

For a really big pot, stir it in a sink of ice water for 10 to 15 minutes before you portion it out.

If you cook a lot of soup and stew, a pot that is the right size helps, too. ## Why Cooked Rice Needs Extra Care

Rice is the one leftover we treat with extra caution. Raw rice can carry spores of a bacterium called *Bacillus cereus*. Spores are a tough, dormant form of the bacteria. They survive boiling. If cooked rice sits warm, the spores wake up, multiply and make a toxin that reheating does not destroy.

Our rice rules:

- **Cool it fast.** Get cooked rice into the fridge within 1 hour if you can, and never later than 2 hours.
- **Spread it out.** Tip the rice onto a tray in a thin layer so the steam escapes, then pack it once it stops steaming. The [Nordic Ware Natural Aluminum Quarter Sheet](https://www.amazon.com/Nordic-Ware-Natural-Aluminum-Quarter-Sheet/dp/B000RYPHRK/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is the right size for one or two portions. Aluminum pulls heat away fast, and the pan fits in most small fridges.
- **Eat it within 3 to 4 days,** and reheat it only once.
- **Do not leave it in the rice cooker on warm all day.** Keep-warm is for a few hours of serving, not for storage.

Cook smaller batches if you keep throwing rice away. Our guide on [how to cook rice in a rice cooker](/blog/how-to-cook-rice-in-a-rice-cooker/) covers small amounts. If you want a cooker built for one or two people, see our [best rice cooker for brown rice](/blog/best-rice-cooker-for-brown-rice/) list.

## How Do You Label Leftovers?

Labels are the whole system. Without a date, every container becomes a guess by day 3.

Write two things on every label:

1. **What it is.** "Chicken curry," not just "curry."
2. **The date it was cooked.** Better yet, add the last safe day. "Cooked Mon 10/5, eat by Thu 10/8."

Writing the use-by day is the trick that makes this work. You do not have to count days when you open the fridge. You just read.

Our favorite label is the [MESS Dissolvable Food Labels](https://www.amazon.com/MESS-Dissolvable-Food-Labels-1x2/dp/B07YF3JB32/?tag=kitchenpot-20). Each roll has 200 labels, 1 x 2 inches. They stick firmly in the fridge and freezer, then dissolve under running water in about 30 seconds. No gummy residue left on your containers. Write on them with a [Sharpie Fine Point Permanent Marker](https://www.amazon.com/Sharpie-Permanent-Markers-Fine-Black-12/dp/B00006IFHD/?tag=kitchenpot-20). It dries fast and stays put on plastic lids and freezer bags, where a ballpoint skips. Dry the surface first. Keep one marker and the roll of labels in the drawer nearest the fridge.

The free version works, too. A strip of masking tape and a marker does the same job.

## A Leftover Rotation System for Small Households

A fridge with five mystery containers is not a storage problem. It is a system problem. Here is the rotation we recommend for one or two people.

### Set Up an "Eat First" Bin

Pick one clear bin and put it at eye level on the middle shelf. Everything cooked goes in it. Nothing cooked lives anywhere else.

The [iDesign Stackable Fridge Bin, Set of 2](https://www.amazon.com/iDesign-Stackable-Fridge-Bin-Set-of-2/dp/B00TFDFYA4/?tag=kitchenpot-20) works well here. Each bin is 14.5 x 8 x 4 inches, which fits most small fridge shelves. Use one for leftovers and one for open ingredients like half an onion or a cut lemon. When you open the fridge looking for lunch, you look in one place.

### Follow First In, First Out

Put new containers at the back of the bin. Slide older ones to the front. This is how restaurants rotate stock, and it works just as well at home.

### Hold a Day 2 Check

Every evening, glance at the bin. Anything on its second or third day gets one of three fates:

- **Eat it tomorrow.** Plan it as lunch or dinner.
- **Freeze it tonight.** It still has days left, so it will be good after thawing.
- **Turn it into something else.** See our ideas below.

### Cook Less in the First Place

The best leftover system starts at the stove. If you are throwing out food every week, cook smaller. Pair it with a shorter shopping list from [how to grocery shop for one person](/blog/how-to-grocery-shop-for-one-person/).

Freezing is the best tool a solo cook has. It turns one big pot of chili into five quick dinners spread over two months.

### How to Thaw Leftovers Safely

Thaw frozen leftovers in the fridge overnight. Once thawed, eat them within 3 to 4 days. Food thawed in the fridge can go back in the freezer if you change your mind, though it may lose some texture.

If you thaw in the microwave or in cold water, reheat and eat the food right away. Never thaw leftovers on the counter.

## Reheat Leftovers to 165°F

Reheating is the last safety step. Bring every leftover to **165°F** in the center. Soups, sauces and gravies should come to a full rolling boil.

The only way to know you hit 165°F is to check. We recommend the [ThermoWorks ThermoPop 2](https://www.amazon.com/ThermoWorks-ThermoPop-2-Instant-Read-Thermometer/dp/B0DC8FD41B/?tag=kitchenpot-20), an instant-read thermometer. That means you poke it in and get a reading in a few seconds instead of leaving it in the food. Check the thickest part and one or two other spots. After you use it, clean the probe as we show in [how to clean a meat thermometer](/blog/how-to-clean-a-meat-thermometer/).

### Microwave Reheating

Microwaves heat unevenly and leave cold spots, so a little care makes a big difference.

1. Spread the food in an even layer in a microwave-safe dish.
2. Cover it with a vented lid or a damp paper towel. The steam helps heat the food evenly.
3. Heat in short bursts and stir halfway through. Rotate the dish if you have no turntable.
4. Let it stand for 1 to 2 minutes so the heat spreads.
5. Check the temperature in more than one spot.

If your microwave sits in an awkward spot, our guide on [where to put a microwave in a small kitchen](/blog/where-to-put-microwave-in-small-kitchen/) has better spots that keep the air vents clear.

### Stovetop, Oven and Air Fryer

The stovetop is best for soups, stews and rice. Add a splash of water and keep a lid on. The oven, toaster oven or air fryer suits anything that should stay crisp, like pizza, fried chicken or roast potatoes. Our guides on [how to reheat pizza](/blog/how-to-reheat-pizza/) and [how to reheat chicken wings](/blog/how-to-reheat-chicken-wings/) give exact times. Run any heat appliance on an open counter with air around it, never inside a cabinet.

Do not reheat leftovers in a slow cooker. It warms food too slowly and can keep it in the danger zone for too long.

### Reheat Only What You Will Eat

Take out one portion and leave the rest in the fridge. Every trip out of the fridge and back again adds time in the danger zone. Rice should only be reheated once.

## How to Use Leftovers Before They Expire

The easiest way to beat the 4-day clock is to plan a second meal from the first one. Here are the swaps we recommend most.

| Leftover | Turn it into | How |
|---|---|---|
| Roast or rotisserie chicken | Fried rice, quesadillas, soup | Shred the meat; add it in the last few minutes |
| Cooked rice | Fried rice, rice bowls | Cold rice fries best; heat to 165°F |
| Chili or stew | Baked potato topping, pasta sauce | Reheat to a boil, then spoon over |
| Roasted vegetables | Frittata, grain bowl, blended soup | Chop small, add to eggs or broth |
| Cooked pasta | Pasta bake, cold pasta salad | Bake with sauce and cheese, or toss cold with dressing |
| Taco meat | Stuffed peppers, nachos, burritos | Reheat, then build fresh |
| Plain grains or beans | Salad base, soup, breakfast bowl | Mix with greens, broth or eggs |

## Common Leftover Mistakes

- **Leaving food on the stove to cool.** It spends hours in the danger zone. Pack it within 2 hours, warm or not.
- **Storing a whole pot in the fridge.** The middle stays warm far too long. Divide it into shallow containers.
- **Trusting the sniff test.** Food poisoning bacteria do not change the smell. Go by the date.
- **Counting from the wrong day.** The clock starts when the food was cooked, not when you opened the container.
- **Not labeling.** By day 3, nobody remembers. Write the cook date and the use-by date.
- **Freezing too late.** Freezing on day 4 leaves no fridge days after thawing. Freeze by day 2 or 3.
- **Reheating only until warm.** Warm is not safe. Heat to 165°F, and boil soups and sauces.
- **Never checking the fridge temperature.** A fridge at 45°F shortens every time in this guide. Keep a thermometer inside.

A tidy fridge also keeps pests away, since spills and old food draw them in. Our guide on [how to keep pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/) covers the rest of the room.
