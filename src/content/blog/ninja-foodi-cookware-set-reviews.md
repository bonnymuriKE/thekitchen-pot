## Ninja's NeverStick Claims, Checked

NeverStick sold on bold claims. If you own a set, or you're eyeing leftover stock, it helps to know what each one meant.

### "Made at 30,000°F"

Ninja said NeverStick was made by fusing plasma ceramic particles to the pan at up to 30,000°F. It said this created "a super-hard, textured surface that interlocks with our exclusive coating."

The key word is "coating." The plasma step roughened the aluminum. A nonstick coating then went on top and gripped that rough texture. The pan itself was never 30,000°F. That figure describes the plasma spray, not the metal.

The claim drew a [proposed class action in October 2023](https://science.slashdot.org/story/23/10/20/202210/frying-pan-company-sued-for-claiming-temperatures-that-rival-the-sun). It argued the figure was physically impossible. Whatever the legal outcome, the practical point stands. The texture helped the coating stick to the pan. It didn't make the coating immortal.

### "Won't Stick, Chip or Flake"

Ninja described NeverStick as a coating that "won't chip, flake or stick." That's a marketing line, not a lab result. Owner complaints about chipping and peeling do appear in NeverStick reviews, as they do for most nonstick pans.

### The 10-Year Guarantee

Ninja backed NeverStick with a 10-year guarantee. It still does for NeverStick bakeware, which says it is "fully backed by the Ninja 10 Year Guarantee." Like most guarantees, it applied when the pans were used as directed.

NeverQuit's guarantee is 3 years on the nonstick coating. That's shorter on paper. But it's long for ceramic, which usually wears faster than PTFE.

### PFAS and PTFE

Ninja marketed NeverStick as PFOA-free. It did not market it as PTFE-free. [Published third-party analysis](https://therationalkitchen.com/ninja-cookware-review/) says the NeverStick coating contains PTFE, the same polymer family as Teflon. Our explainer on [what nonstick cookware is](/blog/what-is-nonstick-cookware/) covers how PTFE works and what "PFOA-free" really means.

NeverQuit is different. Ninja says its ceramic coating is designed without PFAS, PFOA, PTFE, lead and cadmium. If avoiding PTFE is your goal, NeverQuit is the Ninja line that fits.

## Is Ninja Cookware Safe?

Yes, when used as directed. Both lines are sold as food-safe cookware, and neither uses PFOA.

The real difference is PTFE. PTFE is stable at normal cooking heat. It can release fumes if an empty pan overheats well past normal cooking temperatures. That's why nonstick makers warn against preheating empty pans on high.

Ceramic coatings avoid PTFE entirely. They are mostly silica-based, a glass-like layer. That's why NeverQuit can list PFAS-free on the box. Our article on whether [ceramic-titanium cookware is safe](/blog/is-ceramic-titanium-cookware-safe/) covers the chemistry of these coatings in more detail.

"Nontoxic" is a marketing word, not a legal standard. On NeverQuit, Ninja defines it as a coating made without lead, cadmium, PFAS, PFOA, PTFE or hard anodization. That's a clear list. It's worth reading, rather than trusting the single word.

## Still Own NeverStick Pans? How to Make Them Last

You don't need to throw out working NeverStick pans. Ninja retiring the line doesn't make your pans unsafe.

A few habits will keep them going longer:

1. **Preheat on low to medium.** Don't leave an empty pan on high. PTFE coatings suffer most from dry, extreme heat.
2. **Use a little fat.** Even great nonstick releases better with a thin film of oil.
3. **Skip cooking sprays.** They can leave a sticky film that builds up over time.
4. **Hand wash.** Our guide on [how to clean hard anodized cookware](/blog/how-to-clean-hard-anodized-cookware/) covers the right sponges and cleaners for NeverStick's body.
5. **Stack with protectors.** A cloth or felt pad between pans stops the rim of one scraping the coating of the next.

A pan that sounds different can also warn you early. Our guide to [what your cookware's sounds are telling you](/blog/what-your-cookware-sounds-are-telling-you/) explains the sizzle and hiss of an overheating pan.

When food starts sticking in the center of a NeverStick pan, the coating is worn. At that point, check whether your purchase is still inside the 10-year guarantee. If it isn't, it's time to replace the pan.
