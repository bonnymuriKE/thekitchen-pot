You bought a pack of chicken on Saturday with big plans, and now it's Tuesday night and you're staring at it in the fridge wondering if it's still okay. Or there's half a rotisserie chicken in there from a few days ago and you can't remember exactly when you bought it. Everyone has been there.

How long chicken lasts in the fridge comes down to two things: whether it's raw or cooked, and how you've stored it. The good news is that the official guidance is simple, and once you know it you can plan your cooking and freezing around it. This guide uses the storage times from the USDA Food Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS) and FoodSafety.gov, plus practical tips for making them work in a small fridge.

> **Quick answer:** Raw chicken lasts 1 to 2 days in the fridge, and cooked chicken lasts 3 to 4 days, as long as your refrigerator stays at 40°F or below. If you won't use it in that time, freeze it. Chicken kept frozen at 0°F stays safe indefinitely, though it tastes best within 9 to 12 months raw and 2 to 6 months cooked.

## How Long Does Chicken Last in the Fridge? The Full Chart

Here are the storage times from FoodSafety.gov's cold storage chart and the FSIS "Chicken from Farm to Table" guide. Fridge times are for safety. Freezer times are for quality only, because food kept frozen at 0°F is always safe.

| Type of chicken | Refrigerator (40°F or below) | Freezer (0°F or below) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw whole chicken | 1 to 2 days | 1 year |
| Raw chicken pieces (breasts, thighs, wings) | 1 to 2 days | 9 months |
| Raw ground chicken or turkey | 1 to 2 days | 3 to 4 months |
| Raw chicken in a marinade | Up to 2 days | Freeze before or after marinating |
| Raw chicken thawed in the fridge | 1 to 2 days before cooking | Can be refrozen raw |
| Cooked chicken leftovers | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 6 months |
| Cooked chicken in broth or gravy | 3 to 4 days | About 6 months |
| Rotisserie or fried chicken | 3 to 4 days | About 4 months |
| Chicken nuggets or patties (cooked) | 3 to 4 days | 1 to 3 months |
| Chicken salad | 3 to 4 days | Doesn't freeze well |
| Chicken soup or stew | 3 to 4 days | 2 to 3 months |

If you only remember one line from this chart, make it this: **raw chicken, 1 to 2 days; cooked chicken, 3 to 4 days.**

## How Long Does Raw Chicken Last in the Fridge?

Raw chicken lasts **1 to 2 days** in the fridge. That's the same whether it's a whole bird, bone-in thighs, boneless breasts or wings. Ground chicken follows the same rule.

This window is short because raw poultry commonly carries bacteria like *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*. Cold slows them down but doesn't stop them. Cooking to 165°F kills them, which is why the rule jumps to 3 to 4 days once the chicken is cooked.

### What about the sell-by date?

The date on a pack of chicken is not the deadline many people think it is. FSIS explains that a **sell-by date** tells the store how long to display the product and is not a safety date. A **use-by** or **best if used by** date describes peak quality, not safety.

The practical rule: count the 1 to 2 days from the day you bring the chicken home, and don't buy chicken that's already past its date. If you freeze chicken before its date passes, it stays safe for as long as it stays frozen.

### Marinated raw chicken

FSIS says chicken can marinate in the refrigerator for **up to 2 days**. Always marinate in the fridge, never on the counter. If you want to use some of the marinade as a sauce, set a portion aside before it touches raw chicken, or bring the used marinade to a full boil first.

Marinated chicken also freezes well. Put raw chicken and marinade in a freezer bag, press out the air, and freeze. It marinates as it thaws in the fridge, which makes it one of the easiest meal-prep shortcuts around.

## How Long Is Cooked Chicken Good in the Fridge?

Cooked chicken is good for **3 to 4 days** in the fridge. That covers almost everything: roasted or grilled chicken, shredded chicken, chicken stir-fry, chicken soup, casseroles, fried chicken, nuggets and store-bought rotisserie chicken.

Two rules make that 3 to 4 day window safe:

1. **Refrigerate within 2 hours.** FSIS says leftovers should go in the fridge within 2 hours of cooking, or within 1 hour if the air temperature is above 90°F (think summer cookouts or a hot car). Bacteria multiply fastest between 40°F and 140°F.
2. **Cool it fast.** Split large amounts into shallow containers so they chill quickly. A big pot of soup or a whole roast chicken takes a long time to cool in the middle.

You don't need to wait for food to cool on the counter before refrigerating it. FSIS says hot food can go straight into the fridge, or you can speed things up with an ice or cold water bath first.

### Rotisserie chicken

Store-bought rotisserie chicken follows the same 3 to 4 day rule. The 2-hour clock starts when you buy it, not when you get home. FSIS suggests that if you won't eat it within 2 hours, cut it into several pieces and refrigerate it in shallow, covered containers. Pulling the meat off the bones as soon as you're home also saves fridge space.

### Chicken salad and deli chicken

Homemade or store-bought chicken salad lasts 3 to 4 days in the fridge but doesn't freeze well, because mayonnaise separates when it thaws. For packaged deli chicken, follow the date on the package before opening and use it within a few days once it's open.

## How to Tell If Chicken Has Gone Bad

Your senses can warn you that chicken has spoiled, but they can't tell you it's safe. Here's what to look for, and why storage time matters more.

| Sign | Raw chicken | Cooked chicken |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smell | Sour, sulfur-like or strongly off odor | Sour or rancid smell |
| Texture | Slimy or sticky coating that stays on your fingers | Slimy surface, unusually mushy |
| Look | Dull gray or greenish areas | Mold (fuzzy spots of any color) |
| Package | Bloated or puffy packaging | Container lid bulging |
| Time | More than 2 days in the fridge | More than 4 days in the fridge |

If you see any of these signs, throw the chicken away. Don't rinse it and hope for the best.

### Why the smell test isn't enough

The bacteria that make food go "off" (spoilage bacteria) are different from the ones that make you sick (pathogens). According to USDA, pathogens usually don't change how food looks, smells or tastes. That means chicken can seem perfectly fine at day 5 and still cause food poisoning.

Color is also unreliable. FSIS notes that a change in the color of meat or poultry isn't, by itself, a sign of spoilage. Raw chicken can look a little darker or paler over its normal shelf life without being unsafe.

The FoodSafety.gov rule is the one to live by: **when in doubt, throw it out.** And never taste chicken to see if it's still good.

## How to Store Raw Chicken in the Fridge

Good storage won't stretch the 1 to 2 day window, but poor storage can shorten it and spread bacteria to other food.

1. **Check your fridge temperature.** It should be at 40°F or below. The dial on many fridges is just numbers, so a cheap [appliance thermometer](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=refrigerator+thermometer&tag=kitchenpot-20) is the only way to know. Keep the freezer at 0°F.
2. **Get chicken home and cold quickly.** Make it one of the last things you pick up at the store, and put it in the fridge first when you get home. On a hot day or a long drive, an insulated bag helps. Even a [lunch cooler](/blog/best-lunch-cooler-for-construction-workers/) with an ice pack works.
3. **Keep it on the bottom shelf.** Raw chicken juices can drip. Storing it on the lowest shelf means nothing drips onto ready-to-eat food.
4. **Contain the package.** Leave chicken in its original packaging, but set it on a plate, tray or in a sealed container to catch leaks. Store-bought wrap often isn't leak-proof.
5. **Don't wash raw chicken.** FSIS doesn't recommend it, because splashing spreads bacteria around your sink and counter. Cooking kills the bacteria. Rinsing doesn't.
6. **Write the date on it.** A strip of masking tape with the purchase date takes two seconds and ends the "when did I buy this?" guessing game.

After handling raw chicken, wash your hands, cutting board and knife in hot soapy water. If your counter space is tight, an [over-the-sink cutting board](/blog/best-over-the-sink-cutting-board/) keeps raw meat prep contained right next to the tap.

## How to Store Cooked Chicken So It Lasts the Full 3 to 4 Days

- **Use airtight containers.** Sealed containers keep chicken from drying out and absorbing fridge smells. Glass or BPA-free plastic both work. Our guide to the [best airtight food storage containers](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/) covers stackable sets that fit small fridges.
- **Store in portions.** Small containers cool faster and let you take out one meal without opening everything.
- **Label with the cook date.** Write "cooked" and the date. That makes the day-4 cutoff easy to spot.
- **Keep it separate from raw chicken.** Cooked chicken goes on a higher shelf than raw.
- **Freeze on day 1 or 2 if you're unsure.** Don't wait until day 4 to decide. Freezing earlier keeps the texture better.

When you reheat leftovers, bring them to **165°F**, and bring soups and gravies to a rolling boil. For crispy pieces, see our guide on [how to reheat chicken wings](/blog/how-to-reheat-chicken-wings/). The oven and air fryer methods work for fried chicken and drumsticks too, and some of the same tricks from our guide on [how to reheat pizza](/blog/how-to-reheat-pizza/) apply to breaded chicken.

## How Long Does Chicken Last in the Freezer?

Frozen chicken lasts much longer, and technically forever. FSIS says food stored constantly at 0°F or below will always be safe. The freezer times in the chart above are about quality: after that point chicken can get dry, tough or pick up off flavors, but it won't make you sick.

### How to freeze chicken the right way

1. **Freeze it early.** Raw chicken should go into the freezer within 1 to 2 days of purchase, ideally the day you buy it if you know you won't cook it soon.
2. **Wrap it well.** FSIS says it's safe to freeze chicken in its original packaging, but that wrap lets air through, so quality drops over time. For anything longer than a month or two, overwrap it with foil or freezer paper, or move it into a freezer bag.
3. **Remove the air.** Air causes freezer burn, those dry, grayish-white patches. Freezer burn doesn't make chicken unsafe, just dry in spots. You can cut those areas off.
4. **Portion before freezing.** Freeze chicken in the amounts you'll cook at once so you never have to thaw a whole family pack for one dinner.
5. **Label it.** Write what it is and the date. Frozen chicken breasts all look the same after three months.

A vacuum sealer is the best tool for long freezer storage because it removes nearly all the air. Here's [how to use a vacuum sealer](/blog/how-to-use-a-vacuum-sealer/), and our roundup of the [best vacuum sealers for sous vide](/blog/best-vacuum-sealer-for-sous-vide/) covers machines that double as freezer tools. In the fridge, vacuum sealing at home doesn't change the 1 to 2 day rule for raw chicken. Its real payoff is in the freezer.

## How to Thaw Frozen Chicken Safely

Never thaw chicken on the counter. The outside warms into the danger zone long before the middle thaws. FSIS lists three safe methods.

| Method | How long it takes | After thawing | Can you refreeze it raw? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Refrigerator | About 1 to 2 days, longer for a whole bird | Cook within 1 to 2 days | Yes |
| Cold water | About 1 hour or less for 1 lb boneless breasts, 2 to 3 hours for a 3 to 4 lb bird | Cook right away | No, cook first |
| Microwave | Minutes | Cook immediately | No, cook first |
| Cook from frozen | About 50% longer cooking time | Eat or refrigerate as usual | n/a |

### Refrigerator thawing

This is the safest method and the only one that lets you refreeze raw chicken without cooking it. Put the chicken on a plate or in a container on the bottom shelf. Boneless breasts and thighs usually thaw overnight. A whole chicken can take a day or more.

### Cold water thawing

Seal the chicken in a leak-proof bag and submerge it in cold tap water. Change the water every 30 minutes so it stays cold. FSIS says a 1-pound package of boneless breasts can thaw in an hour or less. Cook it as soon as it's thawed.

### Microwave thawing

Use your microwave's defrost setting, then cook the chicken immediately. Some parts can start to cook during defrosting, and those warm spots are where bacteria grow if you wait.

### Cooking chicken from frozen

You can safely cook chicken straight from the freezer in the oven, on the stovetop, or in a pressure cooker. It just takes about 50% longer than thawed chicken. Use a [meat thermometer](/blog/best-meat-thermometer-for-smoking/) to confirm 165°F in the thickest part, and clean it afterward with these steps for [how to clean a meat thermometer](/blog/how-to-clean-a-meat-thermometer/). Don't cook frozen chicken in a slow cooker, since it can sit in the danger zone too long while it heats up.

## Can You Refreeze Thawed Chicken?

Sometimes. Here's the short version, based on FSIS guidance:

- **Thawed in the fridge and still raw:** Yes, you can refreeze it within the 1 to 2 day window. Expect some loss of texture.
- **Thawed in cold water or the microwave:** Cook it first, then you can freeze the cooked chicken.
- **Cooked chicken made from frozen raw chicken:** Yes, it can be frozen.
- **Left out more than 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F):** No. Throw it away.

## What to Do With Chicken After a Power Outage

A power cut can throw your whole storage plan off. According to FoodSafety.gov, a closed refrigerator keeps food safe for about **4 hours**. A full freezer holds its temperature for about **48 hours**, and a half-full freezer for about **24 hours**, if you keep the door shut.

Throw out raw or cooked chicken that has been above 40°F for more than 2 hours. Frozen chicken that still has ice crystals, or is at 40°F or below, can be refrozen, though the quality may suffer.

## Making Chicken Last in a Small Fridge

Short shelf life plus a small fridge is a tricky mix, especially when you cook for one and most chicken is sold in packs meant for four. A few habits help:

- **Divide family packs the day you buy them.** Keep what you'll cook in the next day or two, and freeze the rest in single portions. Not sure how much one portion is? Our guide to [how much a chicken breast weighs](/blog/how-much-does-a-chicken-breast-weigh/) breaks it down.
- **Freeze flat.** Lay portions flat in freezer bags, freeze them on a tray, then stand them up like files. They take a fraction of the space of a lumpy package.
- **Cook once, eat twice, freeze the rest.** Roast a batch on Sunday, eat it Monday and Tuesday, and freeze the rest in portions. Our [easy meal prep ideas for one person in a small kitchen](/blog/easy-meal-prep-ideas-for-one-person-in-a-small-kitchen/) plans meals around exactly this.
- **Don't overpack the fridge.** Cold air has to circulate to keep everything at 40°F. A crammed fridge can have warm spots.
- **Watch mini fridges closely.** Compact and dorm fridges can struggle to hold a steady 40°F, especially when the door opens a lot or the freezer compartment ices up. Keep a thermometer inside, and learn [how to defrost a mini fridge](/blog/how-to-defrost-a-mini-fridge/) so ice buildup doesn't hurt its cooling.
- **Keep the right gear handy.** A thermometer, a few stackable containers and freezer bags cover almost everything. Our checklist on [how to stock a small kitchen for solo cooking](/blog/how-to-stock-a-small-kitchen-for-solo-cooking/) lists the basics.

If your fridge is simply too small or too old to hold temperature, it may be time for an upgrade. ## Common Chicken Storage Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's risky | Better habit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Going by the sell-by date | It's a display date, not a safety date | Count 1 to 2 days from purchase |
| Thawing chicken on the counter | The outside warms into the danger zone | Thaw in the fridge, cold water or microwave |
| Leaving leftovers out overnight | Bacteria multiply fast above 40°F | Refrigerate within 2 hours |
| Storing raw chicken on a top shelf | Juices drip onto other food | Bottom shelf, on a plate or in a container |
| Trusting the sniff test | Pathogens don't always smell | Follow storage times |
| Partly cooking chicken to finish later | Bacteria survive partial cooking | Cook fully in one go |

## The Bottom Line on How Long Chicken Lasts in the Fridge

Raw chicken lasts 1 to 2 days in the fridge, cooked chicken lasts 3 to 4 days, and frozen chicken stays safe indefinitely at 0°F. Keep your fridge at 40°F or below, store raw chicken on the bottom shelf, get leftovers cold within 2 hours, and freeze anything you won't use in time. When you're not sure how old something is, throw it out.
