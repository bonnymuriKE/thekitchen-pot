If you are trying to work out how to organize a small kitchen without cabinets, you are not stuck. You just need storage that stands on the floor or hangs on the wall.

Many studios, older rentals and basement units come with a sink, a stove and almost nothing else. This guide shows you what to buy, where each item should live, and how to keep open storage looking calm instead of cluttered.

**The short version:** Pick one tall freestanding unit, like a wire shelving unit or bakers rack, as your main "cabinet." Add a rolling cart beside the stove and a wall rail for daily tools. Store dishes near the sink, pans near the stove and food in clear, sealed containers. Cut what you own to what fits, because open storage shows every extra item.

## Start by Cutting Down What You Own

Cabinet-free kitchens punish extra stuff. With doors, a messy shelf is hidden. Without them, every spare mug is on display and in your way.

So before you buy a single shelf, sort everything into three piles: use weekly, use monthly and rarely use. Keep the weekly pile in the kitchen. Move the monthly pile to a bin on a high shelf. Donate or store the rest somewhere else in the home.

For a solo cook or a couple, this is a working set that fits easily in open storage:

- 4 plates, 4 bowls, 4 mugs and 4 glasses
- One 8-inch and one 10-inch skillet, a 2-quart saucepan and a 3-quart pot
- One quarter sheet pan and one 8-inch baking dish
- One chef's knife, one paring knife and one serrated knife
- About 8 to 10 utensils, not a crock of 25

If that list looks small, read our guide to [stocking a small kitchen for solo cooking](/blog/how-to-stock-a-small-kitchen-for-solo-cooking/). It walks through what one person actually reaches for. Our [cookware size guide for one](/blog/best-cookware-sizes-for-cooking-for-one/) explains why smaller pans save space without slowing you down.

Our rule of thumb: if you cannot fit an item into your new storage plan, it needs a strong reason to stay.

## How Do You Organize a Kitchen With No Cabinets? Use Zones

The trick is to stop thinking about "where is there space?" and start thinking about "where do I use this?" Split the room into four zones. Then give each zone its own storage.

| Zone | Where It Sits | What Lives There | Best Storage Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cleanup | Around the sink | Dishes, glasses, dish soap, towels, trash | Open shelf or rack, sink caddy, hooks |
| Prep | Your main stretch of counter | Knives, cutting boards, mixing bowls, measuring tools | Wall rail, magnetic strip, slim cart |
| Cooking | Beside the stove | Pans, oils, salt, spatulas, pot holders | Rolling cart, rail with hooks, pan rack |
| Food | Coolest spot away from the stove | Dry goods, cans, snacks, produce | Shelving unit with bins, bakers rack |

Keep the food zone away from the stove and away from sunny windows. Heat and light make oils go rancid faster and shorten the life of flour, nuts and spices.

If your kitchen is one wall, the zones run left to right: food, prep, sink, cooking, or a similar order. In a galley, put cleanup and prep on one side and cooking on the other. Either way, the goal is the same. You should be able to cook a meal without walking back and forth across the room.

## What Can I Use Instead of Kitchen Cabinets?

You have more options than most people think. Each one solves a different problem, so most kitchens need two or three working together.

| Storage Piece | Typical Size | Best For | Watch Out For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wire shelving unit | 36 or 48 in wide, 14 to 18 in deep, 72 in tall | Pantry food, dishes in bins, small appliances | Looks busy without bins; items tip through wires |
| Bakers rack | About 30 to 36 in wide, 14 to 16 in deep | Microwave, dishes, a few baskets | Many have a fixed shelf height |
| Rolling cart | About 16 in wide, 12 in deep | Cooking tools beside the stove, extra prep space | Small capacity; lock the wheels |
| Sideboard or buffet | 30 to 36 in tall, 16 to 20 in deep | Hidden storage plus a new counter top | Takes floor space; heavy to move |
| Bookcase | 12 in deep, various heights | Dishes and pantry food on adjustable shelves | Must be anchored to the wall |
| Wall shelves | 8 to 12 in deep | Daily dishes, glasses, jars | Needs screws into studs for heavy loads |
| Rails, hooks, pegboard | Any width | Utensils, mugs, pans, towels | Too many hooks look cluttered |

If you only buy one piece, make it a 36-inch-wide wire shelving unit with 18-inch-deep shelves. It holds the most for the floor space it takes. It also lets you set every shelf height to match what sits on it.

## Bakers Racks and Wire Shelving Units

A tall shelving unit is the closest thing to a full wall of cabinets. Treat it like one.

### How to Set It Up

1. **Measure first.** Check the wall width, the depth you can spare and the ceiling height. Leave at least 36 inches of clear walkway in front of it.
2. **Set the bottom shelf 6 to 8 inches off the floor.** This lets you sweep or mop under it and keeps food above any spills.
3. **Put the heaviest items low.** Cast iron, bags of rice and small appliances go on the bottom two shelves.
4. **Put daily items at waist to shoulder height.** This is the band between about 30 and 60 inches off the floor.
5. **Put light, rarely used items up top.** Spare paper towels, holiday platters and backup supplies fit here.
6. **Anchor it.** Strap any unit taller than about 3 feet to the wall with an anti-tip kit. This matters even more if kids or pets live with you.

### Make Wire Shelves Work Better

Wire shelves have two small problems. Narrow items tip over between the wires, and the open grid looks busy. Fix both with shelf liners and bins.

Clear plastic or solid shelf liners cost little and stop jars from wobbling. Matching bins or baskets turn a messy shelf into neat blocks. We recommend sticking to one bin style and one color across the whole unit. Mixed baskets are the fastest way to make a kitchen look cluttered.

A bakers rack is the softer-looking cousin. It usually has a wood or metal top shelf that can hold a microwave or toaster oven. If you are deciding where to put appliances, our guide to the [best small appliances for cooking for one](/blog/best-small-kitchen-appliances-for-cooking-for-one/) helps you choose machines that fit on a rack shelf. Keep at least a few inches of air space around any appliance that gets hot.

## Rolling Carts Fill the Gaps

A rolling cart is the most flexible piece you can own in a cabinet-free kitchen. It parks in a gap, moves to where you need it, and adds a small work surface.

We like a three-tier cart beside the stove as a "cooking station." Use the top tier for oils, salt and pepper. Put the middle tier to work for spatulas, tongs and a spoon rest. Give the bottom tier to one or two pans.

Slim carts, about 5 to 7 inches wide, slide into the gap between the fridge and the wall. They are great for cans, spice jars and bottles. For more on putting gaps and walls to work, see our [vertical storage ideas for small kitchens](/blog/vertical-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/).

A taller cart with a butcher-block top doubles as a mini island and extra prep space. It pairs well with the tips in our guide to [baking in a small kitchen without extra counter space](/blog/how-to-bake-in-a-small-kitchen-without-extra-counter-space/).

Always lock the wheels when you cut or mix on a cart. A cart that slides while you hold a knife is not a work surface.

## Sideboards, Hutches and Bookcases

Furniture from other rooms works well in a kitchen with no cabinets. The big benefit is doors. A sideboard hides the ugly stuff, like cleaning supplies and a stack of plastic containers.

A sideboard or buffet is usually 30 to 36 inches tall. That is close to standard counter height of 36 inches. So its top becomes a second counter for a coffee maker, a fruit bowl or a cutting board.

A hutch adds upper shelves on top of a sideboard. It works like a full run of base and upper cabinets in one piece. Look for one with adjustable shelves inside.

A plain bookcase also works. Most are about 12 inches deep, the same as a standard upper cabinet. That depth suits plates, bowls, glasses and jars. Anchor it to the wall, and skip particleboard shelves for heavy stacks of plates, since they sag over time.

Keep wood furniture at least a foot or two from the cooktop, and wipe up sink splashes right away.

## Where Do You Put Dishes If You Have No Cabinets?

Dishes belong as close to the sink as you can get them. You wash them there, so they should go back within a step or two.

Here is the setup we recommend:

- **Plates and bowls:** On an open wall shelf or rack above or beside the sink. Keep stacks short, about 4 to 6 plates, so they are easy to lift.
- **Mugs:** On hooks under a shelf or on a wall rail. Four hooks free up a whole shelf.
- **Glasses:** Upside down on a shelf, or in a shallow tray so dust stays out.
- **Silverware:** In a caddy on the counter or in a drawer insert on a cart.
- **Extra dishes:** In a lidded bin on a high shelf. Lids keep dust and grease off the things you rarely use.

A plate rack mounted on the wall does two jobs. It dries plates and stores them in the same spot. That also frees counter space, which matters in a kitchen like this.

An [over-the-sink cutting board](/blog/best-over-the-sink-cutting-board/) adds prep space without taking up any shelf at all. If you are thinking about plates, our explainer on [what ceramic dinnerware is](/blog/what-is-ceramic-dinnerware/) helps you pick sets that stack tightly. Lighter, thinner dishes are easier on open shelves too.

Keep dishes away from the stove. Grease from cooking settles on anything nearby, and you will end up washing clean plates.

## Wall Rails, Hooks and Pegboard

Your walls are the biggest empty storage space in a cabinet-free kitchen. Use them for the items you grab every day.

### Rails and Hooks

A metal rail with S-hooks runs along the backsplash and holds utensils, ladles, a whisk, pot holders and small pans. Hang the tools you use daily at eye level. Hang the rest higher.

A [magnetic knife strip](/blog/best-magnetic-knife-strip/) is a smart move here. It replaces a bulky knife block and clears counter space. Mount it away from the edge of the counter so a knife never hangs where a hip or elbow could bump it.

### Pegboard

Pegboard is the most flexible wall storage there is. Standard boards have holes 1 inch apart, so you can move hooks and small shelves any time. Mount it on furring strips, which are thin wood strips that leave about a half inch of space behind the board. Hooks need that gap to lock in.

Pegboard works best for pans, lids, measuring cups and tools. Trace an outline around each item with a marker so everything goes back in the same spot. For pan ideas, our guide on [how to store pots and pans in a small kitchen](/blog/store-pots-and-pans-in-a-small-kitchen/) has layouts that fit a pegboard or rail. Lids get their own tricks in our guide to [organizing pot lids](/blog/how-to-organize-pot-lids-in-a-small-kitchen/).

### What Not to Hang

Do not hang anything flammable right above the stove. That includes towels, pot holders and wooden spoons. Keep the wall behind the cooktop for a tile backsplash or a steel panel, not storage. Our [range hood and ventilation guide](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) explains how grease and heat travel above the stove.

## How Do I Add Storage to a Kitchen Without Cabinets?

Wall shelves add the most space for the least money. The key is mounting them right.

Most homes have wall studs every 16 inches. A stud is a vertical wood or metal beam inside the wall. Screws in studs hold far more weight than screws in plain drywall. Use a cheap stud finder to locate them.

| Mounting Method | Rough Hold | Good For | Renter-Friendly? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adhesive hooks and strips | A few pounds each | Towels, measuring spoons, light utensils | Yes |
| Tension rod | Light loads | Hanging spray bottles, cloths, small baskets | Yes |
| Over-the-door rack | Light to medium | Spices, wraps, foil, small cans | Yes |
| Drywall anchors | Light to medium | Small shelves with spices or glasses | Small holes, usually OK |
| Screws into studs | Heavy | Dish shelves, pan rails, plate racks | Small holes, check your lease |
| French cleat | Heavy | Long shelves or full wall systems | Small holes, check your lease |

A French cleat is two strips of wood cut at a 45-degree angle. One is screwed to the wall and the other to the back of the shelf. The shelf hooks onto the wall strip and can be lifted off when you move.

Always check the weight rating on the box. Then stay under it. A shelf of plates is heavier than it looks.

### Where to Put Wall Shelves

Standard upper cabinets start about 18 inches above the counter. That spacing works for open shelves too. It leaves room for a toaster or coffee maker under the shelf and keeps plates within reach.

Space shelves 10 to 12 inches apart for dishes and 8 inches apart for jars and spices. An 8 to 10-inch depth is enough for most plates and keeps the shelf from sticking out into your head space.

## Small Kitchen Storage Ideas Without Cabinets on a Budget

You do not need to spend much to make this work. Start with the cheap fixes: hooks, a tension rod and an over-the-door rack. Live with them for a few weeks. Then buy the one big piece, usually a wire shelving unit, since it gives the most space per dollar.

Secondhand furniture is the best deal of all. A used bookcase or dresser can become a pantry or dish station for very little. Check that drawers slide well and shelves do not sag.

Every small appliance you give up also frees a shelf. Our list of [eco-friendly alternatives to common kitchen appliances](/blog/eco-friendly-alternatives-to-common-kitchen-appliances/) shows simple tools that replace bulky machines. A hand-crank chopper takes far less room than a food processor.

## Storage Ideas Without Cabinets: DIY Options

If you can use a drill and a saw, a few weekend builds add a lot of storage.

1. **Plank and bracket shelves.** Cut a 1x10 board to length and mount it on two or three steel brackets screwed into studs. Seal the wood with polyurethane.
2. **Sink curtain.** If the sink has open space below it, mount a tension rod and hang a short curtain. You get hidden storage without building doors.
3. **Crate wall.** Screw wooden crates into studs to make cubbies for bowls, jars or towels.
4. **Pegboard panel.** Paint a 2x4-foot pegboard sheet and mount it on furring strips. It is the cheapest full wall organizer you can make.

A shelf that holds plates must be fixed into studs, not just drywall. When in doubt, add a third bracket.

## Where to Keep Pantry Food Without a Cabinet

Food on open shelves needs a little more care. Light, heat and air shorten its life, and open bags attract pests.

Our rules for open-shelf food storage:

- **Decant dry goods.** Move flour, rice, pasta, oats and sugar into [airtight food storage containers](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/). Clear square ones stack well and show you when you are running low.
- **Label everything.** Write the item and the date on a strip of tape. It takes seconds and saves guessing.
- **Use a lazy Susan for oils and sauces.** One turn shows you every bottle. Our roundup of [lazy Susan organizers](/blog/8-best-lazy-susan-organizers-for-your-kitchen/) covers sizes that fit a shelf.
- **Group by type.** Breakfast, baking, canned goods, snacks. Give each group one bin.
- **Keep produce separate.** Onions, garlic and potatoes like a cool, dark, airy spot. Store potatoes away from onions, since onions make them sprout faster. Our guide on [how long potatoes last](/blog/how-long-do-potatoes-last/) covers the best conditions.

For a full plan, read our guide on [how to organize a small kitchen with no pantry](/blog/how-to-organize-a-small-kitchen-with-no-pantry/). It goes deeper into food storage, while this guide covers the room as a whole.

If your fridge is the only cold storage, keep it organized too. Set it to 40°F or below. If you have a mini fridge, our steps for [defrosting a mini fridge](/blog/how-to-defrost-a-mini-fridge/) keep its freezer box from icing over and losing space.

## Keep Open Storage Clean and Pest-Free

Open storage is easy to reach. So are dust, grease and bugs.

- **Wipe shelves every week** with warm soapy water before grease builds up.
- **Run the range hood or a fan when you cook.** Less grease in the air means less on your dishes.
- **Cover anything you touch less than once a week** with a lidded bin.
- **Never leave food open.** Clip bags shut or move food into sealed containers.
- **Check bins once a month** for crumbs, spills and expired food.

Our guide on [keeping pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/) explains how to seal gaps so ants and pantry moths have nothing to find.

## How Do You Organize a Kitchen With Very Few Cabinets?

Maybe you have one or two cabinets, not zero. That changes the plan a little. The goal is to give those cabinets to the things that most need doors.

Our rule: bulky, heavy and ugly items go behind doors. That means pots, baking sheets, small appliances you use weekly or less, cleaning supplies, plastic containers and foil. Pretty, light and daily items go on display. That means plates, glasses, mugs on hooks, utensils in a crock and pantry food in clear containers.

Inside the cabinets, double the space with shelf risers, stacking bins and door-mounted racks. ## Keep Your Counter From Becoming the Cabinet

When there are no cabinets, the counter fills up fast. Everything with no home ends up there. That kills your prep space.

Set a limit: no more than three items stay on the counter full time. For most people, that is a coffee maker, a knife strip or crock, and a dish rack. Everything else needs a shelf, a hook or a cart.

## Common Mistakes When Organizing a Kitchen Without Cabinets

These are the mistakes we see most often. Avoid them and your kitchen will feel bigger right away.

1. **Buying storage before cutting down.** You end up with more shelves full of things you never use. Sort first, then buy.
2. **Mixing too many bin styles.** Five kinds of baskets make the room feel crowded. Stick to one style and one color.
3. **Storing food next to the stove.** Heat shortens shelf life. Keep oils, spices and dry goods at least a few feet away.
4. **Skipping the wall anchor.** A full shelving unit can tip forward. Strap it to the wall, every time.
5. **Overloading drywall.** Heavy dishes need screws in studs. Drywall anchors alone can pull out.
6. **Putting daily items up high.** If you need a step stool to reach your plates, your plan will fall apart within a week.
7. **Leaving everything open.** Some things look messy on display. Give cleaning supplies and plastic containers a lidded bin or a curtain.
8. **Forgetting airflow around appliances.** A toaster oven pushed against a shelf wall can overheat. Leave a few inches of space on all sides.

## A Sample Setup for a Cabinet-Free Studio Kitchen

Here is how we would set up a typical studio kitchen with a sink, a two-burner stove and a small fridge in about 8 feet of wall.

- **Left of the sink:** A 36-inch wire shelving unit for pantry food in clear bins on the middle shelves. Small appliances go on the bottom. Spares go on top.
- **Above the sink:** Two 10-inch-deep wall shelves for plates, bowls and glasses. Mug hooks under the bottom shelf.
- **Between sink and stove:** A wall rail for utensils and a magnetic knife strip.
- **Right of the stove:** A three-tier rolling cart with oils, salt and spatulas on top. Pans go on the bottom.
- **Beside the fridge:** A slim pull-out cart for cans and spices.

This gives every item a home within a step of where you use it.
