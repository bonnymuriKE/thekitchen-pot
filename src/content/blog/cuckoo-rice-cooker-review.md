## What Does Pressure Actually Change in the Rice?

Three things: speed, texture and how well brown rice cooks.

At normal pressure, water boils at 212°F. Seal the pot and build pressure, and the water can get hotter before it boils. Hotter water pushes into each grain faster. So the rice cooks quicker and the starch inside softens more fully.

That full softening is where the sticky, chewy texture comes from. Pressure-cooked short-grain rice turns glossy and clings together. It is the texture Korean and many Japanese dishes call for. Long-grain rice also cooks well under pressure, but it loses some of its separate, fluffy feel.

Brown rice gains the most. Its bran layer slows water down. Pressure forces water through that layer, so brown rice comes out tender instead of chewy in the middle. For a bigger picture of pressure cooking in general, see our comparison of the [Instant Pot vs a pressure cooker](/blog/instant-pot-vs-pressure-cooker/).

## High Pressure vs No Pressure: When Twin Pressure Helps

Twin pressure answers the one complaint about pressure rice: not every meal wants sticky grains.

Use **high pressure** for:

- Short-grain white rice for bowls, kimbap and sushi
- Brown rice, mixed grains and GABA rice
- Porridge and congee that should turn creamy

Use **no pressure** for:

- Jasmine and basmati rice that should stay loose
- Rice meant for fried rice tomorrow
- Steaming vegetables or dumplings

If you roll your own sushi, high pressure on short-grain rice gives the right cling. Our [sushi making kit guide](/blog/best-sushi-making-kit/) covers the rest of the setup.

## What Induction Heating (IH) Changes

A standard cooker has a heating plate under the pot. Heat travels up from the bottom, so the bottom layer of rice gets more heat than the top.

Induction works differently. Coils around the pot make a magnetic field. That field makes the metal pot itself heat up, on the sides and the bottom at once. The cooker can also raise or lower heat almost instantly, because there is no hot plate that has to cool down first.

In the bowl, that means rice that is even from top to bottom, with fewer hard grains at the edges. The same physics is why [induction cooktops](/blog/best-induction-cooktop-for-the-money/) respond so quickly when you turn them down.

## GABA Brown Rice, Explained Simply

GABA rice is brown rice that has started to sprout. Most Cuckoo models with a GABA setting soak the brown rice in warm water, around 104°F, for a couple of hours before cooking. That warm soak wakes up the germ at the tip of each grain.

The result is softer, slightly sweeter brown rice. It takes longer, so most people start it on the timer in the morning.

## Voice Navigation: Helpful or Annoying?

Most Cuckoo pressure and induction models talk. They announce the mode, the start, the steam release and when rice is ready, in English, Korean or Chinese.

The voice warns you before the steam release, so you do not lean over the valve. It also helps anyone who finds small display text hard to read.

In a small apartment, though, a talking cooker gets old fast. Most models let you turn the voice down or mute it from the settings menu, and the manual lists the button steps. If silence matters most, the Prestige Silence is the better starting point.

## How to Clean a Cuckoo Pressure Lid and Gasket

Smells and starch build up in the pressure lid, so give it a few minutes after each use. Rinse rice before cooking too. A [Japanese rice washing bowl](https://www.amazon.com/Inomata-Delicious-Japanese-Vegetable-Drainers/dp/B00IZ9IIPW/?tag=kitchenpot-20) with side drains makes that quick, and less loose starch means less sticky foam on the lid.

1. **Unplug and let it cool.** Never clean a hot pressure lid.
2. **Remove the inner lid.** On most pressure models it twists or pulls off. Wash it in warm, soapy water.
3. **Wipe the rubber packing.** This is the gasket ring on the inner lid. Clean it with a damp cloth and check for cracks or rice stuck under the edge.
4. **Clear the steam cap.** Pull it off, rinse it, and check the small holes for dried starch.
5. **Run auto clean once a week.** Add about 2 cups of water to the pot and start the auto clean cycle. Steam flushes the lid and the valve.
6. **Empty the drain tray.** Water collects here during cooking and starts to smell if left.
7. **Dry everything before closing.** Leave the lid open for an hour so the gasket dries.

Wash the nonstick inner pot by hand only. Use the plastic paddle that came with the cooker or a soft one like the [Marna stand-up rice paddle](https://www.amazon.com/Stand-Paddle-Shamoji-K-386W-Marna/dp/B003B66YRI/?tag=kitchenpot-20), which stands on its own and keeps the tip off the counter. Metal spoons are the main reason Cuckoo coatings peel.

Replace the rubber packing every couple of years with regular use. Signs it is time: steam leaking around the lid, a lid that is hard to close, or rice that comes out undercooked. Most 6-cup Cuckoo pressure models, including the CRP-ST0609F, CRP-LHTR0609F and Prestige Silence, take the [CCP-DH06 rubber packing](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=61971&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=cuckoo-rice-cooker-review&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fcuckooamerica.com%2Fproducts%2Fccp-dh06). Swap the inner pot at the same time if its coating is worn.

## Cuckoo Rice Cooker vs Instant Pot

An Instant Pot is a pressure cooker that can also make rice. A Cuckoo pressure model is a rice cooker that uses pressure.

A Cuckoo has water lines in the pot, grain programs, GABA mode and a keep-warm that holds rice soft. On an Instant Pot, you set the time yourself.

The Instant Pot wins for stews, beans, yogurt and meat. If those matter to you, read [what an Instant Pot is](/blog/what-is-an-instant-pot/) and how it stacks up in our [Instant Pot vs Ninja Foodi](/blog/instant-pot-vs-ninja-foodi/) comparison. If rice is the main event, a Cuckoo is the better tool.

## What Size Cuckoo Rice Cooker Do You Need?

## Is a Cuckoo Rice Cooker Worth It?

For regular rice eaters, yes. A good Cuckoo turns rice into something you can set and forget, and the pressure models make brown rice something you will actually cook on a weeknight.

A one-touch Cuckoo works about like any basic cooker. The real step up is the Micom, then twin pressure. Induction adds polish, but the gain in rice is smaller than the gain in price.

If you cook mostly for yourself, the 3-cup CRP-MHTR0309F pairs well with the habits in our guide to [cooking for one without wasting food](/blog/cook-for-one-without-wasting-food/).
