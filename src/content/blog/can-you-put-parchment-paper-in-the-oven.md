A sheet of parchment is the reason cookies slide off a pan and a roasting tray goes back in the cupboard clean. So can you put parchment paper in the oven? Yes. It is made for it.

The catch is heat. Parchment has a limit, printed on the box, and most rolls stop somewhere between 420°F and 450°F. Push past that, or let it touch a heating element, and it turns from a helper into a problem. Below are the exact rules, the jobs where foil or a silicone mat does better, and the small tricks that make parchment easier to use.

**The fast answer:** Parchment paper is oven safe up to the temperature on its box, usually 420°F to 450°F. Above that it browns, gets brittle and can char at the edges. Never use it under the broiler, and never let it touch an element, a gas flame or the oven walls. Either side can face up, since both are coated. Wax paper is not a substitute and does not belong in a hot oven.

## Can You Put Parchment Paper in the Oven?

Yes. Parchment paper is paper treated to resist heat and moisture, then coated with a thin layer of silicone. That coating is what makes food release and stops grease from soaking through. It is designed to sit in a hot oven on a baking sheet or in a pan.

Three conditions keep it safe:

1. **Stay at or under the rated temperature.** Read the box. That number is the real limit for that roll.
2. **Keep it inside the pan.** Trim anything that hangs more than an inch over the edge. Loose flaps are what reach the elements.
3. **Put food or a pan on it.** Parchment needs weight to stay down. An empty sheet in a hot oven can lift and drift.

Follow those three and parchment is one of the safest liners in the kitchen. It is the default for cookies, sheet pan dinners, roasted vegetables and cakes. In a convection oven, the fan makes rule three even more important. Our guide to [baking cookies in a convection oven](/blog/how-to-bake-cookies-in-a-convection-oven/) covers the rest of that setup.

## What Temperature Is Parchment Paper Safe To?

Most parchment is rated somewhere between 420°F and 450°F. A few brands go a little higher. Commercial sheets sold for bakeries usually sit in the same range.

Here is how that plays out with common recipes:

| Oven setting | Is parchment fine? | What to expect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 325°F to 375°F (cookies, cakes, muffins) | Yes, any roll | Paper stays pale and clean |
| 400°F (roasted vegetables, sausages) | Yes, any roll | Slight tan on exposed edges |
| 425°F | Yes if the box says 425°F or higher | Edges brown, paper still holds together |
| 450°F | Only if rated to 450°F | Exposed paper turns dark and crisp |
| 475°F to 500°F, short bakes | Risky; keep it brief | Edges blacken and crumble |
| 500°F+ for long bakes | No | Paper chars and can smolder |
| Broil | No | Direct flame or glowing element can ignite it |

### Can You Put Parchment Paper in the Oven at 425?

Usually, yes. Most rolls handle 425°F, which covers roasted potatoes, chicken thighs and most sheet pan meals. The paper around the food will tan. The paper under the food stays lighter, because the food shields it.

### Can You Put Parchment Paper in the Oven at 450?

Only with a roll rated to 450°F. Many common rolls stop at 420°F or 425°F. At 450°F a lower-rated roll will not burst into flames on a pan, but it darkens fast and crumbles when you lift it. For crispy 450°F roasting, foil or a bare, oiled pan does the job better. Our guide to [cooking sausages in the oven](/blog/how-to-cook-sausages-in-the-oven/) shows the difference both make to browning.

## Can Parchment Paper Catch on Fire in the Oven?

Yes, but only in the wrong spot. On a pan with food on it, at its rated heat, parchment does not catch fire. It browns. The risk comes from three situations:

- **Direct contact** with a heating element, a gas flame or a broiler.
- **Loose edges** that hang over the pan and drift toward the walls or the top element.
- **High heat for a long time**, where exposed corners blacken and start to smolder.

If parchment ever catches fire, turn the oven off and keep the door shut. The flame will run out of air in a few seconds. Opening the door feeds it. Once it is out, let the oven cool, then pull out the burnt paper. If you are cleaning up char and ash afterward, our guide to [cleaning an oven](/blog/how-to-clean-an-oven/) covers the gentle way.

## When Not to Use Parchment Paper

Parchment is the right liner most of the time. These are the times it is not.

### Under the Broiler

Never put parchment under the broiler. A broiler sits a few inches above the food and runs well past 500°F. The paper will char, curl up toward the element and can catch fire. When you broil, line the pan with foil instead. A sturdy roll like [Reynolds Wrap Heavy Duty Aluminum Foil](https://www.amazon.com/Reynolds-Wrap-Heavy-Aluminum-Square/dp/B00QIT8T6E/?tag=kitchenpot-20) holds its shape around the pan edges and does not tear when you scrape off melted cheese.

### Very Hot Pizza for Long Bakes

Pizza at 500°F to 550°F is a gray area. Parchment is a handy way to slide dough onto a hot stone or steel. The fix is to pull the paper out after 3 to 5 minutes, once the crust has set. Leave it in for a full 10 to 15 minutes at 550°F and it will blacken and flake onto the crust. If you bake on a stone often, see our guide to the [best pizza stone](/blog/best-pizza-stone/), and for leftovers, our tips on [reheating pizza](/blog/how-to-reheat-pizza/) cover the slice side. Outdoor ovens that run at 700°F and above are no place for parchment at all, which is why our [outdoor pizza oven](/blog/best-outdoor-pizza-oven/) guide sticks to flour and a peel.

### In a Toaster Oven Near the Elements

Toaster ovens put the elements only a few inches from the pan. Parchment is fine there, but trim it so no edge sticks up or out. Leave at least an inch of space between paper and element on every side. Our guide to [baking in a toaster oven](/blog/can-you-bake-in-a-toaster-oven/) covers rack position and pan sizes for that kind of oven.

### On the Rack by Itself or During Preheating

Do not lay an empty sheet on the rack while the oven heats. Hot air lifts it, and in a convection oven the fan can blow it straight into the element. Put the paper in with the food, not before.

### With Very Wet Food for Long Braises

Parchment resists moisture, but a long soak in liquid weakens it. For a stew or braise in a Dutch oven, a parchment lid pressed onto the surface is fine. A sheet lining the bottom of a pan full of sauce for two hours will tear when you lift it.

## Which Side of Parchment Paper Goes Up?

Either side. Nearly all parchment sold for home use is coated with silicone on both sides. Food releases from both faces the same way.

Some rolls feel slightly shinier on one side. That comes from how the paper was rolled and pressed, not from a missing coating. The only reason to pay attention to sides is the curl. Placing the paper curl-side down, so the edges bend toward the pan, helps it lie flat.

Pre-printed paper is the one exception. Grid sheets with printed lines are meant to go ink-side down, so the printed side never touches food.

## Bleached vs Unbleached Parchment Paper

White parchment is bleached to make it white. Brown parchment skips that step. Both carry the same silicone coating, both handle the same heat range, and both release food the same way.

The difference is mostly preference. Unbleached paper is less processed, and many people simply like that. White paper makes it easier to see how dark cookie bottoms are getting. Neither one changes how your food tastes.

For an everyday unbleached roll, [If You Care Parchment Baking Paper](https://www.amazon.com/If-You-Care-Unbleached-Greaseproof/dp/B09MSMYXYD/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is chlorine-free, silicone coated and comes on a sturdy roll that tears cleanly. It is also compostable when it only held dry baked goods, which matters to people who bake often.

## Parchment Paper vs Wax Paper

These two get mixed up more than any other pair in the kitchen. They look alike, they sit side by side in the store, and both are called "baking paper" in some places. They are not the same.

- **Parchment paper** is coated with silicone. It handles oven heat.
- **Wax paper** is coated with paraffin or soybean wax. The wax melts in a hot oven, smokes and can catch fire.

So wax paper does not go in the oven on a bare pan. The one old exception is a cake pan liner fully covered by batter. The batter shields the paper from direct heat. Even then, parchment does the same job without any risk, so there is no reason to choose wax paper when you have parchment.

Wax paper still earns its place. Use it to:

- Line a counter before rolling out cookie dough.
- Separate burger patties or cookies before freezing.
- Wrap sandwiches and cheese.
- Catch drips when you dip strawberries in chocolate.

## Parchment vs Aluminum Foil vs Silicone Baking Mats

Each liner wins at something. Here is how they compare in a regular oven.

| | Parchment paper | Aluminum foil | Silicone baking mat | Wax paper |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Oven safe | Yes, to its rating | Yes, any oven heat | Yes, usually to about 480°F | No |
| Broiler safe | No | Yes | No | No |
| Food release | Very good | Poor unless greased | Very good | Good, but only cold |
| Bottom browning | Even, moderate | Strong, fast | Light, slower | Not for heat |
| Acidic foods (tomato, citrus) | Fine | Can react and stain | Fine | Not for heat |
| Reusable | A few times | Rarely | Hundreds of times | No |
| Shapes into packets or tents | Yes, folds | Yes, best | No | No |
| Cut to fit | Yes | Yes | No, never cut | Yes |
| Best job | Cookies, cakes, sheet pan meals | Broiling, high-heat roasting, tenting | Cookies, candy, kneading surface | Cold prep and freezing |

### When Foil Wins

Foil handles any heat your oven makes, including the broiler. It molds tightly around a pan and makes tents and packets that hold their shape. It also browns food harder, because metal passes heat straight through. The downside is sticking. Without oil, cookies and fish glue themselves to foil, and tomato or lemon can react with it.

### When a Silicone Mat Wins

A silicone mat is a reusable liner made of food-grade silicone over a woven fiberglass layer. It lasts for years and never blows around. The classic is the [Silpat Premium Non-Stick Silicone Baking Mat, Half Sheet](https://www.amazon.com/Silpat-Premium-Non-Stick-Silicone-Baking-Mat/dp/B00008T960/?tag=kitchenpot-20), sized for a standard 13 x 18-inch half sheet pan. It is the best surface for sticky jobs like caramel, brittle and macarons.

Mats do change results. They insulate a little, so cookie bottoms bake paler and some doughs spread wider. For crusty bread rolls and baguettes, a perforated mat lets steam escape underneath. The [Demarle Silpain Perforated Baking Mat](https://www.awin1.com/cread.php?awinmid=88997&awinaffid=1956629&clickref=can-you-put-parchment-paper-in-the-oven&ued=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.bakedeco.com%2Fdetail.asp%3Fid%3D30146) from Kerekes Kitchen covers a half sheet and handles up to 480°F. Small holes run across the whole surface, so bread crisps on the bottom the way it would on a bare pan.

Never cut a silicone mat, and never use one under the broiler. If you are short on drawer space, a mat rolls loosely and stores flat inside a sheet pan. Our list of [space-saving baking tool essentials](/blog/space-saving-baking-tool-essentials/) has more pieces that earn their spot.

### When Parchment Wins

Parchment is the flexible middle. It releases food like a mat, folds like foil and costs little per use. It cuts to any pan shape, which neither foil nor a mat does well. For cakes, en papillote, and anything you want to lift out of the pan whole, parchment is the right liner.

## How to Make Parchment Paper Lie Flat

Paper off a roll wants to curl back into a tube. It rolls over your cookie dough and flips up at the corners. Four fixes work:

1. **The crumple trick.** Tear off a sheet, crush it into a tight ball, then open it up and smooth it into the pan. The creases break the curl. This also makes it easier to press paper into a loaf pan or a square cake pan.
2. **A dab of fat.** Put a small dot of butter, oil or cooking spray in each corner of the pan before laying the paper down. The paper sticks in place. This is also the easiest way to stop it from sliding when you spread batter.
3. **Flip the curl.** Lay the sheet so the edges curl down toward the pan, then press it flat with your palm.
4. **Buy flat sheets.** Pre-cut sheets come flat out of the box. The [Katbite Parchment Paper Baking Sheets, 12 x 16 Inch](https://www.amazon.com/Katbite-Unbleached-Parchment-Non-stick-Steaming/dp/B07L9S9C7D/?tag=kitchenpot-20) fit a standard half sheet pan with no trimming. If you prefer a roll, [Reynolds Kitchens Stay Flat Parchment Paper with SmartGrid](https://www.amazon.com/Reynolds-Kitchens-Parchment-SmartGrid-Non-Stick/dp/B00C37QN2C/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is made to resist curling, and its printed grid helps you space cookies evenly.

A flat, heavy pan helps too. Thin sheets that twist in the heat lift the paper with them, which is the problem our guide on [why baking sheets warp in the oven](/blog/why-baking-sheets-warp-in-the-oven/) explains. A thick aluminum pan like the [Nordic Ware Natural Aluminum Commercial Baker's Half Sheet](https://www.amazon.com/Nordic-Ware-half-sheet-pan/dp/B000G0KJG4/?tag=kitchenpot-20) stays flat and gives a 12 x 16 sheet a snug home. ## How to Line a Round Cake Pan With Parchment

A parchment circle on the bottom of a cake pan is the surest way to get a layer out in one piece. Grease alone often leaves a patch stuck in the middle.

Here is the fold-and-snip method:

1. Tear off a square of parchment a little bigger than the pan.
2. Fold it in half, then in half again, so you have a small square.
3. Fold that square corner to corner into a triangle. Fold it once more into a thin cone.
4. Hold the point of the cone at the center of the upside-down pan.
5. Snip the wide end where it meets the pan's edge.
6. Unfold. You have a circle the size of the pan's base.

Grease the pan lightly, press the circle in, then grease the top of the paper too. The fat under the paper holds it still. The fat on top helps the cake release. Flour the sides as usual.

For cheesecake or a tall cake, add a strip of parchment around the inside wall. Cut it about 1 inch taller than the pan so you can lift the cake out by the collar.

## Cooking En Papillote: Fish and Vegetables in Parchment

En papillote is French for "in paper." You seal food in a parchment packet, and it steams in its own juices in the oven. It is one of the best things parchment can do, and it leaves no pan to scrub.

How to make a packet for one fillet:

1. Cut a sheet about 15 x 12 inches. Fold it in half and cut a large half-heart shape from the fold, so it opens into a heart.
2. Lay thin vegetables near the fold on one side: sliced zucchini, cherry tomatoes cut in half, thin onion, a few green beans.
3. Set a 1-inch fish fillet on top. Add salt, pepper, a pat of butter or a spoon of olive oil, and a slice of lemon.
4. Fold the other half over. Starting at the top of the heart, make small overlapping folds along the edge, pressing each one hard. Twist the tip to lock it.
5. Bake on a sheet pan at 400°F for 12 to 15 minutes. The packet will puff.
6. Cut it open at the table, away from your face. The steam is hot.

Fish is done at 145°F in the thickest part. Thinner fillets need about 10 minutes, and thicker salmon may need 18. Chicken breast sliced thin works too but must reach 165°F, so give it 20 to 25 minutes.

Foil packets do the same job, but acidic lemon and tomato can react with aluminum, and foil hides the puff that tells you the packet is steaming. Parchment does not have either problem. ## Can You Reuse Parchment Paper?

Yes, for dry, clean bakes. A sheet that held cookies can usually go two or three more rounds. Wipe off crumbs, let it cool, and lay it back on the pan.

Throw it away when:

- It is dark brown, brittle or cracking at the edges.
- It held raw meat, fish or anything greasy.
- It has soaked through with butter or juice.
- It tears when you lift it.

Reusing works best at moderate heat. A sheet that baked cookies at 350°F holds up far better than one that roasted vegetables at 425°F. If you go through a lot of paper, that is the point where a silicone mat pays for itself.

## Parchment in the Air Fryer, Microwave and Steamer

Parchment works in more places than the oven, but each has its own rule.

- **Air fryer:** Yes, with food on top so it cannot blow into the element. ## When Parchment Goes Wrong: Quick Fixes

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper turned dark and brittle | Oven above the paper's rating | Check the box; switch to foil above that heat |
| Smoke or burning smell | Edge touching an element or wall | Trim paper to the pan; leave 1 inch of space |
| Paper flew up and stuck to food | Convection fan or preheating empty | Add paper with the food; weigh corners with dough |
| Cake stuck to the paper | Peeled too late or no grease on top | Grease the paper; peel while the cake is still warm |
| Cookies spread too much | Warm dough, not the paper | Chill dough 30 minutes; use a cool pan |
| Paper stuck to caramel or meringue | Sugar fused to the surface | Lay the paper on a damp towel for a minute, then peel |
| Bottom of food pale and soft | Paper slows heat a little; rack too high | Move the pan one rack lower, or use foil for that recipe |
| Wax smell and smoke | Wax paper used by mistake | Turn the oven off, let it cool, replace with parchment |

Two habits prevent most of these. First, read the temperature on the box once and remember it. Second, trim every sheet to the pan before it goes in. In a small oven, those two habits matter even more, since the elements sit closer and the fan, if there is one, works harder. ## Do You Need Special Parchment for Baking?

No special type is needed for most home baking. Any roll labeled parchment and silicone coated works for cookies, cakes and roasting. The only real choices are format and color.

- **Rolls** are cheapest per sheet and cut to any size. They curl.
- **Pre-cut sheets** lie flat and fit a half sheet pan exactly. They cost a little more per sheet.
- **Pre-cut rounds** save time for layer cakes, steamers and air fryer baskets.
- **Grid or printed sheets** help with spacing cookies and piping even shapes.

Butcher paper is a different product. It is plain, uncoated paper used to wrap smoked meat at low temperatures. It does not release baked goods well, so it is not a parchment swap. Freezer paper has a plastic coating on one side and should never go in the oven.
