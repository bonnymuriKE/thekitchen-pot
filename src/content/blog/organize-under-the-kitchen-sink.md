Knowing how to organize under the kitchen sink turns the most awkward cabinet in the house into useful storage. Pipes, a disposal and dampness make it tricky.

Most under-sink cabinets fail for the same reasons. Bottles get shoved in around the drain, trash bags slide behind the pipes, and a slow drip goes unnoticed for months. Below is the plan we recommend, from measuring around the plumbing to choosing organizers that fit, storing cleaners safely and catching leaks early.

**The short version:** Empty the cabinet, check for drips and lay down a waterproof mat first. Then measure each open zone around the pipes and garbage disposal, and fill each zone with one organizer that fits it. Hang spray bottles on a tension rod, put flat items on the door and give trash its own pull-out if there is room. Keep chemicals locked or up high if children visit, and never store bleach next to ammonia.

## How to Organize Under the Kitchen Sink in 7 Steps

Here is the order we follow every time. Doing it in this order stops you from buying organizers that do not fit.

1. **Empty everything.** Take every item out and set it on a towel on the floor.
2. **Check for leaks.** Run the water, then look and feel along every pipe and joint. The leak section below shows exactly where.
3. **Clean and dry the floor.** Wipe it with warm soapy water and leave the doors open for an hour.
4. **Lay down a mat.** A waterproof mat with a raised edge protects the cabinet floor from future drips.
5. **Measure the open zones.** Note the width, depth and height of every space the pipes leave free.
6. **Sort what you own.** Keep only what you use at the sink. Move the rest to a better home.
7. **Fit one organizer per zone.** Add sliding drawers, a tension rod or door racks to match each space.

The cabinet under the sink should hold sink jobs only: washing dishes, wiping counters, taking out trash. Our [small kitchen cabinet organization ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-cabinet-organization-ideas/) cover the other cabinets, so overflow has somewhere to go.

## What Is Under Your Sink: Know the Plumbing First

You cannot organize around pipes you do not understand. Open the doors and find these parts before you buy anything.

- **Drain and P-trap.** The P-trap is the U-shaped bend in the drain pipe. It holds a little water to block sewer smells. It usually sits near the middle of the cabinet.
- **Supply lines and shut-off valves.** These are the two small hoses for hot and cold water, each with a valve (an oval or round handle) near the back wall. You must be able to reach them fast in an emergency.
- **Dishwasher lines.** If you have a dishwasher, a drain hose and a water line run through the side wall of the cabinet.
- **Garbage disposal.** A cylinder hanging under one sink bowl, often about a foot tall. It drops the usable height on that side to a few inches.
- **Outlet or switch box.** Disposals and some dishwashers plug into an outlet inside the cabinet.

Our rule is simple: **never block the shut-off valves.** If a supply line bursts, you need to turn off the water in seconds, not dig through bottles first. Leave a clear path from the door to both valves.

## How to Measure Under the Sink for Organizers

Most failed organizers were bought by guessing. A tape measure and five minutes save you a return trip.

### The Five Measurements That Matter

Measure from inside the cabinet, not the outside. Write each number down and sketch the pipes on paper.

1. **Door opening width.** Measure between the inside edges of the face frame (the wood border around the opening). This is often 2 to 4 inches narrower than the inside of the cabinet. Pull-out organizers must fit through this gap.
2. **Center post.** Many double-door cabinets have a vertical strip between the doors. Measure the opening on each side of it separately.
3. **Interior depth.** Measure from the back of the face frame to the back wall. Sink base cabinets sit under a 24-inch counter, so expect about 21 to 23 inches inside.
4. **Clear height on each side.** Measure from the cabinet floor to the lowest pipe on the left and on the right. This is your real height limit.
5. **Pipe gaps.** Measure from each side wall to the nearest pipe. That number is the widest organizer that can sit there.

Subtract half an inch from every number for wiggle room. Also open and close the doors while you look. Hinges can stick into the opening by an inch or more, and they will catch a drawer that fits on paper.

### Typical Sink Cabinet Sizes

These are common sink base widths and what usually fits inside them. Use them as a starting point, then trust your own tape measure.

| Sink cabinet width | Typical open space | What usually fits |
|---|---|---|
| 30 inches | Two side zones of 8 to 10 inches each | Two narrow sliding drawers or one drawer plus a door rack |
| 33 inches | Two side zones of 9 to 11 inches each | Two sliding drawers and a tension rod |
| 36 inches | Two side zones of 10 to 12 inches each | Two-tier drawers on both sides, a tension rod and a small bin |
| Corner sink (diagonal) | Deep, wide space with an awkward back corner | Lazy Susan or half-moon turntable, door caddy |
| 24-inch sink in a studio | One side zone of 6 to 8 inches | Slim door caddy, tension rod, stacking bins |

## How to Organize Under a Kitchen Sink With a Garbage Disposal

A garbage disposal takes the middle or one side of the cabinet. The trick is to stop treating that space as storage at all.

### Make the Disposal a No-Storage Zone

Leave the area directly under and around the disposal empty. You need to reach three things there:

- **The reset button.** A small red button on the bottom. Press it when the disposal hums but stops working.
- **The hex key socket.** A small six-sided hole in the center of the bottom. A hex key (a small L-shaped wrench) fits here to free a jam.
- **The power cord and outlet.** Keep bottles away from the plug, where a drip could reach it.

Tape the hex key to the inside of the cabinet door, or hang it on a small adhesive hook. You will thank yourself the next time a fruit pit jams the blades.

### Build Around It, Not Under It

With the middle taken, use the sides, the door and the space above the floor.

- **On the disposal side:** Fit a narrow single-tier drawer or a slim bin on the floor beside the unit. Only use the height that is left.
- **On the open side:** This is your main storage. A two-tier sliding organizer usually fits here.
- **On the doors:** Flat items like sponges, brushes and bags go here, freeing the floor.
- **Up high:** A tension rod above the drain line holds spray bottles by their triggers.

Disposals vibrate when they run. Keep glass bottles and anything tippy away from it, and do not stack bins against the housing.

## Two-Tier Sliding Organizers: The Workhorse of the Cabinet

A two-tier sliding organizer has two pull-out baskets stacked in one frame. You slide each basket out to reach the back instead of kneeling and digging. For most people this is the first thing worth buying.

### Choosing the Right One

Match the organizer to your pipe gaps, not to the full cabinet width.

- **Width:** Most fit spaces from about 8 to 13 inches wide. Measure the pipe gap, then subtract half an inch.
- **Height:** Two tiers need about 14 to 18 inches of clear height. Some models let you move the top shelf to clear a pipe.
- **Depth:** Pick one at least 2 inches shorter than the interior depth, so it does not hit the back wall or the valves.
- **U-shaped units:** These wrap around the drain with a gap in the middle. They suit cabinets with a single centered drain and no disposal.
- **Material:** Choose metal with a rust-resistant coating or sturdy plastic. Plain wood swells when it gets wet.

### What Goes on Each Tier

Put the things you grab daily on the top tier at the front. Put refills and backups on the bottom tier. Heavy items, such as a big jug of dish detergent, belong on the bottom so the frame stays steady when you pull it out.

Pull-out drawers are the same idea we use in the rest of the kitchen. ## Use a Tension Rod to Hang Spray Bottles

A tension rod is a spring-loaded curtain rod that holds itself in place between two walls. It needs no screws, so it suits renters. It is the cheapest upgrade under the sink.

Here is how to set it up:

1. Measure the inside width of the cabinet at about 4 to 6 inches below the top.
2. Buy a rod whose range covers that number, with your width near the middle of the range.
3. Twist it in place above the drain pipe, toward the front of the cabinet.
4. Hang spray bottles by their trigger handles. Add a few S-hooks for gloves and a scrub brush.

A rod holds about four to six spray bottles across a 30 to 36-inch cabinet. That clears the floor for bins and drawers below.

A second rod lower down can hold a roll of trash bags. Slide the rod through the cardboard tube and pull bags off like paper towels.

We use the same idea on walls and inside doors elsewhere in the kitchen. See [vertical storage ideas for small kitchens](/blog/vertical-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/) for more ways to use height.

## Use the Cabinet Doors for Flat Items

The back of each cabinet door is free storage. It holds light, flat things that would otherwise float loose on the floor.

### What Works on a Door

- Sponges and scrub pads in a small wire caddy
- Rubber gloves on adhesive hooks
- A slim bin for trash bags or dish cloths
- The disposal hex key and a small flashlight
- A rolled-up sink mat or drying towel

### How to Mount It

Close the door slowly with the rack held in place before you stick anything down. Pipes, the disposal and drawers will often hit a door rack. You need about 2 inches of clearance between the door and anything behind it.

Use removable adhesive strips rated for the weight, or over-the-door hooks if your door has a thin top edge. Keep everything light. Heavy jugs on a door strain the hinges and make the door sag.

The same door trick works on other cabinets. We show a few more in [small kitchen storage ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-storage-ideas/).

## Small Kitchen Trash Can Under the Sink

Putting the trash under the sink frees floor space and hides the bin from view. It works best in cabinets without a disposal on that side.

### Your Three Options

| Trash setup | Best for | Space needed |
|---|---|---|
| Pull-out frame with one bin | Most sink cabinets, solo cooks | One side zone about 11 to 15 inches wide |
| Pull-out with two bins (trash and recycling) | Couples, homes that recycle a lot | About 15 to 18 inches wide on one side |
| Door-mounted bin | Tiny cabinets, studio kitchens | Clear space behind the door when closed |
| Small can on the cabinet floor | Renters who cannot screw in a frame | A flat floor zone, any width |

Under-sink bins are small, usually 7 to 9 gallons (28 to 36 quarts). That is well under the size of a standard 13-gallon kitchen bag. Plan to take out the trash every day or two, or keep a larger recycling bin elsewhere.

### Keep It Clean and Pest-Free

A bin under the sink sits in a warm, dark spot. That attracts fruit flies and roaches if food scraps sit too long.

- Line the bin with a bag that fits snugly, so nothing leaks onto the cabinet floor.
- Rinse food containers before they go in recycling.
- Take wet scraps out daily, or freeze them in a container until trash day.
- Wipe the bin with hot soapy water once a month.

Our guide on [how to keep pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/) explains why the sink cabinet is a common entry point. Gaps around pipes are easy for bugs to use, so seal them as part of this job.

## How to Organize Under a Corner Kitchen Sink

A corner sink usually sits on a diagonal cabinet. The space inside is deep and wide, but the back corner is hard to reach.

Our favorite fix is a turntable. A lazy Susan, meaning a round tray that spins, brings items from the back to the front with a turn. Pick a two-tier one so you use the height, and measure the pipe gap first so it can spin freely. Our list of the [best lazy Susan organizers](/blog/8-best-lazy-susan-organizers-for-your-kitchen/) includes sizes that suit sink cabinets.

If the drain sits in the middle, use two half-moon turntables, one on each side. Store backup supplies in the far corner in a bin with a handle, so you can pull the whole bin out at once.

Corner sinks often show up in L-shaped layouts. Our [small kitchen layout ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-layout-ideas/) explain how the sink corner fits into the rest of the work zone.

## How to Organize Cleaners Under the Kitchen Sink

Cleaners are the biggest group of items under most sinks. Sort them by job, store them upright and keep them away from food.

### Group by Task

Put each group in its own open-top bin with a handle. Pull out one bin, clean, and put it back.

- **Dishes:** dish soap refill, dishwasher detergent, rinse aid, brushes
- **Counters and stove:** all-purpose spray, degreaser, microfiber cloths
- **Floors:** floor cleaner, a scrub brush
- **Special jobs:** stainless steel polish, oven cleaner, drain cleaner

Most people own far more cleaners than they use. Two or three sprays cover almost every kitchen job. For example, our guide on [how to clean stainless steel pans](/blog/how-to-clean-stainless-steel-pans/) needs little more than dish soap and baking soda. Our steps on [how to clean a dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/) use a cup of vinegar.

Decant only into bottles you label clearly. Never put cleaner in an old drink bottle. That is how accidental poisonings happen.

### Never Mix Bleach and Ammonia

This rule matters more than any organizer. Bleach mixed with ammonia releases toxic chloramine gas. Bleach mixed with acids, like vinegar or some toilet and rust cleaners, releases chlorine gas. Both can injure your lungs in a small, closed room.

- Store bleach in its own bin, away from glass cleaners, which often contain ammonia.
- Do not use two cleaners on the same surface back to back without rinsing between them.
- Read the label for "contains ammonia" or "do not mix with bleach" before you buy.
- Keep the kitchen window open or the range hood fan on while you clean. Our [small kitchen ventilation guide](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) shows how to clear the air fast.

If someone breathes in fumes or swallows a cleaner, get them to fresh air and call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 in the United States.

## Child Safety Under the Sink

The sink cabinet sits at exactly the height of a crawling baby. If young children live in or visit your home, plan for that.

1. **Fit a cabinet lock.** Magnetic locks are hidden inside and open with a key magnet. Adhesive strap locks work on most doors and need no drilling, which suits renters.
2. **Move the strongest products up high.** Drain cleaner, oven cleaner, dishwasher pods and bleach belong in a high cabinet, not just behind a lock.
3. **Keep products in their original bottles.** The label carries the first-aid instructions.
4. **Close caps every time.** Child-resistant caps only work when they are clicked shut.

Dishwasher pods look like candy to small children. Store them in a high cabinet in their sealed tub. A tidy high shelf is easier to set up after you clear the counters, as we show in [countertop organization ideas for a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/).

## How to Protect the Cabinet Under the Sink From Leaks

The cabinet floor under a sink is usually particleboard, which is wood chips glued together. It swells and crumbles once it gets wet, and it rarely dries back flat. Protect it before you put anything back in.

### Lay Down a Leak Mat

A leak mat is a waterproof tray or liner with a raised edge. It catches drips and holds them, so the water sits in the mat instead of soaking into the wood.

| Protection option | What it does | Our take |
|---|---|---|
| Molded mat with raised edge | Holds spills of up to several cups | Our first choice for any sink cabinet |
| Cut-to-fit shelf liner | Stops scratches and small spills | Fine for dry cabinets, not enough under pipes |
| Peel-and-stick vinyl | Seals the wood surface | Good under a mat, not instead of one |
| Battery water alarm | Beeps when water touches its sensor | Cheap insurance, place it under the P-trap |
| Smart leak sensor | Sends an alert to your phone | Worth it if you travel or rent out the unit |

Trim the mat with scissors to wrap around the pipes coming up through the floor if needed. Make sure your organizers sit inside the raised edge, not on top of it.

### Check for Drips Once a Month

Most under-sink leaks start small. A monthly check catches them before they do damage. Set a reminder on your phone.

1. Clear the front of the cabinet so you can see the pipes.
2. Fill the sink a few inches, then pull the plug and let it drain.
3. Run a dry paper towel along the P-trap, every joint, the supply lines and the valves.
4. Look for wet spots, rust, white mineral crust or a musty smell.
5. Check the disposal's bottom and the dishwasher hose connection too.

A damp paper towel tells you exactly where the leak is. A loose slip nut, the large plastic ring that holds the P-trap together, can often be tightened by hand. A drip from a supply line or a valve needs a plumber, or your landlord if you rent.

Also turn each shut-off valve off and back on once or twice a year. Valves that never move can seize, and a stuck valve is no help in an emergency.

## What Should Be Stored Under the Kitchen Sink?

Keep items that belong to sink jobs and can handle a little moisture.

**Good choices:**

- Dish soap refills and dishwasher detergent
- Sponges, scrub brushes and rubber gloves
- Trash bags and recycling bags
- All-purpose spray, glass cleaner and microfiber cloths
- A small bucket for mopping or soaking
- A small flashlight and the disposal hex key

A small fire extinguisher is better near the kitchen exit than deep under the sink. In a fire, you want to grab it on your way toward the door, not reach past a burning stove.

## What Should Never Be Stored Under a Kitchen Sink?

Some things do not belong in a damp, dark cabinet next to pipes.

- **Food and pet food.** Moisture and pests get into it fast. Move it to a dry cabinet. Our guide on [how to organize a small kitchen with no pantry](/blog/how-to-organize-a-small-kitchen-with-no-pantry/) has places to put it, and [airtight food storage containers](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/) keep bugs out.
- **Flammables.** Paint, paint thinner, solvents, lighter fluid and propane need a cool, dry spot away from the home's living area. Our [balcony grilling safety rules](/blog/balcony-grilling-safety-tips-and-rules/) cover where propane should live.
- **Paper goods in cardboard.** Paper towel and napkin stock soak up water and grow mold. Keep only one spare roll in a sealed bin.
- **Small appliances and electronics.** One leak can ruin them. Find them a dry shelf with our tips to [make more counter space in a small kitchen](/blog/make-more-counter-space-in-a-small-kitchen/).
- **Pots, pans and baking sheets.** They rust, and they crowd the pipes. See [how to store pots and pans in a small kitchen](/blog/store-pots-and-pans-in-a-small-kitchen/) instead.
- **Strong chemicals in a home with children.** These go up high, as explained above.

## Under Sink Organization Ideas for Small and Rental Kitchens

Small kitchens need every inch to work. Here are extra ideas that cost little and leave no marks.

- **Stackable clear bins.** You can see what is inside without opening them. Use them for refills and backups.
- **A battery puck light.** Stick one to the top of the cabinet so you can see the back. Our [small kitchen lighting ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-lighting-ideas/) cover other battery lights that need no wiring.
- **An over-the-sink rack or board.** Moving the dish brush and soap above the sink frees space below. An [over-the-sink cutting board](/blog/best-over-the-sink-cutting-board/) also adds prep room.
- **Label the bins.** Short labels like "Dishes" and "Floors" keep the system in place when someone else puts things away.
- **Keep one empty bin.** New products need a landing spot. Without it, they get shoved behind the pipes.

## Common Mistakes When Organizing Under the Sink

These are the mistakes we see most often. Each one either wastes space or causes damage.

- **Buying organizers before measuring.** A drawer that fits the cabinet width but not the pipe gap is useless. Measure each zone first.
- **Blocking the shut-off valves.** In a leak, every second counts. Keep a clear path to both valves.
- **Skipping the leak mat.** A slow drip on bare particleboard can ruin the cabinet floor before you notice it.
- **Storing bleach next to ammonia cleaners.** A spill or a mix-up can release toxic gas. Keep them in separate bins.
- **Overfilling the door.** Heavy bottles on a door rack pull the hinges loose. Doors are for light, flat items only.
- **Cramming the disposal area.** Bottles against the disposal get knocked over, and you cannot reach the reset button.
- **Keeping duplicates.** Three half-empty sprays for the same job take a whole tier. Finish one before you open the next.

Fix these, and the cabinet stays organized, dry and safe for years.
