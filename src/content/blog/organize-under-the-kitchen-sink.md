Knowing how to organize under the kitchen sink turns the most awkward cabinet in the house into useful storage. Pipes, a disposal and dampness make it tricky.

Most under-sink cabinets fail for the same reasons. Bottles get shoved in around the drain, trash bags slide behind the pipes, and a slow drip goes unnoticed for months. Below is the plan we recommend, from measuring around the plumbing to choosing organizers that fit, storing cleaners safely and catching leaks early.

**The short version:** Empty the cabinet, check for drips and lay down a waterproof mat first. Then measure each open zone around the pipes and garbage disposal, and fill each zone with one organizer that fits it. Hang spray bottles on a tension rod, put flat items on the door and give trash its own pull-out if there is room. Keep chemicals locked or up high if children visit, and never store bleach next to ammonia.

## How to Organize Under the Kitchen Sink in 7 Steps

Follow this order so you never buy organizers that do not fit.

1. **Empty everything.** Take every item out and set it on a towel on the floor.
2. **Check for leaks.** Run the water, then feel along every pipe and joint.
3. **Clean and dry the floor.** Wipe it with warm soapy water and leave the doors open for an hour.
4. **Lay down a mat.** A waterproof mat with a raised edge protects the cabinet floor from future drips.
5. **Measure the open zones.** Note the width, depth and height of every space the pipes leave free.
6. **Sort what you own.** Keep only what you use at the sink. Move the rest to a better home.
7. **Fit one organizer per zone.** Add sliding drawers, a tension rod or door racks to match each space.

The cabinet under the sink should hold sink jobs only: washing dishes, wiping counters, taking out trash. Our [small kitchen cabinet organization ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-cabinet-organization-ideas/) cover the other cabinets, so overflow has somewhere to go.

## What Is Under Your Sink: Know the Plumbing First

Find these parts before you buy anything.

- **Drain and P-trap.** The P-trap is the U-shaped bend in the drain pipe that blocks sewer smells. It usually sits near the middle.
- **Supply lines and shut-off valves.** Two small hoses for hot and cold water, each with a valve (an oval or round handle) near the back wall.
- **Dishwasher lines.** A drain hose and water line run through the side wall.
- **Garbage disposal.** A cylinder hanging under one sink bowl, often about a foot tall. It drops the usable height on that side to a few inches.
- **Outlet or switch box.** Disposals and some dishwashers plug into an outlet inside the cabinet.

Our rule is simple: **never block the shut-off valves.** If a supply line bursts, you need to turn off the water in seconds.

## How to Measure Under the Sink for Organizers

### The Five Measurements That Matter

Measure inside the cabinet and sketch the pipes on paper.

1. **Door opening width.** Measure between the inside edges of the face frame (the wood border around the opening). This is often 2 to 4 inches narrower than the inside of the cabinet. Pull-out organizers must fit through this gap.
2. **Center post.** Many double-door cabinets have a vertical strip between the doors. Measure the opening on each side of it separately.
3. **Interior depth.** Measure from the back of the face frame to the back wall. Sink base cabinets sit under a 24-inch counter, so expect about 21 to 23 inches inside.
4. **Clear height on each side.** Measure from the cabinet floor to the lowest pipe on the left and on the right. This is your real height limit.
5. **Pipe gaps.** Measure from each side wall to the nearest pipe. That number is the widest organizer that can sit there.

Subtract half an inch from every number for wiggle room. Watch the hinges too. They can stick into the opening by an inch and catch a drawer.

### Typical Sink Cabinet Sizes

Use this as a starting point, then trust your own tape measure.

| Sink cabinet width | Typical open space | What usually fits |
|---|---|---|
| 30 inches | Two side zones of 8 to 10 inches each | Two narrow sliding drawers or one drawer plus a door rack |
| 33 inches | Two side zones of 9 to 11 inches each | Two sliding drawers and a tension rod |
| 36 inches | Two side zones of 10 to 12 inches each | Two-tier drawers on both sides, a tension rod and a small bin |
| Corner sink (diagonal) | Deep, wide space with an awkward back corner | Lazy Susan or half-moon turntable, door caddy |
| 24-inch sink in a studio | One side zone of 6 to 8 inches | Slim door caddy, tension rod, stacking bins |

## How to Organize Under a Kitchen Sink With a Garbage Disposal

A garbage disposal takes the middle or one side of the cabinet. The trick is to stop treating that space as storage at all.

### Make the Disposal a No-Storage Zone

Leave the area directly under and around the disposal empty. You need to reach three things there:

- **The reset button.** A small red button on the bottom. Press it when the disposal hums but stops working.
- **The hex key socket.** A small six-sided hole in the center of the bottom. A hex key (a small L-shaped wrench) fits here to free a jam.
- **The power cord and outlet.** Keep bottles away from the plug, where a drip could reach it.

Tape the hex key to the inside of the cabinet door, or hang it on a small adhesive hook.

You also need to see the reset button, and sink cabinets are dark. Stick a [Mr Beams MB850 Slim LED Motion Light](https://www.amazon.com/Mr-Beams-MB850-Wireless-Slim-LED-Motion-Light/dp/B008UPDPIG/?tag=kitchenpot-20) to the inside face frame. It runs on batteries, turns on when the door opens and shuts itself off, so there is no wiring near the water.

### Build Around It, Not Under It

With the middle taken, use the sides, the door and the space above the floor.

- **On the disposal side:** Fit a narrow single-tier drawer or a slim bin on the floor beside the unit. Only use the height that is left. The [AmzAge No-Drill Pull Out Cabinet Organizer](https://www.amazon.com/AmzAge-No-Drill-Pull-Out-Cabinet-Organizer/dp/B0DJ7J7LGY/?tag=kitchenpot-20) suits this spot: it is 9.6 inches wide and 8.4 inches tall, and it sticks down with adhesive instead of screws.
- **On the open side:** This is your main storage. A two-tier sliding organizer usually fits here. If the drain pipe cuts across this side too, the [REALINN L-Shaped 2-Tier Pull Out Under Sink Organizer](https://www.amazon.com/REALINN-L-Shaped-2-Tier-Under-Sink-Organizer/dp/B0C5DBMYZF/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has an L shape that wraps around the pipe instead of fighting it.
- **On the doors:** Flat items like sponges, brushes and bags go here, freeing the floor.
- **Up high:** A tension rod above the drain line holds spray bottles by their triggers.

Disposals vibrate when they run. Keep glass bottles and anything tippy away from it, and do not stack bins against the housing.

## Two-Tier Sliding Organizers: The Workhorse of the Cabinet

A two-tier sliding organizer has two pull-out baskets stacked in one frame, so you reach the back without kneeling and digging. For most people it is the first thing worth buying.

### Choosing the Right One

Match the organizer to your pipe gaps, not to the full cabinet width.

- **Width:** Most fit spaces from about 8 to 13 inches wide. Measure the pipe gap, then subtract half an inch.
- **Height:** Two tiers need about 14 to 18 inches of clear height.
- **Depth:** Pick one at least 2 inches shorter than the interior depth, so it does not hit the back wall or the valves.
- **U-shaped units:** These wrap around the drain with a gap in the middle. They suit cabinets with a single centered drain and no disposal.
- **Material:** Choose metal with a rust-resistant coating or sturdy plastic. Plain wood swells when it gets wet.

## Use a Tension Rod to Hang Spray Bottles

A tension rod is a spring-loaded curtain rod that holds itself in place between two walls. It needs no screws, so it suits renters. It is the cheapest upgrade under the sink.

Here is how to set it up:

1. Measure the inside width of the cabinet at about 4 to 6 inches below the top.
2. Buy a [spring tension curtain rod](https://www.amazon.com/spring-tension-curtain-rod/dp/B073Q7J2LG/?tag=kitchenpot-20) whose range covers that number, with your width near the middle.
3. Twist it in place above the drain pipe, toward the front of the cabinet.
4. Hang spray bottles by their trigger handles. Add a few S-hooks for gloves and a scrub brush.

A rod holds four to six spray bottles across a 30 to 36-inch cabinet. A second rod lower down can hold a roll of trash bags. See [vertical storage ideas for small kitchens](/blog/vertical-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/) for more ways to use height.

## Small Kitchen Trash Can Under the Sink

Putting the trash under the sink frees floor space. It works best on the side without a disposal.

## How to Organize Cleaners Under the Kitchen Sink

Sort cleaners by job, store them upright and keep them away from food.

### Group by Task

Put each group in its own open-top bin with a handle. The [mDesign Ligne Stackable Kitchen Storage Bin with Handles, 4 Pack](https://www.amazon.com/mDesign-Ligne-Stackable-Storage-Bin-4-Pack/dp/B07NQLHNQ4/?tag=kitchenpot-20) gives you one clear bin per group below. The plastic wipes dry, and a leaking bottle stays inside the bin.

- **Dishes:** dish soap refill, dishwasher detergent, rinse aid, brushes
- **Counters and stove:** all-purpose spray, degreaser, microfiber cloths
- **Floors:** floor cleaner, a scrub brush
- **Special jobs:** stainless steel polish, oven cleaner, drain cleaner

Two or three sprays cover almost every kitchen job. Our guide on [how to clean stainless steel pans](/blog/how-to-clean-stainless-steel-pans/) needs little more than dish soap and baking soda. Our steps on [how to clean a dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/) use a cup of vinegar.

Never put cleaner in an old drink bottle. That is how accidental poisonings happen.

### Never Mix Bleach and Ammonia

Bleach mixed with ammonia releases toxic chloramine gas. Bleach mixed with acids, like vinegar or some toilet and rust cleaners, releases chlorine gas. Both can injure your lungs in a small, closed room.

- Store bleach in its own bin, away from glass cleaners, which often contain ammonia.
- Read the label for "contains ammonia" or "do not mix with bleach" before you buy.
- Keep the kitchen window open or the range hood fan on while you clean. Our [small kitchen ventilation guide](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) shows how to clear the air fast.

If someone breathes in fumes or swallows a cleaner, get them to fresh air and call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 in the United States.

## Child Safety Under the Sink

The sink cabinet sits at the height of a crawling baby. Plan for that if young children live in or visit your home.

1. **Fit a cabinet lock.** Magnetic locks are hidden inside and open with a key magnet. Adhesive strap locks work on most doors and need no drilling, which suits renters.
2. **Move the strongest products up high.** Drain cleaner, oven cleaner, dishwasher pods and bleach belong in a high cabinet, not just behind a lock.
3. **Keep products in their original bottles.** The label carries the first-aid instructions.
4. **Close caps every time.** Child-resistant caps only work when they are clicked shut.

Dishwasher pods look like candy to small children, so keep them up high in their sealed tub. A tidy high shelf is easier to set up after you clear the counters, as we show in [countertop organization ideas for a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/).

## How to Protect the Cabinet Under the Sink From Leaks

The cabinet floor under a sink is usually particleboard, which is wood chips glued together. It swells and crumbles once it gets wet, and it rarely dries back flat. Protect it before you put anything back in.

### Lay Down a Leak Mat

A leak mat is a waterproof tray or liner with a raised edge. It catches drips and holds them, so the water sits in the mat instead of soaking into the wood.

| Protection option | What it does | Our take |
|---|---|---|
| Molded mat with raised edge | Holds spills of up to several cups | Our first choice for any sink cabinet |
| Cut-to-fit shelf liner | Stops scratches and small spills | Fine for dry cabinets, not enough under pipes |
| Peel-and-stick vinyl | Seals the wood surface | Good under a mat, not instead of one |
| Battery water alarm | Beeps when water touches its sensor | Cheap insurance, place it under the P-trap |
| Smart leak sensor | Sends an alert to your phone | Worth it if you travel or rent out the unit |

Trim the mat with scissors to wrap around the pipes coming up through the floor if needed. Make sure your organizers sit inside the raised edge, not on top of it.

### Check for Drips Once a Month

Most leaks start small. Set a monthly reminder on your phone.

1. Fill the sink a few inches, then pull the plug and let it drain.
2. Run a dry paper towel along the P-trap, every joint, the supply lines and the valves.
3. Look for wet spots, rust, white mineral crust or a musty smell.
4. Check the disposal's bottom and the dishwasher hose connection too.

A damp paper towel tells you exactly where the leak is. A loose slip nut, the large plastic ring that holds the P-trap together, can often be tightened by hand. A drip from a supply line or a valve needs a plumber, or your landlord if you rent.

Also turn each shut-off valve off and back on once or twice a year. Valves that never move can seize, and a stuck valve is no help in an emergency.

## What Should Be Stored Under the Kitchen Sink?

Keep sink-job items that can handle a little moisture.

**Good choices:**

- Dish soap refills and dishwasher detergent
- Sponges, scrub brushes and rubber gloves
- Trash bags and recycling bags
- All-purpose spray, glass cleaner and microfiber cloths
- A small bucket for mopping or soaking
- A small flashlight and the disposal hex key

A small fire extinguisher is better near the kitchen exit than deep under the sink. In a fire, you want to grab it on your way toward the door, not reach past a burning stove.

## Common Mistakes When Organizing Under the Sink

These are the mistakes we see most often. Each one either wastes space or causes damage.

- **Buying organizers before measuring.** A drawer that fits the cabinet width but not the pipe gap is useless. Measure each zone first.
- **Blocking the shut-off valves.** In a leak, every second counts. Keep a clear path to both valves.
- **Skipping the leak mat.** A slow drip on bare particleboard can ruin the cabinet floor before you notice it.
- **Storing bleach next to ammonia cleaners.** A spill or a mix-up can release toxic gas. Keep them in separate bins.
- **Overfilling the door.** Heavy bottles on a door rack pull the hinges loose. Doors are for light, flat items only.
- **Cramming the disposal area.** Bottles against the disposal get knocked over, and you cannot reach the reset button.
- **Keeping duplicates.** Three half-empty sprays for the same job take a whole tier. Finish one before you open the next.

Fix these, and the cabinet stays organized, dry and safe for years.
