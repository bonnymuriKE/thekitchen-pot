## How an Immersion Blender Works

An immersion blender is a motor in a handle, a long shaft, and a small blade at the end. A guard shaped like a bell surrounds the blade.

When the blade spins, it throws food outward. That creates low pressure under the bell, which pulls more food in from below. The food circulates up, out and back in. That loop is what makes soup smooth.

The same low pressure causes the problem everyone hates: suction. If the bell sits flat on the pot bottom, it seals like a suction cup. Makers fight this with bell shapes, vents and internal ribs. That is why the guard design matters as much as the motor.

## Does Wattage Matter?

Only up to a point.

Wattage measures the power the motor draws. It does not measure how well the blender blends. A strong motor behind a poorly shaped guard can still leave lumps. A modest motor with a good guard can puree soup smoothly.

| Task | What Matters Most | Power Needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pureeing cooked soup | Guard design, shaft length | Low to medium |
| Mayonnaise and dressings | Narrow cup, slow start | Low |
| Whipped cream | Whisk attachment | Low |
| Smoothies with soft fruit | Speed control | Medium |
| Frozen fruit or ice | Motor strength, small batches | High |
| Hummus and thick dips | Motor strength, pulse | High |

For thick or frozen blends, more power helps. For everyday soups, pick based on grip, guard and attachments.

## Immersion Blender vs Countertop Blender vs Food Processor

Each tool wins at different jobs.

An immersion blender is best for soups, sauces and small batches. It blends in the pot and cleans in seconds. A countertop blender is better for big batches, ice and very smooth smoothies. A food processor chops, slices and shreds, which neither blender does well.

## How to Use an Immersion Blender Without the Mess

Most splatter comes from lifting the blade while it is still running.

1. **Submerge the blade before you switch it on.** The whole bell guard should sit under the liquid.
2. **Start on low.** Speed up once the food is moving.
3. **Tilt the pot or cup slightly.** That deepens the liquid where you are blending.
4. **Move slowly up and down.** Let the food flow into the blade.
5. **Switch off before lifting.** Wait for the blade to stop, then lift it out.

For small batches, use a tall, narrow container. A wide bowl leaves the guard half exposed. Our full guide on [how to use an immersion blender without the splatter](/blog/how-to-use-an-immersion-blender/) walks through soups, mayo and smoothies step by step.

Hot soup needs one extra rule. Take the pot off the heat first. Boiling liquid splashes, and a steady pot is safer to blend in.

## Corded or Cordless?

Every pick here is corded, and that is deliberate for soup. A corded blender never runs out of power halfway through a thick batch.

Cordless models are handy for quick jobs away from an outlet, like a smoothie on the far side of the counter. The trade-off is a heavier handle, because the battery lives in it, and limited run time. If you do choose cordless, check how long the battery lasts on one charge.

## Cleaning and Storing an Immersion Blender

The quickest clean takes ten seconds. Fill a tall cup with warm, soapy water. Run the blender in it for a few seconds, then rinse.

- **Unplug before touching the blade.** It is sharp and can catch a sponge.
- **Never submerge the motor handle.** Only the shaft and attachments go in water.
- **Check what is dishwasher safe.** Many shafts and attachments are, but check your manual.
- **Dry the shaft fully before storing,** so water does not sit inside the bell.
