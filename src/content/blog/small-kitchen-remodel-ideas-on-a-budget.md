Leave the sink, stove and walls where they are. That one rule keeps small kitchen remodel ideas on a budget affordable, because moving plumbing, gas lines or walls is what blows small budgets apart.

Most small kitchens need less change than their owners expect. A smart refresh or cosmetic remodel can fix the storage, counters and look for a fraction of what a gut job costs. Below are three scope tiers, rough costs, and the specific products we would buy at each step.

**In a hurry?**

- Keep the layout if it works. Then pick a tier: a refresh (about $1,500 to $6,000), a cosmetic remodel (about $10,000 to $25,000) or a mid remodel (about $25,000 to $50,000).
- Put the money into drawers, hinges, counters and lighting. Save on trendy finishes and oversized appliances.
- Hand all electrical, plumbing and gas work to licensed pros.

## Remodel or Refresh: Which One Do You Need?

A kitchen that looks tired but works fine needs a refresh. A kitchen with worn counters, ugly cabinets and a bad sink needs a cosmetic remodel. A kitchen where the cabinets are falling apart, or where there is no room to cook, needs a mid remodel.

If you only want cheap fixes with no construction, read our guide to [small kitchen ideas on a budget](/blog/small-kitchen-ideas-on-a-budget/) first. This guide covers real remodel work, with real money.

Here is a quick test. Walk through a normal dinner and note every time you get stuck. If the problems are clutter and dim light, you need organizing and lighting, not a contractor. If the problems are broken drawers, no prep space and a sink that leaks, it is time to remodel.

## Small Kitchen Remodel Cost by Scope Tier

We split small kitchen remodels into three tiers. "Small" here means roughly 70 to 150 square feet, with about 10 to 20 feet of cabinets. These are broad US ranges. Labor costs vary a lot by region, so a city job can cost far more than the same job in a small town.

| Tier | What changes | Rough cost (small kitchen) | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Refresh | Paint, hardware, lighting, faucet, backsplash | About $1,500 to $6,000 | 1 to 3 weekends |
| Cosmetic remodel | Above, plus new counters, sink, floor, cabinet refacing or pro painting | About $10,000 to $25,000 | 2 to 4 weeks |
| Mid remodel | New cabinets, counters, floor, lighting and appliances, same layout | About $25,000 to $50,000 | 4 to 8 weeks |
| Layout change | Moving plumbing, gas or walls | Add $10,000 or more | Adds weeks |

Treat these as planning numbers, not quotes. Always hold back a contingency fund of 10 to 20 percent for surprises like rotten subfloor or old wiring.

### Tier 1: The Refresh

A refresh changes the look and fixes small function problems. Nothing structural moves. You keep the cabinet boxes, counters and appliances.

A typical refresh includes:

- Painting the cabinets, walls and ceiling
- New pulls, knobs and soft-close hinges
- Plug-in or hardwired under-cabinet lighting
- A new faucet in the same spot
- A new backsplash, either tile or peel-and-stick
- Inside-the-cabinet upgrades like pull-out bins and shelf inserts

This tier is where DIY saves the most. Paint and hardware are very doable for a beginner with patience. Measure the hole spacing on your old pulls before you buy new ones. For 5-inch spacing, the [Ravinte Matte Black Slim Cabinet Pulls](https://www.amazon.com/Ravinte-Matte-Black-Cabinet-Pulls-5-Inch/dp/B09D83G753/?tag=kitchenpot-20) come 25 to a pack, enough for most small kitchens. For 3-inch spacing, the [Amerdeco Brushed Brass Cabinet Pulls](https://www.amazon.com/Amerdeco-Brushed-Brass-Cabinet-Pulls-3-Inch/dp/B0B9X2PL5J/?tag=kitchenpot-20) screw into the old holes, so you skip filling and redrilling.

Our [small kitchen lighting ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-lighting-ideas/) guide shows how to light a small kitchen well, and lighting is the upgrade most people underrate.

### Tier 2: The Cosmetic Remodel

A cosmetic remodel replaces the surfaces you touch and see every day. The cabinet boxes stay if they are solid. The layout stays the same.

A typical cosmetic remodel includes everything in a refresh, plus:

- New countertops, usually laminate, butcher block or quartz
- A new sink, often an undermount if the counter allows it
- Cabinet refacing (new doors and drawer fronts on the old boxes) or professional painting
- New flooring, such as luxury vinyl plank or tile
- A tiled backsplash, installed properly

For most small kitchens, this is the sweet spot. New counters and doors change the room completely, and you skip the cost of new cabinet boxes. If your sink is on the list, our guides to [white undermount kitchen sinks](/blog/best-white-undermount-kitchen-sink/) and [what gauge stainless steel sink is best](/blog/what-gauge-stainless-steel-sink-is-best/) help you choose one that lasts.

### Tier 3: The Mid Remodel

A mid remodel replaces the cabinets, counters, floor and usually the appliances. The sink, stove and fridge stay roughly where they are. That one rule keeps a mid remodel affordable.

A typical mid remodel includes:

- New cabinet boxes, ideally with more drawers and taller uppers
- New counters and backsplash
- New flooring
- New lighting, often with an electrician adding circuits or fixtures
- New appliances sized for the room
- A new or upgraded range hood

This is the tier where you can redesign the storage from scratch, so plan carefully. Read our [small kitchen layout ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-layout-ideas/) before you sign off on a cabinet plan.

## Can You Remodel a Kitchen for $5,000?

You can do a very good refresh for $5,000. You cannot do a full remodel for that. New cabinets alone for a small kitchen usually cost more than $5,000 once they are installed.

Here is how we would spend $5,000 in a small kitchen, doing most of the work yourself:

| Item | Rough budget |
|---|---|
| Cabinet paint, primer, rollers and degreaser | About $200 to $400 |
| New pulls, knobs and soft-close hinges | About $200 to $500 |
| Pull-out bins and drawer inserts | About $300 to $700 |
| Under-cabinet LED lights | About $100 to $600 |
| New faucet, installed by a plumber | About $400 to $900 |
| Simple tile backsplash, installed | About $600 to $1,800 |
| Laminate counters, if the budget allows | About $800 to $1,500 |

That adds up to about $2,600 to $6,400, so you will need to pick. If the counters are in good shape, skip them and put the money into storage inserts and better lighting. If the counters are cracked or peeling, they come first.

The faucet line is where we would not go cheap. ## What to Prioritize in a Small Kitchen Remodel

Follow this order so you do not spend on looks while the kitchen still does not work.

1. **Safety and repairs.** Leaks, bad wiring, a weak range hood and soft floors come first. Nothing else matters if these are broken.
2. **Storage.** More drawers, taller uppers and smart corner fixes. This is the change you feel every day.
3. **Counter space.** Aim for one clear prep area of at least 24 by 36 inches. Our guide to [making more counter space in a small kitchen](/blog/make-more-counter-space-in-a-small-kitchen/) has ideas that work during and after a remodel.
4. **Lighting.** Under-cabinet task lights plus good overhead light. Use 2700K to 3000K bulbs with a CRI (color accuracy score) of 90 or higher.
5. **Daily-touch items.** Faucet, hinges, drawer slides and pulls. Cheap versions wear out fast and annoy you every day.
6. **Finishes.** Paint colors, tile patterns and hardware style. These are the easiest things to change later.

For lighting on a refresh budget, the [Enbrighten 22-Inch Plug-In Linkable LED Under Cabinet Light](https://www.amazon.com/Enbrighten-22-Inch-Linkable-Plug-In-Under-Cabinet-Light/dp/B01MQXV843/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is 3000K and links bar to bar from one outlet. In a mid remodel, have the electrician hardwire the [Kichler 4U Series 30-Inch LED Under Cabinet Light](https://www.amazon.com/Kichler-4U-Series-30-Inch-LED-Under-Cabinet-Light/dp/B01ETARS2O/?tag=kitchenpot-20) instead. It is 3000K, dimmable and only 1 inch tall, so it hides behind the cabinet lip. Overhead, put [Philips Ultra Definition 2700K A19 bulbs](https://www.amazon.com/Philips-Ultra-Definition-LED-A19-Bulbs-2700K-8-Pack/dp/B0B927WMTY/?tag=kitchenpot-20) in regular fixtures and [Feit Electric BR30 2700K 90 CRI bulbs](https://www.amazon.com/Feit-Electric-BR30-LED-2700K-90-CRI-4-Pack/dp/B081BSDSYS/?tag=kitchenpot-20) in recessed cans. Both have a CRI of 90 or higher, so food looks the way it should.

Also check your landing space while you plan. You want at least 15 inches of counter beside the fridge and on one side of the stove, and 24 inches beside the sink. If your current kitchen fails this, fix it in the remodel.

## What Adds the Most Value in a Small Kitchen Remodel?

Value means how much better the kitchen works for you and how buyers see it later. The same changes win both.

Solid drawers, good counters, a decent sink and working appliances count for more than a trendy tile. What adds the least value is spending far above the rest of the home. A luxury range in a basic apartment kitchen rarely pays back, so match the remodel to the home.

## Where to Spend and Where to Save

Spend on what you touch, what holds weight and what is hard to replace. Save on what is easy to swap later.

| Spend here | Why | Save here | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drawer boxes and full-extension slides | Used dozens of times a day | Cabinet door style | Simple flat doors look clean and cost less |
| Soft-close hinges | Cheap hinges sag and fail | Backsplash tile | Plain white tile looks good for decades |
| Faucet | A bad one leaks and wobbles | Light fixtures | Simple fixtures with good bulbs work well |
| Counters on your main prep area | Takes the most wear | Hardware finish | Easy to swap in an afternoon |
| Licensed electrician, plumber and gas fitter | Safety and permits | Paint | Mid-price paint is fine on walls |
| Range hood that vents well | Small kitchens trap smoke and grease | Oversized appliances | Compact models fit better and use less power |

## How to Gain Storage in a Small Kitchen Remodel

These changes gain the most space, from biggest to smallest.

### Swap Base Doors for Drawers

Lower cabinets with doors and one shelf waste space. Deep drawers fix this, because you see everything from above.

In a new cabinet plan, make most base cabinets drawer stacks. A 30-inch-wide base with three drawers holds pots, lids and food storage far better than a door cabinet. If you keep your old cabinets, roll-out shelves installed inside the doors get you most of the same benefit. It needs only an 11.5-inch door opening and runs 22 inches deep. It screws to the cabinet floor, so it suits owners, not renters. ### Add a Slim Pull-Out Pantry

A 9- to 12-inch-wide gap next to the fridge or at the end of a run can become a pull-out pantry. It holds cans, jars and spices on shelves you can see from both sides. If you have no pantry at all, read [how to organize a small kitchen with no pantry](/blog/how-to-organize-a-small-kitchen-with-no-pantry/) for ways to fill the gap.

### Fix the Blind Corner

Corner cabinets hide a lot of dead space. A lazy Susan, a swing-out shelf or a simple pull-out fixes most of them. For a blind corner, where the back half hides behind the next cabinet, the [Rev-A-Shelf Blind Corner 2-Tier Pullout](https://www.amazon.com/Rev-A-Shelf-5PSP-18-CR-Blind-Corner-Pullout/dp/B00H4Q79CU/?tag=kitchenpot-20) slides four baskets out to you through an 18-inch opening. ### Use the Toe Kick and the Walls

The toe kick is the recessed strip at the bottom of base cabinets, about 4 inches tall. Toe-kick drawers fit baking sheets, cutting boards and trays. On the walls, a rail system or a [magnetic knife strip](/blog/best-magnetic-knife-strip/) clears tools off the counter. ### Plan the Inside of Every Cabinet

New boxes are only half the job. Plan dividers, lid racks and shelf inserts before the cabinets arrive, so every inch is used. ## Should Small Kitchen Cabinets Go to the Ceiling?

In most small kitchens, yes. Here is the simple math for a standard 8-foot (96-inch) ceiling. The counter is 36 inches high, and upper cabinets usually start about 18 inches above it. That puts the bottom of the uppers at 54 inches. That leaves 42 inches to the ceiling, which is exactly the height of a standard 42-inch upper cabinet.

Most builders install 30-inch uppers, which leaves a 12-inch gap on top. Going to 42-inch uppers gains about one full shelf in every cabinet. Across 10 feet of wall, that is a lot of space.

Cabinets to the ceiling also end the greasy gap on top, and the walls look taller. Use the top shelf for holiday platters, paper goods and backups, and keep a small step stool handy. The [COSCO 2-Step Folding Steel Step Stool](https://www.amazon.com/Cosco-2-Step-Big-Step-Folding-Stool/dp/B0032JRVPI/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has two wide steps and folds about 2 inches flat, so it slides in beside the fridge. For ceilings over 8 feet, stacked uppers (a short cabinet on top of a standard one) do the same job.

On a refresh, a carpenter can close the gap in a day with short cabinets or a trim panel.

## A Simple Small Kitchen Remodel Cost Estimator

Use this four-step method to build a rough budget before you call contractors.

1. **Measure your cabinet runs.** Add up the length of every wall with base cabinets, in feet. Most small kitchens have 10 to 20 feet.
2. **Pick your tier.** Use the tier table above. Start at the low end if you will do some work yourself, and at the high end if you will hire everything out or live in a high-cost area.
3. **Add your appliances.** If you are replacing them, a basic set of fridge, range, dishwasher and hood often runs about $2,500 to $6,000. Compact or premium models move that number in either direction.
4. **Add 10 to 20 percent contingency.** Older homes need the higher number.

Then get at least three written quotes, with labor and materials listed separately. That shows you where DIY can save money.

## The Right Order of Work

Doing things in the wrong order costs money. A counter set on cabinets that are not level will crack. Follow this order:

1. **Plan and measure.** Finalize the layout, cabinet plan and appliance sizes, since cabinets are built around the appliances.
2. **Order long-lead items.** Cabinets and some appliances take weeks, so order them before demolition.
3. **Pull permits.** Electrical, plumbing, gas and structural work usually need one.
4. **Demolition.** Remove old cabinets, counters and flooring. Shut off water and power first.
5. **Rough-in work.** Licensed pros move or add wiring, plumbing and gas lines. An inspector often checks this before walls are closed.
6. **Walls and paint.** Patch drywall, prime and paint the walls and ceiling.
7. **Flooring.** Some floors go under cabinets and some go around them. Floating vinyl plank usually goes around, so ask your installer.
8. **Cabinets.** Install, level and secure them to the studs, which are usually 16 inches apart.
9. **Counters.** Fabricators measure (template) after the cabinets are in, then install a week or two later.
10. **Backsplash.** Tile goes on after the counters are set.
11. **Sink, faucet and appliances.** The plumber hooks up the sink and dishwasher. The electrician connects fixtures and lights.
12. **Hardware and finish work.** Pulls, knobs, toe kicks, trim and paint touch-ups.

Plan for a few weeks without a working kitchen in a cosmetic or mid remodel. Set up a temporary station with a microwave, a kettle and a small electric cooker. Our [easy meal prep ideas for one person](/blog/easy-meal-prep-ideas-for-one-person-in-a-small-kitchen/) work well for cooking ahead before demolition starts.

## DIY vs Hiring a Pro

Doing part of the work yourself is the fastest way to cut costs. Our rule is simple: all electrical, plumbing and gas work goes to a licensed pro. Mistakes there cause fires, floods and gas leaks, and unpermitted work can cause trouble with insurance and resale.

| Job | DIY or pro | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Painting walls and cabinets | DIY | Slow but simple; prep is most of the work |
| Swapping pulls, knobs and hinges | DIY | An afternoon with a drill |
| Peel-and-stick backsplash | DIY | Good for renters and quick updates |
| Tile backsplash | DIY or pro | Doable for patient beginners; pros are faster |
| Demolition | DIY or pro | Shut off water and power first |
| Floating vinyl plank floor | DIY or pro | Click-together floors are DIY-friendly |
| Installing cabinets | Pro recommended | Must be level and secured to studs |
| Stone or quartz counters | Pro | Heavy, and cut to fit by the fabricator |
| Any electrical work | Always pro | Includes new outlets, circuits and hardwired lights |
| Any plumbing work | Always pro | Includes moving or adding lines |
| Any gas work | Always pro | Includes connecting a gas range |

Renters should get the landlord's written permission before any change beyond hardware and peel-and-stick. Keep the old hardware so you can put it back when you move out. For a renter backsplash, we recommend [Smart Tiles Peel and Stick Backsplash in Subway Soft White](https://www.amazon.com/Smart-Tiles-Peel-Stick-Backsplash-Subway/dp/B07XCHLLGP/?tag=kitchenpot-20). The sheets have a raised, tile-like look and come off when you leave.

## Small Kitchen Remodel Ideas for 2026 That Last

Pick ideas that will still work in ten years:

- **Flat-panel or simple Shaker doors.** Clean, easy to wipe and hard to date.
- **Light, warm colors on uppers.** Darker lowers are fine, but light uppers keep a small room open.
- **Quartz or good laminate counters.** Both are low-maintenance. Laminate has improved a lot and saves real money.
- **Induction cooktops.** They heat fast, keep the kitchen cooler and are easy to clean. See our picks for [induction cooktops](/blog/best-induction-cooktop-for-the-money/).
- **Integrated outlets and charging.** An outlet strip under the uppers keeps the backsplash clean. This is electrician work.
- **Sealed gaps.** Seal around new pipes and cabinets so pests have fewer ways in. Our guide on [keeping pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/) covers what to seal.

Efficient appliances and LED lighting cut bills for years too. Our guide to [reducing kitchen energy use in a small apartment](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/) shows where the savings come from.

## Remodel Mistakes That Waste the Most Money

In a small kitchen, each of these eats a share of the budget you could have spent on storage.

- **Moving the sink or stove without a strong reason.** Relocating plumbing and gas can add $10,000 or more. Keep them where they are unless the layout truly does not work.
- **Picking finishes before the storage plan.** Choose tile last. Plan drawers, shelves and appliance sizes first.
- **Skipping the contingency fund.** Old kitchens hide water damage and bad wiring. Without 10 to 20 percent set aside, one surprise stalls the project.
- **Buying the cheapest drawer slides and hinges.** They sag, stick and fail within a few years. This is not the place to save.
- **Doing electrical, plumbing or gas work yourself.** It risks your safety, your insurance and your sale price later.
- **Filling the new space with clutter.** A remodel is the best time to declutter. Our [small kitchen storage ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-storage-ideas/) and [countertop organization ideas](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/) help you keep it clear.

## How to Keep the Budget Under Control

A budget slips one "while we are at it" decision at a time. These habits keep it in line:

- Write down your must-haves and nice-to-haves before you talk to a contractor.
- Get a fixed-price written quote with a clear scope of work.
- Make all choices, from tile to pulls, before work starts. Changes mid-job cost the most.
- Pay in stages tied to finished work, never most of the money up front.

If the budget runs short, cut finishes, not function. A plain backsplash can be upgraded next year. A badly planned cabinet run cannot.
