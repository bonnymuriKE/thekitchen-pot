Your salt grinder and your pepper grinder should not have the same thing inside them. Salt eats steel, pepper blunts ceramic, and most cheap pairs ignore both facts.

That is the single most useful thing to know before you buy. Get the mechanisms right and a $50 set will still be working in ten years.

Below are six pairs and mills worth buying, split between classic manual sets and the rechargeable models that let you season with one hand. Like a good [vegetable chopper](/blog/best-vegetable-choppers/), the right pair disappears into your routine instead of fighting you.

## Salt and pepper are two different jobs

A grinder is a pair of burrs, one turning inside the other, with crystals or corns falling into the gap. Simple enough. The complication is that salt and pepper behave nothing alike inside that gap.

**Pepper** is hard, dry and oily. It needs sharp machined teeth to crack the shell and shear the fragments. Steel does that best, which is why Peugeot uses case-hardened steel in its pepper mills and Cole & Mason uses carbon steel in its precision pepper mechanism.

**Salt** is softer but chemically hostile. Chloride attacks steel, and once a carbon steel burr starts rusting the grind goes gritty and the mechanism seizes. The fix is a material that does not corrode. Cole & Mason uses white alumina ceramic in its salt mechanisms, around 60% stronger than the older steatite ceramic in budget mills, so it cuts rock salt without chipping. Peugeot takes a different route and builds its salt mills from corrosion-resistant 18/10 stainless steel rather than the case-hardened steel it puts in pepper mills.

That is why a proper set contains two different mills that only look like twins. It is also why swapping contents between them, even once, is a bad idea. Corrosion behaves the same way on cookware, which is the reason a [sauté pan](/blog/best-saute-pan/) made of bare carbon steel and one made of stainless need completely different care.

| | Pepper mill | Salt mill |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical mechanism | Carbon or case-hardened steel | Alumina ceramic or 18/10 stainless |
| Why | Sharp teeth crack a hard shell | Will not corrode in contact with salt |
| Grind settings offered | Often 5 or 6 | Often 3 |
| Fails from | Rust, damp refills, oversized corns | Moisture bridging, fine table salt |
| Safe to swap contents | No | No |

Notice the settings row. Manufacturers give pepper more grind steps because pepper is used across a wider range, from powder in a sauce to cracked on a steak. Salt is mostly wanted at one or two sizes, so three positions covers it. Cole & Mason's Derwent set advertises nine grinding levels in total, and that is six on the pepper mill plus three on the salt.

### How the grind setting changes what comes out

Turning the adjuster raises or lowers the inner burr, widening or narrowing the gap to the outer ring. A narrow gap traps particles until they have been cut small, giving you fine grind. A wide gap lets bigger fragments escape after one pass, giving you coarse.

With salt that gap does something extra. Coarse salt crystals are irregular, so at a wide setting you get an uneven scatter of large and small grains, which is exactly what you want on a finished dish. At the finest setting the mill works harder and output drops noticeably, which surprises people who expect salt to flow like pepper.

## What salt and what pepper to load

The mill is only half of it. What you put in decides whether it works.

**For the salt mill**

- Coarse sea salt or rock salt, dry, with crystals roughly 2 to 4mm.
- Avoid fine table salt. It falls through unground and usually contains anti-caking agents that gum the burr.
- Avoid damp or flaky finishing salts. Moisture makes salt bridge into a solid plug above the mechanism.
- Store the refill jar away from the stove and the kettle. Steam is what ruins salt mills.

**For the pepper mill**

- Whole black peppercorns under about 5mm. Tellicherry corns are simply larger black corns picked later, with more developed flavour.
- White peppercorns work and are milder, good in pale sauces.
- Rainbow blends look good in a clear mill but grind unevenly because the corns differ in hardness.
- Buy in small amounts. Whole corns hold their aroma for a year or more; once ground, it fades in weeks.

A cool, dark cupboard is all either needs, the same basic storage logic that applies to [potatoes and onions](/blog/how-long-do-potatoes-last/) or anything else you keep out of the fridge. If your cupboards are already full, the approaches in [organising a small kitchen with no pantry](/blog/how-to-organize-a-small-kitchen-with-no-pantry/) will free up a shelf.

## Fixing the four things that go wrong

1. **The salt mill spins but nothing comes out.** Moisture has bridged the salt into a plug. Empty it, dry the hopper, refill with fresh coarse salt.
2. **The pepper grind has gone gritty and uneven.** Either the burr is worn, or salt has been in it and started corrosion. Steel burrs do not recover from rust; replace the mill.
3. **The adjuster has gone stiff.** Grind two or three turns to clear pressure off the burr before forcing the knob. Forcing it is how threads strip.
4. **Output has slowly dropped.** Old pepper dust and oil have caked inside. Empty the mill, tap it out upside down and brush the burr with a dry pastry brush.

Wipe stainless bodies with a damp cloth and dry them, exactly as you would with a [stainless steel pan](/blog/how-to-clean-stainless-steel-pans/). Wooden bodies get a barely damp cloth and nothing else; treat them like a wooden [cutting board](/blog/best-over-the-sink-cutting-board/) and keep them away from standing water.

## Where to keep them

Grinders live in one of three places, and each has a cost.

- **On the counter.** Fastest to reach, but two mills take real space, and steam from the stove is the enemy of both. Keep them at the far end from the hob.
- **In a drawer.** Protected and out of sight, but you will season less often simply because it is more effort. Slim mills like the Kuhn Rikon suit this best.
- **On the table.** Good-looking pairs earn their place here, and the OXO set exists precisely so it does not leave marks on the tablecloth. Keep them with the [serving pieces](/blog/best-metal-spatula-set/) you reach for at every meal rather than in a far cupboard.
