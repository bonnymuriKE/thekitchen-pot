Knowing what to put on top of kitchen cabinets turns a dusty strip of dead space into real storage. In a small kitchen, that strip may be the biggest empty shelf you have.

Most people either leave it bare or fill it with fake plants that turn sticky. We think it should hold the things you use a few times a year, packed so they stay clean. Below is our full plan for the cabinet tops and the space above the fridge.

**The short version:** Use the tops of your cabinets for light, non-food items you need a few times a year, like holiday platters, a roasting pan and party supplies. Pack them in lidded bins or baskets with handles, and line the tops with washable mats. Keep everything off the cabinets over and beside the stove, because heat and grease rise there. Above the fridge, use pull-forward bins for trays and spare paper goods.

## What to Put on Top of Kitchen Cabinets: Our Short List

The top of an upper cabinet is high, warm and hard to reach. So the best things to store there are light, sturdy, not food, and not needed every week.

Here is what we put up there:

- **Seasonal serving pieces:** the turkey platter, a punch bowl, cake stands and a big salad bowl.
- **Big cookware you rarely use:** a roasting pan, a 12-quart stockpot or a canning pot.
- **Holiday baking gear:** cookie cutters, tart pans and cookie tins.
- **Party supplies:** paper plates, napkins, cups and table runners.
- **Spare paper goods:** extra paper towel rolls and backup trash bags.
- **Small-batch gear:** a fondue set, a pasta maker or a waffle iron you bring out twice a year (stored there, never used there).

The test is simple. If you reach for it more than once a month, it does not belong on top of the cabinets. Move it into the cabinets with the ideas in our [small kitchen cabinet organization guide](/blog/small-kitchen-cabinet-organization-ideas/), and free the tops for the true once-in-a-while items.

This is how a small kitchen gains real space. Every big platter that moves up frees a shelf at eye level for things you use daily. It is the same idea behind our [vertical storage ideas for small kitchens](/blog/vertical-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/): the higher the spot, the less often you should need it.

## Is It OK to Store Things on Top of Kitchen Cabinets?

Yes, it is fine, as long as you follow three rules.

1. **Keep it light.** The top of most upper cabinets is a thin panel, not a shelf built for weight. Store only what you can lift down with one hand while standing on a step stool.
2. **Keep it away from the stove.** Heat and grease rise from the range. Nothing flammable goes on the cabinets over the stove or right beside the range hood.
3. **Keep food off.** The top of the cabinets is the warmest spot in the kitchen. Heat makes oils go rancid faster, fades spices and can spoil anything sealed in a jar.

There is also a cleaning catch. Anything left open up there collects a film of dust and grease. That is why we pack items in bins with lids instead of setting them out loose.

## Measure the Gap Before You Buy Anything

What fits depends on how much space sits between the cabinet top and the ceiling. Measure from the top of the cabinet box to the ceiling, then subtract an inch so you can tip bins in and out.

Also measure the depth. A standard upper cabinet is 12 inches deep. Bins deeper than the cabinet top will hang over the edge, which looks messy and can tip.

Here is how the gap usually works out in a room with an 8-foot ceiling. Counters sit at 36 inches, and the upper cabinets start about 18 inches above them.

| Upper cabinet height | Gap to an 8-foot ceiling | What fits on top |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 inches | About 12 inches | Lidded bins up to about 10 inches tall, baskets, a stockpot |
| 36 inches | About 6 inches | Flat trays, platters lying down, low baskets, cookie tins |
| 42 inches | Little or none | Nothing; the cabinets reach the ceiling |
| Any height, 9-foot ceiling | 12 inches more than above | Taller bins, but only if you can still reach them safely |

### What to Put on Top of Kitchen Cabinets That Don't Go to the Ceiling

A 12-inch gap is the sweet spot. It holds real storage bins with room to lift them out.

A 6-inch gap is trickier. Use it for flat things: sheet pans, large trays and platters stacked with a paper towel between each one. Low baskets work too.

If your gap is more than 18 inches, do not stack bins two high. A tall stack is hard to lift down and easy to drop. One row of bins plus empty air above is safer.

## Zone the Cabinet Tops: Heat, Grease and Steam

Not every cabinet top is equal. The spot over the stove and the spot over a dishwasher are very different places to store things.

Walk along your cabinets and mark each section by what sits below it. Then use this guide.

| Location | What happens there | What to store | What to keep off |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Over the stove or range hood | Heat, grease, steam | Nothing | Baskets, paper, fabric, plastic, cookbooks, candles |
| Right next to the range hood | Grease drift, warmth | Metal or glass items only, or nothing | Anything flammable or meltable |
| Over the sink | Steam | Lidded plastic bins | Wicker, paper goods, wood |
| Over the dishwasher | Hot steam when it opens | Lidded plastic bins | Wicker, cardboard, paper |
| End of the run, far from the stove | Dust only | Baskets, bins, paper goods, platters | Food, oils, heavy items |
| Above the fridge | Warm air from the fridge | Light items in bins | Heat appliances, food, heavy items |

The rule over the stove is strict. Nothing flammable goes near the range hood, ever. A grease fire on the stovetop can reach the cabinets above, and a basket of paper napkins up there gives it fuel.

Even without a fire, the heat warps plastic and bakes grease onto anything left there. Leave that section bare and wipe it often.

A working range hood helps every other section too. It pulls grease out of the air before it settles on the cabinet tops. If yours is weak or missing, our [small kitchen range hood and ventilation guide](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/) shows what to fix first. Then keep the filter clean on the schedule in [how often to clean a range hood filter](/blog/how-often-to-clean-a-range-hood-filter/).

## The Best Things to Store Above Your Cabinets

### Holiday and Party Gear

Platters, gravy boats, cake stands, cookie cutters, a punch bowl and party napkins sit unused for ten or eleven months a year.

Group them by event. One bin for Thanksgiving, one for birthdays, one for summer cookouts. When the day comes, you pull down a single bin and everything is there.

### Big Pots and Pans You Rarely Use

A roasting pan, a large stockpot or a canning pot takes a whole cabinet shelf. Most solo cooks and couples use them a few times a year.

Put the lid on the pot and store small items inside it, like a turkey baster and a roasting rack. This keeps dust out and uses the air inside the pot. Our guide to [storing pots and pans in a small kitchen](/blog/store-pots-and-pans-in-a-small-kitchen/) covers where the daily pans should go instead.

### Backstock of Paper Goods

Extra paper towel rolls, napkins and trash bags are light and bulky. That makes them a good fit for the far end of a cabinet run, well away from the stove.

Keep them in a lidded bin so grease does not soak into the paper. Paper goods near the stove are a fire risk, so this only works on the cool end of the kitchen.

### What We Keep Off the Cabinet Tops

- **Food of any kind.** Not even sealed cans or bulk rice. Heat ruins it, and you will forget it is there. Use the ideas in [how to organize a small kitchen with no pantry](/blog/how-to-organize-a-small-kitchen-with-no-pantry/) instead.
- **Oils, vinegars and spices.** Heat makes them go stale and rancid fast. They belong in a cool cabinet, as we explain in our [spice storage ideas for small kitchens](/blog/spice-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/).
- **Wine.** The warm air above the cabinets ages it badly.
- **Heavy glass or cast iron.** A dropped Dutch oven from that height breaks tile and toes.
- **Anything you use weekly.** If you climb up every Sunday, the item is in the wrong spot.

Loose items on top of cabinets look cluttered and get filthy. Bins solve both problems. They make one clean line along the top, and each bin comes down in one move.

## How to Keep the Top of Kitchen Cabinets Clean

The tops of upper cabinets get dirty in a special way. Cooking grease floats up, settles, and then catches dust. After a few months, you get a sticky gray layer that a dry cloth just smears.

The fix is to give the grease something to land on that you can lift off and wash.

### What to Put on Top of Kitchen Cabinets to Keep Clean

Line the cabinet tops before you set anything on them. Reach for the [16-Piece Washable Refrigerator Liners](https://www.amazon.com/Refrigerator-Liners-Washable-Fridge-Shelf-Mats-16-Pack/dp/B0FSJXHWTD/?tag=kitchenpot-20). They are thin, cut to fit with scissors, and wash in the sink. Trim them to the depth of the cabinet top so no edge shows from below.

Every few months, lift the mats, wash them in hot soapy water and lay them back. The cabinet top underneath stays clean, which matters a lot to renters at move-out time.

Then add lids to everything. Lids protect the contents, and liners protect the cabinets.

### A Simple Cleaning Routine

| How often | What to do | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every month | Dust the tops and bin lids with an extendable duster | 5 minutes |
| Every 3 months | Lift the liners, wash them, wipe the bin lids with warm soapy water | 20 minutes |
| Twice a year | Take every bin down, check what is inside, donate what you did not use | 30 minutes |
| Any time you see a sticky film | Wipe with warm water and a few drops of dish soap, then dry | 10 minutes |

For the monthly dusting, the [Swiffer Duster Kit with 6 ft Super Extendable Handle](https://www.amazon.com/Swiffer-Duster-Super-Extendable-Handle-Heavy/dp/B0DWP4Z5YX/?tag=kitchenpot-20) reaches the tops from the floor. You do not need to climb up, so you will actually do it. It also reaches the top of the fridge and the range hood.

Skip harsh degreasers on painted cabinets. They can dull or strip the finish. Warm water with dish soap cuts kitchen grease well enough, and a dry towel after stops water marks.

Clean tops also matter for pests. Crumbs and grease up high can draw ants and roaches to a spot you rarely look at. Our guide on [how to keep pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/) covers the other spots to check.

## What Can I Do With the Space Above My Fridge?

The space above the fridge is the other forgotten zone in a small kitchen. It usually comes in one of two forms: a short, deep cabinet over the fridge, or an open gap with no cabinet at all.

Both are worth using. But the fridge brings its own rules.

- **Leave the gap your fridge needs.** Check the manual for the clearance above and behind the fridge. Do not pack bins tight against the top, or the fridge has to work harder to shed heat.
- **No heat appliances on top.** A microwave, toaster oven or air fryer on top of the fridge traps heat and has no air around it. It is also too high to lift hot food down safely. Our guide on [where to put a microwave in a small kitchen](/blog/where-to-put-microwave-in-small-kitchen/) has better spots.
- **No food.** The top of the fridge is warm, so it is a bad place for bread, snacks or produce.
- **Keep it light.** You are reaching up and over the fridge doors. Heavy items are hard to control at that height.

## Decor That Earns Its Spot

You can put decor on top of kitchen cabinets. Just be choosy, because every object up there is another thing to dust.

Our rule: one or two large pieces beat ten small ones. A few big baskets, a pair of ceramic crocks or a long wooden dough bowl look calm. A row of small figurines looks cluttered and collects grease in every crack.

- **Good:** matching baskets with lids, large ceramic pitchers, big wooden bowls, a few cookbooks in a lidded box at the cool end.
- **Skip:** fake plants and silk flowers (they trap grease and cannot be washed), real plants (too little light, too hard to water), candles (heat softens them, and they are flammable), and anything made of paper near the stove.

Matching containers matter more than the objects inside. Three identical bins read as part of the cabinetry. Three different boxes read as clutter. This is one of the easiest ways to [make a small kitchen look bigger](/blog/make-a-small-kitchen-look-bigger/), since the eye skips over a clean, even line.

If you want the cabinet tops to blend in, choose bins in the same color as the cabinets or the wall. Our [small kitchen colors guide](/blog/small-kitchen-colors/) explains why fewer colors make a small room feel larger.

## Should You Fill the Gap Above Kitchen Cabinets?

Sometimes the best thing to put on top of kitchen cabinets is nothing at all, because you close the gap instead.

**Close the gap if:** you own your home, the gap is under about 12 inches, and you are tired of cleaning it. A carpenter can add trim, crown molding or a soffit (a boxed-in panel) to run the cabinets right up to the ceiling. Grease and dust then have nowhere to land.

**Use the gap if:** you rent, you need the storage, or the gap is 12 inches or more. Lidded bins in a clean row give you a lot of space for little cost.

**Add cabinets if:** you are remodeling anyway. Stacked upper cabinets or taller 42-inch uppers turn the gap into closed storage with doors. Our [small kitchen remodel ideas on a budget](/blog/small-kitchen-remodel-ideas-on-a-budget/) cover when this is worth it.

Renters should not glue trim or panels to the cabinets. Stick with freestanding bins and washable liners. The [renter-friendly kitchen upgrades](/blog/renter-friendly-kitchen-upgrades/) guide lists more changes that come out clean at move-out.

## How to Reach the Top of Your Cabinets Safely

A chair is not a ladder. It tips, it spins and its seat is soft. Use a real step stool with a wide top step and a handle.

The [COSCO 2-Step Big Step Folding Steel Step Stool](https://www.amazon.com/Cosco-2-Step-Big-Step-Folding-Stool/dp/B0032JRVPI/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has two wide steps, a hand grip and a 200-pound rating. It folds to about 2 inches flat, so it slides into the gap beside the fridge.

Follow these steps every time:

1. Open the stool fully and check that both locks click.
2. Stand on the step that puts the cabinet top at chest height, not overhead.
3. Pull the bin toward you by its handle before you lift it.
4. Hand it down to someone, or rest it on the counter before you step off.
5. Never step down while holding something heavy in both hands.

If you cannot lift a bin from chest height with one hand, it is too heavy for the cabinet top. Split it into two bins.

## Keep a List of What Lives Up There

The biggest risk with high storage is buying a second roasting pan because you forgot the first. Keep a short list on your phone or taped inside a cabinet door: bin number, then contents. "Bin 1: Thanksgiving platters, gravy boat, baster. Bin 2: birthday plates, candles, banner."

Update it when you do the twice-a-year check. If a bin went a full year without coming down, look hard at what is inside. Donate what you no longer need and free the space.

## Common Mistakes With the Top of Kitchen Cabinets

These are the mistakes we see most often in small kitchens.

- **Storing anything above the stove.** This is the big one. Heat and grease rise straight up, and baskets, paper and plastic are fuel. Leave the cabinet tops over and beside the range hood empty.
- **Putting food up there.** The top of the cabinets is the warmest spot in the room. Oils turn rancid, spices fade and cans get forgotten.
- **Leaving items loose.** Open items collect a sticky film in weeks. Use lids, or at least liners.
- **Filling the space with fake plants.** They trap grease, cannot be washed and make a small kitchen look busy.
- **Stacking bins too high.** A tall stack is hard to lift and easy to drop. Keep one row in most gaps.
- **Storing heavy things.** Cast iron and glass do not belong overhead. Lift test: one hand, from a stool.
- **Putting an appliance on top of the fridge.** A microwave or air fryer up there has no airflow and is too high to use safely.
