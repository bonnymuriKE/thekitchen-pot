Full-size appliances are built for families. A 6-quart air fryer, a 12-cup coffee maker and a big slow cooker are too much for one person and one small counter.

Here are nine compact appliances sized for single portions, and our advice on which two or three are worth your counter space.

**The short version:** Most solo cooks should start with one cooking appliance. Choose the [Cosori Lite 2.1-Quart Air Fryer](https://www.amazon.com/Cosori-Lite-Small-Air-Fryer-Gray/dp/B0BDFRZX3F/?tag=kitchenpot-20) for crispy food, or the [Breville Mini Smart Oven](https://www.amazon.com/Breville-BOV450XL-Mini-Smart-Oven/dp/B006CVVA7I/?tag=kitchenpot-20) if you also want toast and small bakes. Add the [Instant Pot Duo Mini 3-Quart](https://www.amazon.com/Instant-Pot-Duo-Mini-3-Qt/dp/B06Y1YD5W7/?tag=kitchenpot-20) if you cook beans, grains and stews. In a studio with one or two burners, the [Duxtop 9600LS induction burner](https://www.amazon.com/Duxtop-1800-Watt-Induction-Countertop-9600LS/dp/B01FLR0ET8/?tag=kitchenpot-20) gives you an extra cooking zone you can put away.

## Start With What You Actually Cook

Before buying anything, think back over the last two weeks of meals. The right appliance follows from them.

- **Mostly roasted vegetables, chicken and frozen foods?** An air fryer or toaster oven.
- **Grains, beans, soups and stews?** A multicooker or rice cooker.
- **Coffee or tea every morning?** A single-serve brewer or kettle.
- **Short of burners?** A portable induction burner.

Most solo cooks need two or three of these, not all nine.

## 1. [Cosori Lite 2.1-Quart Air Fryer](https://www.amazon.com/Cosori-Lite-Small-Air-Fryer-Gray/dp/B0BDFRZX3F/?tag=kitchenpot-20): Best Small Air Fryer

- **Capacity:** 2.1 quarts
- **Power:** 900 watts
- **Temperature:** 170 to 400°F
- **Functions:** Air fry, roast, bake and reheat
- **Typical price:** About $60

If we had to pick one appliance for a solo cook, this would be it. A small air fryer does the jobs you would otherwise heat a full oven for. Think a portion of fries, roasted vegetables, a chicken breast, or leftover pizza that comes out crisp instead of soggy.

The size is the reason we chose it over the popular 5 and 6-quart models. A big basket holding one portion of fries is mostly empty air, and the machine takes up a third of a small counter. This one is about 10 inches wide, roughly the footprint of a large bag of flour.

At 900 watts, it draws half the power of many larger fryers, and it is hot in a couple of minutes. Shake the basket halfway through and food crisps evenly.

The trade-off is obvious: it cooks for one. If you regularly feed two, go up to a 4-quart model. For more options, see the [best air fryers under $100](/blog/best-air-fryers-under-100/).

**What we like:**

- Sized for one portion, with a small footprint
- Heats up in minutes
- Low 900-watt draw
- Affordable

**What to know before you buy:**

- Too small to cook for guests
- One portion of fries at a time

**Who should buy it:** Solo cooks who want crispy food without heating the oven.

## How to Choose When Counter Space Is Tight

Most small kitchens have room for two or three appliances on the counter. Choose by role, not by trend.

**Two-appliance kitchen:** An air fryer or toaster oven, plus a kettle or coffee maker. That covers cooking and drinks.

**Three-appliance kitchen:** Add the Instant Pot or rice cooker for grains and soups.

**No-stove studio:** An induction burner, a toaster oven and a microwave cover almost everything.

Store occasional appliances in a cupboard. The waffle maker and rice cooker can live on a shelf until you need them. See [countertop organization ideas for a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/).

## Air Fryer vs Toaster Oven for One

Both cook single portions well. The choice depends on what you eat.

| Feature | Small Air Fryer | Compact Toaster Oven |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crispy food | Better | Good |
| Toast | No | Yes |
| Baking | Limited | Good |
| Footprint | Smaller | Wider |
| Cleanup | Basket | Tray and crumb tray |

If you eat a lot of frozen foods and roasted vegetables, choose the air fryer. If you want toast and small bakes, choose the toaster oven. For more detail, see [NuWave oven vs air fryers](/blog/nuwave-oven-vs-air-fryers/) and the [best small ovens and toaster ovens](/blog/best-small-ovens-and-toaster-ovens-for-baking-in-small-kitchens/).

## What to Skip When You Cook for One

- **Full-size stand mixers.** Heavy and huge. A hand mixer or stick blender handles small batches.
- **12-cup coffee makers.** You will pour most of it away.
- **Large slow cookers.** Too big for one portion. The 3-quart Instant Pot slow cooks too.
- **Full-size food processors.** A 3-cup mini chopper suits single portions.
- **Bread machines.** Bulky and single-use.
- **Large countertop grills.** A skillet or air fryer does the same job.

## Energy Use and Small Appliances

Small appliances often use far less energy than a full oven for one portion. A toaster oven or air fryer heats a small space, so it preheats fast and runs for less time.

An induction burner puts more of its energy into the pan than a coil does. An electric kettle boils water faster than a pot on the stove.

For more, see [most energy-efficient small kitchen appliances](/blog/most-energy-efficient-small-kitchen-appliances/) and [how to reduce kitchen energy use in a small apartment](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/).

## Check Your Outlets First

Small kitchens often have few outlets, and some share one circuit. An 1800-watt toaster oven and an 1800-watt induction burner running together can trip a breaker.

Plug high-wattage appliances into separate outlets where you can, and avoid cheap power strips. See [most energy-efficient small kitchen appliances](/blog/most-energy-efficient-small-kitchen-appliances/) for an energy-monitoring plug that shows what each appliance draws, and our guide to the [best induction cooktop for the money](/blog/best-induction-cooktop-for-the-money/) for higher-power burners. Count your outlets before you buy three plug-in appliances.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- **Buying the family size.** A 6-quart air fryer or 10-cup rice cooker wastes space and energy.
- **Buying for one recipe.** If it does one dish, skip it.
- **Ignoring the footprint.** Measure your counter before you order.
- **Owning two appliances that do the same job.** You rarely need an air fryer and an air fryer toaster oven.
- **Leaving everything on the counter.** Put rarely used appliances away.

## Pair Appliances With the Right Cookware

Compact appliances work best alongside right-sized pans. An 8-inch skillet and a 2-quart saucepan cover what your appliances do not.
