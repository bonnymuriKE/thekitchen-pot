Most Instant Pot vegetables come out soggy for one reason: they were sitting in water, not above it. That is a hardware problem, not a recipe problem.

Steaming needs a gap. Water below, food above, and enough room in between for steam to move all the way around the load.

The catch is that a basket sized for a 6-quart pot will not drop into a 3-quart one, and the listings rarely make that obvious. So below: how steaming actually works inside a pressure cooker, the size chart nobody prints, and six baskets worth buying.

## How Steaming Works Inside a Pressure Cooker

An Instant Pot does not cook with hot air. It cooks with water vapour under pressure, and understanding that explains every design choice in a good basket.

You add a cup or so of water. The pot heats it to boiling, steam fills the sealed chamber, pressure rises, and because pressure and boiling point move together, the steam ends up hotter than 212°F. That hotter steam condenses on the surface of your food and dumps a large amount of energy into it very quickly. That is why broccoli takes zero minutes at pressure and potatoes take twelve.

Three consequences follow, and they are what separate a good basket from a bad one.

**Steam has to reach every surface.** Condensation happens where steam touches food. Anywhere steam cannot reach cooks slower. So a basket needs open sides and an open bottom, and the food needs gaps between pieces. Packing a basket to the rim is the fastest way to get crunchy centres and mushy edges in the same batch.

**Food must stay above the water line.** This is the one that ruins most people's vegetables. Water at the bottom of the pot is boiling hard. Anything sitting in it is being boiled, and boiled vegetables leach flavour and water-soluble vitamins into the liquid. The whole point of a raised basket is to keep the food in the vapour phase.

**The gap underneath is the circulation path.** Steam rises from the water, but it also needs somewhere to move sideways and come up through the bottom of the load. If a basket sits flat on the pot floor, you get a dead zone underneath: the bottom layer of food touches hot metal and sits in condensate, while the top layer steams properly. The difference is visible on a batch of green beans.

### Trivet height, and why it is not just a stand

The wire trivet that came with your Instant Pot does two jobs. It holds the load clear of the water, and it sets the height of that circulation gap.

Most Instant Pot trivets are roughly an inch and a half to two inches tall. That is deliberate. It clears the one to one and a half cups of water most recipes call for, with room left over so a hard boil does not splash up into the food.

Baskets solve this in one of two ways. Some have built-in feet, which set the gap themselves and mean you can skip the trivet entirely. Others are flat-bottomed and are designed to sit on the trivet. Neither approach is better, but you need to know which one you have bought, because a flat-bottomed basket placed straight on the pot floor will boil the bottom of your food.

**The quick rule:** if the basket has no feet, put it on the trivet. If it has feet and you still want more clearance for a long cook, use both.

## The 3-Quart, 6-Quart and 8-Quart Fit Problem

This is where most steamer basket purchases go wrong, and it is easy to avoid once you see the numbers.

| Instant Pot size | Inner pot, widest diameter | Inner pot depth | Basket to buy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3-quart Mini | About 7 in | About 4.75 in | 3-quart basket only |
| 6-quart | About 8.6 in | About 6 in | 6-quart basket |
| 8-quart | About 9.3 in | About 6.8 in | 8-quart, or a 6-quart with room to spare |

A typical 6-quart basket, like the Salbree, measures about 8.1 inches across and 5.2 inches tall. Drop that into a 6-quart pot with 8.6 inches of clearance and it fits with a finger's width to spare. Try it in a 3-quart Mini with a 7-inch opening and it simply will not go in.

Three things to check before you order:

1. **Buy for your pot, not for the brand.** Salbree, Hatrigo and Avokado all sell separate 3-quart, 6-quart and 8-quart versions. Salbree's mini is [here](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07BKQN5ZP/?tag=kitchenpot-20) and its 8-quart is [here](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B077NQY5QN/?tag=kitchenpot-20); Hatrigo's are [3-quart](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0789D4TKZ/?tag=kitchenpot-20) and [8-quart](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0789M3LDW/?tag=kitchenpot-20).
2. **Going smaller works, going bigger does not.** A 6-quart basket sits happily in an 8-quart pot, you just lose some capacity. The reverse is impossible.
3. **Remember the lid.** The basket plus its handle has to clear the sealing ring so the lid can close. Tall handles on a shallow pot are a common annoyance, which is why several of the picks below use a folding or removable handle.

Because the inner pot tapers slightly towards the base, a basket that is a close fit at the rim may sit higher than you expect. That is usually fine, but it eats into headroom.

## Steaming Times Worth Knowing

Pressure steaming is fast, and the difference between right and overcooked is often a single minute. These are pressure-cook times at high pressure with a quick release, using one cup of water in a 6-quart pot.

| Food | Time at pressure | Release |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Broccoli or cauliflower florets | 0 minutes | Quick |
| Green beans | 1 to 2 minutes | Quick |
| Carrots, sliced | 2 to 3 minutes | Quick |
| Eggs, in shell | 5 minutes | 5 minutes natural, then quick |
| Baby potatoes, whole | 8 to 10 minutes | Quick |
| Fish fillets | 3 to 4 minutes | Quick |

Yes, zero minutes is a real setting. The pot reaches pressure, the timer immediately ends, and the time spent coming up to pressure is enough to cook delicate vegetables. Anything longer turns broccoli grey.

Quick release matters just as much as the timer. During a natural release the pot is still hot and food keeps cooking, which is fine for potatoes and ruinous for green beans.

## Getting the Most Out of a Steamer Basket

- **Do not pack it.** Steam has to move between the pieces. A loosely filled basket cooks evenly; a packed one cooks in layers. Cut pieces to a similar size, which is easier with a [mandoline](/blog/how-to-use-a-mandoline/) or a [vegetable chopper](/blog/best-vegetable-choppers/).
- **Use the minimum liquid, not more.** Extra water does not make more steam once the pot is at pressure; it just raises the water level towards your food and lengthens the time to pressure.
- **Salt after, not before.** Steam does not carry salt to the food the way boiling water does, so seasoning in the water is wasted.
- **Lift with the handle, not tongs on the rim.** A basket tipped by tongs dumps its contents into the water, which is the one mistake that undoes the whole point.
- **Make a foil sling for handle-less inserts.** Fold a long strip of foil into a band, sit the pan on it, and use the ends as lifting straps for stacked pans.

Steamed food also keeps well, which is why this is such a useful weeknight technique. ## Cleaning and Storing Your Basket

Stainless baskets are easy. Rinse straight after use while the residue is soft, then dishwasher or hot soapy water. If something has baked on, soak it rather than attacking it with a scouring pad, the same principle as in [how to clean stainless steel pans](/blog/how-to-clean-stainless-steel-pans/).

Mesh needs a little more attention, since starch and protein lodge in the grid. A soft brush under running water does it. The approach in [cleaning an air fryer basket](/blog/how-to-clean-an-air-fryer-basket/) works here too.

Silicone goes in the dishwasher, but if it picks up a smell, leave it in the sun for a few hours or soak it in a baking soda solution.

For storage, the basket that gets used is the one that is easy to reach. Nesting sets and collapsible baskets can live inside the inner pot itself. Everything else does better standing on edge than stacked flat, which is the same principle behind [how to store pots and pans in a small kitchen](/blog/store-pots-and-pans-in-a-small-kitchen/) and the decluttering approach in [countertop organization ideas for a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/).
