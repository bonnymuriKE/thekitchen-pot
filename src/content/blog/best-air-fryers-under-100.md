## How an air fryer actually crisps food

There is no frying happening. Inside the housing sits a coiled heating element and a fan, usually directly above the basket. The element heats the air, the fan drives it down and around the food at speed, and the perforated crisper plate lets it flow underneath as well as over the top.

Speed is the whole trick. Still air insulates food; moving air strips away the thin layer of cooler, damp air that clings to the surface. That lets the outside dry out and hit browning temperature fast while the inside is still cooking through. The browning itself comes from the Maillard reaction and sugar caramelization, which need a dry surface, real heat and a little fat.

Three practical rules fall out of that:

- **One layer only.** Stacked food traps steam and steams itself pale. This is why usable surface area matters more than the quart number on the box.
- **A little oil helps.** A teaspoon or two tossed through the food transfers heat into the surface and feeds browning. Our guide to the [best oil for an air fryer](/blog/best-oil-for-air-fryer/) covers which ones handle 400°F.
- **Shake the basket.** The food touching the crisper plate browns faster than the food on top, so a shake at the halfway point evens things out.

Because the cooking chamber is tiny compared with a full oven, it reaches temperature in a couple of minutes and barely heats your kitchen. That is also why air fryers are one of the [most energy-efficient small kitchen appliances](/blog/most-energy-efficient-small-kitchen-appliances/) for one or two portions, and a real help if you are trying to [reduce kitchen energy use in a small apartment](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/).

## How to choose an air fryer under $100

### Ignore the quart number, look at the floor

This is the single most useful thing to know. Quarts measure volume, but food only browns in one layer, so what matters is how much flat space the crisper plate gives you. In its air fryer testing, [America's Test Kitchen](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/equipment_reviews/2331-air-fryers) found that a larger claimed capacity often did not mean more usable cooking space, and that models with cooking surfaces bigger than about 10 by 10 inches performed best.

| If you cook for | Aim for | What that holds in one layer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One person | 2 to 3 quarts | One chicken breast plus vegetables |
| Two people | 4 quarts | About 2 lbs of fries, or two cutlets |
| Three or four | 5 to 6 quarts | Around 3 lbs of wings |
| Five or more | 7 to 8 quarts | A full side dish for the table |

### Wattage matters when the basket is full

Most models here run 1,500 to 1,750 watts. On a light load the difference is invisible. On a packed basket, the higher-wattage unit recovers temperature faster after you open the drawer, and that shows up as better browning. If you routinely fill the basket, lean toward more watts. If you are watching your electricity bill, the small-appliance-versus-oven maths in our piece on [how many watts an Instant Pot uses](/blog/how-many-watts-does-an-instant-pot-use/) applies here too.

### Check the temperature range at both ends

- **Top end:** 400°F is standard and covers nearly everything. 450°F helps with frozen breaded food and hard-roasted vegetables.
- **Bottom end:** below 120°F means you can dehydrate fruit and make jerky. Above 165°F means you cannot.

### Digital or dial?

Dials are cheaper, more durable and quicker to set. Digital panels give you presets, precise temperatures and often a shake reminder. Neither cooks better. Buy the one you will not resent using at 7pm.

### Think about where it will live

An air fryer that lives in a cupboard gets used half as often. Before you buy, measure the counter depth, the clearance to the upper cabinet and the space behind the unit for the exhaust vent. Most manufacturers ask for about five inches of clearance at the back. If your counters are already full, the fixes in [how to organize a small kitchen with no pantry](/blog/how-to-organize-a-small-kitchen-with-no-pantry/) are worth reading first.

## Getting better results from any air fryer

A few habits do more for your food than an extra $100 of machine.

1. **Preheat for two or three minutes.** A hot basket starts browning on contact instead of slowly warming up.
2. **Dry the food first.** Surface water has to boil off before browning starts. Pat chicken and potatoes dry with a paper towel.
3. **Toss with oil in a bowl, not in the basket.** You use less, and it coats evenly. See our roundup of the [best oils for air frying](/blog/best-oil-for-air-fryer/) for smoke points.
4. **Shake or flip at the halfway point.** The bottom browns first. This is the fix.
5. **Leave gaps.** Food should not touch on all sides. If it does, run two batches.
6. **Use a thermometer for meat.** Poultry needs 165°F internal. Our guide to [cleaning a meat thermometer](/blog/how-to-clean-a-meat-thermometer/) covers doing that safely, and the [best meat thermometers](/blog/best-meat-thermometer-for-smoking/) roundup has picks.
7. **Clean the element, not just the basket.** Grease builds up on the coil and starts to smoke. Unplug, let it cool, and wipe it with a damp cloth every few weeks.

### What air fryers are best at

| Food | Setting that works | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frozen fries | 400°F, shake at halfway | No oil needed; they are pre-fried |
| Chicken wings | 380°F, then 400°F to finish | Pat very dry for crisp skin |
| Reheated pizza | 350°F, 3 to 4 minutes | Beats a microwave badly |
| Roasted vegetables | 400°F, tossed in oil | Cut to even sizes |
| Bacon | 350°F on the crisper plate | Drain the drawer between batches |
| Reheated fried chicken | 375°F | Skin re-crisps rather than softens |

Frozen fries are the classic starting point, and our step-by-step on [cooking frozen french fries in an air fryer](/blog/how-to-cook-frozen-french-fries-in-an-air-fryer/) has exact times. For leftovers, the air fryer beats the microwave for [reheating chicken wings](/blog/how-to-reheat-chicken-wings/) and for [reheating pizza](/blog/how-to-reheat-pizza/) without a soggy base.

### What they are not good at

Wet batters drip through the crisper plate and burn on the element. Large roasts do not fit. Anything that needs gentle, even heat, like custard, belongs in an oven. And a small air fryer will not replace an oven for baking, though a [convection oven](/blog/how-to-bake-cookies-in-a-convection-oven/) uses the same principle at a larger scale. ## Care that keeps the basket working

Nonstick coatings in air fryers fail for predictable reasons: metal tools, aerosol cooking sprays, dishwasher detergent over hundreds of cycles, and scrubbing baked-on grease with an abrasive pad. Treat the basket the way you would treat any [nonstick cookware](/blog/what-is-nonstick-cookware/) and it lasts years instead of months.

Soak the basket in hot soapy water for ten minutes before you touch it with a sponge. Nothing baked on survives that, and you avoid scraping. The same gentle approach that works on a [ceramic pan](/blog/how-to-clean-ceramic-pan/) applies here, since most air fryer baskets use a ceramic-based coating.

Store leftovers properly rather than leaving them in the basket. A set of [airtight food storage containers](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/) keeps reheated food worth reheating.
