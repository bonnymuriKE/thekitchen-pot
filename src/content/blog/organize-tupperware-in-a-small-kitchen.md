Most people blame the cabinet. Wrong culprit. To organize Tupperware in a small kitchen, cut the number of shapes, because round tubs and square boxes from five brands never nest on any shelf.

Takeout tubs, a gift set, a few odd glass bowls and lids that fit nothing pile up fast. Below is how to cut that pile down to one set that nests, pick a lid system and give it a home that stays tidy.

**Quick answer:**

- Pull every container out and keep only the ones with a matching lid.
- Replace the leftovers with one nesting set in two or three sizes, and set a cap, such as 10 containers for one person.
- Nest the bases in a deep drawer or on a pull-out shelf, and file the lids upright right beside them.

## Mixed Shapes Are the Real Problem

When every container is a different brand and shape, nothing nests. A round tub will not sit inside a square box. A tall soup tub will not fit under a short shelf. So you stack them in leaning towers, and the lids slide into the gaps.

Three habits make it worse:

- **Keeping every takeout tub.** They are free, so they never leave.
- **Storing lids separately.** You lose track of which lid fits which base.
- **Putting things away lidded.** A closed container takes up its full size. Nested ones take up the space of the largest one.

Fix the mix and most of the mess goes away. That is why the first job is a cull, not a shopping trip. The same rule runs through all our [small kitchen storage ideas](/blog/small-kitchen-storage-ideas/): fewer, matched things fit where a jumble never will.

## Step One: Cull Your Collection

Do this on a clear counter or the kitchen table. It takes about 30 minutes.

1. **Empty the cabinet or drawer completely.** Pull out every container and every lid, including the ones in the dish rack.
2. **Match every lid to a base.** Snap each one on. Set aside any base with no lid and any lid with no base.
3. **Recycle the orphans.** A container without a lid is a bowl, and you already own bowls.
4. **Toss anything damaged.** Cracked corners, warped lids that will not seal, cloudy plastic and stains that will not wash out all go.
5. **Count what is left.** Compare it to the numbers in the next section.
6. **Pick your keepers.** Keep the most of one shape or brand that nests. Let the odd ones go.

Be honest about takeout tubs. Keep a few if they are all the same size, since matching tubs stack well. A drawer full of ten different tubs is clutter.

## How Many Food Storage Containers Do You Need?

Fewer than you think. Most small kitchens hold twice as many containers as the household uses in a week. Here is our rule of thumb.

| Household | Containers to keep | Sizes we suggest |
|---|---|---|
| One person, cooks a few times a week | 8 to 10 | 1-cup, 2- to 3-cup, 4- to 5-cup |
| One person who meal preps every week | 10 to 14 | Add one 3-cup container per planned lunch |
| Two people | 12 to 16 | 1-cup, 3-cup, 4- to 5-cup, one large 8-cup |
| Family of four | 18 to 24 | Four sizes, plus a few large ones for soup and batch cooking |

Small 1-cup containers hold sauces, dressings and leftover rice. The 2- to 3-cup size is your lunch box. The 4- to 5-cup size holds a full batch of leftovers or cut fruit. One large container covers soup or a salad for two.

## Choose a Set That Nests, Not Just Stacks

This is the single biggest decision. "Stackable" means closed containers sit on top of each other. "Nesting" means the empty bases fit inside each other, like mixing bowls. In a small kitchen, nesting matters more, because containers spend most of their life empty.

Look for three things:

- **Two or three sizes, not seven.** Fewer sizes means fewer lid types.
- **Shared lids.** The best sets use one lid for several base heights.
- **One shape.** All rectangles or all rounds. Rectangles fit drawers and fridge shelves with less wasted space.

## How to Organize Tupperware Lids

Lids cause most of the frustration. They are flat, slippery and different sizes. The answer is to file them upright, like folders in a drawer, sorted by size.

### File Lids Upright

Stand every lid on its edge, largest at the back and smallest at the front. You can see each one at a glance and pull it out without moving the others.

The [YouCopia StoraLid Food Container Lid Organizer](https://www.amazon.com/YouCopia-StoraLid-Food-Container-Lid-Organizer/dp/B081F3SG2N/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is made for exactly this. Its tall dividers adjust to the size of your lids and hold them upright in a cabinet or a drawer. It is the fastest way to turn a slippery pile into a neat row.

On a tight budget, a simple dish rack does the same job. The [Lipper International Bamboo Plate Rack and Pot Lid Holder](https://www.amazon.com/Lipper-International-887-Bamboo-Plate-Rack/dp/B000Y50YKW/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is 15.4 inches long and holds flat lids on edge in slots. Set it at the back of a drawer behind your nested bases.

### Keep Lids Next to Their Bases

Do not give lids their own cabinet across the kitchen. Store them in the same drawer or on the same shelf as the containers they fit. When you grab a base, the lid is right there.

### Cut the Number of Lids

The best lid organizer is owning fewer lids. With a set that shares lids across sizes, a whole drawer of containers might need only three lid sizes. That is the real fix behind every tidy container drawer.

Pot lids are a separate problem with a different answer. We cover those in [how to organize pot lids in a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-organize-pot-lids-in-a-small-kitchen/).

## Should You Store Containers With Lids On?

Usually, no. A container with its lid on cannot nest. Five lidded containers take up five times the space. Five nested bases take up about the space of the biggest one.

There are two exceptions:

- **Sealed sets made to stack closed.** Some glass and latching sets are designed to stack lidded. If you have the shelf height and only a few containers, storing them closed is fine and keeps dust out.
- **Containers for dry snacks or pantry food.** These live full, so of course their lids stay on.

One small tip: leave lids off or loosely set on while a container dries after washing. A sealed container that goes away damp can trap moisture and smell musty.

## How to Organize a Tupperware Drawer

A deep drawer is the best home for food storage containers. You look down into it and see everything at once. Nothing hides at the back.

### Set Up the Drawer

1. **Measure the drawer** inside: width, depth and height. A base cabinet drawer is often about 20 to 22 inches deep and 6 to 10 inches tall inside.
2. **Split it into zones** with dividers. Nested bases on one side, filed lids on the other.
3. **Nest bases by size**, largest on the bottom. Keep each nested stack below the drawer height so the drawer closes without catching.
4. **File lids upright** in the other zone, largest at the back.
5. **Leave a gap of an inch or two** so you can get your fingers in.

Use the deep drawer closest to the fridge or the dishwasher. You pack leftovers near the fridge and unload clean containers near the dishwasher. ## How to Organize Tupperware in a Cabinet

Cabinets are harder because you look in from the front. Anything behind the first row disappears. A standard upper cabinet is 12 inches deep, and a base cabinet is about 24 inches deep, so the back half of a base cabinet is easy to lose.

### Upper Cabinets

## Drawer or Cabinet: Where Should Food Containers Go?

Here is how we decide.

| Your kitchen | Best home for containers | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Has a deep drawer to spare | Deep drawer | You see everything from above |
| Only shallow drawers | Upper cabinet with a lid organizer | Shallow drawers fit lids, not bases |
| Deep base cabinet, owner | Base cabinet with a screw-in pull-out | Uses the full 24-inch depth |
| Deep base cabinet, renter | Base cabinet with bins or a no-drill pull-out | Slides out, leaves no holes |
| Very few cabinets | Rolling cart or open shelf with bins | Containers stay in reach without a cabinet |

## How to Organize Tupperware in a Small Kitchen With No Spare Space

Sometimes there is no free drawer and no free cabinet. Here is what we would do.

1. **Cut the count harder.** Six good containers fit in a single bin.
2. **Switch to deli containers.** One lid size and tight nesting take the least room of any option.
3. **Use silicone bags for half your storage.** The [Stasher Silicone Reusable Sandwich Bag](https://www.amazon.com/Stasher-Silicone-Reusable-Sandwich-Bag-Clear/dp/B01DZQT9CU/?tag=kitchenpot-20) folds flat when empty and goes in the freezer, microwave and dishwasher. A stack of bags fits in a slim drawer that could never hold boxes.
4. **Store containers in a space you already use.** A nested stack can sit inside your largest mixing bowl or a stock pot you rarely use.
5. **Keep a few in the fridge.** If you always have a container of cut fruit or cooked grains going, that container is not taking cabinet space.

## Organize Containers in the Fridge and Freezer

Containers do not stop needing order once they are full. A tidy cabinet means little if the fridge is a jumble of mystery tubs.

### In the Fridge

- **Use one shape.** Rectangles sit side by side with no wasted gaps.
- **Label with the date.** A strip of masking tape and a marker works. Cooked leftovers keep 3 to 4 days in a fridge at 40°F or below.
- **Front means eat first.** Put the oldest leftovers at the front, at eye level.
- **Raw meat goes on the bottom shelf** in a sealed container or tray, so nothing drips onto ready-to-eat food.

Our full plan for small fridges is in [how to organize a small fridge](/blog/organize-a-small-fridge/). For how long specific foods last, see [how long chicken lasts in the fridge](/blog/how-long-does-chicken-last-in-the-fridge/).

## How to Keep Tupperware Organized for Good

Getting it tidy is the easy part. Keeping it that way takes four habits.

1. **Set a number.** Write it on a sticky note inside the cabinet door if you need to. One container comes in only when one goes out.
2. **Put away nested and filed, every time.** Never toss clean containers into the drawer loose. It takes 20 seconds to nest them.
3. **Say no to freebies.** Recycle takeout tubs unless they match the size you already keep.
4. **Check once a month.** Pull any base with a missing lid and any container that is stained or warped.

Wash containers soon after you empty them. Dried tomato sauce stains plastic, and old food smells set in. Put plastic on the top rack of the dishwasher, away from the heating element, so lids do not warp. If your dishwasher leaves a film on containers, it may need a clean; see [how to clean a dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/).

Clean containers that dry on the counter for hours eat up workspace. Dry and put them away the same day. For more ways to keep the counter clear, see our [countertop organization ideas for a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/).

## What Not to Do With Food Storage Containers

- **Buying organizers before you cull.** You end up organizing clutter. Cull first, then measure, then buy.
- **Keeping every size a set comes with.** If you never use the tiny 1/2-cup cups, give them away.
- **Mixing brands.** Two brands means two lid systems and stacks that do not nest. Pick one.
- **Storing lids in a pile.** Flat piles slide and tangle. File them on edge.
- **Using the deepest cabinet with no pull-out.** The back half becomes a graveyard of lids.
- **Storing glass up high.** Heavy glass belongs at waist height or below.
- **Putting containers away wet.** Trapped moisture smells musty. Let them dry with the lids off.
- **Ignoring stains and warping.** A warped lid will not seal. Replace it rather than fight it.
