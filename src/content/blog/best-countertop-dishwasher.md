That makes it the most practical dishwasher for apartments, studios and rentals. The catch is size, so the right one depends on your plates, your faucet and how much counter you can give up.

## How Does a Countertop Dishwasher Connect to a Faucet?

Most models use a quick-connect adapter. You unscrew the aerator, the small mesh cap at the tip of your faucet, and screw the adapter on in its place. A hose carries clean water from it to the dishwasher, and a drain hose empties into the sink.

To run a cycle, snap the hose on and turn the tap to warm or hot. When the cycle ends, turn the tap off and unclip the hose.

### Will It Work With My Faucet?

It works with most standard threaded faucets. It usually does not work with pull-down or pull-out sprayers, many touch-free faucets, or faucets with no threads at the tip.

Unscrew your aerator and check for threads, and note whether they are on the outside (male) or inside (female). Hardware stores sell small adapters that bridge most size gaps.

If your faucet will not take an adapter, a water tank model is the clean fix. That is far easier than swapping a faucet in a rental.

### Can You Use the Sink While It Runs?

Not the faucet. It is tied up for the whole cycle, which can run close to two hours on a normal setting. You can still use the sink basin, as long as the drain hose sits where it will not get knocked loose.

A [cutting board that fits over the sink](/blog/best-over-the-sink-cutting-board/) helps here. You keep a prep surface while the hose and drain do their work below.

## Faucet Hookup vs. Water Tank: Which One Do You Need?

This is the first choice to make, and it decides the rest.

| | Faucet hookup | Water tank |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it fills | From the tap through a hose | You pour about 5 liters in by hand |
| Faucet needed | Standard threaded faucet | Any, or none nearby |
| Typical capacity | 6 place settings | 2 to 4 place settings |
| Ties up the faucet | Yes, during each cycle | No |
| Best for | Daily use, two or more people | Pull-down faucets, RVs, dorms |

A faucet hookup model suits most kitchens, since it holds three times as much. A tank model wins when the faucet will not cooperate, and many can switch to a faucet hookup later.

## How Many Dishes Really Fit?

"Six place settings" sounds like dinner for six. In practice, it is closer to one day of dishes for two people.

A place setting is a standard test set: a dinner plate, side plate, bowl, cup, saucer, glass and utensils. Real kitchens add mixing bowls, a pot and food storage tubs, which fill space fast.

### Measure Your Dinner Plates First

Plates that are too big are the most common reason people return a countertop dishwasher.

Most 6-place countertop models take plates up to about 10 inches across. Many modern dinner plates are 10.5 to 11 inches. Measure yours across the widest part, rim included.

If yours are too wide, lay them flat and accept fewer per load, pick the SPT, or switch to smaller plates. Our guide to [what ceramic dinnerware is](/blog/what-is-ceramic-dinnerware/) helps if you are choosing new plates.

### What About Pots and Pans?

Small saucepans and lids fit. A 10-inch skillet usually fits only if nothing else is in the rack.

Some cookware should not go in any dishwasher. Nonstick coatings wear faster, and you can read why in our explainer on [what nonstick cookware is](/blog/what-is-nonstick-cookware/). Cast iron rusts. Good knives dull and their handles crack, so keep them on a [magnetic knife strip](/blog/best-magnetic-knife-strip/) and wash them by hand.

## Do Countertop Dishwashers Dry Well?

They dry glass and ceramic well. Plastic is another story.

Most countertop machines use residual heat. The final rinse is hot, and that heat helps water evaporate after the cycle ends. Glass and ceramic hold heat, so they dry. Plastic does not, so tubs and lids come out wet.

Rinse aid helps water sheet off instead of beading. A bottle of [Finish Jet-Dry rinse aid](https://www.amazon.com/Finish-Jet-Dry-rinse-aid/dp/B00824TD86/?tag=kitchenpot-20) goes into the same dispenser a full-size machine uses. Opening the door a crack after the cycle lets steam out, which is what the auto-open door on the Midea tank model does for you.

Plastic containers can finish on a rack or a towel. Our tips for [organizing food storage containers](/blog/organize-tupperware-in-a-small-kitchen/) also help you keep the lids sorted after washing.

## How Long Does a Cycle Take?

A normal cycle on a countertop dishwasher runs about two hours. Heavy cycles run a little longer. Quick or rapid cycles can finish in about 40 minutes, but they use less heat and suit lightly soiled dishes.

Long cycles are not a fault. Lower heat and longer soaking use less energy than a short, hot blast. Use delay start so the time does not matter: start it before bed and empty it in the morning.

## Water and Energy Use in Plain Terms

A certified 6-place countertop dishwasher uses about 2 to 3 gallons of water per load. That is less than one minute of a kitchen faucet running, since most faucets flow at up to 2.2 gallons a minute.

Energy use is low as well. The ENERGY STAR models here are rated around 155 kWh a year. At roughly 16 cents per kWh, that is about $25 a year.

## Where Will It Go? Counter Space and Placement

Place it right next to the sink, so the roughly 5-foot hoses reach the faucet and basin without stretching.

Check these four numbers before you order:

1. **Width and depth:** Most 6-place models need about 22 by 20 inches of counter.
2. **Height under cabinets:** Most are about 17 inches tall. Standard upper cabinets sit about 18 inches above the counter, so there is little room to spare.
3. **Door swing:** The door drops down and forward. With it open, the machine can need over 30 inches of depth.
4. **Power:** Plug it straight into a grounded outlet. Do not use an extension cord with a water appliance.

## Tips for Renters

A countertop dishwasher is one of the best [renter-friendly kitchen upgrades](/blog/renter-friendly-kitchen-upgrades/) because it changes nothing permanent. A few habits keep it that way:

- **Keep the old aerator.** Put it in a labeled bag under the sink so you can put it back when you move out. Our guide to [organizing under the kitchen sink](/blog/organize-under-the-kitchen-sink/) has a spot for it.
- **Skip rolling carts.** At about 45 pounds empty, it needs a solid counter.
- **Disconnect between uses.** Unclip the hose after each cycle. Then the faucet is free and nothing leaks while you are out.

## How to Keep It Running Well

Clean the filter at the bottom of the tub every week or two, since a small filter clogs fast. Once a month, run an empty hot cycle with a dishwasher cleaner. Our guide on [how to clean a dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/) walks through each step.

## Is a Countertop Dishwasher Worth It?

For most people with no built-in dishwasher, yes. It saves time, uses less water than a running tap and moves with you. It is worth it if:

- You cook at home most days and wash dishes daily.
- You have about 22 by 20 inches of counter beside the sink.
- Your plates are 10 inches or smaller, or you can lay them flat.

It is not worth it if:

- You cook a few times a week and wash by hand in minutes.
- That counter space is your only prep area.
- You need to wash large pots and sheet pans most of the time.

Cooking for one? A countertop machine handles one person's dishes with room to spare, and our guide to [cooking for one without wasting food](/blog/cook-for-one-without-wasting-food/) keeps the pile small.

Countertop dishwashers with a faucet hookup usually sell for about $250 to $400. Water tank models fall in about the same range, even though they are smaller, because the tank and pump add parts. An 18-inch portable usually runs about $550 to $700. A known brand with parts and support is the safer buy for something that sits next to water every day.

## Which One Should You Buy?

If your faucet has a standard threaded tip and your plates are 10 inches or less, get the **Midea MDC22P1AWW**. It is quiet, efficient and covers almost every need.

If your plates run larger, the **SPT SD-2224DS** gives you that extra quarter inch. If tall glasses and bottles are the issue, choose the **Danby DDW631SDB**. If you want to run it overnight or at off-peak hours, the **BLACK+DECKER BCD6W** has the longest delay timer.

If your faucet is a pull-down sprayer, skip the adapter hunt and buy the **Midea MDC17P4CWW** with its own tank. And if you have floor space but want more capacity, the **SPT SD-9263SS** portable is the step up.
