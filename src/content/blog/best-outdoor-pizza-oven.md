A home oven tops out around 500°F. A small outdoor pizza oven hits 900°F in about 15 minutes, and that gap is the whole reason these ovens exist.

At that heat, a thin pizza bakes in 60 to 90 seconds. The crust puffs up and gets dark spots before the middle dries out. No home oven can copy that, even with a good stone.

The picks below are portable ovens that sit on a patio table or a small backyard stand. They cover gas, real wood and one electric oven for homes where open flames are not allowed.

**The short version:** For most people, the [Gozney Roccbox](https://www.amazon.com/Gozney-Roccbox-Outdoor-Portable-Restaurant-Grade/dp/B0D6PQJ2PF/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is the best outdoor pizza oven: thick stone, a cool-touch outer jacket and steady gas heat. On a tighter budget, get the [Solo Stove Pi Prime](https://www.amazon.com/Solo-Stove-Pi-Prime-Pizza-Oven-Propane/dp/B0CG2MB8YB/?tag=kitchenpot-20). Before you buy any of them, confirm your lease and local fire code allow an open flame where you plan to cook.

## How to Choose the Best Outdoor Pizza Oven

Baking on a stone in your home oven is a different job; our guide to the [best pizza stone](/blog/best-pizza-stone/) covers that. These ovens exist for one thing: very high heat from above and below at the same time.

### Gas, Wood or Electric: Which Fuel Is Best?

| | Gas (propane) | Wood or charcoal | Electric |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Heat-up time | About 15 to 20 minutes | About 15 to 20 minutes, plus fire building | About 15 to 20 minutes |
| Top heat | 950°F | 950°F | Around 700 to 850°F |
| Effort while cooking | Turn a dial | Add wood every few minutes | Press a button |
| Flavor | Clean | Light smoke | Clean |
| Where it can go | Outdoors only | Outdoors only, away from neighbors' windows | Indoors (some models outdoors too) |
| Best for | Most people | Fire lovers | Renters and winter cooking |

Gas is the easy choice for most homes. You run it off a standard 20-pound propane tank, the same kind a gas grill uses. Keep an eye on the level with the tips in our guide on [how to tell if your propane tank is empty](/blog/how-to-tell-if-your-grills-propane-tank-is-empty/). If the flame looks small or lazy, our fix list for a [weak gas grill flame](/blog/why-your-gas-grill-has-a-weak-flame/) applies to pizza ovens too.

Wood gives flavor and a show, but it asks for attention.

Electric is the only safe option where flames are banned. Our guide to [gas vs electric grills for small spaces](/blog/gas-vs-electric-grill-for-small-spaces/) walks through the same trade-offs for grilling.

### Why 700 to 950°F Matters for Neapolitan Pizza

Neapolitan pizza is the soft, thin style from Naples with a puffy, spotted rim. It is meant to bake in about 60 to 90 seconds. That only happens when the stone and the air above it are both very hot, roughly 700 to 900°F.

Speed is what makes the texture. In a fast bake, water in the dough turns to steam and puffs the rim before the crust can dry out. In a 500°F home oven, the same dough needs 8 to 10 minutes and comes out crisp and cracker-like instead.

The two heat sources need to match. If the stone is hotter than the air, the bottom burns before the top cooks. If the flame is hotter than the stone, the cheese browns over a pale base. Let the oven heat fully, check the stone, then launch.

Good dough helps too. A dough that rests overnight in the fridge handles high heat better. A [bread proofing basket](/blog/best-bread-proofing-basket-reviews/) is handy for dough balls, and a [stand mixer](/blog/best-stand-mixer/) saves your arms on big batches.

### 12-Inch or 16-Inch: What Size Pizza Oven Do You Need?

A 12-inch oven bakes a pizza that feeds one hungry adult or two light eaters. Each bake takes a minute or two, so a small group is easy.

A 14- or 16-inch oven makes pizzas big enough to share. The trade-off is weight, space and propane use. Bigger ovens also need a bigger peel and a sturdier table.

For a couple or a small family, 12 inches is enough. If you host often, go for 14 inches or more. Our ideas on [vertical storage for small kitchens](/blog/vertical-storage-ideas-for-small-kitchens/) can free a shelf for the peel and gear.

### Launching and Turning: The Skill That Takes Practice

Most first-pizza disasters happen at the launch, not in the oven. The dough sticks to the peel, folds over at the mouth and lands as a calzone.

A few habits fix most of this:

1. Dust the peel lightly with semolina or flour before the dough goes on.
2. Build the pizza quickly, right on the peel. Wet toppings soak through if it sits.
3. Give the peel a small shake. If the pizza slides, it is ready.
4. Hold the peel at the back of the stone and pull it out with one quick motion.

A perforated metal peel makes launching easier. The holes let extra flour fall away so it does not burn on the stone. The [Ooni 12-Inch Perforated Pizza Peel](https://www.amazon.com/Ooni-12-Perforated-Pizza-Peel/dp/B07T1JPCNK/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has a light aluminum blade and a heat-resistant handle.

Turning is the second skill. The side nearest the flame cooks fastest, so you rotate the pizza a quarter turn every 15 to 20 seconds. A small round turning peel does this without pulling the pizza out. The [Gozney Utility Turning Peel](https://www.amazon.com/Gozney-Utility-Pizza-Turning-Peel/dp/B0CZBQNVP2/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has a 6-inch stainless blade and is 31.5 inches long overall, which keeps your hand out of the heat.

### Why an Infrared Thermometer Helps

The number on a dial tells you about the air. What matters most is the stone. An infrared thermometer reads the surface from a distance with a laser dot.

Check that it reads high enough. Many cheap models stop around 700°F, which is too low for a pizza oven. The [Ooni Digital Infrared Thermometer](https://www.amazon.com/Ooni-Digital-Infrared-Thermometer/dp/B0CBV8YS6Q/?tag=kitchenpot-20) reads up to 999°F and has a setting for cordierite stones. Aim for the middle of the stone at about 750 to 850°F before launching a Neapolitan pizza.

This is a different tool from a probe thermometer. Our guide to the [best meat thermometer for smoking](/blog/best-meat-thermometer-for-smoking/) covers probes for meat. A good pair of [heat-resistant gloves](/blog/10-best-heat-resistant-gloves-for-cooking/) is also worth having when you move a hot oven or pan.

## Can You Use an Outdoor Pizza Oven on a Balcony?

Usually, no. Gas and wood pizza ovens have an open flame. Many apartment leases and local fire codes ban open-flame cookers on balconies, especially in buildings with several units. Many also limit propane tanks on balconies.

Rules differ by city and building, so check two things before you buy. Read your lease, and look up your local fire department's rules. Our guide to [balcony grilling safety rules](/blog/balcony-grilling-safety-tips-and-rules/) explains where to find them and what renters' insurance may say.

If flames are out, the Breville Pizzaiolo above is the safe route. For grilling under the same limits, see our lists of [small grills for balconies and patios](/blog/best-small-grills-for-balconies-and-patios/) and [portable grills for apartment living](/blog/best-portable-grills-for-apartment-living/). Many of those are electric.

## Where to Set Up an Outdoor Pizza Oven

Pick a spot that is open to the sky and away from walls, fences, railings and overhangs. Pizza ovens push heat out of the front and top, and a wood oven sends smoke and sparks out of the chimney.

Set it on a stable, heat-safe surface. A metal table, a stone counter or a dedicated oven stand works well. Avoid plastic tables and anything wobbly.

Point the oven's mouth away from the wind. A strong breeze blowing into the opening can push the flame around or put a gas flame out. For more layout ideas, our guide to [small-space grilling accessories](/blog/small-space-grilling-accessories-you-actually-need/) covers side tables and tool hooks that keep a small patio tidy.

## Rain, Covers and Storage

A pizza oven can live outside if it is covered, but the stone must stay dry. A wet stone can crack when it heats, as water inside turns to steam. If the stone gets soaked, let it air-dry for a day, then run the oven on low for a while before you turn it up.

Never put cold water on a hot stone. To clean it, let the oven run hot for a few minutes after cooking. Food burns to ash, and you brush it off once cool. Do not use soap on the stone.

Smaller ovens like the Roccbox and Tread can come indoors for winter. Store the peel and tools together on a hook or shelf. Our guide to [storing small appliances in a small kitchen](/blog/store-small-appliances-in-a-small-kitchen/) has ideas that work for pizza gear too.

## What Else Can You Cook in a Pizza Oven?

Anything that likes very high heat for a short time works well. Flatbreads, garlic bread, roasted peppers and thin steaks are all good starting points.

Use a small cast iron pan for anything with fat or juice. A pan like the one in our [Lodge cast iron skillet review](/blog/lodge-cast-iron-skillet-review/) keeps grease off the stone. Turn the flame down for thicker food so the outside does not burn before the middle cooks.

Leftover slices are another use. A hot oven crisps a cold slice in a minute. Our guide on [how to reheat pizza](/blog/how-to-reheat-pizza/) covers the skillet and oven methods for nights when the outdoor oven is packed away.

## How Much Should You Spend?

Most portable 12-inch gas ovens fall between about $300 and $500. Wood and multi-fuel ovens sit in the same range, with the gas burner as an extra cost. Larger 14- to 16-inch ovens usually run $700 and up.

Factor in the extras too. A peel, a turning peel, an infrared thermometer and a cover add up.

If you are setting up a backyard cooking area from scratch, a pizza oven is a second cooker, not a replacement for a grill. Our roundup of [budget gas grills](/blog/best-gas-grills-under-500/) covers the everyday cooker that does burgers and vegetables.
