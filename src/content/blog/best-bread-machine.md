## Horizontal or Vertical Pan: What Changes in the Loaf?

The pan shape decides what your slices look like.

A **horizontal pan** lies flat, like a loaf tin. It makes a long, low loaf with slices that fit a toaster and a sandwich. Because the pan is long, one paddle in the middle cannot reach the ends well. So good horizontal machines use two paddles, one at each end. The Zojirushi Virtuoso Plus, Zojirushi Supreme and West Bend work this way.

A **vertical pan** stands upright. It makes a tall, almost square loaf. The slices are large, and they may stick out of a narrow toaster slot. The upside is a smaller footprint, since the machine can be narrow and deep instead of wide. One paddle is enough for a pan this shape.

| | Horizontal pan | Vertical (upright) pan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loaf shape | Long and low, like store bread | Tall and square |
| Paddles | Usually two | Usually one |
| Counter width | Wider | Narrower |
| Slices in a toaster | Fit easily | Can stick out of the top |
| Typical price | Mid to high | Budget to mid |

If you mainly make toast and sandwiches, a horizontal pan is the better choice. If the loaf is mostly for tearing and dipping, the shape matters less. Pair it with a [2-slice toaster](/blog/best-2-slice-toaster/) that has wide slots.

Loaf size is the weight of the finished bread. Most machines offer two or three sizes, and you pick one before you start.

Homemade bread has no preservatives, so it goes stale faster than store bread. A smaller loaf you finish is better than a big one you throw away. Store the finished loaf in a bread box or an [airtight container](/blog/best-airtight-food-storage-containers/) once it has fully cooled. Slice and freeze what you will not eat in three days.

## Single vs Dual Paddles, and the Paddle Hole

The paddle is the small blade at the bottom of the pan that kneads the dough. It sits on a metal shaft that turns from underneath.

Here is the catch. The paddle stays in the pan while the bread bakes, so the loaf forms around it. When you tip the loaf out, the paddle either comes with it or leaves a hole. A two-paddle machine leaves two holes.

You have three ways to deal with it:

1. **Pull the paddle out early.** Wait for the last knead to finish, then lift the dough, take out the paddle and put the dough back. Use wet or oiled hands.
2. **Use a folding paddle.** The Breville Custom Loaf folds its paddle flat before baking, which leaves a much smaller mark.
3. **Use the dough setting and bake in a tin.** You get no hole at all, and any loaf shape you like.

Most people simply stop caring after a few loaves. Still, if the hole bothers you, choose your machine with that in mind.

## What the Settings Actually Do

### Gluten-Free

Gluten is what makes wheat dough stretchy. Gluten-free dough has none, so long kneading does nothing useful. It is closer to a thick batter. The gluten-free setting mixes it well, gives it one rise and bakes it, skipping the punch-down that would knock the air out. Use a gluten-free bread mix or a recipe written for your machine. Keep your gluten-free flours in [sealed containers in a cool spot](/blog/store-baking-supplies-in-a-small-kitchen/) so they stay fresh.

### Jam

The jam setting heats chopped fruit, sugar and pectin while the paddle stirs slowly. You get a small batch of soft jam in about an hour, with no pot to watch on the stove. Stick to the fruit amounts in the manual so it does not bubble over.

### Dough

This is the setting many owners end up using most. The machine mixes and kneads, then lets the dough rise once in the warm pan. When it beeps, you take it out, shape it and bake it in your oven. It is the easiest way to make pizza dough, rolls, bagels and cinnamon rolls. For crisp pizza, bake it on a [pizza stone](/blog/best-pizza-stone/).

### Delay Timer

The delay timer lets you load the pan at night and set the finish time. Most machines here allow 13 hours, and the KBS allows 15. Do not use the delay timer with fresh milk, eggs or other foods that spoil at room temperature. Use dried milk powder in delayed recipes instead. Also keep the yeast away from the water and salt until mixing starts. Most recipes do this by adding liquid first and yeast last, in a small dip on top of the flour.

## Can You Use a Bread Machine Only for Dough?

Yes, and it is a smart way to get crusty bread. The machine is good at kneading and keeping dough warm. It is less good at baking a crisp, open crust, because the pan is closed and the heat is gentle.

So use the dough cycle, then shape the loaf and bake it in a hot pot. This is how you get a crackly, artisan-style crust from a machine. A heavy lidded pot traps steam for the first part of the bake. Our guide to the [best Dutch oven](/blog/best-dutch-oven/) covers good pots for this.

For a free-form loaf, a proofing basket holds the shape during the final rise. A [hand mixer](/blog/best-hand-mixers/) cannot knead stiff bread dough well, so the machine fills that gap.

## Will a Bread Machine Fit in a Small Kitchen?

A bread machine is one of the bulkier small appliances. The widest model here is 18 inches across. The slimmest is 8.5 inches. All are about 11 to 13 inches tall, so most fit under upper cabinets.

Measure two things before you buy:

- **Counter width.** Leave a few inches on each side for air to move.

If it will not live on the counter, plan where it goes between bakes. A low cabinet near an outlet works best, because these machines are heavy. ## How Loud Is a Bread Machine?

The kneading stage is the loud part. The motor hums and the pan can thump as the dough moves. It lasts about 15 to 30 minutes per cycle, split into short bursts. Rising and baking are close to silent.

The beeps can be more annoying than the motor. Most machines beep for add-ins and again when the loaf is done. The Breville has a mute button. If you set a delay timer to finish early in the morning, check that the kneading will not start while people are asleep nearby.

Place the machine on a solid, level surface. A wobbly table makes the kneading noise much worse, and a heavy machine can creep toward the edge.

## How Much Should You Spend on a Bread Machine?

| Budget | What you get | Examples on this list |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under $130 | Upright or horizontal pan, gluten-free setting, delay timer | Hamilton Beach, West Bend, KBS |
| About $300 to $400 | Better build, two paddles or a folding paddle, custom settings | Zojirushi Supreme, Zojirushi Mini, Breville |
| About $400 and up | Lid heater, many special-diet courses, best loaf shape | Zojirushi Virtuoso Plus |

A cheap machine makes good bread. What you pay more for is loaf shape, even browning, quieter running and a machine that lasts many years of weekly use. If you are not sure you will bake often, start at the low end. Our piece on [whether expensive cookware is actually worth it](/blog/is-expensive-cookware-actually-worth-it/) follows the same logic.

Bread machines also use little energy for what they do. They draw 450 to 830 watts, far less than a full oven. That makes them a sensible pick among [energy-efficient small kitchen appliances](/blog/most-energy-efficient-small-kitchen-appliances/), especially in summer when you do not want to heat the whole flat.

## Getting Good Bread From Day One

- **Measure flour by weight if you can.** Too much flour is the most common cause of a short, dense loaf.
- **Use bread flour for wheat loaves.** It has more protein, so the loaf rises higher.
- **Add ingredients in the order the manual gives.** Machines are designed around that order.
- **Let the loaf cool for an hour before slicing.** It keeps baking inside as it cools. Use a serrated knife on a [sturdy cutting board](/blog/best-cutting-board/).
- **Wipe the pan, do not scrub it.** Soak stuck bits with warm water, then wipe with a soft cloth.

Keep your flour, yeast and mixes together in one spot. Our [small pantry organization ideas](/blog/small-pantry-organization-ideas/) help with that, and [space-saving baking tools](/blog/space-saving-baking-tool-essentials/) cover the few extras worth owning.
