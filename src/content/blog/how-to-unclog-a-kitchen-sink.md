Water that will not go down, with bits of onion skin floating on top, means something has closed off the pipe somewhere between the drain hole and the wall. The fix depends on where it sits.

This is how to unclog a kitchen sink in the right order: find the likely cause, then try the gentlest fix first and move up only when it fails. Most clogs clear in under 30 minutes with a few cheap tools and no harsh chemicals.

**What to do first:** Check whether one basin or both are backed up, and whether the disposal hums or stays silent. Bail standing water into a bucket, then try a few tablespoons of dish soap and very hot tap water. If the water still sits, use a flat cup plunger with the other drain sealed. If that fails, take off the P-trap and clean it, then run a hand auger into the wall pipe. Skip chemical drain cleaners entirely.

## Why Kitchen Sinks Clog

A kitchen clog is almost never one big object. It is a slow build-up that finally closes the pipe.

- **Grease and oil.** Bacon fat and cooking oil go down warm and harden into a waxy coat in the cold pipe. Food sticks to it, and the opening shrinks every week.
- **Starchy and swelling food.** Rice, pasta, oats and potato peels soak up water and turn into a paste that sets in the trap.
- **Coffee grounds and eggshells.** They do not dissolve. They settle in the low point of the pipe like sand.
- **Garbage disposal jams.** A fruit pit, a bottle cap or a pile of stringy celery stops the disposal from spinning, and the water sits.
- **Dishwasher waste.** Most dishwashers drain into the sink plumbing. Food that gets past a dirty filter ends up in the same pipe. Our guide to [cleaning a dishwasher filter](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/) covers that side of it.

### One Basin or Both? Where the Clog Sits

Before you grab any tool, look at which side is backed up. A double sink has two short pipes that join into one before the trap. That join tells you a lot.

| What you see | Where the clog is | Best first move |
|---|---|---|
| Only one basin is full | In that basin's tailpiece or disposal | Plunge that side; check the disposal |
| Both basins are full | In the P-trap or the wall pipe past it | Seal one side and plunge, then pull the trap |
| Water rises in the other basin when one drains | Below the join | P-trap, then auger |
| Water comes up when the dishwasher drains | Shared line or the disposal inlet | Clear the sink first, then the dishwasher hose |
| Sink drains slowly but does not fill | Partial grease coat | Dish soap and hot water, then enzyme upkeep |
| Other sinks or the tub gurgle too | Main drain line | Stop and call a plumber |

## How to Unclog a Kitchen Sink With Standing Water

Standing water makes every method harder. Hot water cools in it, soap gets diluted, and a plunger splashes dirty water everywhere. So start by emptying it.

1. Put on a pair of [Playtex Living Reusable Rubber Cleaning Gloves](https://www.amazon.com/Playtex-Living-Reuseable-Rubber-Cleaning/dp/B07DCVVSXL/?tag=kitchenpot-20). Sink water that has sat for hours is full of old food.
2. Scoop the water into a bucket with a mug or a small pot. Leave about 2 inches in the basin if you plan to plunge.
3. Pull out any food you can see in the drain opening or strainer basket.
4. Pour the bucket into a toilet, not back into another sink.

Lay a couple of old towels on the counter and the cabinet floor. A spare set of [kitchen towels](/blog/best-kitchen-towels/) you do not mind staining is ideal here.

## Hot Water: The Right Temperature for Your Pipes

Hot water softens grease so it can move. It is the gentlest fix and often all a slow drain needs. But boiling water is not safe for every sink.

Look under the sink before you pour. White or gray plastic pipe is PVC. Its glued joints can soften and loosen with boiling water. Porcelain and fireclay sinks can crack from sudden heat, and an undermount sink's sealant can loosen too. If you own one of the sinks in our guides to [porcelain sinks with drainboards](/blog/best-porcelain-kitchen-sink-with-drainboard/) or [white undermount sinks](/blog/best-white-undermount-kitchen-sink/), stick to tap water.

| Sink and pipes | Safe water | How to pour |
|---|---|---|
| Stainless steel sink, metal pipes | Kettle water just off the boil | Pour slowly in 3 batches, a minute apart |
| Stainless steel sink, PVC pipes | Hottest tap water | Run it for 2 to 3 minutes |
| Porcelain, fireclay or composite sink | Hottest tap water | Run it for 2 to 3 minutes |
| Sink full of standing water | Bail it out first | Hot water does nothing sitting on top |

Most home water heaters are set around 120°F, which is warm enough to loosen fresh grease. If you heat water in a kettle, let it rest a minute off the heat. A scaled-up kettle heats slowly, so [descale your electric kettle](/blog/how-to-clean-an-electric-kettle/) if it takes forever.

## Dish Soap and Hot Water for a Grease Clog

When the cause is grease, dish soap is your best friend. It is made to break up fat, and it is safe for every pipe and sink.

1. Empty the standing water.
2. Squirt about 2 to 3 tablespoons of liquid dish soap straight into the drain.
3. Wait 10 minutes so it can coat the grease.
4. Pour a gallon of very hot water (safe for your sink, as above) in a steady stream.
5. Repeat once if the water starts to move but slowly.

This works best on a kitchen sink clogged with grease that is still soft. It will not shift a hard plug of rice or a bottle cap. If two rounds do nothing, the clog is solid, and you need force.

## Does Baking Soda and Vinegar Unclog a Kitchen Sink?

This is the most shared drain trick there is, so here is the plain truth. The fizz looks busy, but it is the two ingredients cancelling each other out. What is left is mostly salty water. It has very little power to push or dissolve a real clog.

It still has a use. On a slow drain, the bubbling and a hot rinse can lift a thin film and freshen a smelly pipe.

1. Pour 1/2 cup of baking soda into the empty drain.
2. Follow with 1 cup of white vinegar and cover the drain with a plug.
3. Wait 15 to 30 minutes.
4. Flush with hot water.

Never use it right after a chemical drain cleaner. The vinegar can react with what is still in the pipe and splash back. And do not expect it to fix a sink full of standing water. Treat it as upkeep, like the deodorizing step in a [smelly kitchen guide](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/), not a repair.

## How to Plunge a Kitchen Sink the Right Way

A plunger is the first tool with real force. Most people fail with it for two reasons: they use the wrong plunger, and they let the air escape through the other drain.

### Use a Cup Plunger, Not a Toilet Plunger

A sink needs a flat-bottomed cup plunger. A toilet plunger has a soft flange that sticks out underneath, and it will not seal on a flat drain. Keep a separate plunger for the kitchen. The [Korky Beehive Mini Sink and Drain Plunger](https://www.amazon.com/Korky-94-4A-Beehive-Drain-Plunger/dp/B073Z9FRB2/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is sized for sink drains, and its ridged, beehive-shaped cup grips the basin instead of sliding around. It is short enough to store under the sink.

### Block Every Escape Route

Water and air take the easiest path. If you plunge one drain while the other is open, the push just pops out of the second basin.

- **Double sink:** Put the stopper in the other drain and hold it down, or press a wet rag over it. Better still, have someone hold it.
- **Dishwasher:** Its drain hose connects to the disposal or the drain pipe. Clamp the hose gently with a spring clamp or a binder clip, or the push can send dirty water into the dishwasher.
- **Overflow hole:** A few kitchen sinks have one. Tape over it or cover it with a wet rag.

### The Plunging Method

1. Leave about 2 inches of water in the basin, enough to cover the cup.
2. Set the plunger over the drain and tilt it to push out trapped air.
3. Press down gently for the first push to seal it.
4. Pump up and down hard and fast, 15 to 20 times, keeping the seal.
5. Pull the plunger off sharply on the last stroke.
6. Watch the water. If it swirls away, run hot water for a minute to flush.

Try three rounds before giving up. If nothing moves, the clog is packed tight or sits further down the line.

## How to Unclog a Kitchen Sink Without a Plunger

No plunger? Use dish soap and hot water, then go straight to the P-trap. For a clog sitting near the top of the drain, under the strainer basket, a short barbed strip can help. The [Zip-it 25-inch Drain Clog Remover](https://www.amazon.com/Zip-Removal-25-inch-Cleaner-Seconds/dp/B0B196HVDL/?tag=kitchenpot-20) slides down the side without a disposal and pulls up the mat of peels and grit caught there. It is short and soft, so it will not reach past the trap. For anything deeper, use an auger.

## How to Clean the P-Trap Under a Kitchen Sink

The P-trap is the U-shaped bend under the sink. It holds a little water to block sewer gas, and it is also where heavy food settles. Pulling it off and cleaning it fixes a large share of kitchen clogs with no force at all.

Clear the cabinet out first. If yours is packed with cleaners and bins, our guide to [organizing under the kitchen sink](/blog/organize-under-the-kitchen-sink/) shows how to keep the trap reachable next time.

### What You Need

- A bucket or a wide pan that fits under the trap
- Rubber gloves and an old towel
- A pair of tongue-and-groove pliers, like the [Channellock 420 Tongue and Groove Pliers](https://www.amazon.com/Channellock-420-2-Inch-Capacity-Tongue/dp/B000040OUZ/?tag=kitchenpot-20), for stuck nuts
- A bottle brush or an old toothbrush

### Step by Step

1. Bail out as much sink water as you can. The trap will drain into your bucket when it comes off.
2. Set the bucket directly under the U-bend.
3. Loosen the two large slip nuts at each end of the U-bend. Most plastic nuts turn by hand. If one is stuck, wrap it in a cloth and use the pliers gently. Too much force cracks plastic.
4. Lower the trap and tip it into the bucket. Mind the rubber or plastic washers inside each nut.
5. Clean the trap out with a brush in the bucket, not in the clogged sink.
6. Look into the short pipe going into the wall (the trap arm). If you can see a plug of grease, pull it out with a gloved finger.
7. Refit the trap with the washers in place. Tighten the nuts by hand, then add a quarter turn with the pliers on metal pipes only.
8. Run water for a minute and check every joint for drips with a dry paper towel.

Tell everyone in the home not to use the sink while the trap is off. It sounds obvious, but a running tap fills the cabinet in seconds.

### Kitchen Sink Clogged Past the P-Trap?

If the trap is clean and water still backs up, the clog sits in the trap arm or the pipe inside the wall. That is a job for a drain snake.

## How to Unclog a Kitchen Sink With a Snake

A drain snake (also called a hand auger) is a coiled steel cable in a drum with a crank. Its tip bores through the clog or grabs it. Kitchen clogs often sit 5 to 15 feet in, past the wall, so you need real length. The [DrainX 25 FT Heavy Duty Drain Auger](https://www.amazon.com/DrainX-Heavy-Ergonomic-Support-Drain/dp/B08QYND7JC/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has a 25-foot steel cable and a second handle, so one hand steadies the drum while the other cranks. That control matters in a tight sink cabinet.

### Go Through the Wall Pipe, Not the Drain Hole

Feeding a snake down the sink opening means fighting the bend of the trap, and you cannot do it through a disposal at all. Take the P-trap off, as above, and feed the cable straight into the trap arm.

1. Put on gloves and set a bucket and towel under the open pipe.
2. Pull about a foot of cable out of the drum and push it into the wall pipe.
3. Tighten the lock screw on the drum, then crank clockwise while pushing gently forward.
4. Loosen the lock, pull out another foot of cable, and repeat.
5. When you feel resistance, keep cranking and push steadily. A soft give means you have broken through. A hard stop that will not turn may be a pipe bend; back off a little and try again.
6. Crank as you pull the cable back out. Wipe it with a rag as it comes, because it will carry grease.
7. Refit the trap and flush with hot water for 2 to 3 minutes.

Rinse and dry the cable before storing it, or it will rust in the drum. A rusted cable snaps in the pipe.

## How to Unclog a Kitchen Sink With a Garbage Disposal

When the side with the disposal is backed up, the disposal itself is often the problem. Listen when you flip the switch. The sound tells you what to do.

| What happens at the switch | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Humming, no spinning | Jammed blades | Power off, free it with a hex key |
| Silence | Overload tripped or no power | Press the red reset button; check the breaker |
| Spins, but water will not drain | Clog below the disposal | Plunge, then clean the P-trap |
| Spins, grinding metal sound | Object inside | Power off, remove it with tongs |
| Water leaking from the bottom | Worn seal | Call a plumber; replacement is usually needed |

### Freeing a Jammed Disposal

Power off is not optional. Turn the wall switch off and unplug the disposal from the outlet under the sink, or switch off its breaker. Never put your hand inside a disposal, even with the power off.

1. Look under the disposal for a small six-sided hole in the center of the bottom.
2. Insert a 1/4-inch hex key. The [InSinkErator WRN-00 Jam-Buster Wrench](https://www.amazon.com/InSinkErator-WRN-00-Wrenchette-Size-Silver/dp/B000BQ7WE0/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is made for this hole and has a longer handle than a plain Allen key, so you get more turning force. Many disposals ship with one that gets lost.
3. Work the key back and forth until it turns a full circle freely.
4. Shine a flashlight into the disposal from above. Pull out anything loose with tongs or pliers.
5. Wait 10 to 15 minutes for the motor to cool.
6. Press the red reset button on the bottom until it clicks and stays in.
7. Plug it back in, run cold water and switch it on.

If the disposal now spins but the sink still holds water, the clog is in the trap or wall pipe. Plunge the disposal side with the other drain sealed, or pull the trap.

### Dishwasher Water Backing Up Into the Sink

The dishwasher hose usually joins the disposal, so a sink clog below that point sends dishwasher water into your sink. Clear the sink first. If it started right after a new disposal went in, the plastic knockout plug in the dishwasher inlet was probably never removed.

## Enzyme Drain Cleaners: Slow but Safe

Enzyme and bacteria cleaners feed on grease and food film. They work slowly, over one or more nights, and they do not harm pipes, disposals or septic systems. They will not clear a sink that does not drain at all, because a full sink just dilutes them. Use them after you clear a clog, or on a slow drain.

[Bio-Clean Drain Septic](https://www.amazon.com/Bio-clean-Drain-Septic-Bacteria-2/dp/B001N09KN4/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is a powder you mix with warm water and pour in at night, when the sink sits unused. Its bacteria eat the grease coating the pipe walls. A monthly dose keeps a grease-prone drain moving.

## Why Chemical Drain Cleaners Are a Bad Idea

Gel and crystal drain openers work by heat and harsh chemistry, usually lye or acid. On a kitchen clog they often fail, and then you have a sink full of caustic water. We keep them out of the kitchen:

- **They sit on the clog.** A kitchen clog is often a long grease plug. The chemical eats a small hole, then pools in the trap.
- **They can harm pipes and sinks.** Heat and caustic chemicals weaken old metal pipes and PVC joints, and can mark a sink finish. A good stainless sink, like the ones in our [stainless steel sink gauge guide](/blog/what-gauge-stainless-steel-sink-is-best/), deserves better.
- **Disposals and chemicals do not mix.** They can damage seals and metal parts inside the unit.
- **Never plunge after using one.** The plunger throws chemical water onto your face, hands and counters.
- **Never mix them.** Pouring a second product or vinegar on top of a drain cleaner can cause a violent reaction and toxic fumes. The same goes for mixing bleach with ammonia, a rule we also cover for [oven cleaners and other kitchen sprays](/blog/how-to-clean-an-oven/).

If someone already used one and the sink is still full, do not plunge or snake it. Leave the sink alone, run the kitchen fan and tell the plumber exactly what product went in.

## Kitchen Sink Clogged and You Tried Everything

Some clogs are past what hand tools can reach. Call a plumber when:

- Water backs up into a bathroom sink, tub or floor drain when the kitchen drains. The main line is blocked.
- A 25-foot auger goes all the way in and the sink still holds water.
- You see leaks at the wall, under the cabinet floor or around the disposal.
- There is a sewage smell or gurgling from other drains.
- A chemical drain cleaner is sitting in the pipe.
- The clog keeps coming back every few weeks, which can point to a sagging pipe in the wall.

## Method Cheat Sheet: Least to Most Force

| Method | Best for | Time | Risk to pipes or sink |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hot tap water | Slow drain, fresh grease | 5 minutes | None |
| Kettle water (metal pipes only) | Grease in metal pipes | 10 minutes | Cracks porcelain, softens PVC |
| Dish soap and hot water | Grease clogs | 15 to 20 minutes | None |
| Baking soda and vinegar | Smell and thin film | 30 minutes | None, but weak |
| Cup plunger | Most food clogs | 5 to 10 minutes | Low |
| P-trap cleaning | Food and grit in the trap | 20 to 30 minutes | Low if nuts are not forced |
| Hand auger | Clogs past the wall | 30 to 45 minutes | Low with care |
| Disposal hex key and reset | Hum or no sound | 15 minutes | None with power off |
| Enzyme cleaner | Upkeep after clearing | Overnight | None |
| Chemical drain cleaner | Not recommended | Varies | High |

## A Note for Renters

In most rentals, a simple clog is your job and real plumbing faults are the landlord's. Check your lease for where the line sits.

- **Fine to do yourself:** hot water, dish soap, plunging, resetting the disposal and using the hex key.
- **Ask first if your lease says so:** taking off the P-trap or running an auger. It is not difficult, but a cracked trap or a leak becomes your cost.
- **Skip completely:** chemical drain cleaners. If they damage the pipes or the disposal, you may be charged for repairs.
- **Report right away:** leaks, recurring clogs and backups into other drains. Text or email the landlord so you have a dated record.

Keep a plunger and a hex key in the cabinet. They are as useful as any of the [renter-friendly kitchen upgrades](/blog/renter-friendly-kitchen-upgrades/) that make a rental kitchen work better.

## How to Keep a Kitchen Sink From Clogging Again

### Keep Grease Out of the Drain

Pour cooled fat into a container, not the sink. The [Norpro Stainless Steel Grease Catcher](https://www.amazon.com/Norpro-Stainless-Grease-Catcher-Strainer/dp/B0013V3K98/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has a mesh strainer that catches crumbs, so you can reuse clean bacon fat or throw the whole lot out once it is full. Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing. That matters after searing a [steak in a cast iron skillet](/blog/how-to-cook-steak-in-cast-iron-skillet/) or emptying an [air fryer basket](/blog/how-to-clean-an-air-fryer-basket/), and it makes [cleaning stainless steel pans](/blog/how-to-clean-stainless-steel-pans/) easier too.

### Catch Scraps at the Drain

Put a strainer in every drain without a disposal. The [OXO Good Grips Silicone Sink Strainer](https://www.amazon.com/OXO-Good-Grips-Silicone-Strainer/dp/B000U0K5PU/?tag=kitchenpot-20) is flexible silicone, so you can flip it over the trash and the scraps fall out instead of sticking. Empty it after every dish session.

### Go Easy on the Disposal

A disposal is not a trash can. Keep these out of it:

- Grease, oil and fat
- Coffee grounds and eggshells
- Rice, pasta, bread and oats
- Potato peels, onion skins, celery and corn husks
- Fruit pits, bones and shells

Run cold water before, during and for about 30 seconds after you grind. Cold water keeps fat solid so the disposal can chop it and push it through, instead of melting it into a coat on the pipe.

### Send Scraps Somewhere Else

Scrape plates into a [slim kitchen trash can](/blog/best-slim-kitchen-trash-can/) beside the sink, or into an [electric kitchen composter](/blog/best-electric-kitchen-composter/) that turns peels into dry soil. A closed bin also helps [keep pests out of a small kitchen](/blog/how-to-keep-pests-out-of-a-small-kitchen/). Smaller portions help too; our tips on [cooking for one without wasting food](/blog/cook-for-one-without-wasting-food/) shrink the pile of scraps. When a leftover is past its [safe fridge life](/blog/how-long-do-leftovers-last/), bin it rather than grinding it.

Once a week, run the hottest tap water for 2 minutes after a squirt of dish soap. Keep the plunger, gloves and hex key together in one bin under the sink. That small kit belongs on any [small kitchen stocking list](/blog/how-to-stock-a-small-kitchen-for-solo-cooking/), along with the [small kitchen hacks](/blog/small-kitchen-hacks/) that keep a tight space working.

## Sink Unclogging Mistakes That Make It Worse

- **Overtightening the P-trap.** Plastic nuts crack. Hand-tight plus a little is enough.
- **Stacking products.** Drain cleaner, then vinegar, then bleach is how people get burned by fumes or splashes.
- **Ignoring the dishwasher.** A dirty filter keeps feeding the drain. If you use a [countertop dishwasher](/blog/best-countertop-dishwasher/), check where its drain hose ends up, too.
- **Letting dishes pile up in a slow sink.** Move them to a [small-kitchen dish drying rack](/blog/dish-drying-rack-ideas-for-small-kitchens/) or an [over-the-sink cutting board](/blog/best-over-the-sink-cutting-board/) so you can reach the drain.
