It's 9 p.m., you're hungry, and there's a half-finished bag of frozen fries in the freezer. You could heat the big oven for 20 minutes and still end up with fries that are pale on one side. Or you can pull out the air fryer and have hot, crispy fries in about the time it takes to make a burger.

This guide covers how to cook frozen french fries in an air fryer for every common cut, from skinny shoestrings to thick steak fries. You'll get real temperatures and times, the one mistake behind almost every batch of soggy fries, and a few tricks that help when your air fryer is small and you're only cooking for one or two.

> **Quick answer:** Preheat your air fryer to 400°F. Add frozen fries straight from the bag, filling the basket no more than about half full. Cook shoestring fries for 9 to 10 minutes, steak or waffle fries for 10 to 14 minutes, and regular straight or crinkle cut fries for 15 to 20 minutes, shaking the basket halfway. No thawing and no oil needed.

## What You Need to Air Fry Frozen Fries

You don't need much. Frozen fries are already par-fried at the factory, so the air fryer's job is just to heat them through and crisp the outside.

| Item | Why you need it | Notes |

| Basket air fryer | Circulates hot air around the fries | A small one like the [Dash Tasti-Crisp 2.6 qt](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0815KJCM1/?tag=kitchenpot-20) handles one or two servings |
| Bag of frozen fries | The main event | Any brand or cut works; check the bag for its own air fryer directions |
| Tongs or a heat-safe spatula | Tossing fries or checking doneness | Metal tongs can scratch nonstick baskets, so use silicone-tipped ones |

| Salt and seasonings | Flavor right after cooking | Season while the fries are hot so it sticks |

## How to Cook Frozen French Fries in an Air Fryer, Step by Step

This method works for any standard frozen fry. The times change by cut (see the chart in the next section), but the steps stay the same.

1. **Check the bag first.** Many brands now print air fryer directions right on the package. Those instructions were written for that exact fry, so use them as your starting point. McCain, for example, lists 360°F for 12 to 13 minutes for its [Classic Cut fries](https://mccainfoodsusa.com/products/family-favorite-fries/classic-cut-fries/), but 390°F for 6 to 8 minutes for its thinner Quick Cook Crinkle Cut.
2. **Preheat the air fryer.** Set it to 400°F (or the bag's temperature) and let it run empty for 3 to 5 minutes. McCain recommends preheating for crispier results, and it makes sense: fries that hit a hot basket start crisping right away instead of slowly thawing. If your model has no preheat button, just run it at temperature for a few minutes.
3. **Add the fries straight from the freezer.** Don't thaw them. Break up any clumps with your hands so the fries sit loose.
4. **Don't overfill the basket.** McCain suggests filling the basket only about halfway. For the crispiest fries, aim for one loose layer where fries can touch but don't pile on top of each other.
5. **Cook and shake halfway.** Slide the basket out at the halfway point and shake it hard, or toss the fries with tongs. Philips explains that shaking lets air circulate around food that's stacked in more than one layer, which is how you avoid pale, soft spots.
6. **Check a few minutes early.** Air fryers vary a lot. Pull one fry out a couple of minutes before the timer ends and bite it. It should be crisp on the outside and fluffy inside.
7. **Season and serve right away.** Tip the fries into a bowl, add salt or seasoning while they're hot, and toss. Fries lose their crunch fast as they cool, so eat them soon.

**How done is done?** The FDA suggests cooking frozen french fries to a [golden yellow color rather than a brown color](https://www.fda.gov/food/process-contaminants-food/acrylamide-and-diet-food-storage-and-food-preparation), because deep browning increases acrylamide, a chemical that forms in starchy foods cooked at high heat. McCain's packaging says the same thing in plainer terms: cook to a light golden color and don't overcook. Golden and crisp is the target, not dark brown.

## Air Fryer Frozen Fries Cooking Times by Cut

Thin fries cook fast. Thick fries need longer so the inside turns soft before the outside gets too dark. Use this chart as a starting point, then adjust for your machine.

| Type of frozen fry | Temperature | Cook time | Shake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shoestring or thin fries | 400°F | 9 to 10 min | Once, halfway |
| Curly fries | 400°F | 10 to 12 min | Once, halfway |
| Steak fries | 400°F | 10 to 12 min, longer if very thick | Once or twice |
| Waffle fries | 400°F | 12 to 14 min | Once, halfway |
| Regular straight cut | 400°F | 15 to 20 min | Every 5 min |
| Crinkle cut | 400°F | 15 to 20 min | Every 5 min |
| Sweet potato fries | 400°F | About 16 min | Every 5 min |

These ranges come from recipe testing published by [The Kitchn](https://www.thekitchn.com/frozen-fries-in-air-fryer-recipe-23278989) and Plated Cravings, and they line up with most brand packaging. You'll notice sources disagree a bit on crinkle and straight cut fries, anywhere from 10 to 20 minutes. That's because fry thickness varies so much between brands. A "quick cook" crinkle fry is much thinner than a classic one.

A few things push the time longer:

- **A fuller basket.** Two servings take longer than one, even at the same temperature.
- **A toaster-oven style air fryer.** These have more open space than a basket model. Plated Cravings suggests adding 5 to 10 minutes.
- **An older or lower-wattage machine.** Some small models run cooler than the dial says.

## How to Convert Oven Directions for an Air Fryer

Some fry bags only list oven directions. The common rule of thumb for converting oven recipes to an air fryer is to lower the temperature by about 25°F and cut the cooking time by about 20 percent, then check early.

Here's what that looks like with a bag that says "bake at 425°F for 20 to 25 minutes":

| Oven directions | Air fryer starting point |
| --- | --- |
| 425°F for 20 to 25 min | 400°F for 16 to 20 min |
| 450°F for 15 to 20 min | 425°F if your fryer goes that high, or 400°F for 12 to 16 min |
| 400°F for 18 to 22 min | 375°F for 14 to 18 min |

Most basket air fryers top out at 400°F, so when the math says 425°F, just use 400°F and add a minute or two. Start checking at the low end of the range, and remember that a half-full basket cooks faster than a packed one.

## Do You Need Oil for Frozen Fries in an Air Fryer?

No. Frozen fries are par-cooked in oil before they're frozen, and that coating is enough to crisp them in hot air. Plenty of recipe developers skip extra oil entirely for store-bought fries.

That said, a light mist can help in two cases:

- **You want deeper color.** A little oil helps some brands brown more evenly.
- **You're adding seasoning before cooking.** Garlic powder or paprika sticks better to a lightly oiled surface.

If you do add oil, use a pump mister rather than an aerosol can. Many aerosol cooking sprays contain lecithin and propellants that build up as a sticky film on nonstick baskets. ## Why Your Air Fryer Fries Are Soggy (and How to Fix It)

Most problems with air fryer fries come down to a handful of causes. Match what you see to the fix.

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft and limp all over | Basket overloaded | Fill no more than half full; cook in batches |
| Crisp on top, pale underneath | Not shaken | Shake halfway, or every 5 minutes for thick fries |
| Limp and wet from the start | Fries were thawed first | Cook straight from frozen |
| Dark outside, raw or hard inside | Heat too high for a thick cut | Drop 25°F and add 2 to 4 minutes |
| Burnt tips on thin fries | Cooked too long | Check shoestrings at 7 or 8 minutes |
| Fries stuck to the basket | Residue buildup or damaged coating | Clean the basket well; a light mist of oil helps |
| Smoke from the machine | Old grease in the drawer | Clean the basket and drawer after each use |

The biggest one by far is overcrowding. When fries are piled deep, the ones in the middle sit in their own steam. You'll get better results cooking two small batches back to back than one packed basket, and the second batch goes faster because the machine is already hot.

## Best Frozen Fries for the Air Fryer

Almost any frozen fry can go in an air fryer, but some cuts do better than others.

- **Thin and shoestring fries** crisp up the fastest and are the most forgiving. They're a good pick for small air fryers because they cook in about 10 minutes.
- **Crinkle cut fries** have more surface area, so they get crunchy ridges with a soft middle.
- **Waffle fries** are great for dipping but take up a lot of basket space for the weight. Cook fewer at a time.
- **Steak fries** need more time and may need a temperature drop so the inside cooks through before the outside darkens.
- **Sweet potato fries** have more sugar, so they brown faster and can go soft if overcrowded. Keep them in a single layer and watch the last few minutes closely.
- **Fries labeled "air fryer friendly"** are designed with air fryer directions in mind. Look for them if you use the air fryer more than the oven.

**A food safety note:** Frozen fries are par-fried, not fully cooked, and McCain's packaging states the product must be fully cooked for food safety and quality. Don't eat them straight from the freezer or pull them out while they're still hard in the middle.

## Seasoning Ideas for Air Fryer Fries

Plain salt is classic, but it's easy to change things up. Season fries in a bowl right after they come out, while the surface is still a little oily and hot.

- **Garlic parmesan:** garlic powder, grated parmesan and chopped parsley.
- **Cajun:** a store-bought Cajun blend, or paprika, garlic powder, onion powder and cayenne.
- **Salt and vinegar:** a light spritz of malt vinegar and flaky salt.
- **Everything bagel:** everything bagel seasoning straight from the jar.
- **Smoked paprika and lime:** smoked paprika, salt and a squeeze of fresh lime.

Add fresh herbs and grated cheese after cooking, not before. They burn in the air fryer's direct heat.

## How to Reheat Leftover Fries in the Air Fryer

Microwaved fries turn soft and chewy. The air fryer brings them back much closer to fresh.

1. Spread the cold fries in a single layer in the basket.
2. Heat at 350°F for about 3 to 4 minutes.
3. Shake once halfway through.
4. Check and add a minute if they need it.

Store leftover fries in an airtight container in the fridge and eat them within about 4 days. ## Small Air Fryer Tips for Cooking for One

Small air fryers make a lot of sense in a small kitchen. They heat up fast, use less energy than a full-size oven, and tuck into a cupboard when you're done. But a small basket means you need to think about quantity.

- **Cook one serving at a time.** Dash says its 2.6-quart Tasti-Crisp can fit about a pound of fries, but that's the limit, not the sweet spot. For crispy results, a single serving (roughly a generous handful) in a loose layer works best.
- **Pair fries with something that shares the temperature.** Cook fries first, keep them warm on a plate, then cook chicken tenders or fish sticks at the same temperature. The machine stays hot, so the second item goes fast.
- **Portion the bag when you open it.** Split a large bag into single-serving freezer bags. You won't have to wrestle a giant frozen block every time, and fries that stay sealed are less likely to get freezer burn.
- **Keep it where you'll use it.** If counter space is tight, store the air fryer in a lower cupboard and pull it out when needed. These [countertop organization ideas for a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/) can help you decide what earns a permanent spot.

## Air Fryer vs Oven for Frozen Fries

Both work. The difference is speed and space.

- **Speed:** An air fryer usually preheats in a few minutes and cooks most fries in 10 to 20 minutes. A full oven takes longer to heat, and the bag directions often call for 20 minutes or more, plus a flip halfway through.
- **Texture:** The air fryer's small space and strong fan tend to give crispier results for a single batch.
- **Quantity:** The oven wins for a crowd. A sheet pan holds far more fries than a small air fryer basket.
- **Energy and heat:** A small air fryer heats a small space, so your kitchen stays cooler in summer. The [most energy-efficient small kitchen appliances](/blog/most-energy-efficient-small-kitchen-appliances/) guide goes into how countertop cookers compare.

## Making Your Own Fries Instead?

This guide is about frozen fries, but fresh-cut fries work in the air fryer too. They need more prep: cut them evenly, soak them in water, dry them well and toss them with a little oil. The FDA notes that soaking raw potato slices in water for 15 to 30 minutes before frying or roasting also helps reduce acrylamide.

A [mandoline slicer](/blog/best-mandoline-slicers/) makes even fries fast. If you've never used one, read [how to use a mandoline](/blog/how-to-use-a-mandoline/) first, and always use the hand guard. Store your potatoes somewhere cool and dark, not the fridge. Our guide on [how long potatoes last](/blog/how-long-do-potatoes-last/) covers the best storage spots.

## Keep Your Air Fryer Clean for Better Fries

A dirty basket is one of the most common reasons fries stick or the machine smokes. Grease and crumbs collect in the drawer under the basket, and each time you cook, that old grease heats up again. McCain recommends cleaning your air fryer after every use to avoid grease buildup.

Let the basket cool, wash it with warm soapy water and a soft sponge, and wipe out the drawer. For baked-on residue, see our step-by-step guide on [how to clean an air fryer basket](/blog/how-to-clean-an-air-fryer-basket/).

## The Bottom Line on Air Fryer Frozen Fries

Cooking frozen french fries in an air fryer takes about three habits: preheat, keep the basket half full or less, and shake halfway. Use 400°F as your default, follow the bag's directions when it has them, and check a few minutes early. Pull the fries when they're golden and crisp, not dark brown. Get those right and you'll have fries that beat the oven version in less time, even from the smallest air fryer.
