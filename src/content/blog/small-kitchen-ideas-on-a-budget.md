Clear out first, then spend on light and hooks, not new cabinets. Most small kitchen ideas on a budget only pay off in that order, and the first step costs nothing.

The surprise is where the bigger money should go. At the $300 end, paint and new hardware on the doors you already own change the room more than any organizer. Every upgrade below is sorted by cost: free, under $25, under $100 and under $300. Each one tells you what it gains, whether that is a shelf of storage, a foot of counter or a kitchen that simply looks better.

**If you only do one thing:** Pull out everything you have not used in a year, then put daily items between hip and shoulder height. It is free, and it frees more space than any $25 organizer. Spend money only after that, on whatever still feels tight.

## How Can I Improve My Small Kitchen on a Budget?

Do the work in the right order. Most people buy organizers first and then try to make their stuff fit. That is backward, and it wastes money.

Here is the order we recommend:

1. **Clear out.** Remove anything you have not used in a year. This costs nothing.
2. **Reorganize.** Put daily items between hip and shoulder height. Move rare items up high or out of the kitchen.
3. **Add storage.** Now buy organizers sized for what is actually left.
4. **Improve the work space.** Free up counter and add light where you chop.
5. **Change the look.** Paint, hardware and decor come last, once the room works.

No pantry? Start with [how to organize a small kitchen with no pantry](/blog/how-to-organize-a-small-kitchen-with-no-pantry/). It covers where food should live when there is no closet for it.

## Start Free: Small Kitchen Hacks on a Budget That Cost Nothing

The cheapest upgrade is the stuff you remove.

### Run a One-Year Purge

Empty one cabinet at a time. For each item, ask: did I use this in the last year? If not, it leaves the kitchen.

Most small kitchens hold three kinds of dead weight:

- **Duplicates.** Two can openers, four spatulas. Keep the best one of each.
- **One-job gadgets.** An avocado slicer or a quesadilla maker. A knife or a pan does the job.
- **Oversized cookware.** A 12-quart stockpot you use once a year takes a whole base cabinet. Our guide to [the best cookware sizes for cooking for one](/blog/best-cookware-sizes-for-cooking-for-one/) shows what most small households really need.

### Move Things to Where You Use Them

Store items at the spot where you use them first. Pans go near the stove. Mugs go near the coffee maker. Cutting boards go near your main counter. Dish soap and sponges go at the sink.

### Use the Prime Zone for Daily Items

The prime zone is the space between your hips and your shoulders. In most kitchens, that means the top drawers, the bottom shelf of the upper cabinets and the wall just above the counter. Put only daily items here. Holiday platters and the roasting pan go on the top shelf or in a closet.

### Take Things Off the Counter

Every appliance on the counter costs you work space. A toaster, a coffee maker and a knife block can eat 3 feet of a 6-foot counter. Keep out only what you use daily. Our [countertop organization ideas for a small kitchen](/blog/countertop-organization-ideas-for-a-small-kitchen/) walk through what earns a spot and what does not.

### Shelf Risers

A standard upper cabinet is about 12 inches deep, and many shelves have 10 to 14 inches of open height. A short stack of plates uses only part of that height. The rest is wasted air. A wire riser splits that height into two levels, so you can stack plates below and bowls on top.

## What Is the Cheapest Way to Add Storage to a Small Kitchen?

Go vertical. Walls, the insides of doors and the gap above your cabinets cost nothing to use. You only pay for the hook or rail that holds things there.

Here is where we look first in any small kitchen:

- **Backsplash.** The space between your counter and upper cabinets is usually about 18 inches tall. A rail or magnetic strip here holds tools, knives and spices without using any counter.
- **Inside cabinet doors.** A shallow door rack holds lids, cutting boards or spice jars.
- **Above the cabinets.** On an 8-foot ceiling, there is often about a foot of space above 30-inch upper cabinets. Use matching baskets there for things you rarely need.
- **The side of the fridge.** Magnetic hooks or a magnetic rack hold foil, wraps and towels.
- **The gap beside the fridge.** Many kitchens have a 5 to 8-inch gap here. A slim rolling cart fits right in.

A magnetic knife strip is one of the best cheap moves you can make. It gets rid of a bulky knife block and frees about a square foot of counter. Our picks for the [best magnetic knife strip](/blog/best-magnetic-knife-strip/) cover sizes and how to mount one.

### Paint the Cabinet Doors

Paint is the biggest visual change for the money. Light colors make a small kitchen feel bigger because they bounce more light around the room. White, warm off-white, pale gray and soft sage are safe choices.

Use a cabinet paint or a satin or semi-gloss finish. These wipe clean, which matters near a stove. Flat paint marks easily and is hard to clean.

Here is the short method:

1. Remove the doors and hardware. Label each door with tape so it goes back in the same spot.
2. Clean every surface with a degreaser. Grease is the main reason paint peels.
3. Sand lightly, just enough to dull the shine.
4. Prime with a bonding primer.
5. Apply two thin coats of paint, with full drying time between them.
6. Let the doors cure for several days before you rehang them.

If you rent, do not paint without written permission. Use the hardware and lighting ideas below instead.

### Add One Open Shelf

One open shelf above the counter holds the plates and glasses you use daily, and it looks lighter than a closed cabinet. Stop at one. Several open shelves in a small kitchen quickly look cluttered.

## Small Kitchen Decorating Ideas on a Budget

Decor in a small kitchen should make the room feel bigger or be useful. Our rules:

- **Keep the palette short.** Pick two main colors and one metal finish. Too many colors make a small room feel busy.
- **Go light on the big surfaces.** Walls, cabinets and backsplash look best light. Put stronger color in small things, like a towel, a rug or a plant pot.
- **Add one plant.** A pot of herbs on the windowsill is both decor and food. Basil, parsley and mint grow well in a bright window.
- **Pick useful decor.** A nice wooden cutting board leaned against the backsplash looks good and you use it daily. A wooden bowl holds fruit. A glass jar holds wooden spoons.

### Lighting Changes the Mood Most

If you can only do one decorating project, do lighting. Swap a cold, blue ceiling bulb for a warm white LED, such as the [Philips Ultra Definition 2700K LED A19 Bulb](https://www.amazon.com/Philips-Ultra-Definition-LED-A19-Bulbs-2700K-8-Pack/dp/B0B927WMTY/?tag=kitchenpot-20). It has a CRI of 90 or more, a score for how true colors look, so raw chicken and ripe tomatoes look the way they should. Add under-cabinet lights. A kitchen with warm, even light looks cleaner and more expensive than one with a single harsh bulb.

LED bulbs also use far less power. More ways to cut your bills are in [how to reduce kitchen energy use in a small apartment](/blog/how-to-reduce-kitchen-energy-use-in-a-small-apartment/).

## How Do I Make a Small Kitchen Look Expensive for Cheap?

A kitchen looks expensive when it looks planned. These cheap changes have the biggest effect:

1. **Clear the counters.** A bare counter with two or three good items looks high-end. A crowded counter looks cheap, whatever it is made of.
2. **Swap the cabinet pulls.** New hardware is like new shoes for the room. Matte black, brushed brass and brushed nickel all look current.
3. **Match your containers.** Decant dry goods into one style of clear container. Matching jars look like a design choice.
4. **Use one metal finish.** If your pulls are black, pick a black faucet, rail and light fixture where you can.
5. **Hide the plastic.** Keep bags, sponges and bottles behind doors. Pour dish soap into a glass pump bottle.
6. **Keep it clean.** Clean grout, a shiny sink and a grease-free hood filter make any kitchen look cared for. Here is [how often to clean a range hood filter](/blog/how-often-to-clean-a-range-hood-filter/).

### How to Swap Cabinet Pulls Without New Holes

Measure the distance between the two screw holes on your old pulls, from center to center. Common sizes are 3 inches, 96 mm and 128 mm. Buy new pulls with the same spacing, and you will not need to drill or fill any holes. For 3-inch holes, the [Amerdeco Brushed Brass Cabinet Pulls](https://www.amazon.com/Amerdeco-Brushed-Brass-Cabinet-Pulls-3-Inch/dp/B0B9X2PL5J/?tag=kitchenpot-20) come in a 10-pack with screws. For 128 mm holes, the [Ravinte Matte Black Slim Cabinet Pulls](https://www.amazon.com/Ravinte-Matte-Black-Cabinet-Pulls-5-Inch/dp/B09D83G753/?tag=kitchenpot-20) come 25 to a pack, enough for most small kitchens.

Knobs use one hole, so any new knob fits without drilling. Switching from knobs to pulls means new holes, so renters should stick with knobs.

## How Can I Update My Kitchen Without Replacing Cabinets?

You can skip new cabinets and still get a fresh look. Here is what to do, from least to most work:

- **Clean and fix.** Tighten loose hinges and replace broken drawer slides. Doors that close properly make the whole room feel newer.
- **Swap hardware.** New pulls or knobs, as above.
- **Add under-cabinet lights.** These show off the counter and hide the age of the cabinets.
- **Refresh the backsplash.** Peel-and-stick tile goes over most smooth, clean surfaces. The [Smart Tiles Peel and Stick Subway Backsplash](https://www.amazon.com/Smart-Tiles-Peel-Stick-Backsplash-Subway/dp/B07XCHLLGP/?tag=kitchenpot-20) has a raised, glossy subway look and comes off when you move. Keep it away from direct stove heat unless the product is rated for it.
- **Paint the doors.** The biggest change, covered above.
- **Remove a few doors.** Taking the doors off one upper cabinet makes instant open shelving. Paint the inside first, because it will now show.

Inside the base cabinets, pull-out bins mean you stop crawling on the floor to reach the back.

## Small Kitchen Ideas on a Budget: IKEA Hacks That Work

Not every IKEA piece suits a small kitchen. Here is what to buy and what to skip.

### Worth Buying

- **Wall rail systems.** Rails with hooks, cups and small shelves clear a drawer for very little.
- **Three-tier rolling carts.** The classic metal cart fits beside a fridge or at the end of a counter.
- **Simple wall shelves.** A plain wood shelf on two brackets makes a good open shelf for daily dishes.
- **Wall-mounted spice racks.** The small wooden spice racks are cheap and easy to paint to match your kitchen. Mount them inside a pantry door or on the backsplash.
- **Drawer inserts.** Bamboo or plastic dividers fit standard drawers and stop clutter.

### Two IKEA Hacks We Like

- **Turn a narrow bookshelf into a pantry.** Add baskets, and it holds dry goods and small appliances in a corner or hallway.
- **Hang a rail under the upper cabinets.** Hooks on the rail hold mugs, which frees a full cabinet shelf.

### Skip These

- Freestanding islands in kitchens under 10 feet long.
- Anything you have not measured for.

## Renter-Friendly Upgrades You Can Take With You

Renters need upgrades that leave no damage and move with them.

| Renter-safe | Ask first | Avoid without permission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shelf risers, turntables, bins | Wall rails and open shelves | Painting cabinets |
| Adhesive hooks and strips | Swapping cabinet pulls | Replacing the faucet |
| Tension rods | Hardwired light fixtures | Removing cabinet doors |

## A Sample $300 Small Kitchen Makeover

Here is how we would spend $300 on a typical small rental kitchen, from first step to last:

1. **Week 1, $0.** Purge every cabinet and drawer. Move daily items into the prime zone.
2. **Week 1, about $25.** Two shelf risers and a pack of adhesive hooks for mugs and towels.
3. **Week 2, about $25.** A tension rod under the sink and a drawer divider for utensils.
4. **Week 2, about $30.** A magnetic knife strip. Retire the knife block.
5. **Week 3, about $60.** A slim rolling cart beside the fridge for oils, spices and cans.
6. **Week 3, about $40.** Plug-in LED under-cabinet lights in warm white.
7. **Week 4, about $70.** New cabinet pulls with the same hole spacing as the old ones.
8. **Week 4, about $50.** Four to six matching clear containers for dry goods.

That is about $300 for doubled shelves, a clear counter, better light and an updated look.

## Don't Make These Budget Mistakes

- **Buying organizers before decluttering.** You end up organizing things you should have thrown out. Purge first.
- **Not measuring.** A bin that is 1 inch too deep will not let the cabinet door close. Measure width, depth and height before you buy anything.
- **Filling every wall.** Vertical storage helps, but every surface covered with stuff looks chaotic. Leave some wall blank.
- **Keeping too many appliances out.** Appliances on the counter are the main reason small kitchens feel cramped. Store the ones you use weekly or less.
- **Ignoring airflow.** Grease and steam make every surface look dirty faster. Run the hood every time you cook. See our [small kitchen range hood and ventilation guide](/blog/small-kitchen-guide-range-hood-ventilation-cooking-smell/).
