## What Type of Milk Frother Do You Need?

There are four ways to put air into milk. Each one gives a different result, so this choice matters more than any brand.

| Type | How it works | Heats milk | Texture | Typical price band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Handheld wand | Battery motor spins a coil or impeller | No | Bubbly foam, or microfoam with an impeller | About $10 to $50 |
| Manual plunger | You pump a mesh screen up and down in a jar | No | Thick, dense foam | About $15 to $40 |
| Electric jug | Base heats the jug and spins a whisk | Yes | Foam, or microfoam on better models | About $30 to $200 |
| Steam wand | Machine pushes steam into the milk | Yes | Silky microfoam with practice | Part of an espresso machine |

### Handheld Wands

A handheld wand is a small motor with a spinning head. The common coil head whips in lots of air quickly, which is why the foam is light and bubbly. An impeller head with a screen, like the NanoFoamer Lithium, cuts those bubbles down to microfoam.

Wands are cheap and tiny. They are also the most flexible, since you can froth right in your mug. That makes them a good fit among the [small kitchen gadgets worth buying when you cook for one](/blog/small-kitchen-gadgets-worth-buying-when-you-cook-for-one/).

### Manual Plunger Frothers

A plunger frother looks like a tiny French press. You warm milk in it, then pump the mesh screen up and down until the milk thickens. It needs no power and lasts for years. The foam is thick and dense, closer to a classic cappuccino than to latte-art milk.

### Electric Jugs

An electric jug heats the milk and spins a whisk at the same time. Most use a magnet in the base to turn the whisk, so there is no shaft poking through the jug to leak. You pour, press a button and walk away.

### Steam Wands

A steam wand sits on an espresso machine. It shoots steam into the milk, which heats it and adds air in one step. It gives the best texture of all, but it takes practice. If you want one, look at the machines in our [espresso machine guide](/blog/best-espresso-machines/). The [Keurig K-Café models](/blog/top-5-keurig-coffee-maker/) offer a simpler built-in frother instead.

## Hot Foam vs Cold Foam

Hot foam is the cap on a cappuccino or latte. Warming milk loosens its proteins, so they wrap around air bubbles more easily. The sweet spot is about 140°F to 150°F.

Go much past 160°F and the milk starts to taste cooked and flat. The foam also gets weaker. That is why a frother that stops at a set temperature beats warming milk on high in a pan.

Cold foam is frothed cold milk spooned over iced coffee or cold brew. It needs a frother that can spin without heating. Handheld wands do this by default. With electric jugs, look for a dedicated cold button.

For the stiffest cold foam, use skim or 2% milk. Lower fat lets the proteins hold more air. A splash of syrup or cream added before frothing makes it sweeter and softer. If you make lots of iced drinks, our [iced tea maker guide](/blog/best-iced-tea-maker/) covers another easy cold drink to pair with it.

## Which Milks Froth Best?

Foam depends on two things in milk: protein, which traps air, and fat, which adds creaminess but softens the foam. Cold, fresh milk froths best every time.

| Milk | How it froths | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole dairy milk | Creamy, stable, sweet-tasting | Lattes, flat whites, latte art |
| 2% or skim dairy | Stiff, tall foam, less creamy | Cappuccinos, cold foam |
| Barista oat milk | Smooth and stable, close to whole milk | Plant-based lattes and art |
| Regular oat milk | Thin foam that fades | Stirred-in warm milk |
| Regular almond milk | Big bubbles that collapse quickly | Hot milk, not foam |
| Soy milk | Good foam, can split in very acidic coffee | Cappuccinos |

Barista versions of plant milks have added oil and ingredients that help bubbles hold, so they behave much more like dairy. They are worth buying if you froth plant milk most days. For more on swapping dairy, see our guides to [substitutes for whole milk](/blog/best-substitutes-for-whole-milk/) and [coconut milk substitutes](/blog/best-coconut-milk-substitute/).

Never froth the same milk twice. Once its proteins have been heated and stretched, they will not hold air again.

## Latte-Art Microfoam vs Stiff Foam

These are two different things, and most frothers only make one of them.

Stiff foam is a separate layer of air bubbles sitting on top of the milk. You can scoop it with a spoon. It is what a classic cappuccino has, and it is what coil whisks make.

Microfoam is milk and air blended into one smooth, glossy liquid. It looks like wet paint and pours in a steady stream. That flow is what lets you draw a heart in the crema. You need an impeller and screen, like the NanoFoamer models and the Dreo, or a steam wand.

A quick test: tilt the jug. If the milk slides out with a sheen, it is microfoam. If foam clings to the sides in clumps, it is stiff foam.

## How Much Milk Does a Frother Hold?

Every jug has two fill lines. The lower one is for foam, because frothing roughly doubles the milk's volume. The higher one is for plain hot milk.

A typical 12-ounce latte uses about 6 to 8 ounces of milk. A cappuccino uses about half that. So a jug with a 4-ounce foam line makes one cappuccino, while a large jug like the Breville covers two to four lattes in a single run.

## How to Clean a Milk Frother Without Ruining It

Milk is easy to clean right away and hard to clean an hour later. The proteins dry into a sticky film, then turn brown on the next heating cycle.

1. **Rinse right after use.** Pour in warm water as soon as you finish your drink.
2. **Wash gently.** Use a soft sponge and a drop of dish soap. Most electric jugs have a nonstick lining, and abrasive pads scratch it. Our explainer on [what nonstick cookware is](/blog/what-is-nonstick-cookware/) covers why scratches matter.
3. **Never put the base in water.** The base holds the electrics. Wipe it with a damp cloth only.
4. **Check the jug before the dishwasher.** If the jug is wired to the base, it is hand-wash only. Only lift-off jugs, like the Aeroccino4 and the Breville jug, can go in.
5. **Clean the whisk.** Pull off magnetic whisks and wash them separately. Milk hides under them.
6. **For handhelds,** run the head in a cup of hot, soapy water for a few seconds, then in clean water.

A clean [dishwasher](/blog/how-to-clean-a-dishwasher/) matters too, since a dirty filter leaves film on jugs and whisks.

## Do You Need a Frother If Your Coffee Maker Has One?

Usually not. If your machine has a steam wand or a built-in frother, it already covers hot milk and foam.

A separate frother still helps in three cases. You may want cold foam, which a steam wand cannot make. You may want to froth while the machine brews, to save time. Or you may share a kitchen and want a second station for tea lattes or hot chocolate.

For tea drinkers, a frother pairs well with a good [electric kettle](/blog/best-electric-kettle/) for matcha and chai lattes.
